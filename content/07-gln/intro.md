# L-functions, converse theorems and functoriality for GL(n)

This volume treats the automorphic representations of $GL_n$ over a number field for every $n$. Volume 3 did the case $n = 1$, where the automorphic representations are the Hecke characters and class field theory gives the reciprocity law. Volumes 4 and 5 did the case $n = 2$, where the cusp forms are the modular forms and the Maass forms. For $n \ge 3$ there is no classical theory of $q$-expansions to start from, and the group $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ of the adeles is the natural place to work. The volume needs the representation theory of Volume 6 at the local places. It uses the local Langlands correspondence of Volume 6 only to state results, and it says so at each use.

Chapter 1 defines automorphic forms and cusp forms on $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$, the cuspidal spectrum and the Hecke operators at the unramified places. It proves the Satake isomorphism in the form we need, defines the Satake parameters and the standard $L$-function, and states the theorems of multiplicity one and of Godement and Jacquet. It ends with the condition of Clozel that a cuspidal representation is algebraic, which is a condition on the infinitesimal character at the infinite places. Chapter 2 states the conjectures that organize the subject for $GL_n$: the Ramanujan conjecture, Langlands functoriality from $GL_n$ to $GL_m$ and the reciprocity conjecture of Clozel and of Fontaine and Mazur. It then describes four theorems that prove parts of them: the symmetric power functoriality of Newton and Thorne, the cyclic base change of Arthur and Clozel, the construction of Galois representations by Harris, Lan, Taylor and Thorne and by Scholze, and the global correspondence of Jacquet and Langlands in the form of Eichler.

The later chapters of the volume will treat the Rankin-Selberg method, the converse theorems of Cogdell and Piatetski-Shapiro and the Langlands-Shahidi method. Chapter 2 already uses their results in its accounts of the known cases of functoriality.

The normalizations are those of Volume 0, Chapter 3. In particular the Satake parameters are unitary: $\pi_v$ is tempered at an unramified place $v$ if and only if its Satake parameters have absolute value 1. The Lean project uses the same normalization, and the comparison with Galois representations uses the twist $|\det|^{(1-n)/2}$ of Clozel. Each statement that compares the two sides gives the twist.
