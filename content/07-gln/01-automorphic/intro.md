# Automorphic forms on GL(n)

Let $\Delta(\tau) = q \prod_{m \ge 1} (1 - q^m)^{24} = \sum_{m \ge 1} \tau(m) q^m$ be the cusp form of weight 12 and level 1 of Volume 4. Strong approximation for $SL_2$ gives $GL_2(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}) = GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \, GL_2(\mathbb{R})^+ \, GL_2(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})$. So the formula $\varphi_\Delta(\gamma g_\infty k) = \Delta(g_\infty i) \, j(g_\infty, i)^{-12} (\det g_\infty)^6$ defines a function on $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$. It is invariant under $GL_2(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})$ on the right, it is bounded, and its integral over the unipotent radical of the upper triangular matrices vanishes. At each prime $p$ it is an eigenvector of the two Hecke operators $T_{p,1}$ and $T_{p,2}$, which are sums of right translations by matrices of $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. The eigenvalues are $\tau(p) p^{-5}$ and $1$. The two roots $\alpha_p, \beta_p$ of $X^2 - \tau(p) p^{-11/2} X + 1$ are the Satake parameters of $\varphi_\Delta$ at $p$, and the Euler factor of $L(s, \Delta)$ at $p$ is $(1 - \alpha_p p^{-s})^{-1} (1 - \beta_p p^{-s})^{-1}$ after the shift $s \mapsto s + 11/2$.

Every part of this description makes sense for $GL_n$ in place of $GL_2$ and for a number field $F$ in place of $\mathbb{Q}$. A cusp form on $GL_n$ is a function on $GL_n(F) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$ whose integrals over the unipotent radicals of the parabolic subgroups vanish. At an unramified place $v$ there are $n$ Hecke operators $T_{v,1}, \ldots, T_{v,n}$, and the Satake isomorphism turns their eigenvalues into $n$ numbers $\alpha_{v,1}, \ldots, \alpha_{v,n}$. Gelbart and Jacquet proved that there is a cusp form on $GL_3(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ with the Satake parameters $\alpha_p^2, 1, \beta_p^2$ at every prime $p$, the symmetric square of $\Delta$. No classical object of weight and level describes it as simply as a $q$-expansion describes $\Delta$. So the theory for $GL_n$ starts on the adeles.

Section 1.1 fixes the group $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_F)$, its compact open subgroups and its decompositions. Section 1.2 defines automorphic forms and cusp forms and proves that cusp forms are bounded. Section 1.3 decomposes the space of cusp forms into irreducible representations and proves the tensor product theorem. Section 1.4 computes the Hecke operators at the unramified places, proves the Satake isomorphism in the form we need and defines the Satake parameters. Section 1.5 proves the Whittaker expansion of a cusp form and deduces multiplicity one, and states the strong multiplicity one theorem. Section 1.6 defines the standard $L$-function and outlines the theory of Godement and Jacquet. Section 1.7 defines the algebraic and the regular algebraic representations of Clozel through the infinitesimal character. Section 1.8 has the exercises and the notes.

We follow the book of Getz and Hahn (Graduate Texts in Mathematics 300) for the adelic setting and the Satake isomorphism, and Cogdell's notes on $L$-functions and converse theorems for $GL_n$ for the Whittaker expansion and the Godement-Jacquet theory. In this chapter $F$ is a number field, $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{A}_F$ is its ring of adeles, $\mathcal{O}$ is its ring of integers and $v$ runs over the places of $F$. At a finite place $v$, $\mathcal{O}_v$ is the ring of integers of $F_v$, $\varpi_v$ is a uniformizer and $q_v$ is the order of the residue field. We write $G = GL_n$, $B$ for the upper triangular matrices, $T$ for the diagonal matrices, $N$ for the upper triangular unipotent matrices and $Z$ for the center. The Lean vocabulary of the chapter is in the files `Langlands/Statements/Automorphic.lean`, `GlobalGL.lean` and `NumberFieldGL.lean` of the Lean project, where the number field is called $K$.
