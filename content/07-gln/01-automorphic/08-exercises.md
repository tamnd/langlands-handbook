## Exercises

1. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and $K = \prod_p GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Show that $GL_n(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}) / K \simeq GL_n(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{R})$. Use that $\mathbb{Z}$ has class number $1$ and that $\det : GL_n(\mathbb{Z}_p) \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ is surjective.
2. Let $F$ be a number field with class group $\mathrm{Cl}_F$ and $K = \prod_v GL_n(\mathcal{O}_v)$. Show that $\det$ induces a bijection from $GL_n(F) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}_f) / K$ to $\mathrm{Cl}_F$. Deduce from Theorem 1.1.5 that for $n \ge 2$ the number of connected components of $GL_n(F) \backslash GL_n(\mathbb{A}) / K K_\infty^\circ$ is the order of the narrow class group if $F$ is totally real.
3. Let $P$ be the standard parabolic subgroup of type $(n_1, n_2)$ of $GL_n$ and let $\varphi$ be an automorphic form. Show that the constant term $\varphi_P$ is left invariant under $M_P(F) U_P(\mathbb{A})$. Show that for $n = 2$ the constant term of $\varphi_f$, for $f$ a modular form, is $a_0(f)$ times a function of $g_\infty$, and conclude that $\varphi_f$ is a cusp form if and only if $f$ vanishes at all cusps.
4. Let $n = 2$ and $i = 1$. Write out the $q + 1$ representatives of Lemma 1.4.3 and check that they give the $q + 1$ lattices $L$ with $\varpi \mathcal{O}_v^2 \subset L \subset \mathcal{O}_v^2$ of index $q$. For $n = 3$ list the $q^2 + q + 1$ representatives for $i = 1$ and the $q^2 + q + 1$ representatives for $i = 2$, and verify the formula $a(s) + b(s) = i(n - i)$ in the proof of Proposition 1.4.4.
5. Show that the Gaussian binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{i}_q$, which counts the subspaces of dimension $i$ of $\mathbb{F}_q^n$, equals $\sum_{|s| = i} q^{a(s)}$, where $a(s)$ is the number of the pairs $j < k$ with $j \in s$ and $k \notin s$. Deduce the identity $\binom{n}{i}_q = q^{i} \binom{n-1}{i}_q + \binom{n-1}{i-1}_q$.
6. Let $\chi = (\chi_1, \chi_2)$ be unramified with $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$. Show that $I(\chi)$ is reducible and that its unramified constituent is one-dimensional. Compute the Satake parameters of the trivial representation of $GL_2(F_v)$, and check that they are $q^{1/2}$ and $q^{-1/2}$. Show that the trivial representation of $GL_n(F_v)$ has the Satake parameters $q^{(n-1)/2}, q^{(n-3)/2}, \ldots, q^{(1-n)/2}$.
7. Let $f \in S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ be a normalized eigenform and $p \nmid N$. Using Example 1.4.9, show that the Satake polynomial of $\varphi_f$ at $p$ is $X^2 - a_p p^{-(k-1)/2} X + 1$, and that its roots have absolute value $1$ if and only if $|a_p| \le 2 p^{(k-1)/2}$. For $\Delta$ and $p = 2$, compute $\tau(2) = -24$ and the Satake parameters, and check that they lie on the unit circle.
8. Let $f$ be a newform and $\pi = \pi_f$. Using the Shintani formula of Example 1.5.8 and the factorization of the Whittaker function, show that $a_{p^r}(f) p^{-r(k-1)/2} = \sum_{j=0}^r \alpha_p^j \beta_p^{r-j}$ for $p \nmid N$. Deduce the formal identity $\sum_r a_{p^r}(f) X^r = (1 - a_p X + p^{k-1} X^2)^{-1}$.
9. Show that the Rankin-Selberg local factor $\prod_{j, k} (1 - \alpha_j \bar \alpha_k X)^{-1}$ has nonnegative coefficients as a power series in $X$. Use $\log$ and the identity $\sum_{j,k} (\alpha_j \bar \alpha_k)^m = |\sum_j \alpha_j^m|^2$.
10. Let $n = 1$. Check that the zeta integral of Definition 1.6.3 is Tate's local zeta integral $Z(\Phi, \chi, s)$ of Volume 3, Chapter 5, and that Proposition 1.6.4 reduces to the computation $Z(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}_v}, \chi, s) = (1 - \chi(\varpi) q^{-s})^{-1}$ for $\chi$ unramified and $\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{O}_v^\times) = 1$.
11. Carry out the proof of Proposition 1.6.4 for $n = 2$ and $k = 1, 2$ by hand: list the cosets in $D_1$ and $D_2$ and check that the sums are $q^{1/2} h_1(\alpha)$ and $q h_2(\alpha)$.
12. Compute the Capelli operator for $n = 2$: $C(u) = (D_{11} + u)(D_{22} + u - 1) - D_{21} D_{12}$. Verify that it commutes with the $D_{ij}$ when they satisfy the relations of $\mathfrak{gl}_2$, and compute its eigenvalue on the representation $\mathrm{Sym}^m \mathbb{C}^2$ of highest weight $(m, 0)$.
13. Let $\chi$ be the Hecke character of an imaginary quadratic field $F$ attached to a CM elliptic curve, with $\chi_\infty(z) = z^{-1}$. Show that $\chi$ is algebraic, and compute its Capelli roots at the complex place. Show that a Hecke character of a field with a real place that is algebraic and not of finite order is a power of the norm times a character of finite order.
14. Let $\pi$ be an algebraic cuspidal representation of $GL_n(\mathbb{A})$. Show that $\pi \otimes |\det|^t$ is algebraic if and only if $t \in \mathbb{Z}$. Show that the Hecke polynomial of $\pi \otimes |\det|^{-1}$ at $v$ is $q_v^{-n} H_v(q_v X)$.

## Notes and history

The adelic theory of automorphic forms began with the work of Tamagawa, Satake and Gelfand and Piatetski-Shapiro in the late 1950s. The definition of an automorphic form with the growth condition and the finiteness under the center and $K_\infty$ is due to Harish-Chandra, and it is the definition of Borel and Jacquet in the Corvallis proceedings. The reduction theory of Section 1.1 goes back to Hermite and Minkowski, and in its adelic form to Borel and Harish-Chandra. We follow the account of Getz and Hahn, Part II, for Sections 1.1 to 1.4 and Cogdell's lecture notes on $L$-functions for $GL_n$ for Sections 1.5 and 1.6.

The discreteness of the cuspidal spectrum is due to Gelfand, Graev and Piatetski-Shapiro, and the description of the residual spectrum of $GL_n$ to Moeglin and Waldspurger. The tensor product theorem is due to Flath, with the archimedean case completed by later work, and our proof follows Bump, Chapter 3.

The spherical Hecke algebra and its isomorphism with the symmetric polynomials are due to Satake (1963). The interpretation of the Satake parameter as a conjugacy class in the dual group $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ is the starting point of Langlands' letter to Weil and of his 1970 paper [Lan70], where he defined the $L$-functions $L(s, \pi, r)$ for all representations $r$ of the dual group. The computation of the unramified zeta integral is due to Tamagawa, who treated the case of division algebras. The theorem of Godement and Jacquet appeared in 1972, and Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] had treated $GL_2$ two years before. For $n = 2$ the local theory is in Bushnell and Henniart [BH06], Chapter 6.

The Whittaker expansion is due to Shalika and to Piatetski-Shapiro, independently. The uniqueness of local Whittaker models is due to Gelfand and Kazhdan in the $p$-adic case and to Shalika in general. Strong multiplicity one is due to Piatetski-Shapiro for the places in $S$ finite and to Jacquet and Shalika in general, and the classification of the isobaric representations to Jacquet and Shalika (1981). The converse theorem for $GL_3$ is due to Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, and the general converse theorems are due to Cogdell and Piatetski-Shapiro (1994 and 1999).

The notion of an algebraic automorphic representation is due to Clozel, in his paper on motives and automorphic forms (1990), which also contains the rationality theorem 1.7.10. The distinction between C-algebraic and L-algebraic representations is due to Buzzard and Gee. The Capelli identity is from 1887. The form with the quantum determinant is in Molev's book.

The Lean vocabulary of this chapter is in `Langlands/Statements/Automorphic.lean`, `Langlands/Statements/GlobalGL.lean` and `Langlands/Statements/NumberFieldGL.lean`. It has the adelic group, the cusp forms of a level, the Hecke operators, the Satake parameters, the isobaric data, the Capelli operators and the Hecke polynomial. The project proves the sanity checks for $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ named in the text, and the uniqueness of the Hecke eigenvalues. It does not state the theorems of this chapter, with two exceptions. Hecke's theorem, Example 1.6.9, is `Langlands.HeckeFunctionalEquation` with node LG6NN4B43V. The local Langlands correspondence of Remark 1.6.6 is `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL` with node LG2E74SP8W. Both are stated and not proved.
