# Notation and normalizations

Most errors in the statements of the program are errors of normalization. A Frobenius can be arithmetic or geometric, a reciprocity map can send a uniformizer to either one, an $\varepsilon$-factor depends on an additive character, a Satake parameter can be unitary or arithmetic, and a $q$-expansion depends on the width of the cusp. Each choice changes the statement, and a statement with the wrong choice is often false.

This chapter fixes the notation and the normalizations of the whole handbook. The first section treats local and global fields, the Frobenius, the reciprocity maps and the Weil-Deligne representations, and it ends with a table of the conventions. The second section treats additive characters, Haar measures, $\varepsilon$-factors, Satake parameters and $q$-expansions. A chapter that uses another convention says so at its start.
