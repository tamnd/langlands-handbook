## The volumes and the order of reading

The handbook has fourteen volumes, numbered 0 to 13. Volume 0 is this guide. The other volumes fall into four levels. The background volumes give the number theory and the group theory that the program uses. The core volumes give the complete theory for $GL_1$ and $GL_2$. The advanced volumes give the trace formula, endoscopy, Shimura varieties and modularity. The research volumes give the methods of the present research and the open problems.

| Volume | Title | Level |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Guide: the program, the statements and the map | all |
| 1 | Number fields, local fields and adeles | background |
| 2 | Lie groups, algebraic groups and their representations | background |
| 3 | Class field theory and $GL_1$ | core |
| 4 | Modular forms and elliptic curves | core |
| 5 | Automorphic representations of $GL_2$ | core |
| 6 | Representations of $p$-adic groups and the local Langlands correspondence | core |
| 7 | L-functions, converse theorems and functoriality for $GL_n$ | core |
| 8 | The trace formula | advanced |
| 9 | Endoscopy and Arthur's classification | advanced |
| 10 | Shimura varieties and automorphic Galois representations | advanced |
| 11 | Galois deformations and modularity | advanced |
| 12 | $p$-adic Hodge theory, $p$-adic Langlands and the geometrization of local Langlands | research |
| 13 | Function fields, geometric Langlands and the open problems | research |

The style of a volume depends on its level. A background volume follows one standard textbook closely and says which one. A core volume gives complete proofs and explicit computations, and each chapter opens with an example. An advanced volume gives complete proofs for the smallest groups, such as $GL_2$, $SL_2$ and $U(1)$, and precise statements with references for the general case. A research volume gives the statements, the structure of the proofs, the main examples and a table of what is proved.

### Prerequisites

The reader of Volume 1 must know Galois theory, the basic theory of rings and modules, and point set topology. The reader of Volume 2 must also know the basic theory of manifolds and of compact groups. Volume 4 uses complex analysis in one variable, and Volume 5 uses some functional analysis, such as the spectral theorem for compact operators. From Volume 10 on, the reader must know algebraic geometry at the level of schemes and étale cohomology. Each volume states its own prerequisites in the introduction of its first chapter.

### The order of reading

Each volume needs only the volumes that the table of dependencies in Chapter 5 lists for it. Thus there are several good orders. A reader who wants the arithmetic side first can read Volumes 1, 3 and 4. A reader who wants the representation theory first can read Volumes 2, 3 and 6. Both readers meet in Volume 5, which needs Volumes 2, 3 and 4, and in Volume 7, which needs Volumes 5 and 6. A reader who knows algebraic number theory can start with Volume 3 and look back at Volume 1 when a chapter refers to it.

The volumes are written in a fixed order. Volumes 0, 1 and 3 are written first. Volumes 2, 4 and 5 come next, and then Volume 6.

### Drafts and releases

Each section of a chapter has a status in its front matter. A section is drafted when its first version is complete. It is checked when a second reading and the scripts of the project find no fault. It is reviewed when a mathematician with the right background has read it and signed it. Only a volume whose sections are all reviewed goes into a numbered release. The drafts are in the development builds, and a banner on each page marks them.
