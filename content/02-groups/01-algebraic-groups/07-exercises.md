## Exercises

1. Write down the Hopf algebra $\mathcal{O}(SL_2) = k[a, b, c, d]/(ad - bc - 1)$: give $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$ and $S$ on the generators. Check that $\Delta(ad - bc) = (ad - bc) \otimes (ad - bc)$ in $\mathcal{O}(M_2)$, and explain why this shows that $SL_2$ is a closed subgroup of $GL_2$.
2. Show that the group-like elements of $k[t, t^{-1}]$ are the powers $t^n$ with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, and deduce that $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{G}_m) = \mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{G}_m) = \{\pm 1\}$. Show directly from the Hopf algebras that $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_a, \mathbb{G}_m)$ and $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{G}_m, \mathbb{G}_a)$ are trivial. (Hint: for the second, a homomorphism is given by an element $f \in k[t, t^{-1}]$ with $f(xy) = f(x) + f(y)$.)
3. Show that the endomorphisms of $\mathbb{G}_a$ are the maps $x \mapsto f(x)$ for the polynomials $f$ with $f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y)$. Show that in characteristic $0$ these are the maps $x \mapsto c x$, and that in characteristic $p$ they are the maps $x \mapsto \sum_i c_i x^{p^i}$. Show that the Frobenius map $F(x) = x^p$ is a quotient map with kernel $\alpha_p$, and that it is a bijection on $\bar k$-points but not an isomorphism.
4. Show that $\mu_n$ is smooth if and only if the characteristic of $k$ does not divide $n$. Use the exact sequence $1 \to \mu_2 \to \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m \to 1$, where the second map is the square map, to show that a quotient map can have an infinite cokernel on $\mathbb{Q}$-points.
5. Let $g \in GL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$, where $q$ is a power of $p$. Show that $g_s$ has order prime to $p$ and $g_u$ has order a power of $p$. Deduce that $g$ is semisimple if and only if its order is prime to $p$, and unipotent if and only if its order is a power of $p$. Compute the Jordan decomposition and the order of $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in $GL_2(\mathbb{F}_5)$.
6. Show that a torus of dimension $1$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{G}_m$ or to the circle $S$. Show that $R_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{G}_m$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{G}_m \times S$ over $\mathbb{R}$, although the two groups are isomorphic over $\mathbb{C}$. (Hint: compare the real points, or compare the groups $M^\Gamma / (1 + c) M$ of the character lattices.)
7. Show that the normalizer of $D_n$ in $GL_n$ is the group of monomial matrices, that the centralizer of $D_n$ is $D_n$, and that $W(GL_n, D_n) \cong S_n$, acting on $X^*(D_n) = \mathbb{Z}^n$ by permuting the basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$. Compute $W(SL_2, T)$ and its action on $X^*(T) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
8. Show that the centre of $U_3$ is the subgroup of the matrices $1 + t E_{13}$, and that it is the derived group of $U_3$. Deduce that $U_3$ is nilpotent of class $2$. Show that $(B_n, B_n) = U_n$ for $n \ge 2$ over an infinite field, and that $B_n$ is not nilpotent.
9. Let $\mathbb{G}_m$ act on $\mathbb{G}_a$ by $t \cdot x = t x$, and let $G = \mathbb{G}_m \ltimes \mathbb{G}_a$ be the group of the matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} t & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Show that $\mathbb{G}_a$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ that is not central, and that $\mathbb{G}_m$ is not normal. Explain why this does not contradict Theorem 1.4.12. Show that $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{G}_m^r) \cong GL_r(\mathbb{Z})$, while $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{G}_a^2)$ contains $GL_2(k)$.
10. Prove the formula of Example 1.6.10 for the number of full flags in $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ by counting lines and then using induction. For $n = 3$, check that the number is $1 + 2q + 2q^2 + q^3$, and list the six permutations of $S_3$ with their numbers of inversions.
11. Show directly that the normalizer of $B_n$ in $GL_n$ is $B_n$. Deduce again that the Borel subgroups of $GL_n$ correspond bijectively to the full flags in $k^n$. For any field $k$, show that the subgroups of $SL_2$ over $k$ that are conjugate over $\bar k$ to the upper triangular group and defined over $k$ correspond to the points of $\mathbb{P}^1(k)$.
12. Use Hilbert's Theorem 90 in the form $H^1(k, \mathbb{G}_m) = 0$ to show that $PGL_n(k) = GL_n(k)/k^\times$. Prove that the sequence $SL_n(k) \to PGL_n(k) \to k^\times / k^{\times n} \to 1$ of Example 1.2.9 is exact. Deduce that $PGL_2(\mathbb{R})$ has two connected components as a real Lie group, although $PGL_2$ is connected as an algebraic group. For $k = \mathbb{F}_q$, show that the image of $SL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$ has index $\gcd(n, q - 1)$ in $PGL_n(\mathbb{F}_q)$.

## Notes and history

The theory of linear algebraic groups over an algebraically closed field was created by Kolchin and by Borel. Kolchin proved the theorem of Lie and Kolchin in his paper on the Picard and Vessiot theory of linear differential equations, in the Annals of Mathematics in 1948. Borel's paper "Groupes linéaires algébriques", in the Annals of Mathematics in 1956, introduced the fixed point theorem and proved the conjugacy of Borel subgroups and of maximal tori. Chevalley then classified the semisimple groups over an algebraically closed field in his seminar of 1956 to 1958. The language of group schemes, which this chapter uses from the start, comes from the seminar SGA 3 of Demazure and Grothendieck, held from 1962 to 1964. Cartier proved that group schemes over a field of characteristic $0$ are smooth in the early 1960s. Burnside's theorem on irreducible algebras of matrices dates from 1905. Lie's theorem on solvable Lie algebras goes back to Lie's work on transformation groups in the nineteenth century. Hopf algebras are named after Heinz Hopf, who found their structure in the cohomology of Lie groups.

The exposition follows [Mil17], Chapters 1 to 17, for the definitions and for the statements over an arbitrary field, and [Spr98], Chapters 2 to 6, for the arguments over an algebraically closed field. Milne treats group schemes from the start, and Springer treats varieties with a group structure. The two points of view agree for smooth groups over an algebraically closed field. Waterhouse's *Introduction to Affine Group Schemes* is a short introduction to the language of Hopf algebras. Borel's *Linear Algebraic Groups* and Humphreys' *Linear Algebraic Groups* give the classical theory in the language of varieties. Exercise 3 shows the first difference between characteristic $0$ and characteristic $p$. Exercise 6 is the start of the theory of real tori. The torus $R_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{G}_m$ returns as the Deligne torus in the theory of Shimura varieties in Volume 10. Exercise 12 shows the role of Galois cohomology, which returns in the theory of endoscopy in Volume 9.
