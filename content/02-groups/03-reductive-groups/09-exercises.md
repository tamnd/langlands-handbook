## Exercises

1. Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic not 2, let $n \ge 3$, and let $SO_n$ be the split special orthogonal group of Section 3.7. Show that the standard representation of $SO_n$ on $k^n$ is irreducible. Use the weights of the torus $T$ and the root groups $U_\alpha$. Deduce from Proposition 3.1.4 that $SO_n$ is reductive. What goes wrong for $n = 2$?
2. Let $P \subset GL_3$ be the subgroup of the invertible matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & b \\ 0 & d \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $A$ of size 2. Find the unipotent radical $R_u(P)$ and the radical $R(P)$, and show that $P/R_u(P) \cong GL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m$. Show that $P$ is not reductive.
3. Let $\Psi = (X, \{\pm\alpha\}, X^\vee, \{\pm\alpha^\vee\})$ be a root datum with $X \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. Show that $\Psi$ is isomorphic to the root datum of exactly one of the groups $GL_2$, $SL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m$ and $PGL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m$. Show that the map $SL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m \to GL_2$, $(g, z) \mapsto zg$, induces an isomorphism $(SL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m)/\mu_2 \cong GL_2$, and describe the corresponding map of root data.
4. Let $G = GL_3$ and $B$ the upper triangular subgroup. For each $\sigma \in S_3$, write down the conditions on the numbers $r_{ij}(g)$ of the proof of Theorem 3.5.1 that decide whether $g \in B \sigma B$. Over $\mathbb{F}_q$, compute $|B \sigma B|$ for each $\sigma$, and check that the six numbers add up to $|GL_3(\mathbb{F}_q)|$.
5. Let $W$ be the Weyl group of $Sp_{2n}$, the group of signed permutations of $e_1, \dots, e_n$. Show that $w_0 = -1$ and that $\ell(w_0) = n^2$. Show that $-1$ lies in the Weyl group of $SO_{2n}$ if and only if $n$ is even. For $n$ odd, find $w_0$ for $D_n$ and the automorphism $-w_0$ of the base.
6. Let $n \ge 2$ and $d \mid n$, and let $G = SL_n/\mu_d$. Compute the root datum of $G$ with respect to the image of the diagonal torus. Find the center of $G$ and the group $X^\vee/\mathbb{Z}R^\vee$. Show that the dual root datum is the root datum of $SL_n/\mu_{n/d}$.
7. Let $k$ have characteristic not 2. The group $SL_2 \times SL_2$ acts on $M_2(k)$ by $(g, h) \cdot x = g x h^{-1}$. Show that this action preserves the quadratic form $\det$, that it gives a homomorphism $SL_2 \times SL_2 \to SO(\det) \cong SO_4$ with kernel $\{(1, 1), (-1, -1)\}$, and that this homomorphism is surjective. Then check the isomorphism $SO_4 \cong (SL_2 \times SL_2)/\mu_2$ on root data.
8. Show that $\mathrm{Aut}(\Psi_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ for the based root datum of $GL_n$, for every $n \ge 1$. Deduce that every automorphism of $GL_n$ over $k$ is inner or is an inner automorphism composed with $g \mapsto (g^T)^{-1}$. For $n = 2$, show that $g \mapsto \det(g)^{-1} g$ is an automorphism of $GL_2$ that is not inner.
9. Show that the forms of $\mathbb{G}_m$ over $k$ correspond to the continuous homomorphisms $\Gamma \to \{\pm 1\}$, so to the separable quadratic extensions of $k$ together with the split form. Let $K/k$ be a separable quadratic extension with nontrivial automorphism $\tau$, and let $T$ be the norm one torus. Show that $x \mapsto x/\tau(x)$ induces an isomorphism $K^\times/k^\times \cong T(k)$.
10. Show that $a + bi + cj + dk \mapsto \left(\begin{smallmatrix} a + bi & c + di \\ -c + di & a - bi \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ gives an isomorphism of $SL_1(\mathbb{H})(\mathbb{R})$ onto $SU(2)$. Show that $SL_1(\mathbb{H})(\mathbb{R})$ has no unipotent element other than 1, and deduce again that $SL_1(\mathbb{H})$ is not quasi-split. What is its $\ast$-action?
11. Let $K/k$ be a separable quadratic extension and let $U_3$ be the quasi-split unitary group of Example 3.8.9. Describe the group $T(k)$ for the diagonal torus $T$. Show that the largest split subtorus of $T$ is $\{\mathrm{diag}(a, 1, a^{-1}) : a \in \mathbb{G}_m\}$, and deduce that $U_3$ is not split over $k$. Let $U$ be the unipotent radical of the upper triangular Borel subgroup. Show that $U(k)$ is the set of matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & a & b \\ 0 & 1 & -\tau(a) \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $a, b \in K$ and $b + \tau(b) = -a\tau(a)$, and that the center of $U(k)$ is the subgroup with $a = 0$.
12. Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ with the Chevalley basis $(e, h, f)$ of Example 3.6.16, and let $k$ be a field. For $c \in k^\times$ compute the matrices of $w_\alpha(c)$ and of $h_\alpha(c) = w_\alpha(c) w_\alpha(1)^{-1}$ in the basis $(e, h, f)$. Show that $h_\alpha(c) = \mathrm{Ad}(\mathrm{diag}(c, c^{-1}))$, and that $h_\alpha(c) = h(\chi)$ with $\chi(\alpha) = c^2$. Show that $h(\chi) \in E_{ad}(A_1, k)$ if and only if $\chi(\alpha)$ is a square in $k^\times$, and deduce that $H \cdot E_{ad}(A_1, k) / E_{ad}(A_1, k) \cong k^\times / k^{\times 2}$.
13. Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_3$ with the Chevalley basis of Example 3.6.17, and let $\alpha = \varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2$ and $\beta = \varepsilon_2 - \varepsilon_3$. Compute $N_{\alpha, \beta}$, $N_{\beta, \alpha}$, $N_{-\alpha, \alpha + \beta}$ and $N_{\alpha, -\alpha - \beta}$, and check that each has absolute value 1. Show that $w_\alpha(1) = \mathrm{Ad}(n)$ with $n = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and compute the signs $\eta_{\alpha, \beta}$ and $\eta_{\alpha, \alpha + \beta}$ of Proposition 3.6.13. Deduce that $w_\alpha(1) x_\beta(u) w_\alpha(1)^{-1} = x_{\alpha + \beta}(u)$ and $w_\alpha(1) x_{\alpha + \beta}(u) w_\alpha(1)^{-1} = x_\beta(-u)$.
14. Let $\Phi$ be of type $B_2$, with simple roots $\alpha$ short and $\beta$ long, so that the positive roots are $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\alpha + \beta$ and $2\alpha + \beta$. Fix a Chevalley basis. Using only Theorem 3.6.9, show that $(\mathrm{ad}\, e_\alpha)^2 e_\beta = \pm 2 e_{2\alpha + \beta}$ and that $x_\alpha(t) e_\beta = e_\beta + N_{\alpha, \beta} t e_{\alpha + \beta} \pm t^2 e_{2\alpha + \beta}$. Compute $x_\beta(u) e_\alpha$ in the same way. Explain why the coefficient of $t^2$ is not $0$ in characteristic 2, although $(\mathrm{ad}\, e_\alpha)^2$ is $0$ modulo 2 on $e_\beta$.

## Notes and history

The structure theory of this chapter is due to Chevalley. In his 1955 paper "Sur certains groupes simples" in the *Tôhoku Mathematical Journal* he constructed, for each complex simple Lie algebra and each field, the group now called a Chevalley group. In his seminar of 1956 to 1958 on the classification of algebraic Lie groups he proved the isomorphism and existence theorems over an algebraically closed field. Demazure and Grothendieck extended the theory to reductive group schemes over an arbitrary base in SGA 3 (1962 to 1964), and the notion of a root datum comes from that work. Borel and Tits treated reductive groups over an arbitrary field in their paper "Groupes réductifs" (*Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS* 27, 1965).

Bruhat found the decomposition that carries his name for the classical groups in 1954. Tits isolated the axioms of a BN pair in 1962 and later used them to build buildings. Steinberg's Yale lecture notes *Lectures on Chevalley Groups* (1967) give a direct treatment of the groups $G(k)$ by generators and relations, including the commutator formula. The classification of semisimple groups over an arbitrary field by the $\ast$-action and the anisotropic kernel is due to Satake and Tits, and Tits gave it in his Boulder lectures of 1965.

Langlands introduced the dual group and the L-group in his 1967 letter to Weil and in the following years, with exactly the duality of root data and the $\ast$-action of this chapter. Borel's article [Bor79] remains the standard reference for the passage from the based root datum to the L-group. Conrad's notes "Reductive group schemes" give the theory over a general base in the spirit of SGA 3.
