## The classical groups

This section computes the root data of the split classical groups: $GL_n$, $SL_n$, $PGL_n$, the symplectic groups $Sp_{2n}$ and the split special orthogonal groups $SO_{2n+1}$ and $SO_{2n}$. It then reads off their centers, their fundamental groups and their duals, and it explains the coincidences in low rank. These computations are used throughout the handbook, for example to find the dual groups in Chapter 5. We follow [Spr98], Chapter 7, and [Mil17], Chapter 21. For the orthogonal groups we assume that the characteristic of $k$ is not 2.

The method is the same for each group $G$. We choose a split maximal torus $T$ of diagonal matrices, we write $\mathfrak{g}$ as a sum of weight spaces of $T$, and we find the coroots by the following rule. By Theorem 3.2.5, the coroot $\alpha^\vee$ is a cocharacter of the image of $\varphi_\alpha$, which is the subgroup generated by $U_\alpha$ and $U_{-\alpha}$. Its maximal torus $T \cap \mathrm{im}(\varphi_\alpha)$ is one-dimensional. So if $\mu$ generates the cocharacter group of this torus, then $\alpha^\vee = \pm \mu$ or $\alpha^\vee = \pm 2\mu$, and $\langle \alpha, \alpha^\vee \rangle = 2$ fixes the choice.

### The general linear, special linear and projective linear groups

**Proposition 3.7.1 (type $A_{n-1}$).** Let $n \ge 2$, let $e_1, \dots, e_n$ be the standard basis of $\mathbb{Z}^n$ and $e_1^*, \dots, e_n^*$ the dual basis, and let $\varepsilon = e_1 + \dots + e_n$.
1. For $GL_n$ with the diagonal torus, $X = \mathbb{Z}^n$, $X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n$, $R = \{e_i - e_j : i \ne j\}$ and $R^\vee = \{e_i^* - e_j^* : i \ne j\}$, with $(e_i - e_j)^\vee = e_i^* - e_j^*$. The root datum is isomorphic to its dual by $e_i \mapsto e_i^*$, and the center is $\mathbb{G}_m$.
2. For $SL_n$, $X = \mathbb{Z}^n / \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon$ and $X^\vee = \{\sum a_i e_i^* : \sum a_i = 0\}$, with the images of the same roots and the same coroots. The group is simply connected, and $X/\mathbb{Z}R \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$, so the center is $\mu_n$.
3. For $PGL_n$, $X = \{\sum a_i e_i : \sum a_i = 0\}$ and $X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n / \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon^*$. The group is adjoint, and $X^\vee / \mathbb{Z}R^\vee \cong \mathbb{Z}/n$.
4. The root datum of $PGL_n$ is the dual of the root datum of $SL_n$.

*Proof.* Part 1 is Examples 3.2.2 and 3.2.6. For $SL_n$ the torus is the kernel of $\det = \varepsilon$ on the diagonal torus of $GL_n$. Restriction of characters is surjective with kernel $\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon$, and a cocharacter of the diagonal torus lies in $SL_n$ if and only if its coordinates have sum 0. The root spaces and the maps $\varphi_\alpha$ are those of $GL_n$, so the roots and coroots are the same. The lattice $\{\sum a_i e_i^* : \sum a_i = 0\}$ has the basis $e_i^* - e_{i+1}^*$, $1 \le i < n$, so $X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}R^\vee$. The map $\mathbb{Z}^n \to \mathbb{Z}$, $\sum a_i e_i \mapsto \sum a_i$, has kernel $\mathbb{Z}R$ and sends $\varepsilon$ to $n$, so $X / \mathbb{Z}R \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. By Proposition 3.3.7 the center of $SL_n$ is the diagonalizable group with this character group, which is $\mu_n$. Part 3 is the same computation for the quotient torus $T/\mathbb{G}_m$: its characters are the characters of $T$ that are trivial on the scalars, and its cocharacters are the cocharacters of $T$ modulo the cocharacter $\varepsilon^*$ of the scalars. Part 4 follows by comparing parts 2 and 3. $\square$

The base for the upper triangular Borel subgroup is $\{e_1 - e_2, \dots, e_{n-1} - e_n\}$, and the Weyl group is $S_n$ (Example 3.4.6).

### The symplectic groups

Let $J = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I_n \\ -I_n & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and let $Sp_{2n}$ be the group of $g \in GL_{2n}$ with $g^T J g = J$. It preserves the alternating form $\langle x, y \rangle = x^T J y$, for which $\langle e_i, e_{n+i} \rangle = 1$ and the other pairings of basis vectors are 0. Its Lie algebra is the set of $Y$ with $Y^T J + J Y = 0$. Write $Y = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in blocks of size $n$. Then $Y^T J + J Y = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} C - C^T & D + A^T \\ -A - D^T & B^T - B \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so
$$\mathfrak{sp}_{2n} = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & -A^T \end{pmatrix} : B = B^T, \ C = C^T \right\},$$
of dimension $n^2 + n(n+1) = n(2n+1)$. The diagonal torus is $T = \{\mathrm{diag}(t_1, \dots, t_n, t_1^{-1}, \dots, t_n^{-1})\}$, of rank $n$, and we identify $X$ and $X^\vee$ with $\mathbb{Z}^n$ through the $t_i$.

**Proposition 3.7.2 (type $C_n$).** Let $n \ge 1$. The root datum of $(Sp_{2n}, T)$ has $X = X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n$ and
$$R = \{\pm e_i \pm e_j : i < j\} \cup \{\pm 2 e_i\}, \qquad R^\vee = \{\pm e_i^* \pm e_j^* : i < j\} \cup \{\pm e_i^*\},$$
with $(\pm e_i \pm e_j)^\vee = \pm e_i^* \pm e_j^*$ and $(\pm 2 e_i)^\vee = \pm e_i^*$. The group $Sp_{2n}$ is simply connected, its center is $\mu_2 = \{\pm 1\}$, and its Weyl group is the group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^n \rtimes S_n$ of signed permutations of the $e_i$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Conjugation by $t \in T$ multiplies the entry $A_{ij}$ by $t_i t_j^{-1}$, the entry $B_{ij}$ by $t_i t_j$ and the entry $C_{ij}$ by $t_i^{-1} t_j^{-1}$. The symmetric matrices $B$ with one pair of nonzero entries $B_{ij} = B_{ji}$ give the roots $e_i + e_j$ for $i < j$ and $2e_i$ for $i = j$, and the same holds for $C$ with signs reversed. So the roots are as stated, and there are $n(n-1) + 2 \cdot n(n+1)/2 = 2n^2$ of them, in agreement with $\dim Sp_{2n} = n + 2n^2$.

For $\alpha = e_i - e_j$, the root groups $U_{\pm\alpha}$ lie in the copy of $SL_2$ that acts on $\langle e_i, e_j \rangle$ by $g$ and on $\langle e_{n+i}, e_{n+j} \rangle$ by $(g^T)^{-1}$. Its diagonal torus is the image of $e_i^* - e_j^*$, and $\langle \alpha, e_i^* - e_j^* \rangle = 2$. For $\alpha = 2e_i$, the groups $U_{\pm \alpha}$ lie in the copy of $SL_2 = Sp_2$ that acts on $\langle e_i, e_{n+i} \rangle$. Its torus is the image of $e_i^*$, and $\langle 2e_i, e_i^* \rangle = 2$, so $\alpha^\vee = e_i^*$. The other roots are images of these under the Weyl group elements below, and the coroots follow. Since $e_i^* \in R^\vee$, we get $\mathbb{Z}R^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n = X^\vee$, so $Sp_{2n}$ is simply connected. The lattice $\mathbb{Z}R$ is the set of $\sum a_i e_i$ with $\sum a_i$ even, of index 2 in $X$, so $Z(Sp_{2n})$ is the diagonalizable group with character group $\mathbb{Z}/2$, which is $\mu_2$ embedded as the scalars $\pm 1$.

The Weyl group contains the permutations of the $e_i$, from the matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} \sigma & 0 \\ 0 & \sigma \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and the sign changes $e_i \mapsto -e_i$, from the element that acts as $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ on $\langle e_i, e_{n+i} \rangle$. These generate the group of signed permutations, whose order $2^n n!$ is the order of the Weyl group of $C_n$ (Chapter 2). $\square$

The base for the Borel subgroup of the elements of $Sp_{2n}$ of the form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ 0 & (A^T)^{-1} \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $A$ upper triangular is $\{e_1 - e_2, \dots, e_{n-1} - e_n, 2e_n\}$. For $n = 1$ we get $Sp_2 = SL_2$, with $R = \{\pm 2e_1\}$, as in Example 3.3.6.

### The orthogonal groups

Let $V$ have the basis $e_0, e_{\pm 1}, \dots, e_{\pm n}$ in the odd case and $e_{\pm 1}, \dots, e_{\pm n}$ in the even case, and let
$$q(x) = x_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n x_i x_{-i} \quad (\dim V = 2n+1), \qquad q(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i x_{-i} \quad (\dim V = 2n).$$
These are the *split* quadratic forms. Over $\bar k$ every nondegenerate quadratic form is equivalent to one of them. Let $SO(q)$ be the group of $g \in SL(V)$ with $q(gx) = q(x)$. We write $SO_{2n+1}$ and $SO_{2n}$. The torus $T$ acts by $t e_i = t_i e_i$, $t e_{-i} = t_i^{-1} e_{-i}$ and $t e_0 = e_0$. It is a maximal torus, and we identify $X$ and $X^\vee$ with $\mathbb{Z}^n$.

The Lie algebra of $SO(q)$ is isomorphic, as a representation of $T$, to $\Lambda^2 V$: the vector $u \wedge v$ corresponds to the endomorphism $x \mapsto b(v, x) u - b(u, x) v$, where $b$ is the bilinear form of $q$. The weight of $e_a \wedge e_b$ is the sum of the weights of $e_a$ and $e_b$, where $e_i$ has weight $e_i$, $e_{-i}$ has weight $-e_i$ and $e_0$ has weight 0. The vectors $e_i \wedge e_{-i}$ span $\mathfrak{t}$.

**Proposition 3.7.3 (type $B_n$).** Let $n \ge 1$. The root datum of $(SO_{2n+1}, T)$ has $X = X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n$ and
$$R = \{\pm e_i \pm e_j : i < j\} \cup \{\pm e_i\}, \qquad R^\vee = \{\pm e_i^* \pm e_j^* : i < j\} \cup \{\pm 2 e_i^*\},$$
with $(\pm e_i)^\vee = \pm 2e_i^*$. The group $SO_{2n+1}$ is adjoint, $X^\vee / \mathbb{Z}R^\vee \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, and the root datum is the dual of the root datum of $Sp_{2n}$.

*Proof.* The weights of $e_a \wedge e_b$ with $a \ne -b$ are $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $i < j$, from $a, b \in \{\pm i, \pm j\}$, and $\pm e_i$, from $e_{\pm i} \wedge e_0$. This gives $2n(n-1) + 2n = 2n^2$ roots, and $n + 2n^2 = \dim \Lambda^2 V$. For $\alpha = e_i$, the groups $U_{\pm \alpha}$ act on the three-dimensional space $\langle e_i, e_0, e_{-i} \rangle$ and fix the other basis vectors. So they lie in the copy of $SO_3$ for the form $x_0^2 + x_i x_{-i}$. Its maximal torus is the image of $e_i^*$, and $\langle e_i, e_i^* \rangle = 1$, so $\alpha^\vee = 2 e_i^*$. For $\alpha = e_i - e_j$ the groups $U_{\pm \alpha}$ lie in the copy of $SL_2$ that acts on $\langle e_i, e_j \rangle$ by $g$ and on $\langle e_{-i}, e_{-j} \rangle$ by $(g^T)^{-1}$, so $\alpha^\vee = e_i^* - e_j^*$. The rest follows from the Weyl group, which is again the group of signed permutations. Since $e_i \in R$, $\mathbb{Z}R = X$ and the group is adjoint. The lattice $\mathbb{Z}R^\vee$ is the set of $\sum a_i e_i^*$ with $\sum a_i$ even. Exchanging $X$ with $X^\vee$ and $R$ with $R^\vee$ gives the root datum of Proposition 3.7.2. $\square$

**Proposition 3.7.4 (type $D_n$).** Let $n \ge 2$. The root datum of $(SO_{2n}, T)$ has $X = X^\vee = \mathbb{Z}^n$,
$$R = \{\pm e_i \pm e_j : i < j\}, \qquad R^\vee = \{\pm e_i^* \pm e_j^* : i < j\},$$
with $(\pm e_i \pm e_j)^\vee = \pm e_i^* \pm e_j^*$. The root datum is isomorphic to its dual by $e_i \mapsto e_i^*$. The center of $SO_{2n}$ is $\mu_2 = \{\pm 1\}$, and $X^\vee / \mathbb{Z}R^\vee \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. The Weyl group is the group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n-1} \rtimes S_n$ of permutations of the $e_i$ with an even number of sign changes.

*Proof.* The roots and coroots are found as in Proposition 3.7.3, without the vector $e_0$. There are $2n(n-1)$ roots, and $n + 2n(n-1) = \dim \Lambda^2 V$. The lattice $\mathbb{Z}R$ is the set of $\sum a_i e_i$ with $\sum a_i$ even, of index 2, so the center is $\mu_2$, embedded as $\pm 1$, and the same holds for the coroots. The Weyl group of $D_n$ is the group of permutations with an even number of sign changes (Chapter 2). $\square$

The bases are $\{e_1 - e_2, \dots, e_{n-1} - e_n, e_n\}$ for $B_n$ and $\{e_1 - e_2, \dots, e_{n-1} - e_n, e_{n-1} + e_n\}$ for $D_n$. The group $SO_{2n}$ is neither simply connected nor adjoint when $n \ge 2$. Its simply connected cover is the spin group $\mathrm{Spin}_{2n}$, and its adjoint quotient is $PSO_{2n} = SO_{2n}/\mu_2$. In the same way $\mathrm{Spin}_{2n+1} \to SO_{2n+1}$ is the simply connected cover, with kernel $\mu_2$, and $PSp_{2n} = Sp_{2n}/\mu_2$ is the adjoint quotient of $Sp_{2n}$.

Mathlib has `Matrix.symplecticGroup`, defined by $A J' A^T = J'$ for the matrix $J' = -J$, which gives the same group as above, and `Matrix.specialOrthogonalGroup`, defined by $A^T A = 1$, which is the orthogonal group of the form $\sum x_i^2$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ this group is compact and not split, so it is not the group $SO_n$ of this section, although the two groups become isomorphic over $\bar k$ when the characteristic is not 2.

### Summary and coincidences

The following table collects the results. The column $\pi_1$ is $X^\vee / \mathbb{Z}R^\vee$. By the remark after Corollary 3.3.8, it is the character group of the center of the dual group.

| Group | Type | Center | $\pi_1$ | Dual root datum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $GL_n$ | $A_{n-1}$ | $\mathbb{G}_m$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $GL_n$ |
| $SL_n$ | $A_{n-1}$ | $\mu_n$ | $0$ | $PGL_n$ |
| $PGL_n$ | $A_{n-1}$ | $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}/n$ | $SL_n$ |
| $Sp_{2n}$ | $C_n$ | $\mu_2$ | $0$ | $SO_{2n+1}$ |
| $SO_{2n+1}$ | $B_n$ | $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $Sp_{2n}$ |
| $SO_{2n}$ | $D_n$ | $\mu_2$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ | $SO_{2n}$ |

**Example 3.7.5 ($SO_5$ and $PSp_4$).** The root systems $B_2$ and $C_2$ are isomorphic, and the isomorphism theorem turns this into an isomorphism of groups. The root datum of $PSp_4$ has $X = \mathbb{Z}R(C_2) = \{a e_1 + b e_2 : a + b \text{ even}\}$ and the roots $\pm e_1 \pm e_2$, $\pm 2e_1$, $\pm 2e_2$. Define $f \colon \mathbb{Z}^2 \to X$ by $f(e_1) = e_1 + e_2$ and $f(e_2) = e_1 - e_2$. Its image is the set of $(a + b) e_1 + (a - b) e_2$, which is $X$, so $f$ is an isomorphism of groups. It sends the roots $\pm e_1$, $\pm e_2$, $\pm(e_1 + e_2)$, $\pm(e_1 - e_2)$ of $SO_5$ to $\pm(e_1 + e_2)$, $\pm(e_1 - e_2)$, $\pm 2e_1$, $\pm 2e_2$. Both groups are semisimple, so $f$ respects the coroots (Definition 3.6.1 and the remark after it), and $f$ is an isomorphism of root data. By Theorem 3.6.2, $SO_5 \cong PSp_4$. Concretely, $Sp_4$ acts on $\Lambda^2 k^4$, a space of dimension 6. It preserves the quadratic form $\omega \mapsto \omega \wedge \omega \in \Lambda^4 k^4 \cong k$, and it fixes the vector that corresponds to the symplectic form. This vector is not isotropic, so $Sp_4$ acts on its orthogonal complement, of dimension 5. This gives a map $Sp_4 \to SO_5$ with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$. Passing to simply connected covers, $\mathrm{Spin}_5 \cong Sp_4$.

**Example 3.7.6 (coincidences in low rank).** The isomorphisms of root systems $A_1 = B_1 = C_1$, $D_2 = A_1 \times A_1$ and $D_3 = A_3$ give the following isomorphisms of split groups.
1. $Sp_2 = SL_2$ and $SO_3 \cong PGL_2$. The group $PGL_2$ acts by conjugation on the space of $2 \times 2$ matrices of trace 0 and preserves the quadratic form $\det$, which is split.
2. $SO_4 \cong (SL_2 \times SL_2) / \mu_2$, where $\mu_2$ is embedded diagonally. The group $SL_2 \times SL_2$ acts on $M_2(k)$ by $(g, h) \cdot x = g x h^{-1}$ and preserves $\det$ (Exercise 7).
3. $SO_6 \cong SL_4 / \mu_2$. The group $SL_4$ acts on $\Lambda^2 k^4$ and preserves the quadratic form $\omega \mapsto \omega \wedge \omega$, with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$.

In each case one can check the isomorphism directly on root data, as in Example 3.7.5. The group $SO_2$ is the split torus $\mathbb{G}_m$, and the root system $D_1$ is empty.

**Remark 3.7.7 (the exceptional groups).** The other reduced irreducible root systems are $G_2$, $F_4$, $E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$ (Chapter 2). For $G_2$, $F_4$ and $E_8$ the lattices $P$ and $Q$ are equal, so there is one split group of each type. For $E_6$ and $E_7$ the group $P/Q$ is cyclic of order 3 and 2, and there are two groups of each type, the simply connected group and the adjoint group. The split group of type $G_2$ is the automorphism group of the split octonion algebra.
