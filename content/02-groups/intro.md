# Lie groups, algebraic groups and their representations

The Langlands program is about reductive groups. An automorphic representation is a representation of $G(\mathbb{A}_F)$ for a connected reductive group $G$ over a number field $F$, and a smooth representation of a $p$-adic group is a representation of $G(K)$ for a local field $K$. The parameters on the other side take values in a second group, the dual group $G^\vee$, or in the L-group ${}^L G$. Langlands defined $G^\vee$ from the root datum of $G$, by an exchange of characters and cocharacters. This volume gives the theory that this definition needs: the algebraic groups, their structure and classification by root data, their representations, and the passage to the dual group.

Chapter 1 treats algebraic groups over a field as affine group schemes, that is, as functors on algebras that a Hopf algebra represents. It defines closed subgroups, homomorphisms and quotients, and proves the Jordan decomposition. It studies the diagonalizable groups and the tori, with their character and cocharacter groups, then the unipotent and solvable groups and the theorem of Lie and Kolchin, and it ends with the fixed point theorem of Borel and the conjugacy of Borel subgroups. Chapter 2 treats the Lie algebra of an algebraic group and the semisimple Lie algebras over $\mathbb{C}$: the Killing form, the Cartan subalgebras, the root systems with their Weyl groups, bases, Cartan matrices and Dynkin diagrams, the classification, the representations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and the theory of highest weights. Chapter 3 is the structure theory of reductive groups. It attaches to a split reductive group $G$ with a maximal torus $T$ its root datum, proves the Bruhat decomposition, states the isomorphism and existence theorems, computes the root data of $GL_n$, $SL_n$, $PGL_n$, $Sp_{2n}$ and $SO_n$, and describes the forms of a split group, the quasi-split groups and the action of the Galois group on the based root datum. Chapter 4 treats representations: the classification of the irreducible representations of a split reductive group by their highest weights, the Weyl character formula, the representations of $GL_n$ and $SL_2$ in detail, and the compact Lie groups, with Haar measure, the theorem of Peter and Weyl and the relation between compact groups and complex reductive groups. Chapter 5 defines the dual group and the L-group, computes the examples that the later volumes use, states the Satake isomorphism for unramified groups, and states the local Langlands conjecture as the motivation for the whole construction.

The volume is a background volume. It follows standard textbooks closely, and it says which ones at the start of each chapter. For algebraic groups the main text is Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 170 (Cambridge 2017), which the handbook cites as [Mil17]. The second text is Springer, *Linear Algebraic Groups*, second edition (Birkhäuser 1998), cited as [Spr98]. Milne works with group schemes over an arbitrary field, and Springer works with varieties over an algebraically closed field and then with groups defined over a subfield. For Lie algebras the text is Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 9 (Springer 1972), cited as [Hum72]. For real and compact Lie groups it is Knapp, *Lie Groups Beyond an Introduction*, second edition (Birkhäuser 2002), cited as [Kna02]. For L-groups it is Borel, "Automorphic L-functions", in *Automorphic Forms, Representations and L-functions*, Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics 33, Part 2 (1979), cited as [Bor79]. Chapter 1 follows [Mil17], Chapters 1 to 17, and [Spr98], Chapters 2 to 6. Chapter 2 follows [Hum72]. Chapter 3 follows [Mil17], Chapters 19 to 23, and [Spr98], Chapters 7 to 10 and 16. Chapter 4 follows [Mil17], Chapter 22, and [Kna02], Chapters 4 and 5. Chapter 5 follows [Bor79], Sections 1 to 3 and 6. These books are not papers of the map of the project, so the front matter of the sections lists no sources, and the text cites the books by the keys above.

The volume gives full proofs when they are short. The structure theory of reductive groups contains several long proofs, for example the conjugacy of maximal tori, the isomorphism and existence theorems and the Weyl character formula. For these the volume gives the main steps and the place in the source where the reader finds the details. Most proofs in Chapters 1 and 3 work over an algebraically closed field. The volume then says what changes over a general field, because the groups of the later volumes are defined over number fields and local fields.

The reader needs linear algebra, the commutative algebra of tensor products, localization and the Nullstellensatz, and Galois theory. Chapter 1 also uses the first notions of algebraic geometry: affine and projective varieties, the Zariski topology, dimension, morphisms and complete varieties. Schemes appear only through their functors of points, and no theorem of the volume needs more scheme theory than [Mil17], Appendix A. Chapter 4 uses manifolds and measure theory at the level of a first graduate course. Chapter 5 uses the Weil group of a local field and of a number field from Volume 3, Chapter 1, and the local fields of Volume 1. Volume 3 needs only the compact groups of Chapter 4.

The notation is the notation of Volume 0, Chapter 3, with the following additions. The letter $k$ denotes a field, $\bar k$ an algebraic closure, $k^s$ the separable closure in $\bar k$, and $\Gamma_k = \mathrm{Gal}(k^s / k)$. A $k$-algebra is commutative with a unit. An algebraic group over $k$ is an affine group scheme of finite type over $k$. We write $G(R)$ for its group of points in a $k$-algebra $R$ and $\mathcal{O}(G)$ for its coordinate ring. The standard groups are $\mathbb{G}_a$, $\mathbb{G}_m$, $\mu_n$, $GL_n$, $SL_n$, $PGL_n$, $Sp_{2n}$ and $SO_n$. The Lie algebra of $G$ is $\mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{Lie}(G)$, in Gothic type. A maximal torus is $T$ and a Borel subgroup is $B$. The character group of $T$ is $X^*(T)$, the cocharacter group is $X_*(T)$, and $\langle \ , \ \rangle$ is the pairing between them. The root datum of a split reductive group is $(X^*(T), R, X_*(T), R^\vee)$, with roots $R$ and coroots $R^\vee$, and $W$ is its Weyl group. The dual group $G^\vee$ is a reductive group over $\mathbb{C}$, and the L-group is ${}^L G = G^\vee \rtimes W_F$, with the Weil group $W_F$ of Volume 0, Chapter 3.
