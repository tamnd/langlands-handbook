## Exercises

1. Let $k$ be a field. Show that $\dim \mathfrak{sp}_{2n} = n(2n+1)$. If the characteristic of $k$ is not $2$, show that $\dim \mathfrak{so}_n = n(n-1)/2$. If the characteristic is $2$, show that $\{X : X^t + X = 0\}$ is the space of symmetric matrices, of dimension $n(n+1)/2$, so that the equations $g^t g = 1$ do not give the expected Lie algebra.
2. Let $k$ have characteristic $p$ with $p \mid n$. Show that the differential $\mathfrak{sl}_n \to \mathfrak{gl}_n / k$ of the isogeny $SL_n \to PGL_n$ of Example 2.1.11 has kernel $k \cdot 1$ and that its image is an ideal of codimension $1$. Show that in characteristic $2$ the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is nilpotent, and that its Killing form is $0$.
3. Show that the Killing form of $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ is $\kappa(x, y) = 2n\, \mathrm{tr}(xy) - 2\, \mathrm{tr}(x)\, \mathrm{tr}(y)$. If the characteristic does not divide $n$, deduce that the Killing form of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ is $2n\, \mathrm{tr}(xy)$. (Hint: $\mathfrak{gl}_n \cong V \otimes V^*$ with $V = k^n$.)
4. Let $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ be the real Lie algebra of skew-hermitian $2 \times 2$ complex matrices of trace $0$. Show that its Killing form is negative definite, and that the Killing form of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ has signature $(2, 1)$. Deduce that the two real Lie algebras are not isomorphic, although $\mathfrak{su}(2) \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R}) \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$. Show that $\mathfrak{so}(3) \cong \mathfrak{su}(2)$.
5. (Clebsch-Gordan.) Show that $V(m) \otimes V(n) \cong \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(m, n)} V(m + n - 2k)$ as $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-modules. (Hint: compare the weights, and use Corollary 2.3.6.)
6. Show that $\mathfrak{sp}_4$ has a root system of type $C_2$ and that $\mathfrak{so}_5$ has a root system of type $B_2$. Find an explicit isomorphism of root systems $B_2 \to C_2$, and deduce that $\mathfrak{sp}_4 \cong \mathfrak{so}_5$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$.
7. Let $w_0$ be the longest element of the Weyl group of $A_{n-1}$, as in Example 2.5.16. Show that $-w_0$ maps $\Delta$ to $\Delta$ by $\alpha_i \mapsto \alpha_{n-i}$, and deduce that $-1 \notin W$ for $n \ge 3$. Show that for any semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ the lowest weight of $L(\lambda)$ is $w_0 \lambda$, and that the dual module $L(\lambda)^*$ is $L(-w_0 \lambda)$. What is $(F^n)^*$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_n$?
8. Compute the determinants of the Cartan matrices of $A_\ell$, $B_\ell$, $C_\ell$ and $D_\ell$. (Answer: $\ell + 1$, $2$, $2$, $4$.) Check that $\det C = 1$ for $G_2$. For $D_\ell$, show that $\Lambda / \Lambda_r$ is cyclic of order $4$ if $\ell$ is odd and is $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ if $\ell$ is even.
9. Use Corollary 2.7.12 to show that for $\mathfrak{sp}_4$, with the base $\alpha_1 = \varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2$, $\alpha_2 = 2\varepsilon_2$,
$$\dim L(a\varpi_1 + b\varpi_2) = \frac{(a+1)(b+1)(a+2b+3)(a+b+2)}{6},$$
and that the fundamental representations have dimensions $4$ and $5$. For $G_2$, with $\alpha_1$ short and $\alpha_2$ long, show that
$$\dim L(a\varpi_1 + b\varpi_2) = \frac{(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)(a+2b+3)(a+3b+4)(2a+3b+5)}{120},$$
and that the fundamental representations have dimensions $7$ and $14$.
10. Let $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_n$ and $1 \le k \le n - 1$. Show that the weights of $\Lambda^k F^n$ form a single $W$-orbit, each with multiplicity $1$. Deduce that $\Lambda^k F^n$ is irreducible and isomorphic to $L(\varpi_k)$.
11. Let $\Phi$ be a root system with base $\Delta$. Show that $\Delta^\vee = \{\alpha^\vee : \alpha \in \Delta\}$ is a base of $\Phi^\vee$, and that the positive coroots are the $\alpha^\vee$ with $\alpha \in \Phi^+$. Check that the Cartan matrix of $C_\ell$ is the transpose of the Cartan matrix of $B_\ell$.

## Notes and history

Lie introduced continuous transformation groups and their infinitesimal transformations in the 1870s. Killing found the structure theory of the simple Lie algebras over $\mathbb{C}$ in a series of papers from 1888 to 1890. He introduced the Cartan subalgebra and the roots, and he found the five exceptional types, but some of his proofs had gaps. Cartan's thesis of 1894 gave complete proofs with the criteria of Section 2.2 and the Killing form. Weyl proved complete reducibility and the character and dimension formulas in 1925 and 1926 by integration over a compact group, the method of Chapter 4. Casimir introduced his operator in 1931, and Casimir and van der Waerden gave the first algebraic proof of complete reducibility in 1935. Coxeter classified the finite reflection groups in 1934, and Witt in 1941 and Dynkin in 1946 and 1947 brought the classification of root systems to its present form with diagrams. Chevalley and Harish-Chandra proved the existence and the isomorphism theorems without the case analysis, about 1948 to 1951. Serre's presentation by generators and relations appeared in his book of 1966. Verma studied the modules $M(\lambda)$ in his thesis of 1966. The Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem goes back to Poincaré in 1900, and to Birkhoff and Witt in 1937. On the side of groups, Chevalley constructed the simple groups over all fields in 1955 and classified the semisimple algebraic groups in his seminar of 1956 to 1958. Borel developed the theory of the Lie algebra of an algebraic group in arbitrary characteristic, and Cartier proved that group schemes of finite type in characteristic $0$ are smooth.

Sections 2.2 to 2.7 follow [Hum72], which itself follows the account of Serre's *Algèbres de Lie semi-simples complexes* (1966) in many places. Bourbaki, *Groupes et algèbres de Lie*, Chapters 4 to 6, is the standard reference for root systems and Weyl groups, and its plates list the root systems, the fundamental weights and the highest roots of all types. Section 2.1 follows [Mil17], Chapter 10, and [Spr98], Chapter 4. The proof of Corollary 2.3.6 through Weyl's theorem replaces the use of the preservation of the Jordan decomposition in [Hum72], Section 7. Mathlib follows a different route to existence: `RootPairing.GeckConstruction.lieAlgebra` builds the simple Lie algebra of an irreducible root system as an explicit algebra of matrices, by a construction of Geck, and so avoids Serre's theorem. The $7$-dimensional module of $\mathfrak{g}_2$ in Exercise 9 is the action of $\mathfrak{g}_2$, the derivation algebra of the octonions, on the octonions of trace $0$.
