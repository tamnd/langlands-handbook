# Lie algebras and root systems

An algebraic group $G$ has a tangent space at the identity, and the commutator of $G$ gives this space a bracket. The result is the Lie algebra $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$. It is a linear object, so it is easier to study than $G$, and in characteristic 0 it determines the connected subgroups of $G$. For $G = GL_n$ the Lie algebra is the space of all $n \times n$ matrices with the bracket $[X, Y] = XY - YX$, and for $G = SL_n$ it is the space of matrices of trace 0.

The second part of the chapter is the structure theory of the semisimple Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, for example $\mathbb{C}$. Such a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ has a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$, and $\mathfrak{h}$ acts on $\mathfrak{g}$ with a finite set $\Phi$ of nonzero weights, the roots. The roots form a root system in a real Euclidean space. A root system has a finite group of symmetries, the Weyl group, and a base, which gives an integer matrix, the Cartan matrix. The Cartan matrix is encoded in the Dynkin diagram, and the connected Dynkin diagrams are the four infinite series $A_\ell$, $B_\ell$, $C_\ell$, $D_\ell$ and the five exceptional diagrams $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, $F_4$, $G_2$. The semisimple Lie algebra is determined by its root system up to isomorphism. The last part of the chapter classifies the finite dimensional irreducible representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ by their highest weights.

For the Langlands program the main output of the chapter is the root system with its coroots. Chapter 3 attaches to a reductive group $G$ a root datum $(X, R, X^\vee, R^\vee)$, and Chapter 5 defines the dual group $G^\vee$ by the exchange of roots and coroots. The root system of the present chapter is the part of the root datum that lives in a vector space. The duality $\Phi \mapsto \Phi^\vee$ exchanges the types $B_\ell$ and $C_\ell$, and this is why the dual group of $SO_{2\ell+1}$ is $Sp_{2\ell}(\mathbb{C})$. The theorem of the highest weight is the Lie algebra form of the classification of the representations of a reductive group in Chapter 4.

Section 2.1 defines the Lie algebra of an affine algebraic group, computes it for the classical groups, and proves that it determines the connected subgroups in characteristic 0. Section 2.2 proves the theorems of Engel and Lie, gives Cartan's criterion, and proves that a Lie algebra is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate. Section 2.3 classifies the representations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. Section 2.4 uses this classification to prove the root space decomposition of a semisimple Lie algebra and the properties of its roots. Section 2.5 treats root systems, bases and the Weyl group. Section 2.6 gives the Cartan matrix, the Dynkin diagrams, the classification and the dual root system. Section 2.7 is the theory of the highest weight. Section 2.8 has the exercises, and Section 2.9 the notes.

The chapter follows one textbook closely: Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory* (Springer, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 9, 1972). The handbook cites it as [Hum72]. Humphreys numbers his sections from 1 to 27 through the whole book, and the handbook cites them as "[Hum72], Section 8" or "[Hum72], Section 8.2". Sections 2.2 to 2.7 follow the Chapters I, II, III and VI of [Hum72] in the same order, and they state the main results of the Chapters IV and V. There is one change in the proofs: the classification of the representations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ in Section 2.3 does not use the preservation of the Jordan decomposition. Section 2.1 follows Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (Cambridge University Press, 2017), Chapter 10, and Springer, *Linear Algebraic Groups* (second edition, Birkhäuser, 1998), Chapter 4. The handbook cites these books as [Mil17] and [Spr98]. Milne works with group schemes and Springer with varieties. The two points of view differ only in positive characteristic, and Section 2.1 says where.

The reader needs linear algebra over a field, including the Jordan normal form, and the definition of an affine algebraic group from Chapter 1. Only Section 2.1 uses Chapter 1. Sections 2.2 to 2.7 use no algebraic geometry. In this chapter $\Phi$ is the set of roots, as in [Hum72]. Chapter 3 writes $R$ for the roots of a root datum, as [Mil17] does.
