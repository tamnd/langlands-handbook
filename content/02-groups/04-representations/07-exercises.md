## Exercises

1. Let $G = PGL_3$, with the fundamental weights $\varpi_1, \varpi_2$ of $SL_3$ as in Example 4.1.11. Show that $X^*(T) = \{ a \varpi_1 + b \varpi_2 : a \equiv b \bmod 3 \}$, and that the monoid $X^+$ is generated by $3 \varpi_1$, $3 \varpi_2$ and $\varpi_1 + \varpi_2$ but is not free. Show that the adjoint representation of $PGL_3$ is $L(\varpi_1 + \varpi_2)$.
2. For $SL_n$, show that $-w_0 \varpi_i = \varpi_{n-i}$, and deduce from Proposition 4.2.13 that $(\wedge^i V)^* \cong \wedge^{n-i} V$. Check this directly with the pairing $\wedge^i V \otimes \wedge^{n-i} V \to \wedge^n V = k$. For $Sp_{2n}$, show that $w_0 = -1$, so that every irreducible representation is self-dual, and give the isomorphism $V \cong V^*$ for the standard representation.
3. Let $G$ be a group of type $G_2$, with simple roots $\alpha$ (short) and $\beta$ (long), and write $\lambda = a \varpi_1 + b \varpi_2$ with $\langle \varpi_1, \alpha^\vee \rangle = 1 = \langle \varpi_2, \beta^\vee \rangle$. Using the six positive roots $\alpha, \beta, \alpha + \beta, 2\alpha + \beta, 3 \alpha + \beta, 3 \alpha + 2 \beta$, show that, with $A = a + 1$ and $B = b + 1$,
$$\dim L(\lambda) = \frac{A B (A + B)(A + 2B)(A + 3B)(2A + 3B)}{120}.$$
Deduce that $\dim L(\varpi_1) = 7$ and $\dim L(\varpi_2) = 14$.
4. For $SL_2$ in characteristic $0$, show that $\mathrm{Sym}^2 L(n) \cong \bigoplus_{j} L(2n - 4j)$ and $\wedge^2 L(n) \cong \bigoplus_{j} L(2n - 4j - 2)$, where $j$ runs over the integers $\ge 0$ for which the weight is $\ge 0$. Which of the summands $L(2n - 2j)$ of $L(n) \otimes L(n)$ are symmetric?
5. For $SL_3$ in characteristic $0$, use Theorem 4.3.10 to show that $L(\varpi_1) \otimes L(\varpi_2) \cong L(\varpi_1 + \varpi_2) \oplus L(0)$ and $L(\varpi_1) \otimes L(\varpi_1) \cong L(2 \varpi_1) \oplus L(\varpi_2)$. Identify the summands with $\mathfrak{sl}_3$, the trivial representation, $\mathrm{Sym}^2 V$ and $\wedge^2 V \cong V^*$.
6. Show from Definition 4.4.5 that $s_{(k, 0, \dots, 0)} = h_k$ and $s_{(1, \dots, 1, 0, \dots, 0)} = e_k$ in $n$ variables. Compute $s_{(2,1,0)}(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ as a sum of monomials, and check that its value at $x_1 = x_2 = x_3 = 1$ agrees with Theorem 4.4.6.
7. For $GL_n$ with $n \ge 3$, show that $V \otimes \wedge^2 V \cong \wedge^3 V \oplus L(2, 1, 0, \dots, 0)$, and that $\dim L(2, 1, 0, \dots, 0) = n(n^2 - 1)/3$. Check this value with the dimension formula of Theorem 4.4.6.
8. Show that $|\det g|^{-n} \prod_{i,j} d g_{ij}$ is a left and right Haar measure on $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$. For the group $P$ of the matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $a \neq 0$, show that $da \, db / a^2$ is a left Haar measure and $da \, db / |a|$ is a right Haar measure, compute the modular function, and check part 3 of Proposition 4.5.2.
9. Identify $SU(2)$ with the unit sphere $S^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ as in Example 4.5.3, and write $g = (x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3)$, so that $\mathrm{tr}\, g = 2 x_0$. Show that $g$ is conjugate to $t_\theta$ with $x_0 = \cos \theta$, and compute the image of the Haar measure under $g \mapsto \theta \in [0, \pi]$ directly from the geometry of $S^3$. Check that you get $\frac{2}{\pi} \sin^2 \theta \, d\theta$, and verify by a direct integration that the functions $\sin((n+1)\theta) / \sin \theta$ are orthonormal for this measure.
10. Show that every continuous homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ has finite image, and determine all of them. For $p$ odd, show that $a \mapsto (1 + p)^a$ is a well defined, continuous and injective homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$.
11. Show that $\mathfrak{u}(n) \otimes_\mathbb{R} \mathbb{C} \cong \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb{C})$. Use the polar decomposition $GL_n(\mathbb{C}) = U(n) \cdot P$, where $P$ is the set of positive definite Hermitian matrices, to show that $U(n)$ is a maximal compact subgroup of $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. Show that the algebra of matrix coefficients of $SO(2)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[z, z^{-1}]$, so that $SO(2)_\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{G}_m$ in Theorem 4.6.9.
12. Show that every finite-dimensional unitary representation of $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$ is trivial. (Hint: the differential gives an inner product on $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ for which every $\mathrm{ad}\, X$ is skew-symmetric, if it is injective. Look at the eigenvalues of $\mathrm{ad}\, H$ for $H = \mathrm{diag}(1, -1)$.) Compare with Proposition 4.6.1.

## Notes and history

The representations of the classical groups were studied first through invariant theory. Schur, in his dissertation of 1901, found the polynomial representations of $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ and their characters, the Schur polynomials, and he related them to the representations of the symmetric group. Cartan classified in 1913 the irreducible representations of the complex simple Lie algebras by their highest weights. Weyl, in his papers of 1925 and 1926 on the representations of the semisimple groups, proved complete reducibility by the unitary trick, introduced the integration formula and proved the character formula and the dimension formula by the analytic method of Section 4.6. The theorem of Peter and Weyl appeared in 1927. Haar constructed the invariant measure on locally compact second countable groups in 1933, and von Neumann and Weil removed the countability hypothesis and proved uniqueness. The duality between compact groups and their representations goes back to Tannaka in 1939, and Chevalley, in his *Theory of Lie Groups* of 1946, gave the construction of the complexification of Theorem 4.6.9.

The realization of $L(\lambda)$ as sections of a line bundle on the flag variety is the theorem of Borel and Weil, which Serre presented in the Séminaire Bourbaki in 1954. Bott extended it in 1957 to the higher cohomology groups, and Kempf proved the vanishing theorem in characteristic $p$ in 1976. Freudenthal gave a recursive formula for the weight multiplicities in 1954, and Kostant proved the formula of Theorem 4.3.8 in 1959. Bernstein, Gelfand and Gelfand constructed a resolution of $L(\lambda)$ by direct sums of Verma modules, which gives the character formula at once. For representations in characteristic $p$, Steinberg proved the tensor product theorem in 1963, and Lusztig stated his conjecture on the characters of the restricted irreducible modules in 1980. Andersen, Jantzen and Soergel proved it for $p$ large enough, without an explicit bound, and Williamson showed in 2017 that it fails for many primes larger than the expected bound. The Sato and Tate conjecture was proved by Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor for elliptic curves over totally real fields with a prime of multiplicative reduction, and in general by Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor in 2011.

For the algebraic theory, [Mil17], Chapter 22, is the main source of Sections 4.1 and 4.2, and Jantzen, *Representations of Algebraic Groups*, is the standard reference for characteristic $p$. Fulton and Harris, *Representation Theory*, works out the representations of the classical groups with many examples, including Schur and Weyl duality. For compact groups, [Kna02], Chapters 4, 5 and 8, and Bröcker and tom Dieck, *Representations of Compact Lie Groups*, give complete proofs of the results of Sections 4.5 and 4.6.
