# Representations

A representation of a split reductive group $G$ is a comodule over $\mathcal{O}(G)$, and its restriction to a split maximal torus $T$ is a sum of characters. These characters are the weights of the representation, and they lie in the lattice $X^*(T)$ of the root datum. The theory of the highest weight says that the irreducible representations of $G$ are classified by one weight each: for each dominant weight $\lambda$ there is exactly one irreducible representation $L(\lambda)$ whose weights all lie below $\lambda$. In characteristic $0$ every representation is a direct sum of irreducible ones, and the formula of Weyl gives the character of $L(\lambda)$, and in particular its dimension, as a quotient of two alternating sums over the Weyl group. So the root datum, which Chapter 3 attaches to $G$, determines the whole category of representations of $G$.

The same theory holds for compact Lie groups, and it was found there first. A compact group has a Haar measure of total mass $1$. Averaging over the group makes every finite-dimensional representation unitary, hence completely reducible, and the matrix coefficients of the irreducible representations satisfy the orthogonality relations of Schur. The theorem of Peter and Weyl says that these matrix coefficients span a dense subspace of $L^2(K)$, as the characters $e^{in\theta}$ do for the circle. A compact connected Lie group $K$ has maximal tori and roots, and its complexification $K_\mathbb{C}$ is a complex reductive group with the same root datum and the same representations. This passage between compact groups and complex reductive groups is the reason why the dual group $G^\vee$ of Chapter 5 can be studied through its maximal compact subgroup, and why the Ramanujan conjecture and the Sato and Tate distribution are statements about compact groups.

Sections 4.1 and 4.2 define weights, characters and dominant weights, and prove the classification by the highest weight with the induced modules $H^0(\lambda)$. Section 4.3 treats the formula of Weyl, the dimension formula, the formula of Kostant and tensor products, and Section 4.4 works out $SL_2$ and $GL_n$, with the Schur polynomials as characters. Section 4.5 treats Haar measure, the orthogonality relations and the theorem of Peter and Weyl. Section 4.6 treats compact Lie groups: maximal tori and roots, the integration formula of Weyl with a complete proof of the character formula, and complexification. The examples to keep in mind are $SL_2$ with the representations $\mathrm{Sym}^n(k^2)$, $GL_n$ with its exterior powers, and $SU(2)$ with the measure $\frac{2}{\pi} \sin^2 \theta \, d\theta$ on its conjugacy classes.

The chapter follows [Mil17], Chapter 22, for the algebraic groups, and [Kna02], Chapters 4 and 5, for the compact groups and for the theorem of the highest weight. For the Lie algebra side we use [Hum72], Chapter VI. The reader needs Chapters 1 to 3, the theory of the highest weight for semisimple Lie algebras from Chapter 2, and measure theory and manifolds at the level of a first graduate course. Chapter 5 uses Sections 4.1 to 4.3 for the representations of the dual group, and Volume 3 uses Section 4.5 for the characters of compact and profinite groups.
