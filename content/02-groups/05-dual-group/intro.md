# The dual group and the L-group

A split reductive group $G$ over a field is determined by its root datum $(X, R, X^\vee, R^\vee)$: the characters and the cocharacters of a maximal torus, with the roots and the coroots. The four entries play symmetric roles. If we exchange the characters with the cocharacters and the roots with the coroots, we get the root datum $(X^\vee, R^\vee, X, R)$ of a second reductive group. Langlands took this second group over $\mathbb{C}$ and called it the dual group $G^\vee$. Its maximal torus is $T^\vee = X^*(T) \otimes \mathbb{C}^\times$, so a point of $T^\vee$ is the same as a homomorphism $X_*(T) \to \mathbb{C}^\times$. For a $p$-adic field $K$ the group $T(K)/T(\mathcal{O}_K)$ is $X_*(T)$, so a point of $T^\vee$ is also an unramified character of $T(K)$. This simple remark is the first link between the representations of $G(K)$ and the conjugacy classes of $G^\vee$.

The chapter defines the dual group and computes it for the groups that the later volumes use. The dual of $GL_n$ is $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. The dual of $SL_n$ is $PGL_n(\mathbb{C})$, and the dual of $PGL_n$ is $SL_n(\mathbb{C})$. The dual of $Sp_{2n}$ is $SO_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})$, and the dual of $SO_{2n+1}$ is $Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$. The group $SO_{2n}$ is its own dual. For a group $G$ that is not split, the Galois group acts on the based root datum, and this action passes to $G^\vee$ through a pinning. The L-group is the semidirect product ${}^L G = G^\vee \rtimes W_F$ with the Weil group. For an unramified group over a $p$-adic field, the Satake isomorphism identifies the spherical Hecke algebra with the algebra of class functions on the coset $G^\vee \rtimes \Phi$ of a Frobenius, so that the unramified representations correspond to semisimple conjugacy classes in the L-group. The L-parameters generalize these classes, and the local Langlands conjecture predicts that they classify the irreducible representations of $G(K)$ up to finite ambiguity.

Section 5.1 defines the dual root datum and proves its first properties: the duality of the Weyl groups, of the bases and of the lattices that give the centre and the fundamental group. Section 5.2 defines the dual group with its pinning. Section 5.3 computes the examples. Section 5.4 defines the L-group of a group over a local or a global field, with the examples of tori, unitary groups and restrictions of scalars, and the L-homomorphisms. Section 5.5 treats unramified groups over a $p$-adic field and states the Satake isomorphism. Section 5.6 defines the L-parameters and states the local Langlands conjecture. The example to keep in mind is the pair $SL_2$ and $PGL_2$: their duals are $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ and $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$, and the projective representations of the Weil group give the L-packets of $SL_2$.

The chapter follows [Bor79], Sections 1 to 3 and 6. Borel's text is still the standard reference for the L-group. The original source is Langlands' letter of questions [Lan70]. The chapter uses the root data of Chapter 3, the representations of Chapter 4, the Weil group of a local field from Volume 3, Chapter 1, and the global Weil group from Volume 3, Chapter 7. The normalizations are those of Volume 0, Chapter 3. In particular $\Phi$ is a geometric Frobenius, and the Satake parameters are unitary.
