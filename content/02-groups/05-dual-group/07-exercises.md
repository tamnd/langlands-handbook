## Exercises

1. Show that a root datum $(X, R, X^\vee, R^\vee)$ with $X \cong \mathbb{Z}$ and $R \ne \emptyset$ is isomorphic to $\Psi_{SL}$, to $\Psi_{PGL}$ of Example 5.1.6, or to the non-reduced datum with $R = \{\pm 1, \pm 2\}$ and $R^\vee = \{\pm 2, \pm 1\}$. Then show that the root data of $GL_2$, $SL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m$ and $PGL_2 \times \mathbb{G}_m$ are pairwise non-isomorphic, by computing the torsion of $X/Q$ and of $X^\vee/Q^\vee$, and determine the dual group of each of them.
2. Let $E/F$ be a separable quadratic extension of local fields, let $\sigma \in W_F \setminus W_E$, and let $G = R_{E/F} GL_n$. Show that ${}^L G = (GL_n(\mathbb{C}) \times GL_n(\mathbb{C})) \rtimes W_F$, where $W_E$ acts trivially and $\sigma$ exchanges the two factors. Let $\varphi : W_F \to {}^L G$ be an L-parameter that is trivial on $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$, and write $\varphi(w) = ((a(w), b(w)), w)$ for $w \in W_E$. Show that $\varphi \mapsto a$ induces a bijection from the equivalence classes of such parameters onto the conjugacy classes of semisimple continuous homomorphisms $W_E \to GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. (Normalize $\varphi(\sigma)$ first.)
3. Let $G$ be a split reductive group. Show that $Z(G^\vee)$ is connected if and only if the derived group of $G$ is simply connected. Check the statement for $GL_n$, $Sp_{2n}$ and $SO_{2n+1}$, and compute $Z(G^\vee)$ in each case.
4. Let $\Delta = \{\alpha, \beta\}$ be a base of the root system $G_2$, with $\alpha$ short, so that $\langle \alpha, \beta^\vee \rangle = -1$ and $\langle \beta, \alpha^\vee \rangle = -3$. Let $\Psi$ be the root datum with $X = \mathbb{Z}\alpha + \mathbb{Z}\beta$. Show that $X = P$, and that there is an isomorphism of based root data $\Psi_0^\vee \to \Psi_0$ that sends $\alpha^\vee$ to $\beta$ and $\beta^\vee$ to $\alpha$. Show that under the form $B$ of Proposition 5.1.7 the coroots of the long roots are the short coroots.
5. Let $G$ be a split reductive group with derived group $G_{\mathrm{der}}$ and $D = G/G_{\mathrm{der}}$. Show that $D^\vee = Z(G^\vee)^\circ$ and that $(G_{\mathrm{der}})^\vee = G^\vee / Z(G^\vee)^\circ$, by computing the character and cocharacter groups as in Remark 5.2.9. Check this for $G = GL_n$.
6. Let $\Phi_n$ and $\theta(g) = \Phi_n \, {}^t g^{-1} \Phi_n^{-1}$ be as in Proposition 5.4.5. Show that $\theta$ preserves the diagonal torus, the upper triangular Borel subgroup and the standard pinning of $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. Show that $\theta$ is the identity on $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$, that $\theta(g) = \det(g)^{-1} g$ on $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$, and that $\theta$ is not an inner automorphism of $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ for any $n \ge 1$.
7. Let $G = GL_2$ over a non-archimedean local field, with $K = GL_2(\mathcal{O})$. Let $c_1 = \mathbf{1}_{K \mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1) K}$ and $c_2 = \mathbf{1}_{K \varpi K}$. Show that $\mathcal{S}(c_1) = q^{1/2}(z_1 + z_2)$ and $\mathcal{S}(c_2) = z_1 z_2$, where $z_i = e^{e_i^\vee}$ are the coordinates on the diagonal torus of $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$. Deduce the formula $\mathcal{S}(c_\lambda) = q^{1/2}(z + z^{-1})$ of Example 5.5.10 for $PGL_2$, and compare it with Theorem 5.5.6.
8. Let $E/F$ be an unramified quadratic extension of non-archimedean local fields, and let $\Phi$ act on $\mathbb{C}^\times$ by $z \mapsto z^{-1}$ and on $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^2$ by $(a, b) \mapsto (b, a)$. (a) Show that the norm-one torus $T = U(1)$ has $T(F) = T(\mathcal{O})$, so that its only unramified character is trivial, and show that $\mathbb{C}^\times \rtimes \Phi$ is a single conjugacy class under $\mathbb{C}^\times$. (b) Show that the semisimple classes in $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^2 \rtimes \Phi$ under $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^2$ are classified by the product $ab$, and match them with the unramified characters of $E^\times = (R_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_m)(F)$. Compare both parts with Theorem 5.5.11.
9. Let $\varphi : W_F \to PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ be an unramified parameter for $SL_2$. (a) Show that $\varphi$ lifts to an unramified parameter $\tilde\varphi : W_F \to GL_2(\mathbb{C})$, and that two lifts differ by an unramified character of $W_F$. (b) Let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be unramified characters of $F^\times$. Show that the restriction of the principal series $I(\chi_1, \chi_2)$ of $GL_2(F)$ to $SL_2(F)$ is the principal series $I(\chi)$ of $SL_2(F)$ with $\chi(\mathrm{diag}(t, t^{-1})) = \chi_1(t)\chi_2(t)^{-1}$. Deduce that the Satake parameter of the unramified constituent is the image of $\mathrm{diag}(\chi_1(\varpi), \chi_2(\varpi))$ in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$.
10. Let $V \subset PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ be the Klein four group of Example 5.6.6. Show that the centralizer of $V$ in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is $V$. Show that the preimage of $V$ in $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the quaternion group of order $8$, and that its centralizer in $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is $\{\pm 1\}$. Deduce that $S_\varphi = V$ for the parameter $\varphi$ of Example 5.6.6 as a parameter of $SL_2$, and that $S_{\tilde\varphi}$ is trivial for its lift $\tilde\varphi$ as a parameter of $GL_2$.
11. Let $\pi$ be an unramified representation of $GL_2(F)$ with Satake parameter $\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, \beta)$, and put $X = q^{-s}$. Compute $L(s, \pi, \mathrm{Sym}^2)$ and $L(s, \pi, \mathrm{std} \otimes \mathrm{std})$, and show that $L(s, \pi, \mathrm{std} \otimes \mathrm{std}) = L(s, \pi, \mathrm{Sym}^2) L(s, \omega_\pi)$, where $\omega_\pi$ is the central character. Show that $\mathrm{Ad} = \mathrm{Sym}^2 \otimes \det^{-1}$ factors through $PGL_2(\mathbb{C}) = SL_2^\vee$, so that it defines an L-homomorphism ${}^L SL_2 \to {}^L GL_3$, and compute $L(s, \pi, \mathrm{Ad})$.
12. Let $J_0$ be the antidiagonal $n \times n$ matrix with all antidiagonal entries $1$, and let $\theta_0(g) = J_0 \, {}^t g^{-1} J_0^{-1}$. Show that $\theta_0$ is an involution of $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ that keeps the diagonal torus and the upper triangular Borel subgroup, and that $\theta_0(x_{i, i+1}(t)) = x_{n-i, n+1-i}(-t)$. Find a diagonal matrix $d$ with $\theta = \mathrm{Int}(d) \circ \theta_0$, where $\theta$ is the involution of Lemma 5.4.17. Show that the fixed points of $\theta$ form an orthogonal group for $n$ odd and a symplectic group for $n$ even, and that the fixed points of $\theta_0$ form an orthogonal group for all $n$. Deduce that $\theta_0 = \mathrm{Int}(h) \circ \theta \circ \mathrm{Int}(h)^{-1}$ for some $h \in GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ if $n$ is odd, and that no such $h$ exists if $n$ is even. Explain why only $\theta$ is a pinned automorphism.
13. Let $\theta$ be as in Lemma 5.4.17. Show that $\theta(x_{ij}(t)) = x_{j' i'}((-1)^{j - i + 1} t)$ for $i \ne j$. For $i < j$, prove this a second time from the case $j = i + 1$ of Proposition 5.4.18, by induction on $j - i$ with the commutator formula of Proposition 5.4.13 part 3. Deduce that $\theta(x_{1n}(t)) = x_{1n}((-1)^n t)$.
14. Let $P$ be the root pairing of $GL_n$ over $\mathbb{C}$, with $M = N = \mathbb{C}^n$, the standard pairing, and the roots and coroots $e_i - e_j$ for $i \ne j$. Let $u = (1, \dots, 1)$ and $g(x) = x - \tfrac{2}{n} (\sum_i x_i) u$. Show that $(g, g, \mathrm{id})$ is an automorphism of $P$ that fixes every root and every coroot, and that $g \ne 1$. Deduce that part 3 of Lemma 5.4.10 fails without the hypothesis that the roots span $M$, and that the analogue of Proposition 5.4.15 part 1 fails for the root pairing of $GL_n$.
15. Let $P$ be the root system $A_2$ over $\mathbb{C}$, with the base $\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$. Show that $\mathrm{Aut}(P, \Delta)$ has exactly two elements. Let $g$ be the nontrivial element and $\varphi_g$ the automorphism of Proposition 5.4.11. Show that $\varphi_g([e_1, e_2]) = -[e_1, e_2]$. Compare this with the formula $\theta(x_{13}(t)) = x_{13}(-t)$ of Example 5.4.20, through the isomorphism $\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb{C})$ that maps $e_1$ and $e_2$ to $E_{12}$ and $E_{23}$.

## Notes and history

Langlands introduced the L-group in his letter to Weil of January 1967 and in the lectures [Lan70] of 1970, under the name of the associate group. He used the Galois form, and he defined the unramified L-factors of Definition 5.5.12 with it. He arrived at the dual group from the Satake isomorphism of Satake, "Theory of spherical functions on reductive algebraic groups over $\mathfrak{p}$-adic fields" (Publ. Math. IHES 1963), in which the Weyl group invariants on the cocharacter lattice appear. Langlands observed that $\mathbb{C}[X_*(T)]^W$ is the complexified representation ring of a complex group whose roots are the coroots of $G$. The dual root datum and the classification of reductive groups by root data come from the Séminaire Chevalley and from SGA 3, where Demazure proved the isomorphism and existence theorems over any base. Borel's Corvallis article [Bor79] gave the definitive account of the L-group, the admissible homomorphisms and the desiderata for the local correspondence, and it is still the standard reference. The Weil-Deligne group is due to Deligne, in his Antwerp article on the constants of the functional equation (1973), and the form $W_F \times SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ became standard later. The component groups $S_\varphi$ appeared in the work of Labesse and Langlands on $SL_2$, and Arthur, Vogan and Kottwitz shaped the refined conjecture. The geometric Satake equivalence of Lusztig, Ginzburg, and Mirković and Vilonen gives a construction of $G^\vee$ from $G$ that does not pass through the root datum: $G^\vee$ is the group of automorphisms of a tensor category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian of $G$.

The exposition follows [Bor79], Sections 1 to 3 and 6, for the L-group and the unramified case, and [Spr98], Chapters 7, 9, 10 and 16, for the root data and the classification. The Satake isomorphism follows the survey of Cartier in the Corvallis volume and the paper of Gross, "On the Satake isomorphism" (1998), which is a short and complete account. For the parameters and the conjecture, Knapp's "Introduction to the Langlands program" (1997) and Vogan's paper "The local Langlands conjecture" (1993) are good further reading. Exercises 1, 3, 4 and 5 exercise the duality of root data. Exercises 2, 6 and 8 compute L-groups and their actions. Exercises 7, 9 and 11 check the Satake isomorphism and the L-factors in rank one. Exercise 10 computes the component group in the example of a packet of size four. Exercises 12 to 15 follow the explicit constructions at the end of Section 5.4: the signs in the matrix $J$, the action of $	heta$ on all root elements, the role of the condition that the roots span, and the pinned automorphism of $A_2$. These constructions follow Borel [Bor79], Section 2, Kottwitz, "Stable trace formula: cuspidal tempered terms" (Duke Math. J. 1984), Section 1, and Rogawski's book on unitary groups in three variables (1990), Section 1.8.
