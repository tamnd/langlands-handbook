# Modular forms and Hecke operators

In 1916 Ramanujan studied the function
$$\Delta(\tau) = q \prod_{n \ge 1} (1 - q^n)^{24} = \sum_{n \ge 1} \tau(n) q^n = q - 24 q^2 + 252 q^3 - 1472 q^4 + 4830 q^5 - 6048 q^6 - 16744 q^7 + \cdots,$$
where $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$ and $\tau$ is in the upper half plane. He computed many coefficients and observed three properties. First, $\tau(mn) = \tau(m) \tau(n)$ when $m$ and $n$ are coprime; for example $\tau(6) = -6048 = (-24)(252)$. Second, $\tau(p^{r+1}) = \tau(p) \tau(p^r) - p^{11} \tau(p^{r-1})$ for each prime $p$ and $r \ge 1$; for example $\tau(4) = 576 - 2048 = -1472$. Third, $|\tau(p)| \le 2 p^{11/2}$ for each prime $p$. Mordell proved the first two properties in 1917. The third is much deeper. Deligne proved it in 1974, as a consequence of his proof of the Weil conjectures.

This chapter explains the first two properties. The function $\Delta$ is a cusp form of weight 12 for $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$, and the space of these cusp forms has dimension 1. Hecke defined in 1937 a family of commuting linear operators $T_n$ on each space of modular forms. On a space of dimension 1 each $T_n$ acts by a scalar, and the formulas for the action of $T_n$ on $q$-expansions turn the relations between the operators into Ramanujan's relations between the coefficients. Chapter 2 explains the third property.

Section 1.1 studies the action of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ on the upper half plane. It proves that the usual fundamental domain is a fundamental domain, and it defines the congruence subgroups $\Gamma_0(N)$, $\Gamma_1(N)$ and $\Gamma(N)$ and their cusps. Section 1.2 defines modular forms and cusp forms and their $q$-expansions, and computes the $q$-expansions of the Eisenstein series. Section 1.3 proves the valence formula and computes the dimensions of the spaces of level one. It also proves that the spaces of every level have finite dimension. Section 1.4 defines the Petersson inner product. Section 1.5 defines the diamond operators and the Hecke operators $T_p$, computes their action on $q$-expansions, and proves that they commute. Its last subsection defines $T_p$ on $\Gamma_0(N)$ as the trace of a translate, which is the definition in the Lean project, and proves the same results from this definition. Section 1.6 computes their adjoints for the Petersson inner product and deduces that the spaces of cusp forms have bases of eigenforms. It ends with Mordell's theorem on $\tau(n)$.

We follow Diamond and Shurman, Chapters 1 to 5, and for level one Serre, *A course in arithmetic*, Chapter VII. The proofs of the valence formula and of the dimension formulas are those of Serre. The definitions of the Hecke operators and the proofs of their properties follow Diamond and Shurman, with one change: we define $T_p$ by an explicit set of integer matrices and prove directly that this set is a double coset. The normalizations of $q$-expansions are those of Volume 0, Section 3.2.
