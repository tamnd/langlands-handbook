## Exercises

1. Show that $S$ and $T$ generate $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$, with the algorithm of the proof of Theorem 1.1.4: for $\gamma \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and $\tau$ in the interior of $\mathcal{D}$, find $g$ in the group generated by $S$ and $T$ with $g\gamma\tau \in \mathcal{D}$, and use part 3 of the theorem. Show that $S^2 = (ST)^3 = -I$.
2. Show that $\Gamma_1(N)$ has no element of finite order other than $I$ for $N \ge 4$. Use that an element of finite order other than $\pm I$ in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ has trace $0$ or $\pm 1$. Show that $\Gamma_1(3)$ has an element of order 3 and that $\Gamma_1(2) = \Gamma_0(2)$ contains $-I$.
3. Show that each cusp of $\Gamma_0(N)$ is represented by a fraction $a/c$ with $c \ge 1$, $c \mid N$ and $\gcd(a, c) = 1$. Show that two such fractions $a/c$ and $a'/c'$ represent the same cusp if and only if $c = c'$ and $a \equiv a' \bmod \gcd(c, N/c)$. So the cusps correspond to the pairs $(c, a)$ with $c \mid N$ and $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/\gcd(c, N/c)\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Deduce that $\Gamma_0(N)$ has $\sum_{c \mid N} \varphi(\gcd(c, N/c))$ cusps, and check the case $N = p$ of Example 1.1.10. Show that the width of the cusp $1/c$ is $N/\gcd(c^2, N)$.
4. Let $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}} = -y^2(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2)$. Show that $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}(F \circ g) = (\Delta_{\mathbb{H}} F) \circ g$ for each smooth $F$ on $\mathbb{H}$ and $g \in SL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Check it first for $g = T^t$, for $g = \mathrm{diag}(a, a^{-1})$ and for $g = S$, and show that these generate $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Show that $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}} y^s = s(1 - s) y^s$.
5. Show that $\sigma_3(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} d^3$ and $\sigma_5$ satisfy $11\sigma_9(n) = 21\sigma_5(n) - 10\sigma_3(n) + 5040\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\sigma_3(m)\sigma_5(n-m)$, from $E_4E_6 = E_{10}$. Check the identity for $n = 2$.
6. Show that $E_{12} - E_4^3 = (65520/691 - 720)\Delta$, by comparing the coefficients of $q^0$ and $q^1$ in $M_{12} = \mathbb{C}E_4^3 \oplus \mathbb{C}\Delta$. Deduce Ramanujan's congruence $\tau(n) \equiv \sigma_{11}(n) \bmod 691$. Use that $E_4^3$ has integer coefficients and that $691$ does not divide $65520$.
7. Let $f \in M_k(SL_2(\mathbb{Z}))$ with $v_\infty(f) > k/12$. Show that $f = 0$. This is the Sturm bound in level one. Use it to prove $E_4E_{10} = E_6E_8 = E_{14}$ by comparing one coefficient.
8. Let $f \in S_k(\Gamma)$. Show that $a_n(f) = O(n^{k/2})$, where $f = \sum_n a_n(f) q_h^n$ is the expansion at $\infty$ and $h$ is as in Proposition 1.3.10. Integrate $f(x + iy)e^{-2\pi i n(x + iy)/h}$ over $0 \le x \le h$ at height $y = 1/n$ and use Lemma 1.4.3. This is Hecke's bound.
9. Show that $T_mT_n = \sum_{d \mid \gcd(m,n)} d^{k-1}\langle d\rangle T_{mn/d^2}$ on $M_k(\Gamma_1(N))$. Use Proposition 1.5.11 on each space $M_k(N, \chi)$.
10. Let $p \nmid N$ and $f \in M_k(\Gamma_1(N))$. Show that $T_p f = f|[\Gamma_1\alpha\Gamma_1]$ is also equal to $\sum_{\beta} f|\beta$ where $\beta$ runs over the matrices $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d\end{pmatrix}$ with $ad = p$, $0 \le b < d$, and with $f|\begin{pmatrix} p & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ replaced by $(\langle p\rangle f)|\begin{pmatrix} p & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$. For $N = 1$, show that $(T_p f)(\tau) = p^{k-1} f(p\tau) + p^{-1}\sum_{b=0}^{p-1} f((\tau + b)/p)$.
11. Let $N = 1$ and let $L_\tau = \mathbb{Z}\tau + \mathbb{Z}$. A function $F$ on lattices of $\mathbb{C}$ is *homogeneous of degree $-k$* if $F(\lambda L) = \lambda^{-k}F(L)$. Show that $f(\tau) = F(L_\tau)$ gives a bijection between such functions and functions with $f|_k\gamma = f$ for $\gamma \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. Show that $T_p$ corresponds to $p^{k-1}\sum_{L'} F(L')$, where $L'$ runs over the $p + 1$ sublattices of index $p$ in $L$. This is Hecke's original definition, and Serre's in Chapter VII.
12. Show that the forms $f_1 = \Delta E_4^3$ and $f_2 = \Delta^2$ of Example 1.6.6 satisfy $a_4(f_1) = 12831808$, and check the matrix of $T_2$. Show that $\mathrm{tr}(T_2 | S_{24}) = 1080$ and that the eigenvalues satisfy $|\lambda| \le 2 \cdot 2^{23/2}$.
13. Let $p$ be a prime, $\alpha = \mathrm{diag}(1, p)$ and $H = \Gamma_0(N) \cap \alpha^{-1}\Gamma_0(N)\alpha$, as in Definition 1.5.14. Show that $\alpha H\alpha^{-1} = \Gamma_0(Np)$. Show with Proposition 1.1.8 that $[\Gamma_0(N) : \Gamma_0(Np)] = p + 1$ if $p \nmid N$ and $p$ if $p \mid N$, and compare with Lemma 1.5.16. Show that $H \ne \Gamma_0(Np)$ for $N = 1$ and $p = 2$.
14. Let $p \nmid N$ and $f \in M_k(\Gamma_0(N))$. Use Theorem 1.5.18 twice to show that $a_m(T_p^2f) = a_{mp^2}(f) + p^{k-1}a_m(f) + [p \mid m]\,p^{k-1}a_m(f) + p^{2k-2}a_{m/p^2}(f)$, where $[p \mid m]$ is 1 if $p \mid m$ and 0 otherwise, and $a_{m/p^2} = 0$ if $p^2 \nmid m$. Deduce that $T_p^2 - p^{k-1}$ acts on $q$-expansions by the formula of Proposition 1.5.11 for $n = p^2$, as Definition 1.5.10 predicts. Show that $a_m(U_p^2f) = a_{mp^2}(f)$ if $p \mid N$.
15. Let $f \in M_k(\Gamma_0(N))$. Show with Theorem 1.5.18 that $a_0(T_pf) = (1 + \varepsilon_pp^{k-1})a_0(f)$. Deduce that for $N = 1$ the operator $T_p$ preserves $S_k(SL_2(\mathbb{Z}))$. Explain why for $N > 1$ the equation $a_0(T_pf) = 0$ for a cusp form $f$ does not by itself show that $T_pf$ is a cusp form. Show that $T_pf$ vanishes at every cusp, by writing $\alpha\gamma\sigma$ for $\gamma \in \Gamma_0(N)$ and $\sigma \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ as in the proof of Lemma 1.2.7.
16. Let $N = 1$, $k = 12$ and $p = 3$. Write down the four coset representatives of Lemma 1.5.16 and the matrix $\gamma'$ of the proof of Proposition 1.5.17 for $b = -1$ and $d = 0$. Use $\tau(3) = 252$, $\tau(6) = -6048$, $\tau(9) = -113643$ and $\tau(12) = -370944$ to compute $a_m(T_3\Delta)$ for $m \le 4$, and check that $T_3\Delta = 252\Delta$ up to $O(q^5)$. Deduce $\tau(9) = \tau(3)^2 - 3^{11}$ and $\tau(12) = \tau(3)\tau(4)$ from $S_{12} = \mathbb{C}\Delta$, as in Example 1.5.20.
17. Let $p$ be a prime and $c_p = \varepsilon_p p^{k-1}$, with the operators $T_{p^r}$ on $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ of Definition 1.5.22. Show by induction on $\min(a, b)$, with Proposition 1.5.26, that $T_{p^a}T_{p^b} = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(a, b)} c_p^j\, T_{p^{a+b-2j}}$. Deduce with Theorem 1.5.25 that $T_mT_n = \sum_{d} d^{k-1}T_{mn/d^2}$ for all $m, n \ge 1$, where $d$ runs over the common divisors of $m$ and $n$ that are coprime to $N$. Check the case $m = n = 2$, $N = 1$, $k = 12$ on $\Delta$.
18. Let $M \ge 1$, $f \in S_k(\Gamma_0(M))$ and let $p$ be a prime. (a) Let $d \ge 1$ with $p \nmid dM$. Show with Theorem 1.5.18 and Proposition 1.5.35 that $T_p(B_df) = B_d(T_pf)$, where the left $T_p$ has level $dM$ and the right one level $M$. (b) Let $p \nmid M$, and let $U_p$ be the operator of level $pM$. Show that $U_p(B_pf) = f$ and $U_pf = T_pf - p^{k-1}B_pf$, where $T_p$ has level $M$ and the forms of level $M$ are viewed at level $pM$. Deduce that the span of $f$ and $B_pf$ is stable under $U_p$ if $f$ is an eigenvector of $T_p$, and find the matrix of $U_p$ in the basis $f, B_pf$.
19. Use Example 1.5.37 and Exercise 18 to find the matrices of $U_2$ and $T_3$ on $S_2(\Gamma_0(22))$ in the basis $f_{11}, B_2f_{11}$. Show that the characteristic polynomial of $U_2$ is $X^2 + 2X + 2$, and that its eigenvectors are the forms $f_{11}(\tau) - \beta f_{11}(2\tau)$ of Example 2.1.9. Check $a_2$ and $a_4$ of the eigenvector for $\alpha = -1 + i$ against $a_{2^r} = \alpha^r$.
20. Let $a : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}$ with $a_0 = 0$ and $a_{mn} = a_ma_n$ for coprime $m, n$, let $p$ be a prime and $c \in \mathbb{C}$, and assume $a_{p^{r+2}} = a_pa_{p^{r+1}} - c\,a_{p^r}$ for all $r \ge 0$. Show that $a_{pn} + c\,[p \mid n]\,a_{n/p} = a_pa_n$ for every $n \ge 0$. Write $n = p^rm$ with $p \nmid m$ and treat $n = 0$, $r = 0$ and $r \ge 1$ separately. Explain why, for $c = \chi(p)p^{k-1}$ and $p \mid N$, this gives $a_{pn} = a_pa_n$. This is the lemma that Remark 2.4.7 uses to pass from the eigenform predicate of Definition 2.1.4 to the one of the anthropics port.

## Notes and history

Ramanujan studied $\tau(n)$ in his paper "On certain arithmetical functions" of 1916. He conjectured the multiplicativity, the recursion and the bound $|\tau(p)| \le 2p^{11/2}$, and he observed the congruence of Exercise 6. Mordell proved the first two conjectures in 1917, by a method that is a special case of the Hecke operators. Hecke developed the general theory in two papers in the Mathematische Annalen in 1937, "Über Modulfunktionen und die Dirichletschen Reihen mit Eulerscher Produktentwicklung" I and II. He proved that the operators $T_n$ commute and that the eigenforms have Euler products. He did not prove that the operators are diagonalizable. Petersson introduced the inner product in 1939 and proved that the $T_n$ are normal. The fundamental domain and the groups $\Gamma_0(N)$ go back to Klein and Fricke at the end of the 19th century.

For level one we followed Serre, *A course in arithmetic*, Chapter VII, which proves the valence formula and defines $T_n$ on lattices as in Exercise 11. For general level we followed Diamond and Shurman, *A first course in modular forms*, Chapters 1 to 5. The definition of $T_p$ by the set $\Delta_p$ is a reformulation of their Section 5.2. The definition of $T_p$ on $\Gamma_0(N)$ as the trace of a translate, in the last subsection of Section 1.5, is the form of the double coset operator that the Lean project uses, and the coset representatives there are the classical ones, see Diamond and Shurman, Section 5.2, and Shimura, Chapter 3. Shimura, *Introduction to the arithmetic theory of automorphic functions*, Chapters 1 to 3, treats the Hecke algebras of arbitrary congruence subgroups with the abstract algebra of double cosets. The dimension formulas for $\Gamma_0(N)$ and $\Gamma_1(N)$ are in Diamond and Shurman, Chapter 3, and in Shimura, Chapter 2.

No statement of this chapter has a node on the map of the project. Mathlib contains most of the level one theory of Sections 1.2 and 1.3, including the dimension formula `ModularForm.dimension_level_one`, see Remark 1.3.9, and the Petersson integrand `UpperHalfPlane.petersson` of Remark 1.4.7. It also has the trace `ModularForm.trace` and the translate `ModularForm.translate` of Definition 1.5.14. The Lean project uses the coefficients `Langlands.qCoeff` and `Langlands.qCoeffGamma1` of Remark 1.2.5 and the predicate `Langlands.HasNebentypus` of Remark 1.5.4. Its file `Langlands/Modular/Hecke.lean` defines the operators $T_p$ and $U_p$ on $M_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ and $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$, as `Langlands.Modular.heckeT` and `Langlands.Modular.cuspHeckeT`, and it proves Lemma 1.5.16, Proposition 1.5.17 and Theorems 1.5.18 and 1.5.19, see Remark 1.5.21. Its file `Langlands/Modular/HeckeTn.lean` defines the operators $T_n$ on $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ from the $T_p$, the level raising maps $B_d$ and the oldspace, and it proves Theorem 1.5.25, Theorem 1.5.28 and Corollary 1.5.30, see the last two subsections of Section 1.5. It has no diamond operators and no operators on $M_k(\Gamma_1(N))$. So Proposition 1.5.3, Definition 1.5.10 and Proposition 1.5.11 for a nontrivial character, and the results of Section 1.6, are not in Lean. Chapter 2 explains how the statements of the project use conditions on $q$-expansions instead, and Section 2.1 explains how the project derives these conditions from the operators $T_p$.
