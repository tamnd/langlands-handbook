## Galois representations of eigenforms

This section attaches to each normalized eigenform of weight $k \ge 2$ a representation of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ of dimension 2 over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. The statement uses Frobenius elements, and there are two normalizations of them. We fix them first, and then we show that in dimension 2 the two normalizations give equivalent statements. Let $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ be an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$, let $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the ring of algebraic integers in it, and let $G_\mathbb{Q} = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ with its profinite topology.

### Frobenius elements

Let $p$ be a prime and $\mathfrak{P}$ a maximal ideal of $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}$ that contains $p$. The residue field $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathfrak{P}$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$. The *decomposition group* $D_\mathfrak{P}$ is the stabilizer of $\mathfrak{P}$ in $G_\mathbb{Q}$, and the *inertia group* $I_\mathfrak{P}$ is the group of the $\sigma \in D_\mathfrak{P}$ with $\sigma(x) \equiv x \bmod \mathfrak{P}$ for all $x \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}$. The map $D_\mathfrak{P} \to \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is surjective with kernel $I_\mathfrak{P}$, as in algebraic number theory for each finite Galois extension, by a limit argument.

**Definition 2.4.1 (Frobenius elements).** An element $\sigma \in G_\mathbb{Q}$ is an *arithmetic Frobenius at $\mathfrak{P}$* if $\sigma(x) \equiv x^p \bmod \mathfrak{P}$ for all $x \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}$. It is an *arithmetic Frobenius at $p$* if it is an arithmetic Frobenius at some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{P}$ over $p$. The inverse of an arithmetic Frobenius is a *geometric Frobenius*. We write $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ for an arithmetic Frobenius at $p$ and $\Phi_p$ for a geometric one.

Lean: `Langlands.IsFrobeniusAt`

An arithmetic Frobenius at $\mathfrak{P}$ lies in $D_\mathfrak{P}$: if $x \in \mathfrak{P}$ then $\sigma(x) \equiv x^p \equiv 0$, so $\sigma(\mathfrak{P}) \subset \mathfrak{P}$. The Lean predicate `Langlands.IsFrobeniusAt p σ` says that there is a maximal ideal $Q$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}$ over $p$ with `IsArithFrobAt ℤ σ Q`, the predicate of Mathlib for $\sigma(x) - x^p \in Q$ for all $x$. So it is the arithmetic Frobenius. The fidelity theorem `Langlands.Fidelity.exists_isFrobeniusAt` proves that an arithmetic Frobenius exists at each prime. The handbook writes $\Phi$ for a geometric Frobenius and $\mathrm{Frob}$ for an arithmetic one, as in Volume 0, Definition 3.1.2. The reciprocity map of Volume 3 sends uniformizers to geometric Frobenius elements.

**Definition 2.4.2 (unramified homomorphisms).** A homomorphism $\rho$ from $G_\mathbb{Q}$ to a group $H$ is *unramified at $p$* if $\rho(I_\mathfrak{P}) = 1$ for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak{P}$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}$ over $p$.

**Lemma 2.4.3 (the Frobenius class).** Let $\rho : G_\mathbb{Q} \to H$ be unramified at $p$. Then the elements $\rho(\sigma)$, for $\sigma$ an arithmetic Frobenius at $p$, form one conjugacy class of $H$. If $H = GL_n(F)$ for a field $F$, the characteristic polynomial of $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$ depends only on $p$.

*Proof.* Let $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ be arithmetic Frobenius elements at the same $\mathfrak{P}$. Then $\sigma^{-1}\sigma' \in D_\mathfrak{P}$ acts trivially on $\bar{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathfrak{P}$, so it is in $I_\mathfrak{P}$, and $\rho(\sigma) = \rho(\sigma')$. Let $\mathfrak{P}'$ be another maximal ideal over $p$. The group $G_\mathbb{Q}$ acts transitively on these ideals, so $\mathfrak{P}' = \tau\mathfrak{P}$ for some $\tau$. If $\sigma$ is an arithmetic Frobenius at $\mathfrak{P}$, then $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}(x) = \tau(\sigma(\tau^{-1}x)) \equiv \tau((\tau^{-1}x)^p) = x^p \bmod \tau\mathfrak{P}$, so $\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}$ is an arithmetic Frobenius at $\mathfrak{P}'$. Conversely every element of the class of $\rho(\sigma)$ is $\rho(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1})$ for some $\tau$. $\square$

We need two more facts on Galois representations. Let $\ell$ be a prime and let $F$ be $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ or a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. A continuous representation $\rho : G_\mathbb{Q} \to GL_n(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ takes values in $GL_n(E)$ for a finite extension $E$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, by a Baire category argument, and it is unramified outside a finite set of primes when it comes from geometry, as all the representations of this section do. The *Chebotarev density theorem* says that for a finite set $S$ of primes the conjugates of the arithmetic Frobenius elements at the primes $p \notin S$ are dense in $G_\mathbb{Q}$. With the theorem of Brauer and Nesbitt it gives: two semisimple continuous representations $\rho, \rho' : G_\mathbb{Q} \to GL_n(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$, unramified outside $S$, with $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho'(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$ for all $p \notin S$, are isomorphic. In particular continuous characters are determined by their values at the $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ with $p \notin S$.

The *$\ell$-adic cyclotomic character* $\varepsilon_\ell : G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ is defined by $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^{\varepsilon_\ell(\sigma)}$ for all roots of unity $\zeta$ of $\ell$-power order. It is unramified at $p \ne \ell$, with $\varepsilon_\ell(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = p$, because $\mathrm{Frob}_p(\zeta) \equiv \zeta^p$ modulo $\mathfrak{P}$ and the roots of unity of order prime to $p$ are distinct modulo $\mathfrak{P}$. A Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $N$ is a character of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ through $G_\mathbb{Q} \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$, where $\sigma \mapsto a$ if $\sigma(\zeta_N) = \zeta_N^a$. In the same way $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \chi(p)$ for $p \nmid N$. A complex conjugation $c$ has $\varepsilon_\ell(c) = -1$ and $\chi(c) = \chi(-1)$.

### The theorem of Eichler, Shimura and Deligne

**Theorem 2.4.4 (Eichler-Shimura, Deligne).** Let $N \ge 1$, $k \ge 2$, let $\chi$ be a Dirichlet character modulo $N$ and let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform. Let $\ell$ be a prime and $\iota : \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \to \mathbb{C}$ an isomorphism of fields. There is a continuous representation
$$\rho_{f,\iota} : G_\mathbb{Q} \to GL_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$$
such that for each prime $p \nmid N\ell$, $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is unramified at $p$ and
$$\iota\bigl(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_{f,\iota}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)\bigr) = a_p(f), \qquad \iota\bigl(\det\rho_{f,\iota}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)\bigr) = \chi(p) p^{k-1}.$$

The case $k = 2$ is due to Eichler and Shimura, and the case $k \ge 3$ to Deligne, [Del69, Theorem 6.1]. The outline of the proof for $k = 2$ is below. By Chebotarev and Brauer-Nesbitt the semisimplification of $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is unique up to isomorphism. Ribet proved that $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is irreducible when $f$ is a newform.

The textbook form has values in a finite extension $E_\lambda$ of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ that contains the coefficients; as noted above, a continuous $\rho$ with values in $GL_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ has values in such a field. **Proposition 2.4.6 (the determinant).** Let $\rho = \rho_{f,\iota}$ be as in Theorem 2.4.4, and view $\chi$ as a character of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ with values in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ through $\iota^{-1}$. Then $\det \rho = \chi\,\varepsilon_\ell^{k-1}$. In particular $\rho$ is *odd*: $\det\rho(c) = -1$ for each complex conjugation $c$.

*Proof.* Both sides are continuous characters of $G_\mathbb{Q}$, unramified outside $N\ell$, and at $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ for $p \nmid N\ell$ both take the value $\iota^{-1}(\chi(p))p^{k-1}$. ### The construction in weight 2

We outline the construction of Eichler and Shimura, following Diamond and Shurman, Chapters 6 to 9, and Shimura, Chapter 7. Let $X = X_1(N)$ be the modular curve of Remark 1.1.11, a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ of genus $g = \dim S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$, and let $J = J_1(N)$ be its Jacobian, an abelian variety over $\mathbb{Q}$ of dimension $g$.

1. Over $\mathbb{C}$, the map $f \mapsto f(\tau)\,d\tau$ identifies $S_2(\Gamma_1(N))$ with the space of holomorphic differentials on $X(\mathbb{C})$, and $J(\mathbb{C}) = S_2(\Gamma_1(N))^\vee / H_1(X(\mathbb{C}), \mathbb{Z})$ by the theorem of Abel and Jacobi.
2. A double coset $\Gamma_1\alpha\Gamma_1$ defines a correspondence on $X$, so an endomorphism of $J$, and this endomorphism is defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. So the Hecke algebra $\mathbb{T}$ acts on $J$, and on its Tate module $T_\ell J = \varprojlim J[\ell^n]$, compatibly with the action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$.
3. The module $V_\ell J = T_\ell J \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ has dimension $2g$, and it is free of rank 2 over $\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$, because $H_1(X(\mathbb{C}), \mathbb{Q})$ is free of rank 2 over $\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{Q}$.
4. The eigenvalues of $f$ give a ring homomorphism $\theta_f : \mathbb{T} \to \mathbb{C}$, and $\iota^{-1}\circ\theta_f$ extends to $\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. The space $V_f = V_\ell J \otimes_{\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ has dimension 2, with an action of $G_\mathbb{Q}$. This is $\rho_{f,\iota}$.
5. For $p \nmid N$ the curve $X$ has good reduction at $p$, a theorem of Igusa, so $J$ has good reduction at $p$, and by the criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich $V_\ell J$ is unramified at $p \nmid N\ell$.
6. On the reduction $\tilde{J}$ of $J$ modulo $p$, with Frobenius endomorphism $F$ and Verschiebung $V$, $FV = p$, the *Eichler-Shimura relation* $T_p = F + \langle p \rangle V$ holds. Multiplying by $F$ gives $F^2 - T_pF + p\langle p\rangle = 0$.
7. The reduction map identifies $T_\ell J$ with $T_\ell\tilde{J}$ for $p \ne \ell$, and an arithmetic Frobenius at $p$ acts on $\tilde{J}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p)$ as $F$, since both raise the coordinates of the points to the power $p$. So $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ satisfies $X^2 - a_p X + \chi(p)p$ on $V_f$, and a further argument with the Weil pairing shows that this is its characteristic polynomial.

For $k \ge 3$, Deligne replaces $J$ by the étale cohomology $H^1$ of $X_1(N)$ with coefficients in the sheaf $\mathrm{Sym}^{k-2}$ of the Tate module of the universal elliptic curve, or $H^{k-1}$ of the Kuga-Sato variety, and he proves the analogue of the Eichler-Shimura relation. For $k = 1$, Deligne and Serre construct $\rho$ by congruences with forms of higher weight, and its image is finite; Section 2.5 returns to this case.

### Higher weight by congruences

This subsection gives a second construction of $\rho_{f,\iota}$ for $k > 2$. It starts from the case $k = 2$ of Theorem 2.4.4 and from congruences between $f$ and forms of weight 2, and it does not use the cohomology of Kuga-Sato varieties. The congruences are due to Shimura and Hida. Wiles introduced pseudo-representations of dimension 2 to construct the representations attached to ordinary $\Lambda$-adic forms (Invent. Math. 94 (1988)), and Taylor developed the theory of pseudo-characters in each dimension (Duke Math. J. 63 (1991), Section 1). The method gives a representation with the right traces and determinants at Frobenius, and nothing more. It gives no information on the restriction to a decomposition group at $\ell$, and it does not give the Ramanujan bound of Theorem 2.3.2.

Throughout, $|\cdot|$ is the absolute value of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ with $|\ell| = \ell^{-1}$, and $v_\ell$ is the valuation with $v_\ell(\ell) = 1$. For $x, y \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ we write $x \equiv y \bmod \ell^n$ if $|x - y| \le \ell^{-n}$.

**Definition 2.4.8 (pseudo-characters of dimension 2).** Let $G$ be a group and $A$ a commutative ring. A *pseudo-character of dimension 2* of $G$ with values in $A$ is a pair of functions $t, d : G \to A$ such that for all $g, h \in G$:
1. $t(1) = 2$ and $t(gh) = t(hg)$;
2. $d(1) = 1$ and $d(gh) = d(g) d(h)$;
3. $d(g)\, t(g^{-1}h) - t(g)\, t(h) + t(gh) = 0$.

If $G$ is a topological group and $A$ a topological ring, the pseudo-character is *continuous* if $t$ and $d$ are continuous.

This is the form of Chenevier, in his paper in the LMS Lecture Note Series 414 (2014), Section 7. Condition 3 is the Cayley-Hamilton identity in disguise, as the proof of Lemma 2.4.9 shows. Condition 2 gives $d(g)d(g^{-1}) = d(1) = 1$, so $d$ takes values in $A^\times$; in Lean this is `Langlands.Galois.IsPseudoCharTwo.d_mul_d_inv`. If 2 is invertible in $A$, then $d$ is determined by $t$, by Exercise 25, and one can work with $t$ alone, as Taylor does. A pair of characters gives a pseudo-character: for $\chi_1, \chi_2 : G \to A^\times$ the pair $(\chi_1 + \chi_2, \chi_1\chi_2)$ is one, by Lemma 2.4.9 for the diagonal representation, or by the direct computation of Exercise 25.

**Lemma 2.4.9 (the trace and the determinant).** Let $\rho : G \to GL_2(A)$ be a homomorphism. Then $(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho, \det\rho)$ is a pseudo-character of dimension 2. If $\rho$ is continuous, so is the pseudo-character.

Lean: `Langlands.Galois.isPseudoCharTwo_trace_det`, with the matrix identity `Langlands.Galois.det_mul_trace_inv_mul`

*Proof.* Conditions 1 and 2 hold because $\mathrm{tr}(I) = 2$, $\mathrm{tr}(MN) = \mathrm{tr}(NM)$ and $\det$ is multiplicative. Let $M \in GL_2(A)$. The Cayley-Hamilton theorem gives $M^2 - \mathrm{tr}(M) M + \det(M) I = 0$. Multiply by $M^{-1}$ on the left: $M + \det(M) M^{-1} = \mathrm{tr}(M) I$. Multiply by a matrix $X$ on the right and take the trace:
$$\det(M)\,\mathrm{tr}(M^{-1}X) - \mathrm{tr}(M)\,\mathrm{tr}(X) + \mathrm{tr}(MX) = 0.$$
With $M = \rho(g)$ and $X = \rho(h)$ this is condition 3. The trace and the determinant are polynomials in the entries, so they are continuous. $\square$

**Lemma 2.4.10 (limits).** Let $A$ be a Hausdorff topological ring.
1. Let $(t_i, d_i)$ be a family of pseudo-characters of dimension 2 of $G$ with values in $A$, indexed by a directed set, with $t_i(g) \to t(g)$ and $d_i(g) \to d(g)$ for each $g \in G$. Then $(t, d)$ is a pseudo-character of dimension 2.
2. Let $G$ be a topological group. Two continuous pseudo-characters of dimension 2 of $G$ with values in $A$ that agree on a dense subset of $G$ are equal.

Lean: `Langlands.Galois.IsPseudoCharTwo.of_tendsto` and `Langlands.Galois.IsPseudoCharTwo.eq_of_eqOn_dense`

*Proof.* For part 1 fix $g, h \in G$. The ring operations of $A$ are continuous, so $d_i(g)t_i(g^{-1}h) - t_i(g)t_i(h) + t_i(gh)$ converges to $d(g)t(g^{-1}h) - t(g)t(h) + t(gh)$. Each term of the family is 0, and a limit in a Hausdorff space is unique, so the limit is 0. The other conditions are proved in the same way. For part 2, the set of the $g$ with $t(g) = t'(g)$ is closed, because $A$ is Hausdorff and $t, t'$ are continuous. It contains a dense set, so it is $G$. The same holds for $d$ and $d'$. $\square$

So the condition to be a pseudo-character is a closed condition, while it is not clear that a limit of traces of representations is again a trace. In a normed ring there is a variant for approximate pseudo-characters: if the conditions hold for $(t_n, d_n)$ up to an error of norm at most $\varepsilon_n$, with $\varepsilon_n \to 0$, the limit is a pseudo-character (`Langlands.Galois.IsPseudoCharTwo.of_approx`). The next theorem turns a pseudo-character back into a representation.

**Theorem 2.4.11 (Taylor).** Let $F$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, and let $(t, d)$ be a pseudo-character of dimension 2 of a group $G$ with values in $F$.
1. There is a semisimple representation $\rho : G \to GL_2(F)$ with $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho = t$ and $\det\rho = d$. It is unique up to isomorphism.
2. Let $h \in G$ with $t(gh) = t(g)$ for all $g \in G$. Then $\rho(h) = 1$.
3. Let $G$ be profinite and $F = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$, and let $t$ and $d$ be continuous, with $t$ taking values in a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Then $\rho$ is continuous for a suitable choice in its isomorphism class.

Lean: the case $G = G_\mathbb{Q}$, without the uniqueness, is the hypothesis `Langlands.Bridge.PseudoCharTwoRepresentation`

*Outline of the proof.* Parts 1 and 3 are in Taylor, Duke Math. J. 63 (1991), Section 1, for pseudo-characters of each dimension; Chenevier, Section 7, treats the pairs $(t, d)$ of dimension 2 over each ring. In dimension 2 the idea is this. Extend $t$ linearly to the group algebra $F[G]$ and let $K$ be the kernel of the bilinear form $(x, y) \mapsto t(xy)$. Then $K$ is a two sided ideal, and the identities of Definition 2.4.8 show that $F[G]/K$ is a semisimple algebra of dimension at most 4, so it is $M_2(F)$, $F \times F$ or $F$. The representation $\rho$ is read off from this algebra. We prove part 2. Let $B \subset M_2(F)$ be the span of $\rho(G)$. Since $\rho$ is semisimple and $F$ is algebraically closed, $F^2 = \bigoplus_j W_j^{m_j}$ with distinct irreducible $W_j$, and $B = \prod_j \mathrm{End}(W_j)$ by the theorem of Burnside. So $\mathrm{tr}(ab) = \sum_j m_j \mathrm{tr}_{W_j}(a_jb_j)$ for $a, b \in B$. The trace form of each $\mathrm{End}(W_j)$ is nondegenerate and $m_j \ne 0$ in $F$, so the trace form of $B$ is nondegenerate. Let $x = \rho(h) - 1 \in B$. Then $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(g) x) = t(gh) - t(g) = 0$ for all $g$, so $\mathrm{tr}(bx) = 0$ for all $b \in B$, and $x = 0$. $\square$

Part 2 is how the method proves that $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is unramified: one shows $t(g\sigma) = t(g)$ for $\sigma$ in an inertia group, and part 2 gives $\rho(\sigma) = 1$.

**Proposition 2.4.12 (no congruence with a single eigenform).** Let $k > 2$, let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform, let $\ell$ be an odd prime with $\ell \nmid N$, let $\iota : \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \to \mathbb{C}$ be an isomorphism and let $n \ge 2 + v_\ell(k - 2)$. There is no $m \ge 0$ and no normalized eigenform $g \in S_2(N\ell^m, \psi)$ with $\iota^{-1}(a_a(g)) \equiv \iota^{-1}(a_a(f)) \bmod \ell^n$ for all $a \ge 1$ prime to $N\ell$.

Lean: not stated; the argument is in the module docstring of `Langlands/Bridge/EichlerShimuraHigher.lean`

*Proof.* Suppose that $g$ exists. All $a_a(f)$ and $a_a(g)$ are algebraic integers, so their images under $\iota^{-1}$ have absolute value at most 1. Let $q \nmid N\ell$ be a prime and put $x = \iota^{-1}(a_q(f))$, $y = \iota^{-1}(a_q(g))$. By condition 3 of Theorem 2.1.3, $a_{q^2}(f) = a_q(f)^2 - \chi(q)q^{k-1}$ and $a_{q^2}(g) = a_q(g)^2 - \psi(q)q$. Since $|x^2 - y^2| = |x - y|\,|x + y| \le \ell^{-n}$ and $a_{q^2}(f) \equiv a_{q^2}(g)$, we get $\iota^{-1}(\psi(q)q) \equiv \iota^{-1}(\chi(q)q^{k-1}) \bmod \ell^n$. Now $q$ is a unit at $\ell$, so
$$\iota^{-1}(\psi(q)) \equiv \iota^{-1}(\chi(q))\, q^{k-2} \bmod \ell^n. \qquad (*)$$
Since $\ell \nmid N$, the Chinese remainder theorem and the theorem of Dirichlet give a prime $q$ with $q \equiv 1 \bmod N$ and $q \equiv 1 + \ell \bmod \ell^2$. Then $\chi(q) = 1$. Under $(\mathbb{Z}/N\ell^m\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times \times (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ the class of $q$ is $(1, q)$, and $q \bmod \ell^m$ lies in the kernel of the reduction to $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^\times$, a group of order $\ell^{m-1}$. So $\zeta = \iota^{-1}(\psi(q))$ is a root of unity of order $\ell^r$ for some $r \ge 0$. We need two facts.

(a) $v_\ell(q^j - 1) = 1 + v_\ell(j)$ for $j \ge 1$. Write $j = \ell^s j'$ with $\ell \nmid j'$. Then $q^{j'} - 1 = (q - 1)(1 + q + \dots + q^{j'-1})$, and the second factor is $\equiv j' \bmod \ell$, a unit, so $v_\ell(q^{j'} - 1) = 1$. If $x = 1 + \ell^e u$ with $e \ge 1$ and $u$ a unit, then $x^\ell = 1 + \ell^{e+1}u + \sum_{i \ge 2}\binom{\ell}{i}\ell^{ie}u^i$, and each term of the sum has valuation at least $e + 2$, because $\ell$ divides $\binom{\ell}{i}$ for $i < \ell$ and $\ell e \ge 3e \ge e + 2$ for $\ell \ge 3$. So $v_\ell(x^\ell - 1) = e + 1$, and induction on $s$ gives (a).

(b) If $\zeta \ne 1$, then $|\zeta - 1| > \ell^{-1}$. Let $r \ge 1$ be the exponent of the order. The cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{\ell^r}(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} X^{i\ell^{r-1}}$ gives $\ell = \Phi_{\ell^r}(1) = \prod_a (1 - \zeta^a)$, over the $a \in (\mathbb{Z}/\ell^r\mathbb{Z})^\times$. Each quotient $(1 - \zeta^a)/(1 - \zeta) = 1 + \zeta + \dots + \zeta^{a-1}$ is integral, and so is its inverse, by the same argument with $\zeta^a$ in place of $\zeta$. So all the factors have the same absolute value, and $|1 - \zeta|^{\ell^{r-1}(\ell - 1)} = \ell^{-1}$. Since $\ell \ge 3$, the exponent is at least 2, and $|1 - \zeta| > \ell^{-1}$.

By (a), $|q^{k-2} - 1| = \ell^{-1 - v_\ell(k-2)}$, since $k - 2 \ge 1$. If $\zeta = 1$, then $(*)$ says $\ell^{-1-v_\ell(k-2)} \le \ell^{-n}$, which contradicts $n \ge 2 + v_\ell(k-2)$. If $\zeta \ne 1$, then $|\zeta - 1| > \ell^{-1} \ge |q^{k-2} - 1|$, so $|\zeta - q^{k-2}| = |\zeta - 1| > \ell^{-1} \ge \ell^{-n}$ by the strict triangle inequality, which contradicts $(*)$. $\square$

The proof shows where the obstruction lies. The determinant of $\rho_{g,\iota}$ is $\psi\varepsilon_\ell$ and that of $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is $\chi\varepsilon_\ell^{k-1}$, by Proposition 2.4.6. A congruence of $f$ with one form $g$ of weight 2 modulo $\ell^n$ asks that the character $\varepsilon_\ell^{k-2}$ is congruent modulo $\ell^n$ to a character of finite order, and this fails for large $n$. Exercise 26 treats $\ell = 2$. The way out is to use the Hecke algebra. Let $\mathbb{T}$ be the subring of $\mathrm{End}(S_2(\Gamma_1(N\ell^m)))$ generated by the $T_a$ and the $\langle d \rangle$ with $a, d$ prime to $N\ell$. A ring homomorphism $\theta : \mathbb{T} \to \mathcal{O}/\ell^n\mathcal{O}$ may send $\langle d \rangle$ to $\chi(d)d^{k-2}$, a value that is not a root of unity, and such a homomorphism exists. It is not the eigenvalue map of one eigenform, but it is congruent to a linear combination of eigenvalue maps. This is the next theorem.

**Theorem 2.4.13 (congruences with the Hecke algebra of weight 2; Shimura, Hida).** Let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform with $k \ge 2$, let $\ell$ be a prime, let $\iota : \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \to \mathbb{C}$ be an isomorphism and let $n \ge 0$. There are $m \ge 0$, normalized eigenforms $g_1, \dots, g_r$ with $g_i \in S_2(N\ell^m, \psi_i)$, and complex numbers $c_1, \dots, c_r$, such that for all $a, d \ge 1$ prime to $N\ell$
$$\iota^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^r c_i\, \psi_i(d)\, d\, a_a(g_i)\Bigr) \equiv \iota^{-1}\bigl(\chi(d)\, d^{k-1}\, a_a(f)\bigr) \bmod \ell^n.$$

*Outline of the proof.* The details are in Hida, *Elementary theory of $L$-functions and Eisenstein series*, LMS Student Texts 26, Chapter 7. Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the ring of integers of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ that contains the $\iota^{-1}(a_a(f))$ and the values of $\iota^{-1}\chi$.

1. The deep step. For $m$ large there is a ring homomorphism $\theta : \mathbb{T} \to \mathcal{O}/\ell^n\mathcal{O}$ with $\theta(T_a) = \iota^{-1}(a_a(f))$ and $\theta(\langle d\rangle) = \iota^{-1}(\chi(d))d^{k-2}$, where $\mathbb{T}$ is the Hecke ring of weight 2 and level $N\ell^m$ above. By the theorem of Eichler and Shimura, the eigenvalues of $f$ occur in the parabolic cohomology of $\Gamma_1(N)$ with coefficients in $\mathrm{Sym}^{k-2}(\mathcal{O}^2)$. Modulo $\ell^n$ this module of coefficients maps to a module induced from $\Gamma_1(N\ell^m)$, where $\langle d \rangle$ acts through $d^{k-2}$, and Shapiro's lemma moves the eigenvalues into the cohomology of $\Gamma_1(N\ell^m)$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{O}/\ell^n\mathcal{O}$. The theorem of Eichler and Shimura in weight 2 identifies the Hecke action there with the action of $\mathbb{T}$.
2. The Hecke ring is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank, and it is commutative. By Corollary 1.6.3 its operators are normal and commute, so $\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{C}^r$, through the maps $T \mapsto \lambda_T(g_i)$ for a family of simultaneous eigenvectors $g_1, \dots, g_r$. By the theory of newforms, each of these systems of eigenvalues is that of a newform of level dividing $N\ell^m$, so one can take for $g_i$ normalized eigenforms in $S_2(N\ell^m, \psi_i)$ for the $T_a$ and $\langle d \rangle$ with $a, d$ prime to $N\ell$.
3. Choose a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis of $\mathbb{T}$, lift the values of $\theta$ on it to $\mathcal{O}$, and extend linearly. This gives a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear map $\tilde\theta : \mathbb{T} \to \mathcal{O}$ with $\tilde\theta \equiv \theta \bmod \ell^n$. It is not multiplicative. The $\mathbb{C}$-linear map $\mathbb{T} \otimes \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ that extends $\iota \circ \tilde\theta$ is a linear form on $\mathbb{C}^r$, so it is $T \mapsto \sum_i c_i \lambda_T(g_i)$ for some $c_i \in \mathbb{C}$.
4. Apply this to $T = d\langle d\rangle T_a$. Its eigenvalue on $g_i$ is $\psi_i(d) d\, a_a(g_i)$, and $\theta(T) = \iota^{-1}(\chi(d) d^{k-1} a_a(f))$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.4.14 (weight $k$ from weight 2).** Assume Theorem 2.4.4 for $k = 2$ and all levels. Then Theorem 2.4.4 holds for each $k \ge 2$.

*Proof.* We give the main steps. Let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$, $\ell$ and $\iota$ be as in Theorem 2.4.4, let $G = G_\mathbb{Q}$, and let $S \subset G$ be the set of the arithmetic Frobenius elements at the primes $p \nmid N\ell$. By the theorem of Chebotarev, $S$ is dense in $G$.

1. The field of coefficients. The $a_p(f)$ and the values of $\chi$ generate a number field $K$ (Diamond and Shurman, Section 6.5). Let $E$ be the closure of $\iota^{-1}(K)$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. It is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, so it is complete, and it contains $\iota^{-1}(a_p(f))$ and $\iota^{-1}(\chi(p)p^{k-1})$ for all $p$.
2. Approximations. Fix $n$. Theorem 2.4.13 gives $g_i$, $\psi_i$ and $c_i$, and the case $k = 2$ of Theorem 2.4.4 gives representations $\rho_i$ for the $g_i$. Let $\gamma_i = \iota^{-1}(c_i)$ and define continuous functions on $G$ and on $G \times G$ by
$$t_n = \sum_i \gamma_i\, \mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i, \qquad d_n = \sum_i \gamma_i \det\rho_i,$$
$$P_n(g, h) = \sum_i \gamma_i\, \mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i(g)\,\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i(h), \quad Q_n(g, h) = \sum_i \gamma_i \det\rho_i(g)\,\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i(h), \quad R_n(g, h) = \sum_i \gamma_i \det\rho_i(g)\det\rho_i(h).$$
By Lemma 2.4.9 each $(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i, \det\rho_i)$ is a pseudo-character, and the identities are linear in it. So $t_n(gh) = t_n(hg)$, $Q_n(g, g^{-1}h) - P_n(g, h) + t_n(gh) = 0$, $d_n(gh) = R_n(g, h)$ and $t_n(1) = 2d_n(1)$ hold exactly. The pair $(t_n, d_n)$ itself is not a pseudo-character, because $P_n(g, h) \ne t_n(g)t_n(h)$ in general.
3. Values at Frobenius. Let $\sigma, \sigma' \in S$ be Frobenius elements at $p$ and $p'$. Then $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho_i(\sigma) = \iota^{-1}(a_p(g_i))$ and $\det\rho_i(\sigma) = \iota^{-1}(\psi_i(p)p)$. Theorem 2.4.13 with $(a, d) = (p, 1)$, $(1, p)$ and $(1, 1)$ gives $t_n(\sigma) \equiv \iota^{-1}(a_p(f))$, $d_n(\sigma) \equiv \iota^{-1}(\chi(p)p^{k-1})$ and $d_n(1) \equiv 1$ modulo $\ell^n$. For $P_n$ use the Hecke relations of the $g_i$: $a_p(g_i)a_{p'}(g_i) = a_{pp'}(g_i)$ if $p \ne p'$, and $a_p(g_i)^2 = a_{p^2}(g_i) + \psi_i(p)p$. The congruences with $(a, d) = (pp', 1)$, or with $(p^2, 1)$ and $(1, p)$, and the same relations for $f$ give $P_n(\sigma, \sigma') \equiv \iota^{-1}(a_p(f)a_{p'}(f))$, with the error at most $2\ell^{-n}$. In the same way $(a, d) = (p', p)$ gives $Q_n(\sigma, \sigma') \equiv \iota^{-1}(\chi(p)p^{k-1}a_{p'}(f))$, and $(a, d) = (1, pp')$ gives $R_n(\sigma, \sigma') \equiv \iota^{-1}(\chi(pp')(pp')^{k-1})$.
4. Limits. Let $X$ be a topological space with a dense subset $S'$, and let $\phi_n : X \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ be continuous with $|\phi_n(s) - w(s)| \le \delta_n$ for $s \in S'$, where $w(s) \in E$ and $\delta_n$ decreases to 0. Then for $m \ge n$, $|\phi_n - \phi_m| \le 2\delta_n$ on $S'$, so on $X$ by continuity, and $(\phi_n)$ is uniformly Cauchy. For each $x \in X$ and each $n$, continuity and density give $s \in S'$ with $|\phi_n(x) - \phi_n(s)| \le \delta_n$, so $\phi_n(x)$ is within $2\delta_n$ of the point $w(s)$ of $E$. These points of $E$ form a Cauchy sequence, and $E$ is complete, so $\phi_n(x)$ converges to a point of $E$. The convergence is uniform, so the limit is continuous. Apply this with $\delta_n = 2\ell^{-n}$ to $t_n$ and $d_n$ on $S' = S$, and to $P_n$, $Q_n$, $R_n$ on $S' = S \times S$, which is dense in $G \times G$. This gives continuous limits $t$, $d$, $P$, $Q$, $R$. On $S \times S$ we have $P(\sigma, \sigma') = t(\sigma)t(\sigma')$, $Q(\sigma, \sigma') = d(\sigma)t(\sigma')$ and $R(\sigma, \sigma') = d(\sigma)d(\sigma')$, so these hold on $G \times G$ by continuity. The exact identities of step 2 pass to the limit, and $d(1) = 1$. So $(t, d)$ is a continuous pseudo-character of dimension 2, with values in $E$, and $t(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \iota^{-1}(a_p(f))$ and $d(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \iota^{-1}(\chi(p)p^{k-1})$ for $p \nmid N\ell$.
5. Inertia. Let $p \nmid N\ell$ and let $\sigma$ be in an inertia group at $p$. Each $\rho_i$ is unramified at $p$, since $p \nmid N\ell^m\ell$, so $\rho_i(\sigma) = 1$ and $t_n(g\sigma) = t_n(g)$ for all $g$. In the limit $t(g\sigma) = t(g)$.
6. The representation. Part 3 of Theorem 2.4.11 gives a continuous $\rho : G \to GL_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$ with $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho = t$ and $\det\rho = d$. By step 5 and part 2 of Theorem 2.4.11, $\rho(\sigma) = 1$ for $\sigma$ in the inertia groups at $p \nmid N\ell$, so $\rho$ is unramified there. Step 4 gives the values at Frobenius. $\square$

The proof uses step 3 only for pairs of Frobenius elements, and the auxiliary functions $P_n$, $Q_n$ and $R_n$ carry the products of traces through the limit. One could instead show that $(t_n, d_n)$ is a pseudo-character modulo $\ell^n$ and use the variant of Lemma 2.4.10 for approximate pseudo-characters; the Lean proof follows the route above.

### Arithmetic and geometric normalizations

For a representation $\rho : G \to GL_n(F)$, the *dual* (or contragredient) representation is $\rho^\vee(g) = {}^t\rho(g)^{-1}$, the action on the dual space.

**Proposition 2.4.16 (duals and the two normalizations).** Let $\rho : G_\mathbb{Q} \to GL_n(F)$ be a representation over a field $F$, unramified at $p$.
1. The characteristic polynomial of $\rho^\vee(\Phi_p)$ is that of $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$. So $\rho$ has trace $a$ and determinant $d$ at the arithmetic Frobenius elements at $p$ if and only if $\rho^\vee$ has trace $a$ and determinant $d$ at the geometric Frobenius elements at $p$.
2. If $n = 2$, then $\rho^\vee \cong \rho \otimes (\det\rho)^{-1}$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* For part 1, $\rho^\vee(\mathrm{Frob}_p^{-1}) = {}^t\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$, and a matrix and its transpose have the same characteristic polynomial. For part 2, let $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in GL_2(F)$ a direct computation gives
$$J A J^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} d & -c \\ -b & a \end{pmatrix} = \det(A)\, {}^tA^{-1}.$$
So $\rho^\vee(g) = \det\rho(g)^{-1}\, J\rho(g)J^{-1}$ for all $g$, and $J$ is an isomorphism from $\rho \otimes (\det\rho)^{-1}$ to $\rho^\vee$. $\square$

Let $\rho = \rho_{f,\iota}$ be as in Theorem 2.4.4. By Proposition 2.4.16 and Proposition 2.4.6, the representation
$$\rho' = \rho^\vee \cong \rho \otimes \chi^{-1}\varepsilon_\ell^{1-k}$$
is continuous, unramified outside $N\ell$, and has $\iota(\mathrm{tr}\,\rho'(\Phi_p)) = a_p(f)$ and $\iota(\det\rho'(\Phi_p)) = \chi(p)p^{k-1}$ at the geometric Frobenius elements. Conversely, a representation with these properties at the geometric Frobenius elements has a dual with the properties of Theorem 2.4.4. So the theorem with arithmetic Frobenius and the theorem with geometric Frobenius are equivalent statements, and in dimension 2 the two representations differ only by the twist by the character $\chi^{-1}\varepsilon_\ell^{1-k}$. They have the same image in $PGL_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)$, and one is irreducible if and only if the other is. Constructions in étale cohomology, as in [Del69], give the geometric normalization in a natural way, since $\Phi_p$ acts on $H^1$ with trace $a_p$. The Tate module of item 7 above is dual to $H^1$, and on it the arithmetic normalization is natural.

For a fixed $\rho$, the two normalizations are not interchangeable. By Lemma 2.3.1 the geometric trace of $\rho_{f,\iota}$ is
$$\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\Phi_p) = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)^{-1} = \frac{\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)}{\det\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p)} = \iota^{-1}\bigl(\overline{a_p(f)}\,p^{1-k}\bigr),$$
which is not $a_p(f)$ unless $a_p(f) = 0$, since $|p^{1-k}\overline{a_p}| < |a_p|$ otherwise. So Theorem 2.4.4 with $\Phi_p$ in place of $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is false for the same $\rho$; it is true for $\rho^\vee$. For an elliptic curve $E$ the representation on $V_\ell E = T_\ell E \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ has $\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = a_p(E)$ and $\det = \varepsilon_\ell$, by the argument of item 7 and the Weil pairing. So by Theorem 2.2.11 and Chebotarev, $V_\ell E$ and $\rho_{f_E,\iota}$ have the same characteristic polynomials at Frobenius, and they are isomorphic after semisimplification. The space $H^1_{et}(E_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell) = V_\ell E^\vee$ is the geometric form.
