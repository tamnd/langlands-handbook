# Eigenforms, L-functions and Galois representations

Let $E$ be the elliptic curve $y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let
$$f_{11} = q \prod_{n \ge 1} (1 - q^n)^2 (1 - q^{11n})^2 = q - 2q^2 - q^3 + 2q^4 + q^5 + 2q^6 - 2q^7 - 2q^9 - 2q^{10} + q^{11} + \cdots$$
be the cusp form of Example 1.2.14. For each prime $p \ne 11$ the number $p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$ is the coefficient $a_p$ of $f_{11}$: for $p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29$ both are $-2, -1, 1, -2, 4, -2, 0, -1, 0$. This chapter explains this coincidence from three points of view, and it explains Ramanujan's third property, the bound $|\tau(p)| \le 2p^{11/2}$.

The first point of view is that of $L$-functions. The form $f_{11}$ is a normalized eigenform of all the Hecke operators, so its Dirichlet series $\sum a_n n^{-s}$ has an Euler product, and the Euler factors are those of the $L$-function of $E$. The Mellin transform gives the analytic continuation and the functional equation of $L(f_{11}, s)$, so $L(E, s)$ has them too. The second point of view is that of Galois representations. The group $G_\mathbb{Q} = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ acts on the Tate module $T_\ell E$, a free $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module of rank 2, and the trace of a Frobenius element at $p \nmid 11\ell$ is $p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|$. Eichler and Shimura attached to $f_{11}$ a representation of $G_\mathbb{Q}$ with the trace $a_p$ at Frobenius, and in this case it is the representation on $T_\ell E \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$. The third point of view is that of Weil's Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields. Hasse's bound $|p + 1 - |E(\mathbb{F}_p)|| \le 2\sqrt{p}$ is the bound $|a_p(f)| \le 2p^{(k-1)/2}$ for $f = f_{11}$ and $k = 2$. Deligne proved this bound for all eigenforms of weight $k \ge 2$, through the Galois representations of the forms and his proof of the Weil conjectures.

Section 2.1 proves that the normalized eigenforms are the cusp forms whose coefficients satisfy Ramanujan's relations, and it explains why the Lean project uses these relations as the definition. It also states the theory of newforms. Section 2.2 proves the Euler product and the functional equation, and it states the modularity theorem of Wiles, Taylor-Wiles and Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor, with the Hasse-Weil conjecture for elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ as a consequence. Section 2.3 states the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, proved by Deligne, deduces Ramanujan's bound for $\tau(p)$ and states the Sato-Tate conjecture for elliptic curves. Section 2.4 defines Frobenius elements, compares the arithmetic and the geometric normalizations, and states the theorem of Eichler, Shimura and Deligne. Section 2.5 states Serre's modularity conjecture, proved by Khare and Wintenberger, and the Artin conjecture for odd representations of dimension 2. It also constructs the framed deformation rings, the crystalline and semistable period rings of Fontaine, and the inertial types and Serre weight multiplicities of the Breuil-Mezard conjecture. Section 2.6 is a short introduction to Maass forms, with the conjectures of Selberg and Ramanujan for them.

Most of the theorems of this chapter are deep, and we prove only the parts that the tools of Chapter 1 reach: the characterization of eigenforms, the Euler product, the functional equation, the relation between the two normalizations of Frobenius, and the reductions between the statements. For the other parts we give the statement, a reference and an outline. The main references are Diamond and Shurman, Chapters 5 to 9, Shimura, Chapters 3 and 7, and the original papers on the map of the project.
