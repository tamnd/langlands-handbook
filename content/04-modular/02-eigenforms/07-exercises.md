## Exercises

1. Let $f_{11} = q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)^2(1 - q^{11n})^2$ be the form of Example 1.2.14. Compute $a_n(f_{11})$ for $n \le 12$ and show that they begin $1, -2, -1, 2, 1, 2, -2, 0, -2, -2, 1, -2$. Check the conditions of Theorem 2.1.3 for these coefficients: $a_6 = a_2a_3$, $a_{10} = a_2a_5$, $a_{12} = a_3a_4$, $a_4 = a_2^2 - 2$, $a_8 = a_2a_4 - 2a_2$ and $a_9 = a_3^2 - 3$. Explain why the recursion at $p = 11$ is $a_{11^{r+1}} = a_{11}a_{11^r}$.
2. Let $g \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform and $p \nmid N$ a prime. On $S_k(Np, \chi)$ the operator $U_p$ sends $\sum a_nq^n$ to $\sum a_{pn}q^n$. Show that $U_pg = a_p(g)g - \chi(p)p^{k-1}g(p\tau)$ and $U_p(g(p\tau)) = g$. Deduce that $U_p$ preserves the span of $g$ and $g(p\tau)$, with characteristic polynomial $X^2 - a_p(g)X + \chi(p)p^{k-1}$, and that its eigenvectors are the forms $f_\alpha$ of Example 2.1.9. For $g = f_{11}$ and $p = 2$, find the two eigenvalues of $U_2$ on level 22.
3. Let $w_N$ be the operator of Definition 2.2.6. Show that $w_N(w_Nf) = (-1)^kf$ for each function $f$ on $\mathbb{H}$, and that $w_N$ preserves $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$, since $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ N & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ normalizes $\Gamma_0(N)$. Deduce that for even $k$ the eigenvalues of $w_N$ are $\pm 1$.
4. Let $f \in S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ with $w_Nf = \varepsilon f$, and $\Lambda_N(s) = N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(f, s)$. Show that $\Lambda_N(s) = N^{s/2}\int_0^\infty f(iy)y^{s-1}\,dy$ for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > k/2 + 1$. Split the integral at $y = 1/\sqrt{N}$, substitute $y \mapsto 1/(Ny)$ in the part near 0, and prove Theorem 2.2.7 for $f$ in this way. Where does the factor $i^k$ come from?
5. Use $\tau(2) = -24$ and $\tau(3) = 252$ and the recursion of Theorem 2.1.3 to compute $\tau(4) = -1472$ and $\tau(9) = -113643$. Find the Satake parameters of $\Delta$ at 3 and check that they have absolute value $3^{11/2}$.
6. Let $E : y^2 = x^3 - x$. Show that $a_p(E) = 0$ for each prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, with the map $x \mapsto -x$ and the fact that $-1$ is not a square modulo $p$. Count the points of $E$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$ and $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ and check $a_5 = -2$ and $a_{13} = 6$ of Example 2.3.8.
7. Let $\mu = \frac{2}{\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$ on $[0, \pi]$. Show that $\int (2\cos\theta)^{2m}\,d\mu$ is the Catalan number $\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}$ and that the odd moments vanish. Show that $\mu$ is the image of the Haar measure of $SU(2)$ under the map that sends a matrix with eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$ to $\theta$. Deduce from Theorem 2.3.6 that the mean of $a_p(E)^2/p$ over $p \le x$ tends to 1 for a curve $E$ without complex multiplication.
8. Let $\rho : G \to GL_2(K)$ be a representation and $\rho^\vee(\sigma) = {}^t\rho(\sigma)^{-1}$. Show that $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho^\vee(\sigma) = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\sigma)/\det\rho(\sigma)$ and that $\rho^\vee \cong \rho \otimes \det\rho^{-1}$, with the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. For $\rho = \rho_{f,\iota}$ of Theorem 2.4.4, show with Lemma 2.3.1 that the trace of a geometric Frobenius $\Phi_p$ is $\overline{a_p(f)}p^{1-k}$.
9. Let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform with $f \ne 0$. Show that $\chi(-1) = (-1)^k$. With Proposition 2.4.6 and $\varepsilon_\ell(c) = -1$ for a complex conjugation $c$, show that $\det\rho_{f,\iota}(c) = -1$.
10. Use the congruence $\tau(n) \equiv \sigma_{11}(n) \pmod{691}$ of Exercise 6 of Chapter 1. Show that the reduction of $\rho_\Delta$ modulo a prime over 691 has the same characteristic polynomials of Frobenius as $\mathbf{1} \oplus \bar\varepsilon_{691}^{11}$. With Chebotarev and the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem, deduce that its semisimplification is $\mathbf{1} \oplus \bar\varepsilon_{691}^{11}$, so that it is reducible, as in Proposition 2.5.2.
11. Let $p$ be a prime, $c \in \mathbb{C}$, $u \in \mathbb{C}$ with $|u| = 1$, and $\alpha, \beta$ the roots of $X^2 - cX + u$. Show that if $c = u\bar c$, then $|c| \le 2$ if and only if $|\alpha| = |\beta| = 1$. Show that $c = 2i$, $u = 1$ gives $|c| \le 2$ and $|\alpha| = \sqrt{2} + 1$. Explain why Definition 2.6.8 gives $c = \chi(p)\bar c$ for an eigenvalue of $T_p$ on a Maass cusp form.
12. Let $f$ be a normalized eigenform of weight $k$ and level $N$, $p \nmid N$, and $\alpha, \beta$ the Satake parameters at $p$ divided by $p^{(k-1)/2}$, so that $|\alpha\beta| = 1$. Suppose that for each $m \ge 1$ the Euler factor $\prod_{j=0}^m(1 - \alpha^j\beta^{m-j}p^{-s})^{-1}$ of the symmetric power $L(\mathrm{Sym}^mf, s)$ has no pole with $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$. Show that $|\alpha|^m \le p$ for all $m$, and deduce $|\alpha| = |\beta| = 1$. This is the argument of Langlands that reduces the Ramanujan conjecture to the analytic properties of the symmetric powers, and it is the idea behind the bound of Remark 2.6.11.

## Notes and history

The theory of normalized eigenforms and of their Euler products in level one is due to Hecke, who also proved the functional equation of Theorem 2.2.4 by the Mellin transform. Mordell had proved the multiplicativity of $\tau$ in 1917 before the general theory. Atkin and Lehner introduced newforms for $\Gamma_0(N)$ in 1970, and Li extended the theory to $\Gamma_1(N)$ with characters. We followed Diamond and Shurman, *A first course in modular forms*, Chapter 5 for Section 2.1 and Chapters 6 to 9 for Section 2.4, and Shimura, *Introduction to the arithmetic theory of automorphic functions*, Chapter 7, for the construction of Galois representations in weight 2. Milne's notes on modular functions and modular forms give a short account of the same material.

Eichler proved the congruence relation for some levels in 1954, and Shimura proved it for $\Gamma_0(N)$ and $\Gamma_1(N)$ in 1958. Igusa proved that the modular curves have good reduction at the primes that do not divide the level. Deligne constructed the representations of weight $k > 2$ in [Del69] and proved the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture in [Del74] as a consequence of the Weil conjectures. Deligne and Serre treated weight 1 in [DS74]. The modularity of semistable curves is due to Wiles and to Taylor and Wiles [Wil95], and the general case to Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor [BCDT01]. Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor proved the Sato-Tate conjecture for curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with a $j$-invariant that is not an integer. Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor proved Theorem 2.3.6 for all curves without complex multiplication, and its analogue for the newforms of weight $k \ge 2$ without complex multiplication [BLGHT11]. Serre stated his conjecture in 1987. Khare and Wintenberger proved it [KW09], with the modularity lifting theorems of Kisin [Kis09].

Maass introduced the nonholomorphic forms of Section 2.6 in 1949, from the Hecke characters of real quadratic fields. Selberg stated his conjecture and proved the bound $3/16$ in [Sel65]. The bound $7/64$ is due to Kim and Sarnak [Kim03, Appendix 2]. Iwaniec, *Spectral methods of automorphic forms*, treats the spectral theory of Section 2.6, and Sarnak's notes on the generalized Ramanujan conjectures explain how Conjectures 2.6.5 and 2.6.10 fit into the conjecture for $GL_n$.
