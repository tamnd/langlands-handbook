## Exercises

1. Let $f_{11} = q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)^2(1 - q^{11n})^2$ be the form of Example 1.2.14. Compute $a_n(f_{11})$ for $n \le 12$ and show that they begin $1, -2, -1, 2, 1, 2, -2, 0, -2, -2, 1, -2$. Check the conditions of Theorem 2.1.3 for these coefficients: $a_6 = a_2a_3$, $a_{10} = a_2a_5$, $a_{12} = a_3a_4$, $a_4 = a_2^2 - 2$, $a_8 = a_2a_4 - 2a_2$ and $a_9 = a_3^2 - 3$. Explain why the recursion at $p = 11$ is $a_{11^{r+1}} = a_{11}a_{11^r}$.
2. Let $g \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform and $p \nmid N$ a prime. On $S_k(Np, \chi)$ the operator $U_p$ sends $\sum a_nq^n$ to $\sum a_{pn}q^n$. Show that $U_pg = a_p(g)g - \chi(p)p^{k-1}g(p\tau)$ and $U_p(g(p\tau)) = g$. Deduce that $U_p$ preserves the span of $g$ and $g(p\tau)$, with characteristic polynomial $X^2 - a_p(g)X + \chi(p)p^{k-1}$, and that its eigenvectors are the forms $f_\alpha$ of Example 2.1.9. For $g = f_{11}$ and $p = 2$, find the two eigenvalues of $U_2$ on level 22.
3. Let $w_N$ be the operator of Definition 2.2.6. Show that $w_N(w_Nf) = (-1)^kf$ for each function $f$ on $\mathbb{H}$, and that $w_N$ preserves $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$, since $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ N & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ normalizes $\Gamma_0(N)$. Deduce that for even $k$ the eigenvalues of $w_N$ are $\pm 1$.
4. Let $f \in S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ with $w_Nf = \varepsilon f$, and $\Lambda_N(s) = N^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(f, s)$. Show that $\Lambda_N(s) = N^{s/2}\int_0^\infty f(iy)y^{s-1}\,dy$ for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > k/2 + 1$. Split the integral at $y = 1/\sqrt{N}$, substitute $y \mapsto 1/(Ny)$ in the part near 0, and prove Theorem 2.2.7 for $f$ in this way. Where does the factor $i^k$ come from?
5. Use $\tau(2) = -24$ and $\tau(3) = 252$ and the recursion of Theorem 2.1.3 to compute $\tau(4) = -1472$ and $\tau(9) = -113643$. Find the Satake parameters of $\Delta$ at 3 and check that they have absolute value $3^{11/2}$.
6. Let $E : y^2 = x^3 - x$. Show that $a_p(E) = 0$ for each prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, with the map $x \mapsto -x$ and the fact that $-1$ is not a square modulo $p$. Count the points of $E$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$ and $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ and check $a_5 = -2$ and $a_{13} = 6$ of Example 2.3.8.
7. Let $\mu = \frac{2}{\pi}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$ on $[0, \pi]$. Show that $\int (2\cos\theta)^{2m}\,d\mu$ is the Catalan number $\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}$ and that the odd moments vanish. Show that $\mu$ is the image of the Haar measure of $SU(2)$ under the map that sends a matrix with eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$ to $\theta$. Deduce from Theorem 2.3.6 that the mean of $a_p(E)^2/p$ over $p \le x$ tends to 1 for a curve $E$ without complex multiplication.
8. Let $\rho : G \to GL_2(K)$ be a representation and $\rho^\vee(\sigma) = {}^t\rho(\sigma)^{-1}$. Show that $\mathrm{tr}\,\rho^\vee(\sigma) = \mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\sigma)/\det\rho(\sigma)$ and that $\rho^\vee \cong \rho \otimes \det\rho^{-1}$, with the matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. For $\rho = \rho_{f,\iota}$ of Theorem 2.4.4, show with Lemma 2.3.1 that the trace of a geometric Frobenius $\Phi_p$ is $\overline{a_p(f)}p^{1-k}$.
9. Let $f \in S_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform with $f \ne 0$. Show that $\chi(-1) = (-1)^k$. With Proposition 2.4.6 and $\varepsilon_\ell(c) = -1$ for a complex conjugation $c$, show that $\det\rho_{f,\iota}(c) = -1$.
10. Use the congruence $\tau(n) \equiv \sigma_{11}(n) \pmod{691}$ of Exercise 6 of Chapter 1. Show that the reduction of $\rho_\Delta$ modulo a prime over 691 has the same characteristic polynomials of Frobenius as $\mathbf{1} \oplus \bar\varepsilon_{691}^{11}$. With Chebotarev and the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem, deduce that its semisimplification is $\mathbf{1} \oplus \bar\varepsilon_{691}^{11}$, so that it is reducible, as in Proposition 2.5.2.
11. Let $p$ be a prime, $c \in \mathbb{C}$, $u \in \mathbb{C}$ with $|u| = 1$, and $\alpha, \beta$ the roots of $X^2 - cX + u$. Show that if $c = u\bar c$, then $|c| \le 2$ if and only if $|\alpha| = |\beta| = 1$. Show that $c = 2i$, $u = 1$ gives $|c| \le 2$ and $|\alpha| = \sqrt{2} + 1$. Explain why Definition 2.6.8 gives $c = \chi(p)\bar c$ for an eigenvalue of $T_p$ on a Maass cusp form.
12. Let $f$ be a normalized eigenform of weight $k$ and level $N$, $p \nmid N$, and $\alpha, \beta$ the Satake parameters at $p$ divided by $p^{(k-1)/2}$, so that $|\alpha\beta| = 1$. Suppose that for each $m \ge 1$ the Euler factor $\prod_{j=0}^m(1 - \alpha^j\beta^{m-j}p^{-s})^{-1}$ of the symmetric power $L(\mathrm{Sym}^mf, s)$ has no pole with $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$. Show that $|\alpha|^m \le p$ for all $m$, and deduce $|\alpha| = |\beta| = 1$. This is the argument of Langlands that reduces the Ramanujan conjecture to the analytic properties of the symmetric powers, and it is the idea behind the bound of Remark 2.6.11.
13. Prove Legendre's formula $v_p(n!) = \sum_{i \ge 1} \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor$, and deduce that $v_p(n!) = (n - s_p(n))/(p - 1)$, where $s_p(n)$ is the sum of the digits of $n$ in base $p$. Deduce that $p^m/m! \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ for all $m \ge 0$ and that $p^m$ divides $j!$ when $pm \le j$, as in Propositions 2.5.29 and 2.5.30. Show that $p^n/n! \to 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ if $p$ is odd, and that $2^n/n!$ does not tend to $0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$.
14. Let $p$ be odd, and let $V$ be the representation of Example 2.5.37. Show that the line $\mathbb{Q}_p e_1$ is the only line in $V$ that $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ preserves. Show that $V$ is not a direct sum of two characters. Use that a splitting would give $a \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $c(g) = a(\chi(g) - 1)$ for all $g$, so that $c$ would vanish on $H = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty}))$, and then that each $p$-power root of $p$ would lie in $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$. Show that this is false, because $\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p})$ is not a Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.
15. Let $G = \mathbb{Z}$ act on $V = \mathbb{Q}_p^2$ by $1 \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, with $O = \mathbb{Z}_p$. Show that $L = \mathbb{Z}_p e_1 + \mathbb{Z}_p e_2$ and $L' = \mathbb{Z}_p e_1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p e_2$ are stable lattices. Show that the reduction of $L$ is not semisimple and that the reduction of $L'$ is trivial, so that the two reductions are not isomorphic. Check that both have the trivial representation $\mathbb{F}_p$ with multiplicity $2$, as Theorem 2.5.43 says.
16. Let $0 \le a \le p - 1$ and let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p$. Show that $\mathrm{Sym}^a k^2$ is a simple representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Let $W \ne 0$ be a subrepresentation. Apply $u - 1$, with $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, to an element of $W$ to show that $x^a \in W$. Then show that $W$ contains $(x + ty)^a$ for each $t \in \mathbb{F}_p$, and use a Vandermonde determinant to show that $W$ contains each $x^{a-i} y^i$. Show that $\mathrm{Sym}^p k^2$ is not simple.
17. Let $(\lambda_p)$ be complex numbers, one for each prime $p$. Show that there is exactly one sequence $a = (a_n)_{n \ge 0}$ with $a_0 = 0$ and $a_1 = 1$ that satisfies the Hecke relations of Definition 2.1.12 and has $a_p = \lambda_p$ for all $p$. For the uniqueness use Theorem 2.1.14. For the existence define $a_n$ by part 1 of Theorem 2.1.14 at prime powers and by multiplicativity, and check each relation $(E_{p,m})$. Compare with the second half of the proof of Theorem 2.1.3.
18. Let $p$ be a prime, let $a$ satisfy the Hecke relations with $a_1 = 1$, and let $\alpha_p, \beta_p$ be the roots of $X^2 - a_pX + c_p$. Show that $a_{p^r} = \sum_{i=0}^{r}\alpha_p^i\beta_p^{r-i}$, and that this is $(\alpha_p^{r+1} - \beta_p^{r+1})/(\alpha_p - \beta_p)$ if $\alpha_p \ne \beta_p$. Let $h_r(x, y) = \sum_{i=0}^{r}x^iy^{r-i}$. Prove the identity of polynomials $h_\alpha h_\beta = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(\alpha,\beta)}(xy)^jh_{\alpha+\beta-2j}$ by multiplying both sides by $(x - y)^2$, and deduce the second identity of Proposition 2.1.16 again.
19. Let $a$ satisfy the Hecke relations of Definition 2.1.12. Show that if $a_1 = 0$, then $a_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$. Show that if $a_1 \ne 1$, then $a_p = 0$ for every prime $p$. Let $a_0 = 0$, and for $n \ge 1$ let $a_n = 2\prod_{p}(-c_p)^{v_p(n)/2}$ if every exponent $v_p(n)$ is even and $a_n = 0$ otherwise. Show that $a$ satisfies the Hecke relations, with $a_1 = 2$, and that $a_{mn} \ne a_ma_n$ for some coprime $m$ and $n$ if $c_p \ne 0$ for two primes $p$. So the hypothesis $a_1 = 1$ in Theorem 2.1.14 cannot be omitted.
20. Let $f_{11}$ be the form of Exercise 1, with $N = 11$ and $k = 2$, so $c_p = p$ for $p \ne 11$ and $c_{11} = 0$. Compute $a_8$ and $a_{16}$ with part 1 of Theorem 2.1.14. Check the identities of Theorem 2.1.18 for $(m, n) = (2, 4)$, $(4, 4)$, $(2, 6)$ and $(11, 11)$. In the last case, explain why the divisor $d = 11$ of $\gcd(11, 11)$ does not contribute, and compare with the field `bad` of Definition 2.1.4.
21. Let a monoid $M$ act on a commutative ring $B$, let $b \in B$, and put $c(g) = gb - b$. Show that $c$ is a cocycle in the sense of Definition 2.5.59. Show that $u' = u - b$ is fixed by $M$ in $B[u]_c$, and that $B[u]_c$ is isomorphic to $B[u]$ with the action on the coefficients only. Let $\varphi$ and $q$ be as in Definition 2.5.59 and assume $\varphi(b) = qb$. Show that $\varphi(u') = qu'$ and $N(u') = -1$. Conclude that a cocycle of the form $gb - b$ gives nothing new, and explain why Remark 2.5.65 needs $t \ne 0$ before one can say that the Kummer cocycle is not of this form.
22. Compute the defect $\ell_N((1 + x)(1 + y) - 1) - \ell_N(x) - \ell_N(y)$ of Lemma 2.5.54 for $N = 1$ and $N = 2$, with $a_0 = 1$ and $a_1 = -1/2$. Check the result against part 3 of the lemma, and write each term in the divided power form of Lemma 2.5.55. For $N = 2$, which of these terms lie in $pA_{\mathrm{cris}}^0(R)$ by Lemma 2.5.55, and why does Theorem 2.5.56 need $N > 2pm$ and not only $N > pm$?
23. Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p$. Show that the span of $X_0^p$ and $X_1^p$ in $\mathrm{Sym}^p k^2$ is stable under $GL_2(k)$, so that $\mathrm{Sym}^p k^2$ is not simple. Find the step of the proof of Theorem 2.5.66 that fails for $a = p$. Show also that for $a \ge 1$ the line $kX_1^a$ is stable under $u^-$, so that the hypothesis on $w$ in Theorem 2.5.66 cannot be omitted.
24. Let $E$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_E$ and the residue field $k$, and let $\mathrm{st}$ be the Steinberg representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$, seen as a representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, that is, the functions on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$ modulo the constants. For $P \in \mathrm{Sym}^{p-1} k^2$ let $\phi_P(v) = P(v)$ for $v \in k^2$ with entries in $\mathbb{F}_p$, $v \ne 0$. Show that $\phi_P$ is a function on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_p)$, that $P \mapsto \phi_P$ is injective and commutes with $GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for the action $(g\phi)(v) = \phi(vg)$, and that its image does not contain the constants. Deduce that the reduction of the lattice of the functions with values in $\mathcal{O}_E$ modulo the constants is $\sigma_{p-1,0}$. With Example 2.5.49 and part 3 of Proposition 2.5.73, show that Conjecture 2.5.72 gives $e(R((1, 0), 1 \oplus 1)/p) = \mu_{p-1,0}$.

## Notes and history

The theory of normalized eigenforms and of their Euler products in level one is due to Hecke, who also proved the functional equation of Theorem 2.2.4 by the Mellin transform. Mordell had proved the multiplicativity of $\tau$ in 1917 before the general theory. Atkin and Lehner introduced newforms for $\Gamma_0(N)$ in 1970, and Li extended the theory to $\Gamma_1(N)$ with characters. The proof of multiplicativity from the relations of the single operators $T_p$, at the end of Section 2.1, follows the computation of Diamond and Shurman, Section 5.3, but it works with the coefficients only and never multiplies two operators. Sturm proved his bound of Remark 2.1.21 in "On the congruence of modular forms", Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1240 (1987). We followed Diamond and Shurman, *A first course in modular forms*, Chapter 5 for Section 2.1 and Chapters 6 to 9 for Section 2.4, and Shimura, *Introduction to the arithmetic theory of automorphic functions*, Chapter 7, for the construction of Galois representations in weight 2. Milne's notes on modular functions and modular forms give a short account of the same material.

Eichler proved the congruence relation for some levels in 1954, and Shimura proved it for $\Gamma_0(N)$ and $\Gamma_1(N)$ in 1958. Igusa proved that the modular curves have good reduction at the primes that do not divide the level. Deligne constructed the representations of weight $k > 2$ in [Del69] and proved the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture in [Del74] as a consequence of the Weil conjectures. Deligne and Serre treated weight 1 in [DS74]. The modularity of semistable curves is due to Wiles and to Taylor and Wiles [Wil95], and the general case to Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor [BCDT01]. Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor proved the Sato-Tate conjecture for curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with a $j$-invariant that is not an integer. Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor proved Theorem 2.3.6 for all curves without complex multiplication, and its analogue for the newforms of weight $k \ge 2$ without complex multiplication [BLGHT11]. Serre stated his conjecture in 1987. Khare and Wintenberger proved it [KW09], with the modularity lifting theorems of Kisin [Kis09].

Maass introduced the nonholomorphic forms of Section 2.6 in 1949, from the Hecke characters of real quadratic fields. Selberg stated his conjecture and proved the bound $3/16$ in [Sel65]. The bound $7/64$ is due to Kim and Sarnak [Kim03, Appendix 2]. Iwaniec, *Spectral methods of automorphic forms*, treats the spectral theory of Section 2.6, and Sarnak's notes on the generalized Ramanujan conjectures explain how Conjectures 2.6.5 and 2.6.10 fit into the conjecture for $GL_n$.

Fontaine constructed the rings $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ of Section 2.5 from 1979 on. The standard account is his paper "Le corps des périodes $p$-adiques", Astérisque 223 (1994), with the companion papers in the same volume. The book of Fontaine and Ouyang, *Theory of $p$-adic Galois representations*, and the notes of Brinon and Conrad, *CMI summer school notes on $p$-adic Hodge theory*, give complete proofs of Theorem 2.5.33 and of the bound $\dim D_B(V) \le \dim V$. Faltings proved that the representations of abelian varieties with good reduction are crystalline, and Tsuji proved the semistable comparison in general. Berger proved that de Rham representations are potentially semistable. Breuil and Mezard stated their conjecture in "Multiplicités modulaires et représentations de $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ et de $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p)$ en $\ell = p$", Duke Math. J. 115 (2002), and Henniart proved the uniqueness of the types in the appendix to that paper. Kisin proved most cases of the conjecture in [Kis09] and used them to prove the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture for $GL_2$. The Brauer-Nesbitt argument of Theorem 2.5.43 is in Serre, *Linear representations of finite groups*, Chapter 15. The types of Bushnell and Kutzko for $GL_2$ are in [BH06].
