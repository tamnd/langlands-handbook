# Modular forms and elliptic curves

This volume is about the automorphic forms of the group $GL_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ in their classical form. These are the modular forms: holomorphic functions on the upper half plane that transform in a fixed way under a congruence subgroup of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. They are the first example of the Langlands program beyond $GL_1$. Volume 3 attached $L$-functions to Hecke characters and matched these characters with the characters of Galois groups. This volume does the same for modular forms, and the Galois representations now have dimension 2.

The theory has three parts. The first part is analytic. A modular form has a Fourier expansion $f = \sum a_n q^n$ with $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$, and the Hecke operators act on these expansions by explicit formulas. An eigenform of all the Hecke operators has multiplicative coefficients, and its $L$-function $L(f, s) = \sum a_n n^{-s}$ has an Euler product, an analytic continuation and a functional equation. The second part is arithmetic. Eichler, Shimura and Deligne attached to each eigenform a family of $\ell$-adic Galois representations of dimension 2, and the coefficient $a_p$ is the trace of a Frobenius element at $p$. Deligne deduced the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture from the Weil conjectures. The third part is the converse. The modularity theorem says that the Galois representation on the Tate module of each elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ comes from a modular form of weight 2, and the theorem of Khare and Wintenberger says that each odd irreducible representation of dimension 2 over a finite field comes from a modular form.

Chapter 1 sets up the objects: the upper half plane and its fundamental domain, the congruence subgroups $\Gamma_0(N)$ and $\Gamma_1(N)$, modular forms and their $q$-expansions, the Eisenstein series and the discriminant, the dimensions in level one, the Petersson inner product and the Hecke operators. Chapter 2 studies the eigenforms. It proves the Euler product and the functional equation, states the theorems of Deligne, of Wiles and of Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor, and of Khare and Wintenberger, and ends with the Maass forms, where the analogues of the Ramanujan conjecture are still open. Later chapters treat modular curves, elliptic curves and the proof of the Eichler-Shimura relation in detail.

The main textbook for the volume is Diamond and Shurman, *A first course in modular forms* (Graduate Texts in Mathematics 228, 2005). We follow its normalizations: the slash operator has the factor $\det(\gamma)^{k-1}$, and the Hecke operators are those of its Chapter 5. Shimura, *Introduction to the arithmetic theory of automorphic functions* (1971), is the second reference, and for level one we also follow Serre, *A course in arithmetic*, Chapter VII. The volume needs Volume 1 for number fields and local fields. The conventions on Frobenius elements, Satake parameters and $q$-expansions are those of Volume 0, Chapter 3.

Many statements of this volume are on the map of the project, and the Lean project states them. A line `Node: ... · Lean: ...` under a statement gives the node of the map and the name of the Lean statement. The remarks after these statements compare the Lean form with the textbook form. Most of the deep theorems here are stated in Lean and not proved. The text says so in each case.
