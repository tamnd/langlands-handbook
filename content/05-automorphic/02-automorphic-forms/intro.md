# Automorphic forms and cusp forms

Let $f = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n$ be a cusp form of weight $k$ on $\Gamma_0(N)$, and let $\phi_f$ be the function on $GL_2(\mathbb{Q})\backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A})$ that Chapter 1 attaches to it. The function $\phi_f$ has three properties that do not mention $f$. Under the rotation $\kappa_\theta \in SO(2)$ it transforms by $e^{ik\theta}$. The Casimir operator of $\mathfrak{gl}_2(\mathbb{R})$ acts on it by the scalar $\frac{k}{2}(1 - \frac{k}{2})$. Its integral along the cosets of the unipotent group $\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\}$ is zero. A Maass cusp form gives a function with the same three kinds of properties, with the weight $0$ and the Casimir eigenvalue $\lambda = s(1 - s)$ of the Laplacian. This chapter takes these properties as the definition. An automorphic form on $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$ is a function on the quotient that is finite under the maximal compact subgroup and under the center of the enveloping algebra, and that has moderate growth. A cusp form is an automorphic form with vanishing constant terms. Holomorphic forms and Maass forms are then two cases of one theory, and the difference between them is the representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ that they generate.

The coefficient $a_n$ also has a meaning in this language. The integral of $\phi_f$ against the character $x \mapsto \psi(-x)$ of $\mathbb{Q}\backslash\mathbb{A}$ is a function $W$ on $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$, and $W\left(\begin{smallmatrix} y & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is $a_n n^{-k/2} y_\infty^{k/2} e^{-2\pi y_\infty}$ when the finite part of $y$ generates the ideal $n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. The $q$-expansion of $f$ becomes the expansion of $\phi_f$ in the functions $W$. This expansion, the Fourier-Whittaker expansion, holds for every cusp form. With the uniqueness of local Whittaker models it proves the multiplicity one theorem: each irreducible representation occurs at most once in the space of cusp forms.

Section 2.1 treats $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$, its Lie algebra and the irreducible $(\mathfrak{g}, K)$-modules. Section 2.2 defines automorphic forms, with the examples, and shows that they form a $(\mathfrak{g}, K_\infty) \times GL_2(\mathbb{A}_f)$-module. Section 2.3 treats the constant term, cusp forms, Siegel sets, rapid decay and the discreteness of the cuspidal spectrum. Section 2.4 proves the Fourier-Whittaker expansion and treats local Whittaker models. Section 2.5 proves multiplicity one. Section 2.6 treats the principal series and the discrete series of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$ and their link to the weight. Section 2.7 has the exercises and the notes.

The main reference is Bump, *Automorphic Forms and Representations*, Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 55 (Cambridge 1997), which the handbook cites as [Bum97]. Chapter 2 of [Bum97] treats the representations of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$, and Chapter 3 treats automorphic forms and representations of $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$. We also use Gelbart, *Automorphic Forms on Adele Groups*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 83 (Princeton 1975), cited as [Gel75], and Getz and Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 300 (Springer 2024), cited as [GH24]. These books are not papers of the map, so the front matter of the sections lists no sources for them. The book of Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] is a paper of the map, and Chapter I of it is the source of the local theory that we use. The chapter needs Volume 3 for adeles, Volume 4 for modular forms and Maass forms, and Chapter 1 for the adelic dictionary. The additive character is fixed in Definition 2.3.8, and its relation to the standard character of Volume 0, Section 3.2, is stated there.
