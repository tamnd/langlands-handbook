# Automorphic representations of GL(2)

This volume describes the modular forms of Volume 4 again, in the language of representation theory. A modular form of weight $k$ and level $N$ gives a function on the adelic quotient $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A})$. The group $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$ acts on such functions by right translation. An eigenform generates an irreducible representation of this group, and that representation is a restricted tensor product of local representations, one for each place of $\mathbb{Q}$. The Hecke eigenvalue $a_p$ becomes an invariant of the local representation at $p$, and the weight becomes an invariant of the representation at the real place. This point of view is the one that generalizes to $GL_n$ and to other reductive groups, and it is the language of the rest of the handbook.

Chapter 1 builds the adelic dictionary. It proves strong approximation for $SL_2$, attaches to a modular form $f$ a function $\phi_f$ on $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$, and identifies the Hecke operators with convolution operators. Chapter 2 defines automorphic forms and cusp forms on $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$, proves the Fourier-Whittaker expansion and states multiplicity one, and describes the representations of $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Chapter 3 is the local theory at a finite prime: principal series, special and supercuspidal representations, the spherical Hecke algebra and the Satake isomorphism, the Kirillov model and the theory of newforms. Chapter 4 is the global theory: the tensor product theorem, the correspondence between newforms and cuspidal automorphic representations, strong multiplicity one, the $L$-functions of Jacquet and Langlands and the converse theorem.

The main textbook for the volume is Bump, *Automorphic forms and representations* (Cambridge 1997). We also follow Gelbart, *Automorphic forms on adele groups* (1975), Getz and Hahn, *An introduction to automorphic representations* (Graduate Texts in Mathematics 300, 2024), and the book of Jacquet and Langlands, *Automorphic forms on GL(2)* (Lecture Notes in Mathematics 114, 1970). For the local theory we use Bushnell and Henniart, *The local Langlands conjecture for GL(2)* (2006). The volume needs Volume 3 for adeles and Tate's thesis and Volume 4 for modular forms. Volume 6 continues the local theory with the local Langlands correspondence.

Many statements of this volume are on the map of the project, and the Lean project states them. A line `Node: ... · Lean: ...` under a statement gives the node of the map and the name of the Lean statement. Most of the deep theorems here are stated in Lean and not proved. The text says so in each case.
