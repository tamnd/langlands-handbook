## Supercuspidal representations and the classification

The representations of Section 3.2 all occur in a principal series. This section treats the representations that do not. They are the supercuspidal representations. We give the two definitions and their equivalence, the construction by compact induction, and the statement that compact induction gives all of them. Then we prove the classification of the irreducible admissible representations of $GL_2(K)$. Volume 6, Section 1.4 treats supercuspidal representations of $GL_n(K)$, and we use its results without repeating the proofs.

### Two definitions

For $v \in V$ and $\lambda$ in the smooth dual $V^\vee$, the *matrix coefficient* is the function $c_{v, \lambda}(g) = \lambda(\pi(g) v)$ on $G$. It is locally constant. If $\pi$ has a central character, then $|c_{v, \lambda}|$ is a function on $G / Z$.

**Definition 3.3.1 (cuspidal and supercuspidal).** Let $(\pi, V)$ be an irreducible admissible representation of $G$. It is *cuspidal* if its Jacquet module $r_B(V) = V / V(N)$ is zero. It is *supercuspidal* if each matrix coefficient $c_{v, \lambda}$ has compact support modulo $Z$.

Lean: `Langlands.SmoothRep.IsJacquetCuspidal` and `Langlands.SmoothRep.IsSupercuspidal`.

**Theorem 3.3.2 (Jacquet, Harish-Chandra).** An irreducible admissible representation of $G$ is cuspidal if and only if it is supercuspidal.

Lean: `Langlands.SmoothRep.isSupercuspidal_of_isJacquetCuspidal`, for "cuspidal implies supercuspidal". The other direction is not stated yet.

*Proof.* This is Volume 6, Theorem 1.4.3. For "cuspidal implies supercuspidal", the Cartan decomposition reduces the claim to $c_{v, \lambda}(t) = 0$ for $t = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^m, 1)$ with $|m|$ large. Since $v \in V(N)$, we have $\int_{N_0} \pi(n) v \, dn = 0$ for a large compact open $N_0 \subset N$, and $t N_0 t^{-1}$ fixes $\lambda$ for $m$ large, so $c_{v, \lambda}(t) = 0$. For $m \to -\infty$, use the contragredient. $\square$

**Proposition 3.3.3 (non-cuspidal representations embed in a principal series).** Let $\pi$ be irreducible admissible with $r_B(\pi) \ne 0$. Then there are characters $\chi_1, \chi_2$ of $K^\times$ and an injective map $\pi \to \chi_1 \times \chi_2$.

Lean: `Langlands.exists_injective_principalSeriesInd_of_isAdmissible`, in the unnormalized form of Lean, with the finiteness of $r_B(\pi)$ in `Langlands.SmoothRep.finiteDimensional_jacquetN`.

*Proof.* The representation $\pi$ is generated by one vector, so it is finitely generated. By Jacquet's theorem (Volume 6, Theorem 1.1.11), the Jacquet module of a finitely generated admissible representation is finitely generated and admissible as a representation of $T$. Since $T / T(\mathcal{O})$ is a finitely generated abelian group, $r_B(\pi)$ has finite dimension. The commutative group $T$ acts on the dual space of $r_B(\pi)$, and it has a common eigenvector there. Its kernel is a $T$-stable hyperplane, so $r_B(\pi)$ has a quotient of dimension one on which $T$ acts by a character $\chi_1 \otimes \chi_2$. Theorem 3.1.7 turns the quotient map into a nonzero map $\pi \to \chi_1 \times \chi_2$. Its kernel is a proper $G$-stable subspace of the irreducible $\pi$, so it is zero. $\square$

### Compact induction

Let $J \subset G$ be an open subgroup that contains $Z$ and is compact modulo $Z$. Let $(\Lambda, W)$ be an irreducible smooth representation of $J$. Since $J / Z$ is compact and $\Lambda$ has a central character by Schur's lemma, $\Lambda$ has finite dimension.

**Definition 3.3.4 (compact induction).** The representation $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ is the space of the functions $f : G \to W$ with $f(j g) = \Lambda(j) f(g)$ for $j \in J$, whose support is a finite union of cosets $J g$, with $G$ acting by right translation.

Lean: not stated yet.

This is Volume 6, Definition 1.4.7. For $w \in W$, let $f_w$ be the function supported on $J$ with $f_w(j) = \Lambda(j) w$. The translates of the $f_w$ span $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$.

**Proposition 3.3.5 (Frobenius reciprocity for compact induction).** For a smooth representation $(\pi, V)$ of $G$, the restriction $\Phi \mapsto (w \mapsto \Phi(f_w))$ is an isomorphism
$$\mathrm{Hom}_G(\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda, \pi) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_J(\Lambda, \pi|_J).$$

Lean: not stated yet.

*Proof.* For $j \in J$, the translate $j \cdot f_w$ equals $f_{\Lambda(j) w}$, so $w \mapsto f_w$ is a $J$-map and the restriction lands in $\mathrm{Hom}_J$. The map $w \mapsto f_w$ is injective, and $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ is spanned by the vectors $g \cdot f_w$, so a $G$-map $\Phi$ is determined by its restriction and the restriction is injective. Conversely, given $\phi \in \mathrm{Hom}_J(\Lambda, \pi|_J)$, define
$$\Phi(f) = \sum_{g \in J \backslash G} \pi(g^{-1}) \phi(f(g)).$$
The sum is finite, and each term depends only on the coset $J g$, since $\pi((j g)^{-1}) \phi(\Lambda(j) f(g)) = \pi(g^{-1}) \phi(f(g))$. A change of variable shows that $\Phi$ commutes with $G$, and $\Phi(f_w) = \phi(w)$. $\square$

An element $g \in G$ *intertwines* $\Lambda$ if $\mathrm{Hom}_{J \cap g^{-1} J g}(\Lambda, \Lambda^g) \ne 0$, where $\Lambda^g(x) = \Lambda(g x g^{-1})$.

**Theorem 3.3.6 (irreducibility of compact induction).** If only the elements of $J$ intertwine $\Lambda$, then $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ is irreducible, admissible and supercuspidal.

Lean: not stated yet.

*Proof.* This is Volume 6, Theorem 1.4.8. By Mackey's formula and Proposition 3.3.5, $\mathrm{End}_G(\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda)$ is a sum of intertwining spaces over the double cosets $J g J$, so it is $\mathbb{C}$. The matrix coefficients of the $f_w$ are supported on $J$, and an argument with them shows that each nonzero $G$-stable subspace contains all $f_w$. $\square$

**Example 3.3.7 (depth zero over $\mathbb{Q}_p$).** Let $\theta$ be a character of $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^\times$ with $\theta \ne \theta^p$. Green's construction gives a cuspidal irreducible representation $\lambda_\theta$ of $GL_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ of dimension $p - 1$, with $\lambda_\theta \cong \lambda_{\theta'}$ if and only if $\theta' \in \{\theta, \theta^p\}$. Let $J = \mathbb{Q}_p^\times GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and let $\Lambda$ be $\lambda_\theta$ on $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and a scalar $c$ on $\mathrm{diag}(p, p)$. Volume 6, Example 1.4.9 shows that only $J$ intertwines $\Lambda$, so $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ is supercuspidal, with central character $\theta$ on $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ and $\omega(p) = c$. These are the supercuspidal representations of *depth zero*. For $p = 3$, $\omega = 1$ forces $c = 1$ and $\theta$ trivial on $\mathbb{F}_3^\times$. The group $\mathbb{F}_9^\times / \mathbb{F}_3^\times$ is cyclic of order $4$, on it $\theta^3 = \theta^{-1}$, and the two characters of order $4$ are conjugate. So exactly one depth zero supercuspidal representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_3)$ has trivial central character.

**Theorem 3.3.8 (Kutzko).** Each irreducible supercuspidal representation of $GL_2(K)$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ for some $J$ and $\Lambda$ as in Theorem 3.3.6.

Lean: not stated yet.

This is the case $n = 2$ of Volume 6, Theorem 1.4.10. Kutzko proved it for $GL_2$ in [Kut80], and [BH06], Chapter 4 gives a full account with the pairs $(J, \Lambda)$ made explicit by strata.

### The classification

**Theorem 3.3.9 (classification).** Each irreducible admissible representation $\pi$ of $GL_2(K)$ is isomorphic to exactly one representation in the following list.
1. An irreducible principal series $\pi(\chi_1, \chi_2) = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$, with $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \ne |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$. Here $\pi(\chi_1, \chi_2) \cong \pi(\chi_2, \chi_1)$, and there are no other isomorphisms.
2. A special representation $\eta \mathrm{St}$.
3. A character $\eta \circ \det$.
4. A supercuspidal representation.

The four types are distinguished by $\dim r_B(\pi)$, which is $2$, $1$, $1$ and $0$, and by $\dim \pi$, which is finite only in type 3.

Lean: `Langlands.classification` and `Langlands.IrrRep.isJacquetCuspidal_or_classification`, and for admissible representations `Langlands.classification_of_isAdmissible` and `Langlands.isJacquetCuspidal_or_classification_of_isAdmissible`. These give "each $\pi$ is in the list". That the types are disjoint is not stated yet.

*Proof.* If $r_B(\pi) = 0$, then $\pi$ is supercuspidal by Theorem 3.3.2. Otherwise, by Proposition 3.3.3, $\pi$ is a subrepresentation of some $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$. If $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ is irreducible, then $\pi \cong \chi_1 \times \chi_2$. If not, then $\pi$ is the unique proper $G$-stable subspace of Theorem 3.2.1. In case 2 of that theorem it is $\eta \circ \det$. In case 3 it is $\eta \mathrm{St}$, by Proposition 3.2.5, part 2. So $\pi$ is in the list.

For the uniqueness, the Jacquet module of $\pi(\chi_1, \chi_2)$ has dimension $2$ by Theorem 3.1.8, the one of $\eta \mathrm{St}$ has dimension $1$ by Proposition 3.2.5, and the one of $\eta \circ \det$ has dimension $1$, as we computed in the proof of Proposition 3.2.5. Only the characters have finite dimension. So the types are disjoint. Inside type 1 the isomorphisms are those of Proposition 3.2.3, inside type 2 use Proposition 3.2.5, part 4, and inside type 3 a character of $G$ determines $\eta$ since $\det$ is onto. $\square$

This is Volume 6, Theorem 1.5.5. Jacquet and Langlands prove it in [JL70], Chapter 1 through the Kirillov model. The infinite-dimensional types 1, 2 and 4 are the *generic* representations (Section 3.5), and types 2 and 4 are the *discrete series*, `Langlands.IsDiscreteSeries` in Lean.

**Remark 3.3.10 (dihedral representations).** Let $E / K$ be a separable quadratic extension and $\xi$ a character of $E^\times$ that does not factor through the norm. The Weil representation gives a supercuspidal $\pi_\xi$ ([JL70], Chapter 1), with Langlands parameter $\mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi$. If $p$ is odd, each supercuspidal representation of $GL_2(K)$ is of this form. For $p = 2$ there are also *exceptional* ones. See Volume 6, Chapter 3 and `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL2`. In Example 3.3.7, $E$ is unramified and $\xi$ restricts to $\theta$ on the units.
