## Exercises

1. Let $N = 1$, $f \in M_k(SL_2(\mathbb{Z}))$ and $g_p = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & p \end{pmatrix} \in GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Use Example 1.1.8 to write $\iota_p(g_p) = \gamma\,\iota_\infty(g_\infty)\,k$ as in Theorem 1.2.5, and show that $\phi_f(\iota_p(g_p)) = p^{-k/2}f((i - 1)/p)$. Which point of $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}$ corresponds to $\iota_p(g_p)$ under Corollary 1.2.7?
2. Deduce from Theorem 1.2.2 that $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is dense in $SL_2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$. Show that $SL_2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}) \to SL_2(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ is surjective, and deduce that $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) \to SL_2(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ is surjective.
3. Let $U$ be an open subgroup of $GL_2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$. Show that $\det$ induces a bijection from $GL_2(\mathbb{Q})\backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A})/GL_2^+(\mathbb{R})U$ onto $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times/\det U$. For $U = K(N)$ show that this set has $\varphi(N)$ elements, and that the fiber over the class of $1$ is $\Gamma(N)\backslash GL_2^+(\mathbb{R})$.
4. Show that $\sigma\Gamma_0(N) \mapsto \sigma_fK_0(N)$ is a well defined bijection from $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})/\Gamma_0(N)$ onto $GL_2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})/K_0(N)$. Deduce part 5 of Lemma 1.1.4 from Volume 4, Proposition 1.1.8, or conversely.
5. Define a space $\mathcal{A}_k(N, \chi)$ by the conditions of Definition 1.5.1 for any $k$ and $\chi$, without the assumption $\chi(-1) = (-1)^k$. Show that $\mathcal{A}_k(N, \chi) = 0$ if $\chi(-1) \ne (-1)^k$. Use the rational matrix $-I = \iota_\infty(-I)\,(-I)_f$ and $\iota_\infty(-I) = \iota_\infty(\kappa_\pi)$.
6. Let $f \in M_k(\Gamma_1(N))$, and let $\phi_f$ be the function of Proposition 1.3.5 for the trivial character of $K_1(N)$, as in Remark 1.5.6. Let $k_0 \in K_0(N)$ with $d_{k_0} \equiv d \bmod N$. Show that $\phi_f(gk_0) = \phi_{\langle d\rangle^{-1}f}(g)$. Deduce that $\phi_f(gk_0) = \lambda_\chi(k_0)\phi_f(g)$ for $f \in M_k(N, \chi)$.
7. Let $f \in M_k(N, \chi)$. Show that $(R\phi_f)(\iota_\infty(n(x)a(y)\kappa_\theta)) = e^{i(k+2)\theta}y^{k/2}(2iyf'(\tau) + kf(\tau))$ with $\tau = x + iy$. Show that $LR\phi_f = -k\phi_f$ in two ways: with Proposition 1.4.5, and with Lemma 1.4.2. Deduce that $R\phi_f$ is not of the form $\phi_g$ for $g \in M_{k+2}(N, \chi)$ if $k \ne 0$ and $f \ne 0$.
8. Let $\phi$ be smooth of weight 0 and right invariant under $\iota_\infty(\mathbb{R}_{>0})$, and let $u(x + iy) = \phi(\iota_\infty(n(x)a(y)))$. Show with Proposition 1.4.5 that $(\Delta\phi)(\iota_\infty(n(x)a(y))) = -y^2(u_{xx} + u_{yy})$.
9. Show that $\|gh\| \le 2\|g\|\|h\|$ for $g, h \in GL_2(\mathbb{A})$, and that $\|g\,\iota_p(h)\| \le \|g\|\|h\|_p$ for $h \in GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Compute $\|\iota_\infty(a(y))\|$ and $\mathfrak{h}(\iota_\infty(a(y)))$ for $y \ge 1$. Deduce that $|\phi_{E_k}(\iota_\infty(a(y)))|/\mathfrak{h}^{k/2} \to 1$ as $y \to \infty$, and that no bound $|\phi_{E_k}(g)| \le C\|g\|^M$ holds with $M < k$.
10. Show that $T_p * T_p = 1_{K_p\mathrm{diag}(p^2, 1)K_p} + (p + 1)R_p$ in $\mathcal{H}_p$. Let $f \in M_k(N, \chi)$ be a normalized eigenform and $p \nmid N$. Show that $1_{K_p\mathrm{diag}(p^2, 1)K_p}$ acts on $\phi_f$ by $p^{2-k}a_{p^2}(f) - \chi(p)$. Use the recursion of Volume 4, Definition 1.5.10.
11. Show that each element of $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$ has the form $b\,n\,\mathrm{diag}(t_1, t_2)\,\iota_\infty(\kappa_\theta)\,k_0$ with $b \in B(\mathbb{Q})$, $n \in N(\mathbb{A})$, ideles $t_1$, $t_2$ with positive infinite components, $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ and $k_0 \in GL_2(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})$. Let $k \ge 4$ be even. Show that the constant term of $\phi_{E_k}$ at $n\,\mathrm{diag}(t_1, t_2)\,\iota_\infty(\kappa_\theta)\,k_0$ is $|t_1/t_2|_\mathbb{A}^{k/2}e^{ik\theta}$.

## Notes and history

The idea of treating modular forms as functions on a group goes back to the book of Gelfand, Graev and Piatetski-Shapiro, *Representation theory and automorphic functions* (Russian 1966, English translation 1969). There the cusp forms become vectors in the space of a representation of $SL_2(\mathbb{R})$, and the weight becomes a $K$-type. The adelic form, with the Hecke operators as convolutions at the finite places, is the starting point of [JL70]. Gelbart's book [Gel75] made the dictionary of this chapter available as an introduction, and its Chapter 3 is our model for Sections 1.3 to 1.6. [Bum97], Section 3.6, gives the same dictionary with full proofs, and its Chapter 2 treats the Lie algebra and the Casimir operator as we do in Section 1.4. Borel and Jacquet, *Automorphic forms and automorphic representations*, in Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 33, part 1, 1979 (the Corvallis proceedings), fixed the general definitions that Chapter 2 uses.

Strong approximation for $SL_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is elementary, and the proof of Section 1.2 by elementary matrices is the standard one. The general theorem for simply connected groups is due to Kneser and Platonov, see Remark 1.2.4. The spherical Hecke algebra goes back to Satake, *Theory of spherical functions on reductive algebraic groups over p-adic fields*, Publ. Math. IHES 18 (1963), and the proof of commutativity by the transpose is the trick of Gelfand from his work on spherical functions. The classical Hecke operators and their double cosets are in Shimura, *Introduction to the arithmetic theory of automorphic functions* (1971), Chapter 3. The normalization of Remark 1.6.13 is the one of the Lean project and of Volume 0, Section 3.2. Volume 7, Example 1.4.9, finds the same eigenvalue $p^{1 - k/2}a_p(f)$ in the setting of $GL_n$, and Volume 7, Theorem 1.1.4, proves strong approximation for $SL_n$ over a number field.

No statement of this chapter has a node on the map of the project. The Lean project has the vocabulary for cusp forms on $GL_n$ over a number field, see Remarks 1.1.5, 1.4.16 and 1.6.14, but it does not formalize the lift $f \mapsto \phi_f$ or the dictionary.
