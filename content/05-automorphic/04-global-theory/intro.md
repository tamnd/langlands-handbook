# The global theory

Let $f_{11} = q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)^2(1 - q^{11n})^2$ be the newform of weight 2 and level 11 of Volume 4. Chapter 1 attaches to it a cusp form $\phi_{f_{11}}$ on $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash GL_2(\mathbb{A})$, and the right translates of this function span a representation $\pi$ of $GL_2(\mathbb{A})$. This chapter proves that $\pi$ is irreducible and that it is a restricted tensor product $\bigotimes'_v \pi_v$ of local representations. At a prime $p \ne 11$ the component $\pi_p$ is an unramified principal series, and its Satake parameters $\alpha_p, \beta_p$ are the roots of $X^2 - a_p(f_{11})p^{-1/2}X + 1$. At $p = 11$ the component is the Steinberg representation, because $a_{11}(f_{11}) = 1$. At the real place it is the discrete series $D_2$. The level 11 is the conductor of $\pi$, the functional equation of $L(f_{11}, s)$ is the functional equation of the $L$-function of $\pi$, and the root number $+1$ is a product of the local root numbers $-1$ at 11 and $-1$ at the real place. All the data of the form are local data of the representation.

The chapter has seven sections. Section 4.1 proves the tensor product theorem of Flath. Section 4.2 proves the basic properties of the local components of a cuspidal representation. Section 4.3 matches newforms of weight $k$ and level $N$ with cuspidal representations of conductor $N$ and component $D_k$ at infinity. Section 4.4 proves strong multiplicity one. Section 4.5 proves the functional equation of the global $L$-function from the Hecke integral. Section 4.6 gives the converse theorems. Section 4.7 states the Ramanujan conjecture in adelic form and deduces it from Deligne's theorem for holomorphic newforms of weight $k \ge 2$.

The main source is the book of Jacquet and Langlands [JL70]. We also follow Bump, *Automorphic forms and representations* (Cambridge 1997), cited as [Bum97], Chapter 3, and for the local facts Chapter 4. Gelbart, *Automorphic forms on adele groups* (Annals of Mathematics Studies 83, 1975), cited as [Gel75], and Getz and Hahn, *An introduction to automorphic representations* (Graduate Texts in Mathematics 300, 2024), cited as [GH24], treat the same material. For the local theory we use [BH06]. The chapter uses Chapters 1 to 3: the adelic dictionary, the Fourier-Whittaker expansion and the decomposition of the space of cusp forms, and the classification of the representations of $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with the theory of newforms. It also uses Volume 4, Chapter 2, for newforms and for the functional equation of Hecke, and the normalizations of Volume 0, Chapter 3. We use the unitary normalization of the Satake parameters throughout.

The notation is fixed for the chapter. We write $\mathbb{A}$ for the adeles of $\mathbb{Q}$, $G = GL_2$, $G_v = GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_v)$, $K_p = GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, $K_\infty = O(2)$ and $K_f = \prod_p K_p$. For $x \in \mathbb{A}$ and $y \in \mathbb{A}^\times$ we put
$$n(x) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad a(y) = \begin{pmatrix} y & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
and the same notation is used over $\mathbb{Q}_v$. $B$ is the group of upper triangular matrices, $N = \{n(x)\}$, $Z$ is the centre and $P = \{n(x)a(y)\}$ is the mirabolic subgroup. For characters $\chi_1, \chi_2$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$, $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ is the normalized induced representation, and $\eta\mathrm{St}$ is the twist of the Steinberg representation by $\eta \circ \det$, as in Chapter 3 and Volume 6, Section 1.5. The additive character $\psi = \prod_v \psi_v$ of $\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{Q}$ is the one of Volume 0, Chapter 3.
