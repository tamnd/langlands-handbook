# Number fields, local fields and adeles

This volume gives the algebraic number theory that the rest of the handbook uses. Chapter 1 treats number fields: their rings of integers, the factorization of ideals, the two finiteness theorems and the decomposition of primes in extensions. Chapter 2 treats absolute values, completions and local fields. Chapter 3 puts all the completions of a number field together in the ring of adeles and the group of ideles, and it proves the finiteness theorems of Chapter 1 again in this language.

The volume is a background volume. It follows one standard textbook closely, and the textbook is Neukirch, *Algebraic Number Theory* (Springer, 1999). The handbook cites it as [Neu99]. Chapter 1 follows [Neu99], Chapter I, Sections 2 to 10. Chapter 2 follows [Neu99], Chapter II, Sections 1 to 8, and the part of Chapter III, Section 2 on the different and the discriminant. Neukirch does not treat the adeles in the same detail, so Chapter 3 follows the chapter "Global fields" of Cassels in the book of Cassels and Fröhlich, *Algebraic Number Theory* (Academic Press, 1967), and Neukirch, Chapter VI, Section 1. The handbook cites the book of Cassels and Fröhlich as [CF67]. Its Chapter I, "Local fields" by Fröhlich, is a second source for Chapter 2. Serre's book [Ser79] is a third source for the local theory.

The volume gives full proofs when they are short. For a longer proof it gives the main steps and the place in [Neu99] or [CF67] where the reader finds the details. The order of the results is the order of [Neu99], with two changes. First, the volume proves the finiteness of the class number and the unit theorem in one section, because both proofs use the same lattice point argument. Second, Chapter 3 proves that the group of idele classes of norm one is compact, and it deduces both finiteness theorems from this fact. Thus the reader sees two proofs of each theorem.

The notation is the notation of Volume 0, Chapter 3, and it differs from the notation of Neukirch in one point. The handbook writes $F$ for a number field and $K$ for a local field. Neukirch writes $K$ for both. The absolute value at a complex place is the square of the usual absolute value, so that the product formula holds with no exponents. The arithmetic Frobenius is $\mathrm{Frob}$, and the geometric Frobenius is $\Phi = \mathrm{Frob}^{-1}$.
