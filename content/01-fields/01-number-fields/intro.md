# Number fields

A number field is a finite extension $F$ of $\mathbb{Q}$. Its ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$ has the role that $\mathbb{Z}$ has for $\mathbb{Q}$. In $\mathcal{O}_F$ the factorization of elements into irreducible elements is not unique in general. Dedekind found the correct statement: the factorization of ideals into prime ideals is unique. The class group measures the difference between ideals and elements, and the unit group measures the difference between elements and principal ideals. Both groups are controlled by two finiteness theorems. The class group is finite, and the unit group is finitely generated, with a rank that the theorem of Dirichlet gives.

The chapter then studies an extension $L / F$ of number fields. A prime of $F$ decomposes in $L$ into a product of primes, with ramification indices and residue degrees. When $L / F$ is Galois, the Galois group acts on the primes above a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $F$, and each prime $\mathfrak{P}$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ has a decomposition group and an inertia group. If $\mathfrak{p}$ is unramified, the decomposition group is cyclic with a canonical generator, the Frobenius element. The Frobenius elements are the reason for most of the later volumes: a Galois representation is known through the traces of its Frobenius elements, and the Langlands program compares these traces with the Hecke eigenvalues of automorphic forms.

Section 1.1 defines the ring of integers, the norm, the trace and the discriminant. Section 1.2 proves the unique factorization of ideals in a Dedekind domain. Section 1.3 gives the geometry of numbers of Minkowski and proves the finiteness of the class number and the unit theorem. Section 1.4 treats the decomposition of primes, the decomposition and inertia groups and the Frobenius elements. Section 1.5 computes all these objects for quadratic and cyclotomic fields, and it deduces the law of quadratic reciprocity.

The chapter follows [Neu99], Chapter I, Sections 2 to 10. The reader must know Galois theory, the basic theory of rings and modules over a principal ideal domain, and point set topology. This is the prerequisite of the whole volume.
