# Valuations and local fields

An absolute value on a field measures the size of its elements. On $\mathbb{Q}$ there is the usual absolute value, and for each prime $p$ there is the $p$-adic absolute value, for which an integer is small when it is divisible by a high power of $p$. Ostrowski's theorem says that there are no others, up to equivalence. The completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ for the $p$-adic absolute value is the field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of $p$-adic numbers. In $\mathbb{Q}_p$ a congruence modulo all powers of $p$ becomes an equation, and Hensel's lemma solves such equations by Newton's method.

A local field is a field that is locally compact for a nondiscrete topology. The archimedean local fields are $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$. The others are the finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and the fields $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$. A nonarchimedean local field $K$ has a compact ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ with a unique maximal ideal, and the group $K^\times$ has a simple structure. The finite extensions of $K$ are easier to describe than the extensions of a number field. The unramified extensions correspond to the extensions of the residue field, and they are all cyclic, with a canonical generator, the Frobenius. The tamely ramified extensions are obtained by taking roots of a uniformizer.

Section 2.1 treats absolute values, Ostrowski's theorem for $\mathbb{Q}$ and the approximation theorem. Section 2.2 constructs the completions, proves Hensel's lemma, and determines all absolute values of a number field and their extensions to finite extensions. Section 2.3 defines local fields and computes the structure of $K^\times$ and of its unit filtration. Section 2.4 treats the finite extensions of a local field. Section 2.5 compares the global and the local situation, and it defines the different and the discriminant.
