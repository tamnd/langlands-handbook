## Extensions of local fields

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field with the notation of Section 2.3, and let $L / K$ be a finite separable extension of degree $n$. By Theorem 2.2.7, the absolute value of $K$ extends uniquely to $L$, and $L$ is again a local field. Its ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_L$ is the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_K$, its residue field is $l$, and $e = e(L/K)$ and $f = f(L/K) = [l : k]$ are its ramification index and residue degree. We follow [Neu99], Chapter II, Sections 6 and 7, and [Ser79], Chapters I, III and IV.

### The fundamental identity

**Theorem 2.4.1 (the local fundamental identity).** We have $ef = n$. More precisely, let $\omega_1, \dots, \omega_f \in \mathcal{O}_L$ be lifts of a basis of $l$ over $k$, and let $\varpi_L$ be a uniformizer of $L$. Then the elements $\omega_i \varpi_L^j$ with $1 \le i \le f$ and $0 \le j < e$ form a basis of $\mathcal{O}_L$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$.

*Proof.* Let $M$ be the $\mathcal{O}_K$-module generated by the elements $\omega_i \varpi_L^j$. We first show that they are linearly independent over $K$. Assume that $\sum_{i,j} a_{ij} \omega_i \varpi_L^j = 0$ with $a_{ij} \in K$ not all zero. Multiply by a power of $\varpi_K$ so that all $a_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}_K$ and some $a_{ij}$ is a unit. For each $j$, let $s_j = \sum_i a_{ij} \omega_i$. If some $a_{ij}$ with this $j$ is a unit, then $s_j$ is a unit, because the $\bar\omega_i$ are linearly independent over $k$. If $s_j \ne 0$ in general, let $\varpi_K^r$ be the largest power of $\varpi_K$ that divides all $a_{ij}$ with this $j$. The same argument shows that $s_j / \varpi_K^r$ is a unit, so $v_L(s_j) = re$. Thus $v_L(s_j) \equiv 0 \pmod e$ for all $j$ with $s_j \ne 0$, and $v_L(s_j \varpi_L^j) \equiv j \pmod e$. These valuations are distinct for distinct $j$, so the sum $\sum_j s_j \varpi_L^j$ has the valuation of its smallest term, and it is not zero. This is a contradiction.

Next we show that $M = \mathcal{O}_L$. The elements $\omega_i \varpi_L^j$ with $0 \le j < e$ generate $\mathcal{O}_L / \varpi_K \mathcal{O}_L$ over $k$, because $\varpi_K \mathcal{O}_L = \mathfrak{p}_L^e$ and each $\mathfrak{p}_L^j / \mathfrak{p}_L^{j+1}$ is spanned by $\varpi_L^j$ over $l$. Thus $\mathcal{O}_L = M + \varpi_K \mathcal{O}_L$. Iterating, $\mathcal{O}_L = M + \varpi_K^m \mathcal{O}_L$ for each $m$, so $M$ is dense in $\mathcal{O}_L$. The module $M$ is a finitely generated free $\mathcal{O}_K$-module, so it is compact, and it is closed. Thus $M = \mathcal{O}_L$. Finally $M \otimes K = L$ gives $ef = n$. $\square$

The extension $L / K$ is *unramified* if $e = 1$, *totally ramified* if $e = n$, and *tamely ramified* if $p \nmid e$. Here $l / k$ is separable automatically, because $k$ is finite.

### Unramified extensions

**Theorem 2.4.2 (unramified extensions).** Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K$ of $K$, and let $n \ge 1$.
1. There is exactly one unramified extension $K_n \subset \bar K$ of $K$ of degree $n$. It is $K_n = K(\mu_{q^n - 1})$.
2. The extension $K_n / K$ is Galois, and reduction is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K_n / K) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(k_n / k)$, where $k_n$ is the field with $q^n$ elements. Thus $\mathrm{Gal}(K_n/K)$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by the *Frobenius* $\mathrm{Frob}$, the unique element with $\mathrm{Frob}(x) \equiv x^q \pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{K_n}}$ for $x \in \mathcal{O}_{K_n}$.
3. A finite extension $L \subset \bar K$ of $K$ is unramified if and only if $L \subset K_n$ for some $n$, and then $L = K_{[L:K]}$.

*Proof.* Let $L = K(\zeta)$ with $\zeta$ a primitive $(q^n - 1)$-th root of unity, and let $g$ be the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $K$. It lies in $\mathcal{O}_K[X]$ by Corollary 2.2.5, and it divides $X^{q^n - 1} - 1$. The reduction of $X^{q^n - 1} - 1$ is separable over $k$, because $q^n - 1$ is prime to $p$. Thus $\bar g$ is separable. It is irreducible: a factorization of $\bar g$ into coprime factors would lift to a factorization of $g$ by Theorem 2.2.4. Thus $[L : K] = \deg \bar g = [k(\bar\zeta) : k]$. The element $\bar\zeta$ has order $q^n - 1$ in $l^\times$, so $k(\bar\zeta) = k_n$. Thus $[L : K] = n \le f(L/K)$, and $ef = n$ gives $e = 1$ and $f = n$. So $L$ is unramified of degree $n$.

Conversely let $L'$ be an unramified extension of degree $n$. Its residue field has $q^n$ elements, so $L'$ contains $\mu_{q^n - 1}$ by Theorem 2.3.4. Thus $L' \supset K(\mu_{q^n - 1}) = L$, and both have degree $n$, so $L' = L$. This proves part 1, and part 3 follows: a subfield of $K_n$ is unramified, because $e$ is multiplicative in towers, and an unramified $L$ of degree $m$ is $K_m$.

The field $K_n$ is the splitting field of $X^{q^n} - X$, so it is Galois over $K$. Each $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(K_n/K)$ preserves $\mathcal{O}_{K_n}$ and its maximal ideal, so it induces $\bar\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(k_n/k)$. If $\bar\sigma = 1$, then $\sigma$ fixes the Teichmüller representatives, because $\sigma(\omega(a))$ is a root of $X^{q^n} - X$ that lifts $a$. These generate $K_n$, so $\sigma = 1$. Thus the map is injective, and it is bijective because both groups have order $n$. $\square$

Let $K^{ur} = \bigcup_n K_n$ be the *maximal unramified extension* of $K$ in $\bar K$. By part 2,
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ur} / K) \cong \varprojlim_n \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} = \hat{\mathbb{Z}},$$
with the Frobenius as a topological generator. The *geometric Frobenius* is $\Phi = \mathrm{Frob}^{-1}$. Volume 3 normalizes local class field theory with $\Phi$, so that a uniformizer corresponds to a geometric Frobenius. The residue field of $K^{ur}$ is an algebraic closure $\bar k$ of $k$.

**Proposition 2.4.3 (the maximal unramified subextension).** Let $L / K$ be finite of degree $n$, with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. Let $T = L \cap K^{ur}$. Then $T = K_f$, the residue field of $T$ is $l$, and $L / T$ is totally ramified of degree $e$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.3.4, $L$ contains $\mu_{q^f - 1}$, so $L \supset K_f$. An unramified subextension $T'$ of $L$ has residue degree $[T' : K]$ dividing $f$, so $T' \subset K_f$ by Theorem 2.4.2. Thus $T = K_f$. Its residue field has $q^f$ elements, so it is $l$, and $L / T$ has residue degree 1 and ramification index $e = [L : T]$. $\square$

### Totally ramified extensions

A polynomial $g = X^e + a_{e-1} X^{e-1} + \dots + a_0 \in \mathcal{O}_K[X]$ is an *Eisenstein polynomial* if $a_i \in \mathfrak{p}_K$ for all $i$ and $a_0 \notin \mathfrak{p}_K^2$.

**Theorem 2.4.4 (totally ramified extensions).** An Eisenstein polynomial is irreducible over $K$. If $\pi$ is a root of an Eisenstein polynomial of degree $e$, then $K(\pi) / K$ is totally ramified of degree $e$, $\pi$ is a uniformizer of $K(\pi)$, and $\mathcal{O}_{K(\pi)} = \mathcal{O}_K[\pi]$. Conversely, if $L/K$ is totally ramified, then each uniformizer $\varpi_L$ of $L$ is a root of an Eisenstein polynomial, and $L = K(\varpi_L)$.

Lean: `Polynomial.IsEisensteinAt.irreducible`, from Mathlib, for the irreducibility

*Proof.* Let $g$ be an Eisenstein polynomial of degree $e$, $\pi$ a root of $g$ in $\bar K$, and $L = K(\pi)$ of degree $m \le e$. Let $w = v_L / e(L/K)$ be the valuation of $L$ that extends $v_K$. In the equation $\pi^e = -(a_{e-1} \pi^{e-1} + \dots + a_0)$, the term $a_0$ has $w(a_0) = 1$, and the terms $a_i \pi^i$ with $1 \le i < e$ have $w(a_i \pi^i) \ge 1 + i\,w(\pi) > 1$ if $w(\pi) > 0$. The left side has $w(\pi^e) = e\,w(\pi)$. If $w(\pi) \le 0$ the left side dominates, which is impossible. Thus $w(\pi) > 0$, the right side has $w = 1$, and $w(\pi) = 1/e$. Hence $e(L/K) \ge e \ge m$. Since $e(L/K) \le m$, we get $m = e = e(L/K)$, $g$ is irreducible, and $v_L(\pi) = 1$. By Theorem 2.4.1 with $f = 1$ and $\omega_1 = 1$, the elements $1, \pi, \dots, \pi^{e-1}$ form a basis of $\mathcal{O}_L$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$.

Conversely let $L / K$ be totally ramified of degree $e$, and let $g$ be the minimal polynomial of $\varpi_L$. Its roots are the conjugates of $\varpi_L$ in $\bar K$, and each has valuation $w = 1/e$, by Theorem 2.2.7. The coefficients of $g$ are elementary symmetric functions of the roots, so $a_i \in \mathfrak{p}_K$, and $a_0 = \pm N_{L/K}(\varpi_L)$ has $v_K(a_0) = 1$. Thus $g$ is Eisenstein. Its degree is $[K(\varpi_L) : K] \ge e(K(\varpi_L)/K) = e$, so $L = K(\varpi_L)$. $\square$

### Tamely ramified extensions

**Theorem 2.4.5 (totally tamely ramified extensions).** Let $L / K$ be totally ramified of degree $e$ with $p \nmid e$. Then there is a uniformizer $\varpi_K$ of $K$ with $L = K(\varpi_K^{1/e})$. That is, $L = K(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^e = \varpi_K$.

*Proof.* Let $\varpi_L$ be a uniformizer of $L$ and $\varpi$ one of $K$. Then $\varpi_L^e = u \varpi$ with $u \in \mathcal{O}_L^\times$. The residue field of $L$ is $k$, so $u = \omega \, u_1$ with $\omega \in \mu_{q-1} \subset K$ and $u_1 \in U_L^{(1)}$, by Theorem 2.3.7 for $L$. By Proposition 2.3.8 for $L$, there is $w \in U_L^{(1)}$ with $w^e = u_1$. Let $\alpha = \varpi_L / w$ and $\varpi_K = \omega \varpi$. Then $\alpha^e = \varpi_K$, and $\varpi_K$ is a uniformizer of $K$. The polynomial $X^e - \varpi_K$ is Eisenstein, so $K(\alpha)$ has degree $e$ over $K$ by Theorem 2.4.4. Since $K(\alpha) \subset L$ and $[L : K] = e$, we get $L = K(\alpha)$. $\square$

Let $L / K$ be tamely ramified, with $T$ the maximal unramified subextension. Then $L = T(\varpi_T^{1/e})$ for a uniformizer $\varpi_T$ of $T$, by Theorem 2.4.5 applied to $L / T$. The compositum of all tamely ramified extensions is the *maximal tamely ramified extension* $K^t$. If $K$ contains $\mu_e$, then $K(\varpi_K^{1/e}) / K$ is Galois with group $\mu_e$. In general one has
$$K^t = \bigcup_{p \nmid e} K^{ur}(\varpi_K^{1/e}), \qquad \mathrm{Gal}(K^t / K^{ur}) \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1),$$
where $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1) = \varprojlim_n \mu_{\ell^n}$. The Frobenius acts on this group by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^q$. Volume 3, Chapter 1, uses this description to define the Weil group.

**Example 2.4.6 (the cyclotomic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$).** Let $p$ be odd and $K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$. The polynomial $\Phi_p(X + 1)$ is Eisenstein, because $\Phi_p(X+1) \equiv X^{p-1} \pmod p$ and its constant term is $\Phi_p(1) = p$. Thus $K / \mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p - 1$, and $\zeta_p - 1$ is a uniformizer. This is the local form of Theorem 1.5.4. The extension is tame, and $K = \mathbb{Q}_p((-p)^{1/(p-1)})$ (Volume 3, Exercise 2 of Section 2.6). For $m$ prime to $p$, the extension $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_m)$ is unramified of degree equal to the order of $p$ modulo $m$, by Theorem 2.4.2.

The extensions with $p \mid e$ are *wildly ramified*. They are much more complicated. Their study uses the higher ramification groups, see [Ser79], Chapter IV. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, all the wild ramification of the abelian extensions comes from the fields $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^k})$, by local class field theory.
