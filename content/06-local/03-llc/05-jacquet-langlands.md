## The local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence

The group $GL_2$ has one inner form over $K$ besides itself, the multiplicative group $D^\times$ of the quaternion division algebra $D$. The two groups have the same dual group, so their L-parameters are the same objects. But $D^\times$ is compact modulo its center, and all its irreducible representations are finite-dimensional and square integrable modulo the center. So only the discrete parameters, those that do not factor through a proper Levi subgroup, should occur for $D^\times$. Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] proved that the discrete series of $GL_2(K)$ corresponds bijectively to the irreducible representations of $D^\times$, by a character identity on the elements that the two groups share. The simplest case is the Steinberg representation, which corresponds to the trivial character of $D^\times$.

### The quaternion algebra

By local class field theory the Brauer group of $K$ is $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, see [Ser79]. So there is a unique central division algebra $D$ over $K$ of dimension 4, up to isomorphism, the one with invariant $1/2$. We recall its structure. The valuation $v_K$ extends to $D$ by $v_D(x) = v_K(\mathrm{Nrd}(x))$, where $\mathrm{Nrd} : D^\times \to K^\times$ is the reduced norm. The ring $\mathcal{O}_D = \{x : v_D(x) \ge 0\}$ is the unique maximal order, and its maximal ideal is $\mathfrak{p}_D = \varpi_D \mathcal{O}_D$ for an element $\varpi_D$ with $v_D(\varpi_D) = 1$. We may choose $\varpi_D$ with $\varpi_D^2 = \varpi$. The residue ring $\mathcal{O}_D / \mathfrak{p}_D$ is the field $k_2$ with $q^2$ elements, and conjugation by $\varpi_D$ acts on it as $x \mapsto x^q$. Each quadratic extension $E$ of $K$ embeds in $D$, and $D = E \oplus E j$ for an element $j$ with $j x j^{-1} = \tau(x)$ for $x \in E$, where $\tau$ is the nontrivial automorphism of $E$.

The subgroups $U_D^n = 1 + \mathfrak{p}_D^n$ for $n \ge 1$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of 1 in $D^\times$. They are normal in $D^\times$, because $\mathfrak{p}_D^n$ is a two-sided ideal that is stable under conjugation. The group $D^\times / K^\times \mathcal{O}_D^\times$ has order 2, generated by $\varpi_D$. So $D^\times / K^\times$ is compact.

**Proposition 3.5.1 (the representations of $D^\times$).** Each irreducible smooth representation of $D^\times$ is finite-dimensional. Its restriction to some $U_D^n$ is trivial.

*Proof.* Let $(\rho, V)$ be irreducible and smooth, and let $v \ne 0$. Then $v$ is fixed by some $U_D^n$. Since $U_D^n$ is normal, the space $V^{U_D^n}$ is stable under $D^\times$, and it is nonzero, so it is $V$. The element $\varpi$ is central, so it acts by a scalar, by Schur's lemma, Theorem 1.1.7. Let $g_1, \dots, g_m$ be representatives of the finite group $D^\times / \varpi^{\mathbb{Z}} U_D^n$. It is finite because $\mathcal{O}_D^\times / U_D^n$ is finite and $D^\times / \varpi^{\mathbb{Z}} \mathcal{O}_D^\times$ has order 2. The span of the vectors $\rho(g_i) v$ is stable under $D^\times$, so it is $V$, and $\dim V \le m$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.5.2 (matching elements).** Let $d \in D^\times$, and let $P_d(X) = X^2 - \mathrm{Trd}(d) X + \mathrm{Nrd}(d)$ be its reduced characteristic polynomial.
1. The characteristic polynomial of the $K$-linear map $x \mapsto d x$ on $D$ is $P_d^2$.
2. If $d \notin K$, then $P_d$ is irreducible, and $K(d)$ is a quadratic field.
3. For each $g \in GL_2(K)$ whose characteristic polynomial is irreducible and separable, there is $d \in D^\times$ with $P_d = \det(X - g)$. Then $d$ is determined by $g$ up to conjugacy in $D^\times$.

*Proof.* Part 2: $D$ is a division algebra, so $K(d)$ is a field of degree at most 2 over $K$, and $P_d$ is the minimal polynomial of $d$. Part 1: if $d \in K$, both sides are $(X - d)^4$. Otherwise let $E = K(d)$ and $D = E \oplus E j$. Left multiplication by $d$ acts on each summand as multiplication by $d$ on $E$, and its characteristic polynomial on $E$ over $K$ is $P_d$. Part 3: let $E = K[X] / (\det(X - g))$. This is a separable quadratic extension, so it embeds in $D$, and the image $d$ of $X$ has $P_d = \det(X - g)$. Two such elements generate isomorphic fields, and by the theorem of Skolem and Noether they are conjugate. $\square$

So the elements of $D^\times \smallsetminus K^\times$ with separable reduced characteristic polynomial correspond to the regular elliptic conjugacy classes of $GL_2(K)$, those whose characteristic polynomial is irreducible and separable. Such elements $g$ and $d$ are said to *match*. The Lean definition `Langlands.IsMatching` uses part 1: it says $\det(X - g)^2$ is the characteristic polynomial of left multiplication by $d$. By part 1 this holds if and only if $P_d^2 = \det(X - g)^2$, that is, $P_d = \det(X - g)$, since both are monic.

### The theorem

Let $\pi$ be an irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$. Its character $\theta_\pi$ is a locally constant function on the regular semisimple elements of $GL_2(K)$, with $\mathrm{tr}\, \pi(f) = \int f \theta_\pi$ for $f$ in the Hecke algebra. This is a theorem of Harish-Chandra, and Jacquet and Langlands prove it for $GL_2$ in [JL70]. The pointwise form in the Lean project is `Langlands.SmoothRep.IsCharacterValue`: for each small compact open subgroup $U$, the trace of $\pi(g)$ followed by the projection onto $V^U$ is the value. For a representation of $D^\times$ the character is the trace, by Proposition 3.5.1.

Recall that the discrete series of $GL_2(K)$ consists of the supercuspidal representations and the twisted Steinberg representations $\eta \mathrm{St}$. These are the representations whose parameter is irreducible or of the form $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$, that is, indecomposable.

**Theorem 3.5.3 (the local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence).** There is a unique bijection $\mathrm{JL}$ from the set of isomorphism classes of the discrete series of $GL_2(K)$ onto the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth representations of $D^\times$ such that
$$\theta_\pi(g) = -\mathrm{tr}\, \mathrm{JL}(\pi)(d)$$
for each regular elliptic $g \in GL_2(K)$ and each $d \in D^\times$ that matches $g$. It has the following properties.
1. $\pi$ and $\mathrm{JL}(\pi)$ have the same central character.
2. $\mathrm{JL}(\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det) = \mathrm{JL}(\pi) \otimes \chi \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$, and $\mathrm{JL}(\pi^\vee) = \mathrm{JL}(\pi)^\vee$.
3. $\mathrm{JL}(\eta \mathrm{St}) = \eta \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$, and $\mathrm{JL}$ maps the supercuspidal representations onto the irreducible representations of $D^\times$ of dimension at least 2.
4. The formal degree of $\pi$ equals $\dim \mathrm{JL}(\pi)$, for suitable Haar measures on $GL_2(K)/K^\times$ and $D^\times/K^\times$.

Jacquet and Langlands proved the theorem in [JL70], Chapter III, by a global argument: they compare the trace formulas of $GL_2$ and of a quaternion algebra over a number field that is ramified at a given place, and they read off the local identity. [BH06], Chapter 13, treats the correspondence in the language of admissible pairs. The uniqueness is short, and we prove it.

*Proof of the uniqueness.* Let $\mathrm{JL}$ and $\mathrm{JL}'$ both satisfy the identity, and let $\pi$ be in the discrete series. Let $\Omega$ be the set of $d \in D^\times$ whose reduced characteristic polynomial is separable and irreducible. By Proposition 3.5.2 each $d \in \Omega$ matches some regular elliptic $g$, so $\mathrm{tr}\, \mathrm{JL}(\pi)(d) = -\theta_\pi(g) = \mathrm{tr}\, \mathrm{JL}'(\pi)(d)$ for $d \in \Omega$. The set $\Omega$ is the set where $\mathrm{Trd}(d)^2 - 4 \mathrm{Nrd}(d) \ne 0$ and $d \notin K$. The first condition says that a nonzero polynomial function on $D \cong K^4$ does not vanish, and the second removes a closed subset with empty interior. So $\Omega$ is dense in $D^\times$. The traces are locally constant on $D^\times$, by Proposition 3.5.1, so they agree everywhere. The characters of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of a group over $\mathbb{C}$ are linearly independent, so a finite-dimensional irreducible representation is determined by its character. Hence $\mathrm{JL}(\pi) \cong \mathrm{JL}'(\pi)$. $\square$

The argument also shows that each discrete series representation has at most one possible image. The content of the theorem is that an image exists and that the map is onto. Properties 1 and 2 follow from the uniqueness, since twisting and duality preserve the identity: $\theta_{\pi^\vee}(g) = \theta_\pi(g^{-1})$ and $g^{-1}$ matches $d^{-1}$, and $\det g = \mathrm{Nrd}(d)$ for matching elements. For property 1, the central characters can be read off from the identity on the elements $z g$ and $z d$ for $z \in K^\times$.

### Examples

**Example 3.5.4 (the Steinberg representation).** By Definition 1.5.3 there is an exact sequence $0 \to 1 \to |\cdot|^{-1/2} \times |\cdot|^{1/2} \to \mathrm{St} \to 0$, so $\theta_{\mathrm{St}} = \theta_{|\cdot|^{-1/2} \times |\cdot|^{1/2}} - 1$ on the regular semisimple set. The character of a principal series $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ vanishes on the regular elliptic elements. This is a case of the formula of van Dijk for the character of an induced representation. The reason is that a regular elliptic element is not conjugate to an element of the Borel subgroup $B$, so it has no fixed point on $B \backslash G = \mathbb{P}^1(K)$, and the trace of an operator that moves the supports of all functions is 0. So $\theta_{\mathrm{St}}(g) = -1$ for $g$ regular elliptic, and the identity of Theorem 3.5.3 holds with $\mathrm{JL}(\mathrm{St}) = 1$, the trivial character of $D^\times$. By uniqueness $\mathrm{JL}(\mathrm{St}) = 1$, and by twisting $\mathrm{JL}(\eta\mathrm{St}) = \eta \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$. The parameter of both sides is $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$. For the root numbers of the introduction this says: an elliptic curve with split multiplicative reduction at $p$ gives the trivial character of the quaternion algebra ramified at $p$.

**Example 3.5.5 (depth zero).** Let $\theta$ be a character of $k_2^\times$ with $\theta \ne \theta^q$, and let $\pi = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ be the supercuspidal representation of Example 1.4.9, with $J = K^\times GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ and with $\varpi$ acting by a scalar $c$. On the side of $D$, let $\Lambda'$ be the character of $K^\times \mathcal{O}_D^\times$ that is $\theta$ on $\mathcal{O}_D^\times$, through the reduction map $\mathcal{O}_D^\times \to k_2^\times$, and $c$ on $\varpi$. Let $\rho = \mathrm{Ind}_{K^\times \mathcal{O}_D^\times}^{D^\times} \Lambda'$, of dimension 2. Conjugation by $\varpi_D$ turns $\Lambda'$ into the character that is $\theta^q$ on $\mathcal{O}_D^\times$. Since $\theta^q \ne \theta$, the representation $\rho$ is irreducible, as in Proposition 3.3.6. We check the identity of Theorem 3.5.3 on one family of elements. Let $g \in GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ have a reduction $\bar g$ in $GL_2(k)$ with eigenvalues $x, x^q$ in $k_2 \smallsetminus k$. The group $G$ acts on the tree of $PGL_2(K)$, the vertices are $G / J$, and $g$ fixes the vertex $J$. The neighbours of this vertex correspond to the lines in $k^2$, and $\bar g$ fixes no line, so $g$ fixes no neighbour. The fixed points of $g$ form a subtree, so $g$ fixes only the vertex $J$. The character of a compactly induced representation at an elliptic element is the sum of $\mathrm{tr}\, \Lambda(x^{-1} g x)$ over the cosets $x J$ fixed by $g$, so
$$\theta_\pi(g) = \mathrm{tr}\, \lambda_\theta(\bar g) = -(\theta(x) + \theta(x^q)),$$
by the character table of $GL_2(k)$, see [BH06], Chapter 2. A matching element $d \in \mathcal{O}_D^\times$ has reduction in $k_2^\times$ with the same eigenvalues, say $x$. Then $\mathrm{tr}\, \rho(d) = \theta(x) + \theta(x^q)$, the sum of $\Lambda'$ and its conjugate by $\varpi_D$. So $\theta_\pi(g) = -\mathrm{tr}\, \rho(d)$ on these elements. The full identity holds, and $\mathrm{JL}(\pi) = \rho$, see [BH06], Chapter 13. The parameter of both is $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\Delta \xi)$ of Example 3.3.10.

**Remark 3.5.6 (the correspondence for $D^\times$).** The bijection $\mathrm{rec}_D = \mathrm{rec}_2 \circ \mathrm{JL}^{-1}$ is the local Langlands correspondence for $D^\times$. Its image is the set of indecomposable elements of $\mathcal{G}_2(K)$, the parameters that do not factor through the Levi subgroup of a proper parabolic subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$. So the parameters of $D^\times$ are exactly the relevant ones for $D^\times$, since $D^\times$ has no proper parabolic subgroup. The finite-dimensional representations of $D^\times$ also have $L$-factors and $\varepsilon$-factors, defined by Godement and Jacquet with the same zeta integrals as for $GL_2(K)$. For $GL_n$ the analogue is the correspondence between the discrete series of $GL_n(K)$ and the irreducible representations of $D^\times$ for $D$ of invariant $1/n$, proved by Rogawski (1983) and by Deligne, Kazhdan and Vignéras (1984) in characteristic 0, and extended to all inner forms by Badulescu. Harris and Taylor [HT01] realize it, with $\mathrm{rec}_n$, in the cohomology of the Lubin-Tate tower.

**Remark 3.5.7 (what the Lean statement leaves out).** The proposition `Langlands.JacquetLanglandsGL2` says: for each $K$ and each central division $K$-algebra $D$ of dimension 4, with the topology of a $K$-vector space, there is a unique bijection from the classes of `Langlands.IsDiscreteSeries` representations of $GL_2(K)$ to the classes of irreducible smooth representations of $D^\times$, such that for each regular elliptic $g$ (`Langlands.IsRegularElliptic`) and each matching $d$ (`Langlands.IsMatching`), if $c$ is a value of the character of $\pi$ at $g$ in the sense of `Langlands.SmoothRep.IsCharacterValue`, then $-c$ is a value of the character of $\mathrm{JL}(\pi)$ at $d$. Here `Langlands.IsDiscreteSeries` means supercuspidal, or a subrepresentation of a principal series $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ with $\chi_1 = \chi_2 |\cdot|$, which by Theorem 1.5.1 is $\eta \mathrm{St}$ with $\eta = \chi_2 |\cdot|^{1/2}$. The statement leaves out the following.
1. The existence of the character $\theta_\pi$ as a locally constant function. The identity is an implication between pointwise values, and it does not assert that a value exists.
2. The distributional form $\mathrm{tr}\, \pi(f) = \int f \theta_\pi$ of the character.
3. Properties 1 to 4 of Theorem 3.5.3: the central characters, the twists and the duals, the explicit values $\mathrm{JL}(\eta\mathrm{St}) = \eta \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$, and the formal degrees.
4. The compatibility with $\mathrm{rec}_2$ of Remark 3.5.6, and the correspondence for $D^\times$.
5. The analogue for $GL_n$.
