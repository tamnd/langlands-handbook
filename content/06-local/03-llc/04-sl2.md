## The L-packets of SL(2)

For $GL_n$ the correspondence is a bijection. For other groups it is not. The first example is $SL_2$, and Labesse and Langlands [LL79] found the answer. The dual group of $SL_2$ is $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. A parameter for $SL_2$ is the image in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ of a parameter for $GL_2$, and two representations $\pi$ and $\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ of $GL_2(K)$ give the same projective parameter. So the parameter of an irreducible representation of $SL_2(K)$ should be the projective parameter of the representations of $GL_2(K)$ whose restriction contains it. The restriction of an irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$ to $SL_2(K)$ can have 1, 2 or 4 irreducible constituents, and they all have the same parameter. This set of constituents is the L-packet. Its size is the order of a finite group attached to the parameter, the group of components of its centralizer.

Throughout this section $G = GL_2(K)$, $G_1 = SL_2(K)$ and $Z$ is the center of $G$. We assume that the characteristic of $K$ is not 2. Then the group $K^{\times 2}$ of squares is open of finite index in $K^\times$, and the subgroup $H = Z G_1 = \{g \in G : \det g \in K^{\times 2}\}$ is open, normal and of finite index in $G$, with $G / H \cong K^\times / K^{\times 2}$ through $\det$. For $p$ odd this group has order 4. The Lean statement is made for all $K$.

### Restriction to SL(2)

For an irreducible representation $\pi$ of $G$, let $X(\pi)$ be the group of characters $\chi$ of $K^\times$ with $\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det \cong \pi$. Comparing the central characters gives $\chi^2 = 1$ for $\chi \in X(\pi)$.

**Proposition 3.4.1 (restriction).** Let $\pi$ be an irreducible smooth representation of $G$. Then the restriction $\pi|_{G_1}$ is a finite direct sum of irreducible representations. The group $G$ permutes the summands transitively by conjugation.

*Proof.* The center $Z$ acts on $\pi$ by scalars, so the $H$-stable subspaces of $\pi$ are the $G_1$-stable subspaces, and it is enough to treat $\pi|_H$. Let $g_1, \dots, g_m$ be representatives of $G / H$, and let $v \ne 0$ in $\pi$. Then $\pi$ is spanned by the vectors $\pi(g_i h) v$, so $\pi|_H$ is generated by the finite set $\{\pi(g_i) v\}$. By Zorn's lemma a finitely generated representation has a maximal proper subrepresentation, so $\pi|_H$ has an irreducible quotient $\tau$. Since $H$ is open of finite index, Frobenius reciprocity gives $\mathrm{Hom}_H(\pi, \tau) = \mathrm{Hom}_G(\pi, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \tau)$. This space is nonzero, so $\pi$ embeds in $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \tau$, because $\pi$ is irreducible. By Mackey's formula, $(\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \tau)|_H \cong \bigoplus_i \tau^{g_i}$, where $\tau^{g}(h) = \tau(g^{-1} h g)$. Each $\tau^{g_i}$ is irreducible. So $\pi|_H$ is a subrepresentation of a finite semisimple representation, and it is a finite direct sum of irreducible representations. For the second claim, let $\sigma$ be a summand. The sum of the subspaces $\pi(g) \sigma$ for $g \in G$ is $G$-stable and nonzero, so it is $\pi$, and each summand is isomorphic to some $\sigma^g$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.4.2 (multiplicity one).** Let $\pi$ be an irreducible smooth representation of $G$. Then the summands of $\pi|_{G_1}$ are pairwise not isomorphic. If $\pi$ is infinite-dimensional and $\psi$ is a nontrivial character of $K$, then exactly one summand $\sigma$ has a nonzero $\psi$-Whittaker functional, that is, a nonzero linear form $\lambda$ with $\lambda(\sigma(n_x) v) = \psi(x) \lambda(v)$ for $n_x = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* If $\pi$ is a character, its restriction is the trivial character of $G_1$ and there is nothing to prove. Let $\pi$ be infinite-dimensional, and write $\pi|_{G_1} \cong \bigoplus_{j=1}^k \sigma_j^{\oplus m_j}$ with the $\sigma_j$ pairwise not isomorphic. The group $G$ permutes the $\sigma_j$ transitively and preserves the multiplicities, by Proposition 3.4.1, so all $m_j$ are equal to one integer $m$. The unipotent group $N = \{n_x\}$ lies in $G_1$. So the $\psi$-Whittaker functionals of $\pi$ are the sums of those of the summands, and
$$1 = \dim \mathrm{Hom}_N(\pi, \psi) = m \sum_{j=1}^k \dim \mathrm{Hom}_N(\sigma_j, \psi),$$
where the first equality is Theorem 1.7.4. So $m = 1$, and exactly one $\sigma_j$ has a nonzero $\psi$-Whittaker functional, which is unique up to a scalar. $\square$

Let $\psi_a(x) = \psi(ax)$ for $a \in K^\times$, and let $t_a = \mathrm{diag}(a, 1)$. Then $t_a n_x t_a^{-1} = n_{ax}$. So if $\sigma$ is the $\psi$-generic summand, then $\sigma^{t_a}$ is the $\psi_a$-generic summand. The generic summand depends on $\psi$, through the class of $a$ in $K^\times / K^{\times 2}$ modulo the image of the stabilizer of $\sigma$.

**Proposition 3.4.3 (the number of summands).** Let $\pi$ be an irreducible smooth representation of $G$. Then the number of summands of $\pi|_{G_1}$ is $|X(\pi)|$.

*Proof.* Let $\pi|_H = \bigoplus_{j=1}^k \sigma_j$, with the $\sigma_j$ pairwise not isomorphic, by Theorem 3.4.2. By Schur's lemma, Theorem 1.1.7, applied to the irreducible representations $\sigma_j$ of the $\ell$-group $H$, the algebra $\mathrm{End}_H(\pi)$ is $\mathbb{C}^k$. It has dimension $k$. The finite abelian group $G / H$ acts on it by $g \cdot A = \pi(g)^{-1} A \pi(g)$, which depends only on the class of $g$, because $A$ commutes with $\pi(H)$. The characters of $G / H$ are the characters $\chi \circ \det$ with $\chi^2 = 1$. So $\mathrm{End}_H(\pi)$ is the direct sum of the eigenspaces
$$\{A : \pi(g)^{-1} A \pi(g) = \chi(\det g) A \text{ for all } g\} = \mathrm{Hom}_G(\pi, \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det), \qquad \chi^2 = 1.$$
By Schur's lemma each eigenspace has dimension 1 if $\chi \in X(\pi)$ and 0 otherwise. Since $X(\pi)$ consists of characters with $\chi^2 = 1$, we get $k = |X(\pi)|$. $\square$

**Proposition 3.4.4 (common constituents).** Let $\pi$ and $\pi'$ be irreducible smooth representations of $G$. Then $\pi|_{G_1}$ and $\pi'|_{G_1}$ have a common irreducible summand if and only if $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for a character $\chi$ of $K^\times$. In that case $\pi|_{G_1} \cong \pi'|_{G_1}$.

*Proof.* If $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$, the restrictions are isomorphic, since $\chi \circ \det$ is trivial on $G_1$. Conversely, let $\sigma$ be a common summand. The central characters $\omega$ and $\omega'$ agree on $Z \cap G_1 = \{\pm 1\}$, since both are the central character of $\sigma$ there. So $\mu = \omega' \omega^{-1}$ is trivial on $-1$. Then $x^2 \mapsto \mu(x)$ is a well defined character of the open subgroup $K^{\times 2}$, since the kernel of $x \mapsto x^2$ is $\{\pm 1\}$. It extends to a character $\chi$ of $K^\times$, because $\mathbb{C}^\times$ is divisible, and $\chi$ is continuous because $K^{\times 2}$ is open. Then $\chi^2 = \mu$, and $\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ has central character $\omega'$. Replace $\pi$ by this twist. Now $\pi$ and $\pi'$ have the same central character and a common summand as representations of $H$, so $\mathrm{Hom}_H(\pi, \pi') \ne 0$. By Frobenius reciprocity,
$$\mathrm{Hom}_H(\pi, \pi') = \mathrm{Hom}_G(\pi, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\pi'|_H)) = \bigoplus_{\chi^2 = 1} \mathrm{Hom}_G(\pi, \pi' \otimes \chi \circ \det),$$
because $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\pi'|_H) \cong \pi' \otimes \mathrm{Ind}_H^G 1$ and $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G 1$ is the sum of the characters of $G / H$. So $\pi \cong \pi' \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for some $\chi$. $\square$

Every irreducible smooth representation $\sigma$ of $G_1$ occurs in the restriction of an irreducible representation of $G$. Indeed, $\sigma$ extends to $H$ by any character of $Z$ that agrees with the central character of $\sigma$ on $\{\pm 1\}$. The induced representation from $H$ to $G$ has finite length and $\sigma$ in its restriction, by Mackey's formula. So one of its irreducible constituents contains $\sigma$.

**Definition 3.4.5 (L-packet).** For an irreducible smooth representation $\pi$ of $G$, the *L-packet* $\Pi(\pi)$ is the set of isomorphism classes of the irreducible summands of $\pi|_{G_1}$.

By Proposition 3.4.4 and the paragraph above, the L-packets form a partition of the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth representations of $G_1$, and $\Pi(\pi) = \Pi(\pi')$ if and only if $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for some $\chi$.

### The Galois side

An *L-parameter* for $SL_2$ is a pair $(\bar r, \bar N)$, where $\bar r : W_K \to PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a continuous homomorphism with semisimple image and $\bar N$ is a nilpotent element of the Lie algebra of $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ with $\mathrm{Ad}(\bar r(w)) \bar N = |w| \bar N$, taken up to conjugacy by $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. Each $\sigma = (r, N) \in \mathcal{G}_2(K)$ gives the L-parameter $\bar\sigma$, its image in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. For an L-parameter $\phi$, let $C_\phi$ be its centralizer in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ and $A_\phi = C_\phi / C_\phi^\circ$ its group of components. For $\sigma \in \mathcal{G}_2(K)$, let $X(\sigma)$ be the group of characters $\varphi$ of $W_K$ with $\sigma \otimes \varphi \cong \sigma$.

**Proposition 3.4.6 (projective parameters).** Let $\sigma, \sigma' \in \mathcal{G}_2(K)$.
1. $\bar\sigma = \bar\sigma'$ up to conjugacy if and only if $\sigma' \cong \sigma \otimes \varphi$ for a character $\varphi$ of $W_K$.
2. $A_{\bar\sigma} \cong X(\sigma)$, and $X(\sigma)$ is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^k$ with $k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$.
3. If $\sigma$ is irreducible, then $C_{\bar\sigma}$ is finite, and $C_{\bar\sigma} = A_{\bar\sigma} \cong X(\sigma)$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Part 1. If $\sigma' \cong \sigma \otimes \varphi$, the images in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ are conjugate. Conversely, the nilpotent elements of $\mathfrak{gl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ lie in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$, which maps isomorphically onto the Lie algebra of $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. So if $g \in GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ conjugates $\bar\sigma$ to $\bar\sigma'$, then $g N g^{-1} = N'$. Replacing $\sigma$ by $g \sigma g^{-1}$, we may assume $g = 1$ and $N = N'$. Then $r'(w) = \varphi(w) r(w)$ for a scalar $\varphi(w)$. Since scalars are central, $\varphi$ is a homomorphism, and it is continuous, because $\varphi(w) = r'(w) r(w)^{-1}$. So $\sigma' = \sigma \otimes \varphi$.

Part 2. An element $g \in GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ whose image lies in $C_{\bar\sigma}$ satisfies $g r(w) g^{-1} = \lambda(w) r(w)$ for a character $\lambda$, and $g N g^{-1} = N$, as in part 1. So $g$ is an isomorphism $\sigma \to \sigma \otimes \lambda$, and $\lambda \in X(\sigma)$. This gives a homomorphism $C_{\bar\sigma} \to X(\sigma)$. It is surjective by the definition of $X(\sigma)$. Its kernel is the image of the centralizer of $\sigma$ in $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$. This centralizer is the group of units of the algebra $\mathrm{End}(\sigma)$, which is a nonempty Zariski open subset of a vector space, so it is connected. The group $X(\sigma)$ is finite. Its elements satisfy $\varphi^2 = 1$, because $\det(\sigma \otimes \varphi) = \varphi^2 \det \sigma$. For each $\varphi \in X(\sigma)$ choose an isomorphism $A_\varphi : \sigma \to \sigma \otimes \varphi$. Then $r(w) A_\varphi r(w)^{-1} = \varphi(w)^{-1} A_\varphi$, so the $A_\varphi$ are eigenvectors with distinct characters for the action of $W_K$ on $\mathrm{End}(V)$, and they are linearly independent. So $|X(\sigma)| \le \dim \mathrm{End}(V) = 4$. So the kernel is a connected subgroup of finite index, which is $C_{\bar\sigma}^\circ$, and $A_{\bar\sigma} \cong X(\sigma)$. A finite group of characters with $\varphi^2 = 1$ is a product of copies of $\mathbb{Z}/2$, and its order is at most 4.

Part 3. If $\sigma$ is irreducible, its centralizer in $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the group of scalars, by Schur's lemma. So the kernel in part 2 is trivial. $\square$

**Proposition 3.4.7 (the characters that fix a parameter).** Let $\sigma$ be an irreducible representation of $W_K$ of dimension 2, and let $\varphi \ne 1$ be a character of $W_K$. Then $\varphi \in X(\sigma)$ if and only if $\varphi$ is quadratic and $\sigma \cong \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$, where $E$ is the quadratic extension with $\varphi = \kappa_{E/K}$ and $\xi$ is a character of $E^\times$.

*Proof.* If $\sigma = \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$, then $\sigma \otimes \kappa_{E/K} = \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\xi \cdot \kappa_{E/K} \circ N_{E/K}) = \sigma$ by Proposition 3.3.6, because $\kappa_{E/K}$ is trivial on the norms. Conversely, let $\varphi \in X(\sigma)$ with $\varphi \ne 1$. Then $\varphi^2 = 1$, by Proposition 3.4.6, so $\varphi = \kappa_{E/K}$ for a quadratic extension $E$, and $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} 1 = 1 \oplus \varphi$. By Frobenius reciprocity,
$$\dim \mathrm{End}_{W_E}(\sigma|_{W_E}) = \dim \mathrm{Hom}_{W_K}(\sigma, \sigma \otimes \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} 1) = \dim \mathrm{End}(\sigma) + \dim \mathrm{Hom}(\sigma, \sigma \otimes \varphi) = 2.$$
So $\sigma|_{W_E}$ is reducible, and it contains a character $\xi$. By Frobenius reciprocity again, $\mathrm{Hom}_{W_K}(\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi, \sigma) = \mathrm{Hom}_{W_E}(\xi, \sigma|_{W_E}) \ne 0$, and since both sides have dimension 2 and $\sigma$ is irreducible, $\sigma \cong \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$. $\square$

So $|X(\sigma)| = 1 + m$, where $m$ is the number of quadratic extensions $E$ such that $\sigma$ is induced from $W_E$. The parameter is *primitive* if $m = 0$, which can only happen for $p = 2$. For $p$ odd there are three quadratic extensions, and $m \in \{1, 3\}$, since $X(\sigma)$ is a group.

### The theorem of Labesse and Langlands

**Theorem 3.4.8 (the L-packets of $SL_2$, Labesse and Langlands).** Let $\mathrm{rec}_2$ be the correspondence for $GL_2$.
1. Each irreducible smooth representation of $SL_2(K)$ is a summand of $\pi|_{SL_2(K)}$ for some irreducible representation $\pi$ of $GL_2(K)$.
2. Each restriction $\pi|_{SL_2(K)}$ is a finite direct sum of irreducible representations that are pairwise not isomorphic.
3. $\pi|_{SL_2(K)}$ and $\pi'|_{SL_2(K)}$ have a common summand if and only if $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for a character $\chi$.
4. For each bijection $\mathrm{rec}$ with the twist property of Theorem 3.1.2, $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for some $\chi$ if and only if $\mathrm{rec}(\pi') \cong \mathrm{rec}(\pi) \otimes \varphi$ for some character $\varphi$ of $W_K$.
5. The map $\Pi(\pi) \mapsto \overline{\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi)}$ is a bijection from the set of L-packets of $SL_2(K)$ onto the set of L-parameters for $SL_2$, and $|\Pi(\pi)| = |A_\phi|$ for $\phi = \overline{\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi)}$.

*Proof.* Parts 1 to 3 are the paragraph before Definition 3.4.5, Theorem 3.4.2 and Proposition 3.4.4. Part 4: if $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$, then $\mathrm{rec}(\pi') \cong \mathrm{rec}(\pi) \otimes \chi$ by the twist property. Conversely, if $\mathrm{rec}(\pi') \cong \mathrm{rec}(\pi) \otimes \varphi$, let $\chi = \varphi \circ \mathrm{art}_K$. Then $\mathrm{rec}(\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det) \cong \mathrm{rec}(\pi) \otimes \varphi \cong \mathrm{rec}(\pi')$, and $\pi' \cong \pi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ because $\mathrm{rec}$ is injective. Part 5: by part 3, the L-packets correspond to the twist classes of irreducible representations of $G$. By part 4, these correspond to the twist classes in $\mathcal{G}_2(K)$, and by Proposition 3.4.6 these correspond to the conjugacy classes of the images in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. Each L-parameter for $SL_2$ lifts to $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$. For the representation $\bar r$ this is a theorem of Tate, the vanishing of $H^2(W_K, \mathbb{C}^\times)$ for the trivial action, and the lift of $\bar N$ is the nilpotent element of the Lie algebra of $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ over it. So the map is a bijection. Finally, by the twist property $X(\pi) = X(\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi))$ through $\mathrm{art}_K$. So $|\Pi(\pi)| = |X(\pi)| = |X(\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi))| = |A_\phi|$, by Propositions 3.4.3 and 3.4.6. $\square$

The proof uses only the formal properties of $\mathrm{rec}_2$: it is a bijection, and it is compatible with the twists. The content of [LL79] is in the next step, which this section does not treat. Labesse and Langlands parametrize the members of $\Pi(\pi)$ by the characters of $A_\phi$, and they prove that the sum of the characters of the members of a packet is a stable distribution, while other combinations are not. This is the first case of endoscopy, and Volume 9 treats it.

### Examples

**Example 3.4.9 (principal series).** Let $\pi = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$ be irreducible, so $\sigma = \mathrm{rec}_2(\pi) = \chi_1 \oplus \chi_2$. A character $\chi$ lies in $X(\pi)$ if and only if $\{\chi_1 \chi, \chi_2 \chi\} = \{\chi_1, \chi_2\}$. This holds for $\chi = 1$, and for $\chi \ne 1$ it holds if and only if $\chi = \chi_2 \chi_1^{-1}$ and $\chi^2 = 1$. So $|\Pi(\pi)| = 2$ if $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1}$ is a nontrivial quadratic character, and $|\Pi(\pi)| = 1$ otherwise. On the Galois side, $\bar\sigma$ lands in the diagonal torus $\bar T$ of $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$. Its centralizer contains $\bar T$, which is connected. If $\mu = \chi_1 \chi_2^{-1}$ is a nontrivial quadratic character, the element $w = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $w \sigma(x) w^{-1} = \mathrm{diag}(\chi_2(x), \chi_1(x)) = \mu(x) \sigma(x)$, so $w$ centralizes $\bar\sigma$. Then $C_{\bar\sigma}$ is the normalizer of $\bar T$, and $A_{\bar\sigma} = \mathbb{Z}/2$. For example, let $\mu$ be the unramified quadratic character. Then $1 \times \mu$ is irreducible, and its restriction to $SL_2(K)$ splits into two pieces. One of them has a nonzero vector fixed by $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, and the other has a nonzero vector fixed by the conjugate $t_\varpi SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K) t_\varpi^{-1}$, which is not conjugate to $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ in $SL_2(K)$.

**Example 3.4.10 (Steinberg and the characters).** By Proposition 1.5.4, $\eta \mathrm{St} \otimes \chi \circ \det \cong \eta \mathrm{St}$ only for $\chi = 1$. So the restriction of $\eta\mathrm{St}$ to $SL_2(K)$ is irreducible. On the Galois side, $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta \varphi \cong \mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$ only for $\varphi = 1$, by the uniqueness in Proposition 3.3.2. So $X(\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta) = 1$ and $A_\phi = 1$. The characters $\chi \circ \det$ restrict to the trivial representation. On the Galois side, $\{\chi|\cdot|^{-1/2} \varphi, \chi|\cdot|^{1/2} \varphi\} = \{\chi|\cdot|^{-1/2}, \chi|\cdot|^{1/2}\}$ with $\varphi \ne 1$ would force $\varphi = |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$ and $\varphi^2 = 1$, which is impossible. So $A_\phi = 1$ again.

**Example 3.4.11 (a packet of size 4).** Let $p$ be odd. Then $K^\times / K^{\times 2} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, and the composite $L$ of the three quadratic extensions of $K$ has $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$. The group $V \subset PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ of the images of
$$1, \quad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
is also isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, since these matrices commute up to sign. Let $\phi : W_K \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/K) \cong V$, with $\bar N = 0$. Since $V$ is abelian, $V \subseteq C_\phi$. A lift $\sigma$ of $\phi$ to $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is irreducible: a reducible semisimple lift is conjugate to a diagonal one, and then $\phi$ lands in a torus of $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$, which has only one element of order 2. So $C_\phi$ is finite, of order $|X(\sigma)| \le 4$, and $C_\phi = V$. Let $\pi = \mathrm{rec}_2^{-1}(\sigma)$. It is supercuspidal, and its restriction to $SL_2(K)$ has 4 summands. By Proposition 3.4.7, $\sigma$ is induced from each of the three quadratic extensions. Only one of these is an admissible pair. Since $L/K$ is tamely ramified, $\sigma$ is trivial on $P_K$, so each inducing character is trivial on $U_E^1$, and condition 2 of Definition 3.3.9 forces $E$ to be unramified. Exercise 7 works out this case.

**Remark 3.4.12 (the generic member and the choice of $\psi$).** Let $\Pi$ be a packet of infinite-dimensional representations. By Theorem 3.4.2, for each nontrivial $\psi$ exactly one member of $\Pi$ is $\psi$-generic. The group $G$ acts on $\Pi$ through $G / H \cong K^\times / K^{\times 2}$, and $t_a$ moves the $\psi$-generic member to the $\psi_a$-generic member. In Example 3.4.9 with $\mu$ unramified, the $\psi$-generic member for $\psi$ of conductor $\mathcal{O}_K$ is the piece with a vector fixed by $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, and the $\psi_\varpi$-generic member is the other one. So the bijection between $\Pi$ and the characters of $A_\phi$ is not canonical. It is fixed once one chooses $\psi$, by sending the $\psi$-generic member to the trivial character. This choice of a Whittaker datum is part of the general conjecture for quasi-split groups.

**Remark 3.4.13 (what the Lean statement leaves out).** The proposition `Langlands.LocalLanglandsSL2` is the conjunction of parts 1 to 4 of Theorem 3.4.8, for all nonarchimedean local fields $K$. The group $SL_2(K)$ is `Langlands.slTwo`, the kernel of $\det$, and the restriction is `Langlands.restrictSL2`. Part 2 is stated with an internal direct sum of finitely many stable subspaces whose subrepresentations are irreducible and pairwise not isomorphic. Part 4 quantifies over every bijection $\mathrm{rec}$ that satisfies `Langlands.IsLanglandsMap`, and it is a formal consequence of the twist condition, as the proof above shows. The statement leaves out the following.
1. The L-parameters for $SL_2$ with values in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$, the bijection of part 5 and the lifting theorem of Tate. They are in the docstring only.
2. The sizes of the packets, the groups $X(\pi)$ and $A_\phi$, and Propositions 3.4.3 and 3.4.6.
3. The parametrization of the members of a packet by the characters of $A_\phi$, which needs the choice of a generic character.
4. The generic member of a packet, and the stability of the sum of the characters.
5. The existence of $\mathrm{rec}_2$. Part 4 is a statement about each such map, and it holds vacuously if there is none.
