# The local Langlands correspondence

Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with split multiplicative reduction, for example a Tate curve $\mathbb{G}_m / q_E^{\mathbb{Z}}$ with $|q_E| < 1$. Two objects come from $E$. The first is on the arithmetic side. The Tate parametrization gives an exact sequence $0 \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1) \to T_\ell E \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to 0$ of Galois modules for $\ell \ne p$. The dual $H^1$ of $T_\ell E \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is a Weil-Deligne representation of dimension 2, as in Chapter 2. The inertia group acts on it through a unipotent matrix, so $N \ne 0$, and a geometric Frobenius has eigenvalues $1$ and $p$. In the conventions of this chapter it is the pair $(r, N)$ with $r = 1 \oplus |\cdot|^{-1}$ and $N$ the nilpotent map from the line of $|\cdot|^{-1}$ to the line of $1$. The kernel of $N$ is the line of the trivial character, so $L(s, (r, N)) = (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$, which is the local factor of the Hasse-Weil $L$-function of $E$ at $p$. The second object is on the automorphic side. By Example 1.5.6, the local component at $p$ of the automorphic representation attached to $E$ (when $E$ is defined over $\mathbb{Q}$) is the Steinberg representation $\mathrm{St}$ of $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. This is an irreducible representation of infinite dimension. It has a nonzero vector fixed by the Iwahori subgroup and none fixed by $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.

The local Langlands correspondence matches these two objects. After the twist by $|\cdot|^{1/2}$ that passes to the unitary normalization, the parameter becomes $r = |\cdot|^{1/2} \oplus |\cdot|^{-1/2}$ with the same $N$, and this is $\mathrm{rec}_2(\mathrm{St})$. The $L$-factors agree: $L(s, \mathrm{St}) = (1 - p^{-1/2 - s})^{-1} = L(s + 1/2, E)$. The conductors agree: both are $p$. The $\varepsilon$-factors agree: both are $-p^{1/2 - s}$ for an additive character trivial on $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and not on $p^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_p$, so the root number of $E$ at $p$ is $-1$. The example also shows the role of $N$. The trivial representation of $GL_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is the other constituent of the same principal series, and its parameter has the same $r$ and $N = 0$. Without the monodromy operator the two representations could not be told apart. For a curve with nonsplit multiplicative reduction the same holds with $\mathrm{St}$ replaced by $\eta \mathrm{St}$, where $\eta$ is the unramified quadratic character, and the root number is $+1$.

The correspondence for $GL_n$ is a bijection between the irreducible smooth representations of $GL_n(K)$ and the Frobenius semisimple Weil-Deligne representations of dimension $n$, for a nonarchimedean local field $K$. It is pinned down by the local factors of pairs. Henniart proved that at most one such family of bijections exists, and Harris and Taylor, and then Henniart and Scholze, proved that one exists. For $n = 1$ it is local class field theory. For $n = 2$ the supercuspidal representations correspond to the irreducible representations of the Weil group, and for $p \ne 2$ both sets are described by the admissible pairs of Bushnell and Henniart [BH06]. Other groups bring new features. For $SL_2$ the correspondence is no longer a bijection: a parameter carries a finite set of representations, its $L$-packet, and the packet is controlled by a finite group attached to the parameter. The local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence relates the discrete series of $GL_2(K)$ to the representations of the multiplicative group of a quaternion division algebra, which has the same parameters. For $GSp_4$ the theorem of Gan and Takeda [GT11] gives packets of size 1 or 2.

Section 3.1 states the correspondence for $GL_n$, with Henniart's characterization, and reduces it to the supercuspidal representations. Section 3.2 treats $n = 1$ and the tori, and it ends with a proof of the case $n = 1$ in full, with Schur's lemma for $GL_1(K)$, each condition of the correspondence, and the factors of Tate's thesis. Section 3.3 treats $n = 2$: the explicit form on the representations that are not supercuspidal, the dihedral parameters, and the tame correspondence of [BH06]. Section 3.4 constructs the $L$-packets of $SL_2$ from the restriction of representations of $GL_2(K)$ and computes their size. Section 3.5 gives the local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for $GL_2$. Section 3.6 states the theorem of Gan and Takeda for $GSp_4$. Section 3.7 has the exercises and the notes. Section 3.9 constructs the cuspidal types of [BH06] for $p \ne 2$, proves that they induce irreducible supercuspidal representations, and explains the reduction of the Lean statement for $GL_2$, $p \ne 2$, to six published theorems. Section 3.10 classifies the parameters that are not irreducible in coordinates, attaches a representation to each tame datum, proves that an admissible compact induction is supercuspidal, and reduces two of these six theorems to smaller statements on the principal series and on cuspidal types. Section 3.11 proves that compact induction from a cuspidal type is admissible, with no hypothesis, reduces the $L$-factor of a supercuspidal representation to the Laurent form of its zeta integrals, and proves five of the eight statements on the principal series and the other three from six smaller facts. Section 3.12 proves that the contragredient of the representation of a tame datum comes from the inverse datum, and it reduces the Galois side of the tame parametrization, by Clifford theory and the common eigenvector of a finite $p$-group, to five classical facts on the Weil group. Section 3.13 proves that the representation of a tame datum depends only on the pair, by a descent through the filtration $U^n_{\mathfrak{A}}$, and it proves the three remaining structural facts on the principal series, with the invariant integral over $GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ and the Iwahori subgroup as the main tool. Section 3.14 studies the intertwining of two types: it shows that intertwining types have the same chain order and the same level and are conjugate by an element of the normalizer of the order, and it reduces the injectivity of the tame parametrization to two statements on one chain order. It also computes the Godement-Jacquet $L$-factor of a character of the determinant by a change of variables on $M_2(K)$.

The notation is that of Volume 0, Section 3.1 and of Chapter 1: $K$ is a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}_K$, uniformizer $\varpi$ and residue field $k$ with $q$ elements, and $|\varpi| = q^{-1}$. We write $W_K$ for the Weil group, $I_K$ for the inertia group and $P_K$ for the wild inertia group, and $\Phi$ for a geometric Frobenius element. The reciprocity map $\mathrm{art}_K : K^\times \to W_K^{ab}$ is the map $\mathrm{rec}_K$ of Volume 3, Theorem 2.3.3, in the geometric normalization: it sends uniformizers to geometric Frobenius elements. A character $\chi$ of $K^\times$ gives the character $\varphi_\chi = \chi \circ \mathrm{art}_K^{-1}$ of $W_K$, and we often write $\chi$ for both. In particular $|w|$ is the character of $W_K$ with $|\Phi| = q^{-1}$. The principal series $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$, the Steinberg representation and its twists $\eta \mathrm{St}$ are those of Section 1.5. The Weil-Deligne representations, their $L$-factors and $\varepsilon$-factors, and the Godement-Jacquet factors are those of Chapter 2. A Weil-Deligne representation is a pair $(r, N)$ with $r(w) N r(w)^{-1} = |w| N$.

The Lean project states the main theorems of this chapter in `Langlands/Statements/LocalLanglands.lean` and `Langlands/Statements/GSp4.lean`, over the Weil-Deligne representations of `Langlands/Statements/WeilDeligne.lean`. It proves local class field theory in the form of `Langlands.localReciprocityGL1`, the existence and uniqueness of $\mathrm{art}_K$, and the correspondence for split and induced tori. The file `Langlands/Local/GL1.lean` builds the bijection $\mathrm{rec}_1$ through $\mathrm{art}_K$ and proves Schur's lemma for commutative groups, the conditions on central characters, twists and duals, the trivial case $m = 0$, and the comparison of the $L$-factor of $(\chi \circ \mathrm{art}_K^{-1}, 0)$ with Tate's factor. The file `Langlands/Local/GL1LFactor.lean` proves Tate's local computation of the zeta integral and, from it, `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL 1` with no hypothesis. The same file shows that the first Lean form of the $L$-factor condition, `Langlands.LocalGL1.GL1LFactor`, is false, because the zero representation has only zero zeta integrals, see Remark 3.2.23. The statements for $GL_n$, $GL_2$, $SL_2$, the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence and $GSp_4$ are stated and not proved. Each of them is weaker than the theorem in the text, and each section ends with a remark that says what the Lean statement leaves out.
