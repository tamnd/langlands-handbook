## The case n = 2

This section works out the correspondence for $GL_2$. On the representations that are not supercuspidal it is given by an explicit table, and we prove that the table satisfies the conditions of Theorem 3.1.2. On the supercuspidal representations it is a bijection onto the irreducible representations of $W_K$ of dimension 2. For $p \ne 2$ both sets are parametrized by the admissible pairs of Bushnell and Henniart, and the correspondence is the induction of a character, after a twist by a tamely ramified character $\Delta_\xi$. The example to keep in mind is the depth-zero supercuspidal representation of Example 1.4.9. Its parameter is induced from the unramified quadratic extension, and the twist $\Delta_\xi$ is already forced by the central character.

### The theorem

For $GL_2$ the twists by characters suffice to pin down the correspondence, because the converse theorem of Jacquet and Langlands for $GL_2$ uses only these twists, see [BH06], Chapter 6.

**Theorem 3.3.1 (the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_2$).** There is a unique bijection $\mathrm{rec}_2 : \mathcal{A}_2(K) \to \mathcal{G}_2(K)$ such that for each $\pi \in \mathcal{A}_2(K)$ and each character $\chi$ of $K^\times$:
1. $\det \mathrm{rec}_2(\pi) = \omega_\pi$, through $\mathrm{art}_K$;
2. $L(s, \pi \otimes \chi) = L(s, \mathrm{rec}_2(\pi) \otimes \chi)$ and $\varepsilon(s, \pi \otimes \chi, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \mathrm{rec}_2(\pi) \otimes \chi, \psi)$.

It satisfies all the conditions of Theorem 3.1.2 for $n = 2$. It sends the supercuspidal classes onto the irreducible representations of $W_K$ of dimension 2, and on the other classes it is given by Proposition 3.3.3.

The uniqueness follows from the converse theorem for $GL_2$ by the argument of Corollary 3.1.5. The existence is the main theorem of Kutzko [Kut80]. The book [BH06] gives a complete proof: Chapter 8 for $p \ne 2$, with the explicit form of Theorem 3.3.20 below, and Chapter 12 for $p = 2$. The rest of this section gives the explicit form, with proofs of the parts that are short.

### The Galois side

**Proposition 3.3.2 (the Weil-Deligne representations of dimension 2).** Each element of $\mathcal{G}_2(K)$ is of exactly one of the following kinds.
1. $(\varphi_1 \oplus \varphi_2, 0)$ for characters $\varphi_1, \varphi_2$ of $W_K$, unique up to order.
2. $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \varphi = (\varphi|\cdot|^{1/2} \oplus \varphi |\cdot|^{-1/2}, N)$ for a unique character $\varphi$, where $N$ maps the line of $\varphi|\cdot|^{-1/2}$ onto the line of $\varphi|\cdot|^{1/2}$.
3. $(r, 0)$ with $r$ irreducible.

*Proof.* Let $(r, N)$ have dimension 2. Suppose first that $N \ne 0$. Then $N^2 = 0$, since $N$ is nilpotent, so $L = \ker N = \mathrm{im}\, N$ is a line. It is stable under $r$, because $r(w) N v = |w| N r(w) v$. Let $\varphi_1$ be the character of $W_K$ on $L$. Since $r$ is semisimple, $L$ has a stable complement $L'$, with a character $\varphi_2$. For $v \in L'$, $N v \in L$ is nonzero and $r(w) N v = |w| N r(w) v = |w| \varphi_2(w) N v$. So $\varphi_1 = |\cdot| \varphi_2$, and with $\varphi = \varphi_2 |\cdot|^{1/2}$ the pair is $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \varphi$. Suppose next that $N = 0$. If $r$ is reducible, it is a sum of two characters, because it is semisimple. Otherwise we are in case 3. The three kinds are distinct: $N \ne 0$ only in case 2, and $r$ is irreducible only in case 3. The characters in cases 1 and 2 are determined by the isomorphism class. $\square$

The irreducible elements of $\mathcal{G}_2(K)$ are those of case 3, and the indecomposable ones are those of cases 2 and 3, as Proposition 3.1.6 says in general.

### The representations that are not supercuspidal

By Theorem 1.5.5 each irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$ that is not supercuspidal is an irreducible principal series $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ with $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \ne |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$, a character $\chi \circ \det$, or a twisted Steinberg representation $\eta \mathrm{St}$. Here $\chi_1 \times \chi_2 \cong \chi_2 \times \chi_1$ by Proposition 1.5.2.

**Proposition 3.3.3 (the explicit correspondence).** The map
$$\chi_1 \times \chi_2 \mapsto (\chi_1 \oplus \chi_2, 0), \qquad \chi \circ \det \mapsto (\chi |\cdot|^{-1/2} \oplus \chi |\cdot|^{1/2}, 0), \qquad \eta \mathrm{St} \mapsto \mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$$
is a bijection from the classes that are not supercuspidal onto the classes of kinds 1 and 2 of Proposition 3.3.2. It satisfies conditions 1 and 2 of Theorem 3.3.1, and conditions 3 and 4 of Theorem 3.1.2.

*Proof.* The map is a bijection. An unordered pair $\{\chi_1, \chi_2\}$ gives an element of kind 1. It comes from an irreducible principal series if $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \ne |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$, and from the character $\chi \circ \det$ with $\chi = \chi_1 |\cdot|^{1/2}$ if $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|^{-1}$. These two cases cover kind 1, and by Theorem 1.5.5 the representations on the left are pairwise distinct. The twisted Steinberg representations $\eta \mathrm{St}$ go bijectively to kind 2.

Condition 1. The central character of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ is $\chi_1 \chi_2$, that of $\chi \circ \det$ is $\chi^2$, and that of $\eta \mathrm{St}$ is $\eta^2$, because $\mathrm{St}$ has trivial central character. These are the determinants of the three parameters.

Conditions 3 and 4. A twist by $\chi$ multiplies all the characters on both sides by $\chi$, and does not change $N$. The contragredient of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ is $\chi_1^{-1} \times \chi_2^{-1}$ by Proposition 1.3.4, and that of $\eta \mathrm{St}$ is $\eta^{-1} \mathrm{St}$ by Proposition 1.5.4. The dual of $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$ is $({}^t r^{-1}, -{}^t N)$, with characters $\eta^{-1}|\cdot|^{-1/2}$ and $\eta^{-1}|\cdot|^{1/2}$ and a nonzero nilpotent part, so by Proposition 3.3.2 it is $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta^{-1}$.

Condition 2. By conditions 3 and 4 it suffices to take $\chi = 1$. The Godement-Jacquet factors of these representations are, see Chapter 2 and [BH06], Chapter 6:
$$L(s, \chi_1 \times \chi_2) = L(s, \chi_1) L(s, \chi_2), \quad L(s, \chi \circ \det) = L(s, \chi|\cdot|^{-1/2}) L(s, \chi |\cdot|^{1/2}), \quad L(s, \eta \mathrm{St}) = L(s, \eta |\cdot|^{1/2}),$$
and
$$\varepsilon(s, \chi_1 \times \chi_2, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \chi_1, \psi) \varepsilon(s, \chi_2, \psi), \qquad \varepsilon(s, \chi \circ \det, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \chi|\cdot|^{-1/2}, \psi) \varepsilon(s, \chi|\cdot|^{1/2}, \psi),$$
$$\varepsilon(s, \eta \mathrm{St}, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \eta|\cdot|^{1/2}, \psi) \varepsilon(s, \eta|\cdot|^{-1/2}, \psi) \frac{L(1 - s, \eta^{-1} |\cdot|^{-1/2})}{L(s, \eta |\cdot|^{-1/2})}.$$
On the Galois side, for $N = 0$ the factors of $\varphi_1 \oplus \varphi_2$ are the products of the factors of the characters, by additivity, and these are Tate's factors by Proposition 3.2.3. This settles the first two kinds. For $\sigma = \mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$, the kernel of $N$ is the line of $\eta |\cdot|^{1/2}$. If $\eta$ is ramified, then $V^{I_K} = 0$, so $L(s, \sigma) = 1 = L(s, \eta|\cdot|^{1/2})$. If $\eta$ is unramified, then $(\ker N)^{I_K}$ is this line, on which $\Phi$ acts by $\eta(\varpi) q^{-1/2}$, so $L(s, \sigma) = (1 - \eta(\varpi) q^{-1/2 - s})^{-1} = L(s, \eta|\cdot|^{1/2})$. For the $\varepsilon$-factor we use Deligne's formula from Chapter 2,
$$\varepsilon(s, (r, N), \psi) = \varepsilon(s, r, \psi) \det\left(-r(\Phi) q^{-s} \mid V^{I_K} / (\ker N)^{I_K}\right).$$
If $\eta$ is ramified, the determinant is 1, and $\varepsilon(s, r, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \eta|\cdot|^{1/2}, \psi) \varepsilon(s, \eta|\cdot|^{-1/2}, \psi)$ agrees with the formula for $\eta \mathrm{St}$, whose $L$-factors are both 1. If $\eta$ is unramified, put $x = \eta(\varpi) q^{1/2 - s}$. The quotient $V / \ker N$ is the line of $\eta |\cdot|^{-1/2}$, on which $\Phi$ acts by $\eta(\varpi) q^{1/2}$, so the determinant is $-x$. On the automorphic side
$$\frac{L(1 - s, \eta^{-1}|\cdot|^{-1/2})}{L(s, \eta|\cdot|^{-1/2})} = \frac{1 - x}{1 - x^{-1}} = -x.$$
So the two $\varepsilon$-factors agree. $\square$

**Example 3.3.4 (the Steinberg representation and elliptic curves).** Take $\eta = 1$ and $\psi$ trivial on $\mathcal{O}_K$ and not on $\mathfrak{p}_K^{-1}$. Then $\varepsilon(s, |\cdot|^{\pm 1/2}, \psi) = 1$ by Volume 3, Proposition 4.3.2, and $\varepsilon(s, \mathrm{St}, \psi) = -q^{1/2 - s}$. At $s = 1/2$ this is $-1$. For $\eta$ the unramified quadratic character it is $+1$. These are the local root numbers of an elliptic curve with split and with nonsplit multiplicative reduction, as in the introduction. The exponent of $q^{-s}$ in $\varepsilon$ is the conductor exponent, here 1, in agreement with Proposition 1.5.4: $\mathrm{St}$ has a nonzero vector fixed by the Iwahori subgroup.

**Example 3.3.5 (the Lean form of the explicit correspondence).** The Lean statement `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL2` describes the parameter of each irreducible subrepresentation $\pi$ of a principal series $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$. It says that $\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi)$ has $r = \mathrm{diag}(\varphi_1, \varphi_2)$, and that $N$ is the matrix that sends the second basis vector to the first if $\chi_1 = \chi_2 |\cdot|$ and $N = 0$ otherwise. This agrees with Proposition 3.3.3. If $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|$, then by Theorem 1.5.1 the subrepresentation is the twisted Steinberg representation $\eta \mathrm{St}$ with $\eta = \chi_2 |\cdot|^{1/2}$, and its parameter is $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$, with $\varphi_1 = \eta|\cdot|^{1/2}$ on the first basis vector, which spans $\ker N$. If $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|^{-1}$, the subrepresentation is the character $\chi \circ \det$ with $\chi = \chi_1 |\cdot|^{1/2}$, and $N = 0$. Otherwise $\pi = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$. By Theorem 1.4.5 each irreducible representation that is not supercuspidal is a subrepresentation of some $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$, so the statement fixes $\mathrm{rec}_2$ on all these classes.

### Dihedral parameters

Let $E/K$ be a separable quadratic extension with nontrivial automorphism $\tau$, and let $\xi$ be a character of $E^\times$, seen as a character of $W_E$ through $\mathrm{art}_E$. Let $\xi^\tau = \xi \circ \tau$. We write $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$ for the induced representation $\mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi$, of dimension 2. An irreducible representation of $W_K$ of this form is called *dihedral*, since its image in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is a dihedral group.

**Proposition 3.3.6 (induced representations).** Let $\xi$ and $\xi'$ be characters of $E^\times$.
1. $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$ is irreducible if and only if $\xi \ne \xi^\tau$, and this holds if and only if $\xi$ does not factor through the norm $N_{E/K} : E^\times \to K^\times$.
2. If $\xi \ne \xi^\tau$, then $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi \cong \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi'$ if and only if $\xi' \in \{\xi, \xi^\tau\}$.
3. $\det \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi = \kappa_{E/K} \cdot \xi|_{K^\times}$, and $(\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi) \otimes \chi = \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\xi \cdot \chi \circ N_{E/K})$.

*Proof.* Let $\sigma = \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$. By Mackey's formula, $\sigma|_{W_E} = \xi \oplus \xi^\tau$, where $\xi^\tau(w) = \xi(t w t^{-1})$ for $t \in W_K \smallsetminus W_E$. Through $\mathrm{art}_E$, conjugation by $t$ is the action of $\tau$ on $E^\times$, so this is the character $\xi \circ \tau$. By Frobenius reciprocity, $\mathrm{Hom}_{W_K}(\sigma, \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi') = \mathrm{Hom}_{W_E}(\xi \oplus \xi^\tau, \xi')$. For $\xi' = \xi$ this space has dimension 1 if $\xi \ne \xi^\tau$ and 2 otherwise. The representation $\sigma$ is semisimple, because its restriction to the subgroup $W_E$ of index 2 is semisimple. So $\sigma$ is irreducible exactly when its endomorphisms are scalars, which gives the first claim of part 1, and the same computation gives part 2. For the second claim of part 1: $\xi = \xi \circ \tau$ means that $\xi$ is trivial on the elements $x / \tau(x)$. By Hilbert's theorem 90 these are the elements of norm 1. So $\xi = \xi^\tau$ if and only if $\xi$ is trivial on $\ker N_{E/K}$, that is, if and only if $\xi$ factors through $N_{E/K}$. For part 3, the determinant of an induced representation of dimension 2 is the sign character of $W_K / W_E$ times the transfer of $\xi$, and the transfer $W_K^{ab} \to W_E^{ab}$ corresponds under $\mathrm{art}_K$ and $\mathrm{art}_E$ to the inclusion $K^\times \to E^\times$, by Volume 3, Theorem 2.4.2. The sign character is $\kappa_{E/K}$ by Example 3.2.4. The twist formula is the projection formula $\mathrm{Ind}(\xi) \otimes \chi = \mathrm{Ind}(\xi \otimes \chi|_{W_E})$, and $\chi|_{W_E}$ corresponds to $\chi \circ N_{E/K}$, again by local class field theory. $\square$

*Proof.* The restriction of $\sigma$ to the inertia group $I_K$ has finite image, because $I_K$ is compact and totally disconnected and $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ has no small subgroups, as in the proof of Corollary 3.2.2. So $\sigma(P_K)$ is a finite $p$-group. Its irreducible representations have dimension a power of $p$, and $p > 2$, so $\sigma|_{P_K}$ is a sum of two characters $\mu_1, \mu_2$. If $\mu_1 \ne \mu_2$, the group $W_K$ permutes the two eigenlines, because $P_K$ is normal. The action is transitive, since $\sigma$ is irreducible, so the stabilizer of one line is a subgroup $W_E$ of index 2 that contains $P_K$, and $\sigma = \mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi$ for the character $\xi$ on that line. Since $W_E \supseteq P_K$, the extension $E/K$ is tamely ramified. If $\mu_1 = \mu_2$, then $\sigma(P_K)$ is scalar, and $\sigma(I_K)$ is generated by scalars and the image of one generator of the procyclic group $I_K / P_K$. So $\sigma(I_K)$ is abelian, and $\sigma|_{I_K}$ is a sum of two characters $\nu_1, \nu_2$. If $\nu_1 \ne \nu_2$, the argument above gives $\sigma = \mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi$ with $W_E \supseteq I_K$, so $E/K$ is unramified. If $\nu_1 = \nu_2$, then $\sigma(I_K)$ is scalar, and $\sigma(W_K)$ is generated by $\sigma(I_K)$ and $\sigma(\Phi)$, so it is abelian. Then $\sigma$ is reducible, a contradiction. $\square$

For $p = 2$ there are also *primitive* representations, whose image in $PGL_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the tetrahedral group $A_4$ or the octahedral group $S_4$. Weil classified them in 1974 in "Exercices dyadiques", and they are the hard part of the case $p = 2$.

### Admissible pairs

From now on $p \ne 2$, except where we say otherwise. Proposition 3.3.7 says that each irreducible representation of $W_K$ of dimension 2 is induced from a character of a tamely ramified quadratic extension. The extension and the character are not unique, and a parametrization needs a rule that picks one of them. Bushnell and Henniart found such a rule. It is the notion of an admissible pair, and it gives the same parameter set on the two sides of the correspondence. This subsection and the next follow [BH06], Chapter 5, section 18. The later subsections follow sections 19 and 20 of Chapter 5 and section 34 of Chapter 8. The book writes $F$ for our $K$.

For a nonarchimedean local field $E$ we write $U_E^0 = \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ and $U_E^n = 1 + \mathfrak{p}_E^n$ for $n \ge 1$. These are open subgroups of $E^\times$, and a character of $E^\times$ is smooth if and only if it is trivial on some $U_E^n$. For a quadratic extension $E/K$ and a character $\chi$ of $K^\times$ we write $\chi_E = \chi \circ N_{E/K}$. If $E/K$ is unramified, then $\varpi$ is a uniformizer of $E$ and the residue field of $E$ is the quadratic extension $k_2$ of $k$. If $E/K$ is ramified, then $E = K(\sqrt{\varpi_0})$ for some uniformizer $\varpi_0$ of $K$, because $p \ne 2$ and $K^\times / K^{\times 2}$ is generated by the units and $\varpi_0$. We then take $\varpi_E = \sqrt{\varpi_0}$, so that $\tau(\varpi_E) = -\varpi_E$.

**Definition 3.3.8 (level).** Let $\chi$ be a smooth character of $E^\times$ that is nontrivial on $U_E^0$. The *level* $\ell(\chi)$ of $\chi$ is the integer $n \ge 0$ such that $\chi$ is trivial on $U_E^{n+1}$ and nontrivial on $U_E^n$. The conductor exponent of $\chi$ is then $a(\chi) = \ell(\chi) + 1$.

The level exists and is unique, since the groups $U_E^n$ decrease and $\chi$ is trivial on some of them. A character of level 0 is tamely ramified: it is trivial on $U_E^1$ and not on $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$. [BH06] also gives the unramified characters the level 0. The admissible characters below are never unramified, so the two conventions agree for them.

**Definition 3.3.9 (admissible pair).** An *admissible pair* is a pair $(E/K, \xi)$, where $E/K$ is a tamely ramified quadratic extension and $\xi$ is a smooth character of $E^\times$, such that
1. $\xi$ does not factor through $N_{E/K}$, and
2. if the restriction of $\xi$ to $U_E^1$ factors through $N_{E/K}$, then $E/K$ is unramified.

Two pairs $(E/K, \xi)$ and $(E'/K, \xi')$ are isomorphic if there is a $K$-isomorphism $j : E \to E'$ with $\xi = \xi' \circ j$. The set of isomorphism classes is $\mathbb{P}_2(K)$. The *twist* of $(E/K, \xi)$ by a smooth character $\chi$ of $K^\times$ is $(E/K, \xi \chi_E)$.

Here a character $\eta$ of a subgroup $U$ of $E^\times$ *factors through* $N_{E/K}$ if there is a character $\varphi$ of $K^\times$ with $\eta(u) = \varphi(N_{E/K}(u))$ for all $u \in U$. This holds if and only if $\eta$ is trivial on the elements of $U$ of norm 1. One direction is clear. For the other, $\eta$ then defines a character of the subgroup $N_{E/K}(U)$ of $K^\times$, and this character extends to $K^\times$, because $\mathbb{C}^\times$ is divisible. The definition makes sense for every $p$, and [BH06] uses it for every $p$. For $p = 2$ a tamely ramified quadratic extension is unramified. By Proposition 3.3.6, condition 1 says that $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$ is irreducible. Condition 2 removes the pairs whose induced representation also comes from the unramified quadratic extension, as Theorem 3.3.16 will show.

**Proposition 3.3.10 (first properties).** Let $(E/K, \xi)$ be an admissible pair.
1. $\xi^\tau \ne \xi$. The pair $(E/K, \xi^\tau)$ is admissible, and it is isomorphic to $(E/K, \xi)$.
2. For each smooth character $\chi$ of $K^\times$, the twist $(E/K, \xi \chi_E)$ is admissible. If $(E/K, \xi) \cong (E'/K, \xi')$, then $(E/K, \xi\chi_E) \cong (E'/K, \xi'\chi_{E'})$.
3. $\xi$ is ramified, so it has a level. If $E/K$ is ramified, then $\xi$ is nontrivial on $U_E^1$ and $\ell(\xi) \ge 1$.

### Minimal pairs and the level

The level of $\xi$ is not invariant under twists. A twist by a character $\chi$ of large level gives a character $\xi \chi_E$ of large level, but $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\xi \chi_E) = (\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi) \otimes \chi$ is essentially the same representation. The minimal pairs are the pairs whose level cannot be lowered by a twist. To find them we need the norm map on the filtration quotients.

**Lemma 3.3.11 (the norm on the unit filtration).** Let $E/K$ be a tamely ramified quadratic extension.
1. Let $E/K$ be unramified and $n \ge 0$. Then $N_{E/K}(U_E^n) \subseteq U_K^n$. For $n \ge 1$ identify $U_E^n / U_E^{n+1}$ with $k_2$ and $U_K^n / U_K^{n+1}$ with $k$ by $1 + x \varpi^n \mapsto \bar x$. Then $N_{E/K}$ induces the trace $\mathrm{Tr}_{k_2/k}$. For $n = 0$ it induces the norm $k_2^\times \to k^\times$, $x \mapsto x^{q+1}$. In both cases the induced map is surjective, and the image in $U_E^n / U_E^{n+1}$ of the elements of $U_E^n$ of norm 1 is its kernel.
2. Let $E/K$ be ramified and $p \ne 2$. Then the residue field of $E$ is $k$. For $m \ge 1$ we have $N_{E/K}(U_E^{2m-1}) \subseteq U_K^m$ and $N_{E/K}(U_E^{2m}) \subseteq U_K^m$. Identify $U_E^{2m} / U_E^{2m+1}$ and $U_K^m / U_K^{m+1}$ with $k$ by $1 + a \varpi_0^m \mapsto \bar a$. Then $N_{E/K}$ induces the bijection $\bar a \mapsto 2 \bar a$. Each element of $U_E^{2m}$ of norm 1 lies in $U_E^{2m+1}$, while the elements of $U_E^{2m-1}$ of norm 1 map onto $U_E^{2m-1} / U_E^{2m}$.

### The induced representation of an admissible pair

Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K$, and for each pair a $K$-embedding of $E$ into $\bar K$, so that $W_E$ is a subgroup of index 2 of $W_K$. Fix the reciprocity maps $\mathrm{art}_K$ and $\mathrm{art}_E$ of local class field theory, in the geometric normalization. For a smooth character $\xi$ of $E^\times$ let $\xi_W = \xi \circ \mathrm{art}_E^{-1}$, a character of $W_E$ through $W_E^{ab}$. Put
$$\sigma(E, \xi) = (\mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi_W, 0),$$
a Weil-Deligne representation of dimension 2. In the notation of Proposition 3.3.6 its representation part is $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$. The two embeddings of $E$ into $\bar K$ differ by $\tau$, so changing the embedding replaces $\xi$ by $\xi^\tau$. By part 3 of the next proposition the class of $\sigma(E, \xi)$ does not depend on the choice.

The proof uses three properties of the reciprocity maps, see [Ser79], Chapter XI, and Volume 3, Theorem 2.4.2. Let $t \in W_K \smallsetminus W_E$, so that $t$ acts on $E$ as $\tau$.
1. Conjugation. For $w \in W_E$, $\mathrm{art}_E^{-1}(t w t^{-1}) = \tau(\mathrm{art}_E^{-1}(w))$.
2. Norm. For $w \in W_E$, the image of $w$ in $W_K^{ab}$ is $\mathrm{art}_K(N_{E/K}(\mathrm{art}_E^{-1}(w)))$.
3. Transfer. The transfer $W_K^{ab} \to W_E^{ab}$ is $\mathrm{art}_E \circ \iota \circ \mathrm{art}_K^{-1}$, where $\iota : K^\times \to E^\times$ is the inclusion.

### Two examples

**Example 3.3.17 (an unramified pair over $\mathbb{Q}_3$).** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_3$, so that $q = 3$, and let $E = \mathbb{Q}_9$ be the unramified quadratic extension, with residue field $\mathbb{F}_9$ and uniformizer $3$. The group $\mathbb{F}_9^\times$ is cyclic of order 8. Let $g$ be a generator and $\theta$ the character with $\theta(g) = i$. Then $\theta^3(g) = -i$, so $\theta^3 \ne \theta$. Let $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ and let $\xi$ be the character of $E^\times = 3^{\mathbb{Z}} \times \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ with $\xi(3) = c$ that is $\theta$ composed with the reduction on $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$. It is trivial on $U_E^1$ and nontrivial on $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$, so $\ell(\xi) = 0$. By part 1 of Proposition 3.3.13 and the computation $\xi^\tau|_{\mathcal{O}_E^\times} = \theta^3 \ne \theta$, the pair $(E/K, \xi)$ is admissible and minimal.

We compute $\sigma = \sigma(E, \xi)$. The restriction of $\xi$ to $\mathbb{Q}_3^\times$ sends $3$ to $c$, and on $\mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ it is $\theta$ on $\mathbb{F}_3^\times = \{\pm 1\} = \{1, g^4\}$, which is trivial since $\theta(g^4) = i^4 = 1$. The character $\kappa_{E/K}$ is $x \mapsto (-1)^{v(x)}$. So $\det \sigma$ is trivial on $\mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ and sends a geometric Frobenius $\Phi$ to $-c$, by part 4 of Proposition 3.3.15. Since $\Phi \notin W_E$, the matrix $\sigma(\Phi)$ exchanges the two lines of the basis of the induced representation, so its trace is 0. Its characteristic polynomial is therefore $X^2 - c$. Condition 2 holds, because the proof of Proposition 3.3.13 shows that $\xi|_{U_E^1}$ does not factor through the norm: the element $(1 + \varpi_E)/(1 - \varpi_E)$ has norm 1, and $\xi$ takes the value $e^{4\pi i/p} \ne 1$ on it.

The restriction of $\xi$ to $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times = p^{\mathbb{Z}} \times \mu_{p-1} \times U_K^1$ is trivial: $p = \varpi_E^2$, and $U_K^1 \subseteq U_E^2$. So $\det \sigma(E, \xi)$ corresponds to $\kappa_{E/K}$, which by Example 3.2.4 is the Legendre symbol on $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ and has $\kappa_{E/K}(p) = \kappa_{E/K}(-1) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2}$. The wild inertia group $P_K = P_E$ acts on $\sigma(E, \xi)$ by $\mu \oplus \mu^{-1}$, where $\mu = \xi_W|_{P_K}$ has order $p$. These two characters are distinct, so $E$ is the field found in the first case of the proof of Proposition 3.3.7, and it is recovered from $\sigma(E, \xi)$ as the fixed field of the stabilizer of an eigenline of $P_K$. The conductor formula for induced representations, see [Ser79], Chapter VI, gives the Artin conductor exponent $v_K(d_{E/K}) + f_{E/K}\, a(\xi) = 1 + 2 = 3$.

Twists illustrate Proposition 3.3.14. A twist by a tamely ramified or unramified $\chi$ changes $\xi$ only on $\varpi_E$ and $\mu_{p-1}$, and keeps the level 1. A twist by a character $\chi$ of level $\ell \ge 1$ gives $\xi \chi_E$ of level $\max(1, 2\ell) = 2\ell$, and the twisted pair is admissible and not minimal. Twisting it back by $\chi^{-1}$ is the reduction of part 1 of that proposition. On the automorphic side, $\pi_\xi$ is a supercuspidal representation of depth $1/2$ and conductor exponent 3, with trivial central character $\xi|_{K^\times}$. Its parameter $\sigma(E, \Delta_\xi \xi)$ also has determinant $\kappa_{E/K} \cdot \kappa_{E/K} \cdot \xi|_{K^\times} = 1$, since $\Delta_\xi|_{K^\times} = \kappa_{E/K}$ by Theorem 3.3.20. Exercise 22 computes the group of characters $\chi$ with $\sigma(E, \xi) \otimes \chi \cong \sigma(E, \xi)$.

### The tame parametrization and the tame correspondence

On the automorphic side, [BH06], Chapter 5, attaches to each admissible pair an irreducible supercuspidal representation $\pi_\xi = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda_\xi$ of $G = GL_2(K)$, by the construction of Section 1.4. One first reduces to a minimal pair by Proposition 3.3.14. For a minimal pair of level 0 the construction is that of Example 1.4.9. For a minimal pair of positive level, the restriction of $\xi$ to $U_E^n$ defines a simple stratum, and $\Lambda_\xi$ is built from the character it defines and from $\xi$. Then $\pi_{\xi\chi_E}$ is defined as $\pi_\xi \otimes \chi \circ \det$.

**Theorem 3.3.19 (the tame parametrization theorem, Bushnell and Henniart).** Let $p \ne 2$. The map $(E/K, \xi) \mapsto \pi_\xi$ induces a bijection from $\mathbb{P}_2(K)$ onto $\mathcal{A}^0_2(K)$, the set of classes of irreducible supercuspidal representations of $GL_2(K)$. It satisfies $\omega_{\pi_\xi} = \xi|_{K^\times}$ and $\pi_{\xi \chi_E} \cong \pi_\xi \otimes \chi \circ \det$ for each smooth character $\chi$ of $K^\times$.

Lean: not stated yet

The proof is in [BH06], Chapter 5. The surjectivity is the hard part. It uses the result of Kutzko of Theorem 1.4.10 that each supercuspidal representation is compactly induced, and the classification of the pairs $(J, \Lambda)$. Theorems 3.3.16 and 3.3.19 together give a bijection between the supercuspidal classes and the irreducible representations of $W_K$ of dimension 2, namely $\pi_\xi \mapsto \sigma(E, \xi)$. This bijection is not the Langlands correspondence. The central characters do not match: $\det \sigma(E, \xi)$ corresponds to $\kappa_{E/K} \xi|_{K^\times}$ by Proposition 3.3.15, while $\omega_{\pi_\xi} = \xi|_{K^\times}$. A twist of $\xi$ by a tamely ramified character of $E^\times$ corrects this. This character is the rectifier $\Delta_\xi$ of [BH06], Chapter 8, section 34. We only state where it enters.

**Theorem 3.3.20 (the tame Langlands correspondence, Bushnell and Henniart).** Let $p \ne 2$. For each admissible pair $(E/K, \xi)$ there is a tamely ramified character $\Delta_\xi$ of $E^\times$ with
$$\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi_\xi) = \sigma(E, \Delta_\xi \xi).$$
If $E/K$ is unramified, $\Delta_\xi$ is the unramified character of $E^\times$ of order 2. If $E/K$ is ramified, $\Delta_\xi$ is a character with $\Delta_\xi|_{K^\times} = \kappa_{E/K}$, whose value on a uniformizer of $E$ depends on $\xi$ through a Gauss sum.

Lean: not stated yet

This is the main theorem of [BH06], Chapter 8. Here we write the correspondence from the automorphic side. [BH06], §34, writes it from the Galois side, as $\sigma(E, \xi) \mapsto \pi_{\Delta_\xi \xi}$. In the unramified case the two forms give the same character, since it has order 2. In the ramified case the character of the book and the character of this theorem can differ, but the existence statement is the same. The pair $(E/K, \Delta_\xi\xi)$ is again admissible. If $E/K$ is unramified, then $\Delta_\xi = \chi_E$ for the unramified character $\chi$ of $K^\times$ with $\chi(\varpi) = i$, and Proposition 3.3.10 applies. If $E/K$ is ramified, then $\Delta_\xi$ is trivial on $U_E^1$, so $\Delta_\xi\xi$ and $\xi$ agree on $U_E^1$. The restriction of $\Delta_\xi\xi$ to $U_E^1$ then does not factor through the norm, and this gives both conditions. The proof compares the $\varepsilon$-factors of the twists of the two sides. On the automorphic side $\varepsilon(s, \pi_\xi \otimes \chi, \psi)$ is computed from the explicit model of $\pi_\xi$, see [BH06], Chapter 6, and it is essentially a Gauss sum of $\xi \cdot \chi \circ N_{E/K}$. On the Galois side the inductivity of the $\varepsilon$-factors in degree 0, Volume 3, Theorem 4.3.8, gives
$$\varepsilon(s, \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \mu, \psi) = \lambda(E/K, \psi) \, \varepsilon(s, \mu, \psi \circ \mathrm{Tr}_{E/K}),$$
where $\lambda(E/K, \psi) = \varepsilon(s, \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} 1, \psi) / \varepsilon(s, 1_E, \psi \circ \mathrm{Tr}_{E/K})$ is the Langlands constant, a fourth root of unity. The two expressions differ by $\lambda(E/K, \psi)$ and by a ratio of Gauss sums. The character $\Delta_\xi$ is the unique tamely ramified character that absorbs this difference for all twists at once. Its restriction to $K^\times$ must be $\kappa_{E/K}$ by condition 1 of Theorem 3.3.1. Then Theorem 3.3.1 follows: the map defined by Proposition 3.3.3 and Theorem 3.3.20 is a bijection, since $\xi \mapsto \Delta_\xi \xi$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{P}_2(K)$, and it satisfies the conditions. Its uniqueness comes from the converse theorem.

**Example 3.3.21 (depth zero).** Let $E/K$ be unramified, so that its residue field is the quadratic extension $k_2$ of $k$ and $\varpi$ is a uniformizer of $E$. Let $\theta$ be a character of $k_2^\times$ with $\theta \ne \theta^q$, and let $\xi$ be a character of $E^\times$ whose restriction to $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$ is $\theta$ composed with the reduction map. The nontrivial automorphism $\tau$ of $E/K$ acts on $k_2$ as $x \mapsto x^q$ and fixes $\varpi$. So $\xi^\tau \ne \xi$, and $\xi$ does not factor through the norm, by Proposition 3.3.6. Condition 2 holds since $E/K$ is unramified. So $(E/K, \xi)$ is an admissible pair. The representation $\pi_\xi$ is the depth-zero supercuspidal representation of Example 1.4.9, induced from the cuspidal representation $\lambda_\theta$ of $GL_2(k)$, with $\varpi$ acting by $\xi(\varpi)$. Its central character is $\xi|_{K^\times}$. By Theorem 3.3.20,
$$\mathrm{rec}_2(\pi_\xi) = \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\Delta \xi), \qquad \Delta(x) = (-1)^{v_E(x)}.$$
We check condition 1 by hand. The restriction of $\Delta$ to $K^\times$ is $x \mapsto (-1)^{v_K(x)}$, which is $\kappa_{E/K}$ by Example 3.2.4. By Proposition 3.3.6,
$$\det \mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\Delta \xi) = \kappa_{E/K} \cdot \kappa_{E/K} \cdot \xi|_{K^\times} = \xi|_{K^\times} = \omega_{\pi_\xi}.$$
Without $\Delta$ the determinant would be $\kappa_{E/K} \xi|_{K^\times}$, which is different, so in this case the twist is forced by the central character alone. The conductors also agree. The representation $\pi_\xi$ has conductor exponent 2. On the Galois side, $I_E = I_K$ since $E/K$ is unramified, and the restriction of $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K}(\Delta\xi)$ to $I_K$ is $\xi|_{I_E} \oplus \xi^\tau|_{I_E}$. Each of these characters is tamely ramified and nontrivial on inertia, so each has conductor exponent 1, and the Artin conductor of the sum is 2. The $L$-factors of all twists are 1 on both sides.

### The Lean formalization of the tame side

Four files of the Lean project formalize parts of this section: `Langlands/Local/AdmissiblePairs.lean`, `Langlands/Local/MinimalPairs.lean`, `Langlands/Local/WeilInduction.lean` and `Langlands/Local/TameGalois.lean`. All the declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.LocalGL2`, and we omit this prefix below. The files write $F$ for our $K$, and $\sigma$ for our $\tau$. They contain no `sorry` and no new axioms. Some results take the three compatibilities of class field theory of Remark 3.3.23 as hypotheses.

**Remark 3.3.22 (what is proved without hypotheses).** The first two files work with an abstract quadratic extension $E/F$ of nonarchimedean local fields.
1. Definitions. The norm on units is `normUnits`, the automorphism $\tau$ is `galConj`, the filtration $U_E^n$ is `unitFilt`, and the level of Definition 3.3.8 is `IsLevel` and `level`. By `IsLevel`, a character has level $n$ when it is trivial on $U_E^{n+1}$ and not on $U_E^n$, so an unramified character has no level. The predicates `IsUnramifiedExt`, `IsTotallyRamifiedExt` and `IsTameQuadratic` describe the extension. The last one says that $2 \ne 0$ in $k$ or $E/F$ is unramified, so it allows $p = 2$ with $E/F$ unramified, as [BH06] does. Definition 3.3.9 is `IsAdmissible`, with the smoothness as a field, the twist is `twistChar`, and the bundled pairs are `TameQuadExt` and `AdmissiblePair`, with `AdmissiblePair.Iso`, `AdmissiblePair.conj` and `AdmissiblePair.twist`. Definition 3.3.12 is `IsMinimal` and `AdmissiblePair.IsMinimal`, and the ramification index is `ramIdx`.
2. Facts on the extension. A quadratic extension is separable if $2 \ne 0$ in $F$, `isSeparable_of_two_ne_zero`. Hilbert's theorem 90 is `exists_eq_div_galConj`, and the criterion for factoring through the norm is `exists_eq_comp_normUnits_iff`. The index $e(E|F)$ is 1 or 2, `ramIdx_eq_one_or_two`, so $E/F$ is unramified or totally ramified, `isUnramifiedExt_or_isTotallyRamifiedExt`. In the ramified case the residue fields agree, `exists_sub_mem_ordBall_one`, and $U_E^{2m} = U_F^m U_E^{2m+1}$, `exists_inclUnits_mul`. The bound $N_{E/F}(U_E^k) \subseteq U_F^m$ for $e(E|F)\, m < k + e(E|F)$ is `normUnits_mem_unitFilt_of_lt`. The deep units are norms, `exists_normUnits_eq`, by Hensel's lemma and for every $p$.
3. Facts on the pairs. Smoothness is triviality on some $U_E^n$, `isSmoothChar_iff_exists_unitFilt_le`, and the level is unique, `isLevel_level` and `IsLevel.unique`. Proposition 3.3.10 is `IsAdmissible.comp_galConjUnits_ne`, `IsAdmissible.comp_galConjUnits`, `AdmissiblePair.iso_conj`, `AdmissiblePair.conj_ξ_ne`, `IsAdmissible.twist`, `AdmissiblePair.Iso.twist`, `IsAdmissible.not_unitFilt_zero_le_ker`, `IsAdmissible.not_unitFilt_one_le_ker` and `IsAdmissible.isLevel_level_of_ramified`. The isomorphism of pairs is an equivalence relation, `AdmissiblePair.iso_equivalence`. Part 1 of Proposition 3.3.14 is `IsAdmissible.exists_isMinimal_twistChar` and `AdmissiblePair.exists_isMinimal_iso_twist`. The direction "minimal implies odd level" of part 3 of Proposition 3.3.13 is `IsMinimal.odd_level` and `AdmissiblePair.IsMinimal.odd_level`.
4. Levels of twists. If $\ell(\xi) \ne \ell(\chi_E)$, then $\xi\chi_E$ has level $\max(\ell(\xi), \ell(\chi_E))$, `IsLevel.mul_of_ne` and `isLevel_twistChar_of_ne`. If $E/F$ is ramified, $p \ne 2$ and $\ell(\chi) \ge 1$, then $\ell(\chi_E) = 2\ell(\chi)$, `isLevel_comp_normUnits_of_ramified` and `isLevel_twistChar_of_ramified`, with the square roots of `exists_sq_eq_of_mem_unitFilt`.

The file `WeilInduction.lean` treats induction from a subgroup $H$ of index 2 of an abstract group, with a fixed $s \notin H$. The representation `ind` is given by explicit matrices in the basis $1, s$, and `ind_apply_of_mem` computes it on $H$. With `conjChar` for the conjugate character, it proves Mackey's criterion `isIrreducibleRep_ind_iff`, the isomorphism `repIso_ind_conjChar` of the representations induced from $\xi$ and $\xi^s$, the projection formula `repIso_ind_mul_restrict` for `twistRep`, the determinant formula `det_ind_eq_signChar_mul_transfer` with `signChar` and the transfer, the semisimplicity of each matrix of `ind`, `isSemisimple_ind`, the openness of the kernel `isOpen_ker_ind`, and the easy direction of Frobenius reciprocity `repIso_ind_of_eigenvector`: a representation of dimension 2 with an eigenvector for $H$ on which $H$ acts by $\xi$, and with irreducible induced representation, is isomorphic to it. For Weil groups it proves `index_restrictSubgroup`, defines `indWD`, and proves `isIrreducible_indWD` and `iso_indWD_of_eigenvector`. If the representation part $r$ of a Weil-Deligne representation is irreducible, then $N = 0$, `WeilDeligneRep.N_eq_zero_of_isIrreducibleRep`.

**Remark 3.3.23 (what is proved modulo class field theory).** The file `TameGalois.lean` works with a quadratic extension $L/F$ that is an intermediate field of the algebraic closure of $F$, and with one fixed Artin map $\mathrm{art}_L$, an isomorphism $L^\times \to W_L^{ab}$ with `Langlands.IsLocalArtinMap`. It defines $\xi_W$ as `charW`, the parameter $\sigma(L, \xi)$ of Section 3.3 as `tameParam`, the twist of a Weil-Deligne representation as `twistWD`, and $\kappa_{L/F}$ as `quadChar`. It states the three properties of the reciprocity maps used in Proposition 3.3.15 as propositions, which quantify over all local Artin maps.
1. `ArtinConjCompat`: conjugation by $s \in W_F$ with $s(L) = L$ corresponds to the automorphism of $L$ that $s$ induces.
2. `ArtinNormCompat`: the map $W_L^{ab} \to W_F^{ab}$ induced by the inclusion corresponds to $N_{L/F}$.
3. `ArtinTransferCompat`: the transfer $W_F^{ab} \to W_L^{ab}$ corresponds to the inclusion $F^\times \to L^\times$.

These are theorems of local class field theory, [Ser79], Chapter XI, but the project does not prove them yet, so the file takes them as hypotheses. Under `ArtinConjCompat` it proves part 1 of Proposition 3.3.15, `conjChar_charW`, part 2 for admissible characters, `isIrreducible_tameParam`, part 3, `tameParam_iso` with `eq_of_algEquiv`, and part 6, `eq_or_eq_comp_of_tameParam_iso` and `exists_algEquiv_of_tameParam_iso`, from the abstract statement `eq_or_eq_conjChar_of_repIso`. Under `ArtinNormCompat` it proves part 5, `charW_twistChar`, `tameParam_twist` and `tameParam_isTwist`. Under `ArtinTransferCompat` it proves part 4, `det_tameParam`. Without any of the three hypotheses it proves that $\xi \mapsto \xi_W$ is injective, `charW_injective`, and that `quadChar` is trivial exactly on the norms, `quadChar_eq_one_iff`, from the properties in `Langlands.IsLocalArtinMap`.

### The proofs and the Lean statement

**Remark 3.3.25 (the history of the proof).** For dihedral $\sigma$ the representation $\pi$ was constructed by Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] with the Weil representation, and this covers all $\sigma$ for $p \ne 2$ by Proposition 3.3.7. The construction gives $\pi$ with the right factors for all twists, but it does not show directly that each supercuspidal representation arises. For $p = 2$, Tunnell treated the tetrahedral representations in 1978 with the base change of Langlands, and Kutzko [Kut80] completed the proof. He proved that the map is a bijection by counting the representations with a fixed conductor and central character on the two sides, using his construction of the supercuspidal representations by compact induction. [BH06] proves the theorem without global methods for $p \ne 2$.

**Remark 3.3.26 (what the Lean statement leaves out).** The proposition `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL2` says: for each $K$ and each $\mathrm{art}$ with `Langlands.IsLocalArtinMap`, there is a bijection $\mathrm{rec} : \mathrm{Irr}(GL_2(K)) \to \mathcal{G}_2(K)$ that satisfies the conditions of `Langlands.IsLanglandsMap` for $m = 2$ (central character, twists, contragredient, standard Godement-Jacquet $L$-factor), that has the explicit form of Example 3.3.5 on each irreducible subrepresentation of a principal series, and such that $\pi$ is supercuspidal if and only if $\mathrm{rec}(\pi)$ is irreducible. The statement leaves out the following.
1. The $\varepsilon$-factors of the twists. So the statement does not determine $\mathrm{rec}$ on the supercuspidal representations, and it is weaker than Theorem 3.3.1.
2. The uniqueness.
3. The admissible pairs, the tame parametrization of [BH06], Chapter 5, and the explicit form of Theorem 3.3.20 with the character $\Delta_\xi$. Remarks 3.3.22 to 3.3.24 describe the separate Lean files on the tame side, which do not yet connect to this statement.
4. The conductors.

The supercuspidal part of the statement is a bijection between two sets that preserves the central characters, the twists and the duality. Its existence is still a theorem: it needs Proposition 3.3.7, or Weil's classification for $p = 2$, and the tame parametrization theorem, or Kutzko's counting argument.
