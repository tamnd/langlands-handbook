## Exercises

1. Let $\mathrm{art}'_K(x) = \mathrm{art}_K(x^{-1})$ be the arithmetic normalization, and put $\mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi) = \mathrm{rec}_n(\pi^\vee)$. Show that $\det \mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi) = \omega_\pi \circ \mathrm{art}_K'^{-1}$, that $\mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det) = \mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi) \otimes (\chi \circ \mathrm{art}_K'^{-1})$, and that $\mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi^\vee) = \mathrm{rec}'_n(\pi)^\vee$. For $n = 1$, show that $L(s, \mathrm{rec}'_1(\chi)) \ne L(s, \chi)$ for $\chi = |\cdot|$, and that the equality holds for all $\chi$ if the $L$-factor of a character $\varphi$ of $W_K$ is defined with $\varphi(\Phi^{-1})$ in place of $\varphi(\Phi)$.
2. Let $\eta$ be a ramified character of $K^\times$ with conductor exponent $a(\eta)$, and let $\psi$ be trivial on $\mathcal{O}_K$ and not on $\mathfrak{p}_K^{-1}$. Show that the Artin conductor exponent of $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \eta$ is $2 a(\eta)$, and that $\varepsilon(s, \eta \mathrm{St}, \psi)$ is a constant times $q^{-2 a(\eta) s}$. Compare with the unramified case, where the exponent is 1.
3. Let $E/K$ be a separable quadratic extension, $t \in W_K \smallsetminus W_E$, and $\xi$ a character of $W_E$. Write the matrices of $\mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi$ in the basis given by the cosets $W_E$ and $t W_E$. Show that $\det \mathrm{Ind}_{W_E}^{W_K} \xi (w) = \mathrm{sgn}(w) \, \xi(V(w))$, where $\mathrm{sgn}$ is the nontrivial character of $W_K / W_E$ and $V$ is the transfer. Deduce part 3 of Proposition 3.3.6 from Volume 3, Theorem 2.4.2.
4. Let $k_2 / k$ be the quadratic extension of finite fields. Show that exactly $q^2 - q$ characters $\theta$ of $k_2^\times$ satisfy $\theta \ne \theta^q$. Let $E/K$ be unramified and $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$. Deduce that there are exactly $(q^2 - q)/2$ isomorphism classes of irreducible representations $\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi$ with $\xi$ trivial on $U_E^1$ and $\xi(\varpi) = c$.
5. Compute the Weil-Deligne representation $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \mathrm{Sp}(2)$, with nilpotent part $N \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes N$. Show that it is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Sp}(3) \oplus \mathrm{Sp}(1)$, and that its $L$-factor is $(1 - q^{-s})^{-1} (1 - q^{-1-s})^{-1}$. By Theorem 3.1.2 this is the $L$-factor of the pair $L(s, \mathrm{St} \times \mathrm{St})$.
6. Let $\mu$ be the unramified quadratic character and $\pi = 1 \times \mu$. Show that $\pi^{SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)}$ has dimension 1. Deduce that exactly one of the two summands of $\pi|_{SL_2(K)}$ has a nonzero vector fixed by $SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, and that the other has a nonzero vector fixed by $t_\varpi SL_2(\mathcal{O}_K) t_\varpi^{-1}$.
7. Let $p$ be odd, $E/K$ unramified, $\eta_2$ the quadratic character of $k_2^\times$, and $\theta$ a character of $k_2^\times$ with $\theta^{q-1} = \eta_2$. Let $\xi$ be the character of $E^\times$ with $\xi(\varpi) = 1$ that is $\theta$ on $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$, through the reduction map. Let $L = E(\sqrt{\varpi})$. You may use that $\kappa_{L/E}(u) = \eta_2(\bar u)$ for $u \in \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ and $\kappa_{L/E}(\varpi) = 1$.
   1. Show that $(E/K, \xi)$ is an admissible pair.
   2. Show that $\xi^\tau = \xi \cdot \kappa_{L/E}$.
   3. Let $E'$ be a ramified quadratic extension of $K$. Show that $\kappa_{E'/K} \circ N_{E/K} = \kappa_{L/E}$, and deduce that $|X(\mathrm{Ind}_{E/K} \xi)| = 4$.
   4. Deduce that the restriction of $\pi_\xi$ to $SL_2(K)$ has 4 summands.
8. Show that the reduced norm $\mathrm{Nrd} : D^\times \to K^\times$ is surjective. Deduce that $\eta \mapsto \eta \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$ is injective, that the representations $\mathrm{JL}(\eta \mathrm{St})$ are pairwise distinct, and that $\eta \circ \mathrm{Nrd}$ has central character $\eta^2$, as property 1 of Theorem 3.5.3 requires.
9. In Example 3.5.5, show that the restriction of $\rho$ to $\mathcal{O}_D^\times$ is the sum of the characters $\theta$ and $\theta^q$, through the reduction map, that $\rho$ is irreducible, and that the representations $\rho$ for $\theta$ and $\theta'$, with the same $c$, are isomorphic if and only if $\theta' \in \{\theta, \theta^q\}$.
10. Find elements of $GSp_4(\mathbb{C})$ that conjugate $\mathrm{diag}(t_1, t_2, t_3, t_4)$ to $\mathrm{diag}(t_1, t_3, t_2, t_4)$ and to $\mathrm{diag}(t_3, t_4, t_1, t_2)$. Deduce that the parameters of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2 \rtimes \sigma$, $\chi_2 \times \chi_1 \rtimes \sigma$ and $\chi_1^{-1} \times \chi_2 \rtimes \sigma \chi_1$ in property 5 of Theorem 3.6.4 are conjugate, and check that the three principal series have the same central character.
11. Let $\rho$ be an irreducible representation of $W_K$ of dimension 2, and $\varphi = \rho \oplus \rho$ with the orthogonal sum of the two forms of part 3 of Proposition 3.6.3. Show that $S_\varphi$ is isomorphic to the group $GO_2(\mathbb{C})$ of similitudes of a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on $\mathbb{C}^2$, and deduce that $\varphi$ is not discrete.
12. Let $\mathrm{art} : K^\times \to W_K^{ab}$ be any continuous homomorphism with property 1 of Theorem 3.2.1. Show that $\mathrm{art}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ is contained in the image $I_K^{ab}$ of $I_K$. Deduce that if $\chi \circ \mathrm{art}^{-1}$ is trivial on $I_K$, then $\chi$ is unramified and $L(s, \chi) = L(s, (\chi \circ \mathrm{art}^{-1}, 0))$, without the hypothesis of Remark 3.2.12. Show also that for a fixed uniformizer $\varpi_0$ the map $x \mapsto \mathrm{art}(\varpi_0)^{v_K(x)}$ has property 1 and is trivial on $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$, so that property 1 alone does not give $\mathrm{art}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times) = I_K^{ab}$.
13. Let $\chi$ be a ramified character of $K^\times$ with conductor exponent $a \ge 1$, and use the notation of Theorem 3.2.18. Show that $c_k(1) = 0$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and deduce that $Z(1_{B_N}, \chi, s) = 0$ for every $N$. Let $U^{(a)} = 1 + \mathfrak{p}_K^a$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Show that $Z(1_{\varpi^k U^{(a)}}, \chi, s) = \chi(\varpi)^k q^{-a} \mu(\mathcal{O}_K) \, q^{-ks}$. Deduce that every Laurent polynomial in $q^{-s}$ is a zeta integral of $\chi$, which is the second statement of Volume 3, Corollary 5.2.21.
14. Let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be smooth characters of $K^\times$, and let $\pi$ be the representation of $GL_1(K)$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $g \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(\chi_1(\det g), \chi_2(\det g))$. Show that the span of the zeta integrals of $\pi$, for $\mathrm{Re}(s)$ large, is $L(s, \chi_1) \mathbb{C}[X, X^{-1}] + L(s, \chi_2) \mathbb{C}[X, X^{-1}]$, with $X = q^{-s}$. Suppose that $\chi_1$ and $\chi_2$ are unramified. Show that the span is generated by $L(s, \chi_1) L(s, \chi_2)$ if $\chi_1(\varpi) \ne \chi_2(\varpi)$, and by $L(s, \chi_1)$ if $\chi_1 = \chi_2$. Deduce that, for $\chi_1 = \chi_2 = \chi$ unramified, the generator is not $L(s, \varphi \oplus \varphi)$ with $\varphi = \chi \circ \mathrm{art}_K^{-1}$. Compare with the zero representation of Remark 3.2.23, and explain what this says about the hypothesis of irreducibility in Theorem 2.5.3.
15. Let $A$ and $B$ be square matrices over $\mathbb{C}$ with $A^a = 0$ and $B^b = 0$, and suppose that $AB = BA$. Show that $(A + B)^{a + b - 1} = 0$. Apply this to $A = N \otimes 1$ and $B = 1 \otimes N'$ in Definition 3.1.15, and show that the bound is sharp for $N$ and $N'$ the monodromy operators of $\mathrm{Sp}(a)$ and $\mathrm{Sp}(b)$. Show also that if $\rho$ has dimension $1$, then $\rho \otimes \rho' = (\chi_\rho \circ \mathrm{art}^{-1} \otimes r', N')$ is the twist of $\rho'$, and deduce that condition 1 of Definition 3.1.20 for $m' = 1$ and $\pi'$ the trivial representation of $GL_1(K)$ compares $L(s, \rho)$ with the factor of pairs of $\pi \times 1$.
16. Show that $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgn}}$ of Theorem 3.1.27 satisfies the four conditions of Definition 3.1.17, and that it differs from $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}$ at every $(s, \psi, \mu)$ on the trivial representation of dimension $2$. Next, let $\chi$ be a smooth character, $\psi$ a character with a conductor exponent, and $e_\mu(s)$ Tate's factor of $(\chi, \mu, \psi)$. Show that $e_{c\mu} = c \, e_\mu$ for $c > 0$. Deduce that $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}$ is not additive: for $\rho = \rho' = (1, 0)$ of dimension $1$, the identity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}(\rho \oplus \rho') = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}(\rho) \varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}(\rho')$ fails for some Haar measure.
17. Let $\chi$ be an unramified character, let $\psi$ have conductor exponent $0$, and let $\mu$ be the self dual measure. Compute $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}(s, \mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi, \psi, \mu)$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgn}}(s, \mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi, \psi, \mu)$, and compare them with Example 2.4.12. Here $\mathrm{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi$ is the representation $\chi \oplus \chi\|\cdot\|$ of Example 2.4.12, with $N e_0 = e_1$. Then let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be ramified, with conductor exponents $a_1, a_2 \ge 1$, and $\rho = (\chi_1 \circ \mathrm{art}^{-1} \oplus \chi_2 \circ \mathrm{art}^{-1}, 0)$. Show that $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}(s, \rho, \psi, \mu) = 1$, while the factor of Definition 2.4.10 is a monomial of degree $a_1 + a_2$ in $q^{-s}$. Deduce that $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{gal}}$ is not the factor of Definition 2.4.10.
18. Let $Z$ be a set of functions of $s$, and let $L = P(q^{-s})^{-1}$ generate $Z$ in the sense of Definition 3.1.11. Let $Q$ be a polynomial with $Q(0) = 1$ such that each $f \in Z$ is $A(X) X^{-k} Q(X)^{-1}$ for $\mathrm{Re}(s)$ large. Show that $Q$ is divisible by $P$. Give an example of a set $Z$ for which no function generates $Z$, although each $f \in Z$ is a Laurent polynomial in $q^{-s}$. Finally, let $\pi$ and $\pi'$ be generic representations of $GL_n(K)$ for which an $\varepsilon$-factor of pairs exists for $\mu$, and let $c > 0$. Show that the $\varepsilon$-factor of pairs for $c\mu$ is $c^n$ times the one for $\mu$, where the integrals over $N_n \backslash GL_n(K)$ and the Fourier transform on $K^n$ are made with the products of $c\mu$. Compare with the factor $c^{n^2}$ of a Galois $\varepsilon$-factor of dimension $n^2$ that is additive and satisfies condition 3, on a sum of $n^2$ characters, and explain why condition 2 of Definition 3.1.20 asks for the self dual measure, while Theorem 3.1.25 holds for every Haar measure.
19. Let $p \ne 2$ and let $E/K$ be a tamely ramified quadratic extension. Show that each element of $U_E^1$ of norm 1 is $u / \tau(u)$ for some $u \in U_E^1$, both when $E/K$ is unramified and when it is ramified. Deduce that a character $\eta$ of $U_E^1$ factors through $N_{E/K}$ if and only if $\eta \circ \tau = \eta$ on $U_E^1$. Conclude that a smooth character $\xi$ of $E^\times$ with $\xi^\tau \ne \xi$ satisfies condition 2 of Definition 3.3.9 if and only if $E/K$ is unramified or $\xi^\tau \ne \xi$ on $U_E^1$.
20. Let $p \ne 2$, let $E/K$ be a tamely ramified quadratic extension, and let $\chi$ be a smooth character of $K^\times$ that is ramified, of level $\ell$. Show that $N_{E/K}(U_E^n) = U_K^n$ for every $n \ge 0$ if $E/K$ is unramified, and deduce that $\chi_E$ has level $\ell$. Let $E/K$ be ramified. Show that $\chi_E$ has level $2\ell$ if $\ell \ge 1$. If $\ell = 0$, show that $\chi_E$ is trivial on $U_E^1$, and that $\chi_E$ is unramified if and only if $\chi^2$ is trivial on $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$.
21. Let $E/K$ be unramified and $n \ge 1$, and fix a nontrivial character $\psi_k$ of $k$. For $\alpha \in k_2$ let $\psi_\alpha(x) = \psi_k(\mathrm{Tr}_{k_2/k}(\alpha x))$, a character of $U_E^n / U_E^{n+1} \cong k_2$. Show that $\psi_\alpha \circ \tau = \psi_{\alpha^q}$. Let $\xi$ be a smooth character of $E^\times$ of level $n$ that is $\psi_\alpha$ on $U_E^n$, and let $\chi$ be a character of $K^\times$ of level at most $n$ with $\chi(1 + a\varpi^n) = \psi_k(\beta \bar a)$ for some $\beta \in k$. Show that $\xi \chi_E$ is $\psi_{\alpha + \beta}$ on $U_E^n$. Deduce that if $\alpha \notin k$, then $(E/K, \xi)$ is an admissible pair, and it is minimal, and that if $\alpha \in k$, then the twist of $\xi$ by a suitable $\chi$ is trivial on $U_E^n$. How many characters $\psi_\alpha$ of $U_E^n/U_E^{n+1}$ occur for minimal pairs of level $n$, and how many classes do they form modulo $k$?
22. Let $p$ be odd, and let $E$ and $\xi$ be as in Example 3.3.18. Let $\eta$ be a smooth character of $E^\times$ that agrees with $\xi$ on $U_E^1$, for example $\eta = \xi$ or $\eta = \Delta_\xi \xi$, and let $\sigma = \sigma(E, \eta)$. Show that $\sigma \otimes \kappa_0 \not\cong \sigma$ for the unramified quadratic character $\kappa_0$ of $K^\times$. Deduce that $X(\sigma) = \{1, \kappa_{E/K}\}$, in the notation of Section 3.4, and that the restriction to $SL_2(K)$ of the supercuspidal representation $\pi_\xi$ has exactly 2 summands.
23. Let $\mathfrak{J}$ be the Iwahori order of Definition 3.9.1. Write out $\mathfrak{P}^n_{\mathfrak{J}}$ for $n = -1, 0, 1, 2$, and check that $\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}^2 = \varpi$ and $\mathfrak{P}^{n+1}_{\mathfrak{J}} = \Pi_{\mathfrak{J}} \mathfrak{P}^n_{\mathfrak{J}}$. Show that $\mathfrak{J}^\times / U^1_{\mathfrak{J}} \cong k^\times \times k^\times$ and that $K^\times \mathfrak{J}^\times$ has index 2 in $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{J}}$. Prove the duality $b_{1-n}(j, i) = 1 - b_n(i, j)$ of the bounds for both chain orders, and deduce the trace duality of Proposition 3.9.2, part 4.
24. Let $E/K$ be unramified, let $n = 2l \ge 2$ be even, and let $\alpha \in E$ be minimal of level $n$ with $v_E(\alpha) = -n$. Let $\theta = \psi_\alpha$ on $U^{l+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}$. For $x = 1 + X$ and $y = 1 + Y$ in $U^l_{\mathfrak{M}}$, show that the commutator $[x, y] = x y x^{-1} y^{-1}$ lies in $U^{2l}_{\mathfrak{M}} \subseteq U^{l+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}$ and that $\theta([x, y]) = \psi(\mathrm{tr}(a_\alpha(X) Y))$. Use Proposition 3.9.18 to show that this form is not trivial on $U^l_{\mathfrak{M}} \times U^l_{\mathfrak{M}}$. Deduce that no character of $J_\alpha = E^\times U^l_{\mathfrak{M}}$ restricts to $\theta$ on $U^{l+1}_{\mathfrak{M}}$, so that the set of cuspidal types of Definition 3.9.12 is empty for even $n$.
25. Prove the claims of Example 3.9.26. Show that $J_\alpha \cap GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K) = \mathcal{O}_E^\times U^1_{\mathfrak{M}}$, that its image in $GL_2(k)$ is $k_2^\times$, and that its index in $GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ is $q(q - 1)$. Show that $g \in GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ intertwines $\psi_\alpha$ if and only if $\bar g \bar\beta = \bar\beta \bar g$, and deduce Proposition 3.9.20 for $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{M}} = K^\times GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ in this case without Lemma 3.9.19.
26. Prove the claims of Example 3.9.27. Show that $\alpha = \frac12 \Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}^{-1}$ in the basis $(\varpi_E, 1)$, compute $\psi_\alpha$, and check the compatibility of $(\alpha, \xi)$. Show that $g = \mathrm{diag}(1, p)$ does not lie in $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{J}}$, and find $x \in U^1_{\mathfrak{J}}$ with $g x g^{-1} \in U^1_{\mathfrak{J}}$ and $\psi_\alpha(g x g^{-1}) \ne \psi_\alpha(x)$. Show that $\mathrm{diag}(u, v)$ with $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ intertwines $\psi_\alpha$ if and only if $u \equiv v \pmod p$. Show that $\Lambda(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}) = 1$ and that $\pi_\xi$ has trivial central character.
27. Let $\mathfrak{A}$ be a chain order. For $g \in G$ let $k(g)$ be the largest integer with $g \in \mathfrak{P}^k_{\mathfrak{A}}$. Show that $k(a g b) = k(g) + k(a) + k(b)$ for $a, b \in \mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}}$, and that $\nu(g) = v_K(\det g) - \frac{2}{e_{\mathfrak{A}}} k(g)$ is constant on the double cosets $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}} g \mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}}$. Compute $\nu(D(\varpi^t))$ for $t \ge 0$, and deduce that the double cosets $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}} D(\varpi^t) \mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}}$, $t \ge 0$, are distinct, so that Lemma 3.9.21 gives a bijection between $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}} \backslash G / \mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{A}}$. For $\mathfrak{J}$, show that the Weyl element $w = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ lies in $\mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{J}} D(\varpi) \mathfrak{K}_{\mathfrak{J}}$.
28. Let $\eta$ be a character of $W_K$, $\varphi_1 = \eta |\cdot|^{1/2}$ and $\varphi_2 = \eta |\cdot|^{-1/2}$. Let $\rho = (\mathrm{diag}(\varphi_1, \varphi_2), 0)$. Find an explicit matrix that gives $\rho \cong M(\varphi_2, \varphi_1)$, and use Theorem 3.10.9 to show that $\rho$ is not isomorphic to $M(\varphi_1, \varphi_2)$. Show that the classes of $M(\varphi_1, \varphi_2)$ and $M(\varphi_2, \varphi_1)$ have the same semisimplification, and name the representations of $G$ that correspond to them by Theorem 3.10.16 when $\eta = \varphi_\chi$.
29. Let $\varphi_1$ be ramified and $\varphi_2$ unramified. Compute $L(s, M(\varphi_1, \varphi_2))$ and $L(s, M(\varphi_2, \varphi_1))$ from the definition. Then let $\eta$ be a ramified character of $K^\times$, and compute $L(s, \eta \mathrm{St})$ and $L(s, \eta \circ \det)$ with Theorem 3.10.16, part 4. Compare with the unramified case of Examples 3.10.18 and 3.10.19.
30. Compute the dual of $M(\eta |\cdot|^{1/2}, \eta |\cdot|^{-1/2})$ directly, with the matrix $h$ of Proposition 3.10.10, and show that it is $M(\eta^{-1} |\cdot|^{1/2}, \eta^{-1} |\cdot|^{-1/2})$. Deduce from inputs 2 and 7 of Definition 3.10.14 that $(\eta \mathrm{St})^\vee \cong \eta^{-1} \mathrm{St}$ for each smooth character $\eta$ of $K^\times$.
31. Let $D$ be a tame datum for $(L/K, \xi)$, and let $\kappa = \kappa_{L/K}$ be the quadratic character of $K^\times$ with kernel $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. Show that $D \otimes \kappa$ is a tame datum for the same pair. Deduce from Proposition 3.10.23, part 3, and input 3 of Definition 3.10.27 that $\pi_D \otimes (\kappa \circ \det) \cong \pi_D$. Check that this agrees with the Galois side, where $\sigma \otimes \kappa \cong \sigma$ for $\sigma = \mathrm{Ind}_{W_L}^{W_K} \xi$.
32. Let $J = K^\times GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, $\Lambda = 1$, $V = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G 1$ and $K' = GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$. Show that each $h \in G$ is admissible for $K'$ in the sense of Lemma 3.10.31. Use Exercise 27 for the order $\mathfrak{M}$ to show that $V^{K'}$ has infinite dimension. Deduce that $V$ is not admissible, and find an element of $G \smallsetminus J$ that intertwines $\Lambda$.
33. Let $g \in G$. Use the Iwasawa decomposition $G = K_0 B$ to show that $g N g^{-1} = k N k^{-1}$ for some $k \in K_0$. Deduce that in the alternative of Section 3.11, condition (B) with $g_0 \in G$ is equivalent to condition (B) with $g_0 \in K_0$, and that (A) fails exactly when (B) holds.
34. In Example 3.11.16 take $n = 1$, so $J = E^\times U^1_{\mathfrak{M}}$ and $\Lambda$ is trivial on $U^2_{\mathfrak{M}}$. Show that $J \cap K_0 = \mathcal{O}_E^\times U^1_{\mathfrak{M}}$. Show that the vectors of $V = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ fixed by $U^2_{\mathfrak{M}}$ are exactly the vectors supported in $J K_0$, and that their space has dimension $q(q-1)$. Show also that $V^{U^1_{\mathfrak{M}}} = 0$.
35. Let $T_m$ be the set of $g \in M_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ with $v_K(\det g) = m$. Show that each coset $g K_0 \subseteq T_m$ contains exactly one matrix $\begin{pmatrix} \varpi^a & b \\ 0 & \varpi^c \end{pmatrix}$ with $a + c = m$ and $b$ in a fixed set of representatives of $\mathcal{O}_K / \mathfrak{p}_K^a$, and deduce that $T_m$ has $\sum_{i=0}^m q^i$ such cosets. Let $\chi$ be unramified with $\chi(\varpi) = a$. Compute $Z(1_{M_2(\mathcal{O}_K)}, s, \chi \circ \det)$ with $\mathrm{vol}(K_0) = 1$, and check that it agrees with the fact `PSLFactorDetChar` of Definition 3.11.31.
36. Let $\Pi = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$ with $\chi_1 \ne \chi_2$, let $V(N)$ be the span of the $\Pi(n) f - f$, and let $\gamma$ be as in Section 3.11 and $\gamma^w = \chi_2 |\cdot|^{1/2} \otimes \chi_1 |\cdot|^{-1/2}$. Use Theorem 1.3.11 to show that $V / V(N)$ is the direct sum of a line on which $T$ acts by $\gamma$ and a line on which it acts by $\gamma^w$, and that $\gamma \ne \gamma^w$. Deduce that $\Pi_\gamma$ is the preimage of the first line, so that it contains $V(N)$ and is a proper subspace of $\Pi$. In the Steinberg case $\chi_1 = \chi_2 |\cdot|$, show that the map $\Pi \to \eta \circ \det$ of Theorem 1.5.1, part 3, is zero on $\Pi_\gamma$.
37. Let $(\pi, V)$ be a smooth representation of $G$ and $U$ a compact open subgroup. For $x \in V$ put $e_U(x) = [U : U']^{-1} \sum_{u \in U / U'} \pi(u) x$, where $U' \subseteq U$ is an open subgroup that fixes $x$. Show that $e_U(x)$ does not depend on $U'$, that $e_U$ is linear, that $e_U$ is a projection onto $V^U$, and that $f \circ e_U = e_U \circ f$ for each map $f$ of representations. Use $e_U$ to prove `SmoothDualExtends`. Deduce that for an irreducible $\pi$ in $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$, the restriction map $(\chi_1 \times \chi_2)^\vee \to \pi^\vee$ is surjective.
38. Let $Q_8 \subseteq GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ be the group of Example 3.12.14, generated by $\mathbf{i} = \mathrm{diag}(i, -i)$ and $\mathbf{j} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Show that $Q_8$ has order 8 and no common eigenvector. With $y = \mathbf{i}$ and $h = \mathbf{j}$, compute the scalar $e_h$ of the proof of Proposition 3.12.13, and say which step of the proof fails. Then let $p$ be odd and $G$ a finite $p$-subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$. Use Proposition 3.12.13 to show that $G$ is conjugate to a group of upper triangular matrices, then that $G$ is abelian, and then that $G$ is conjugate to a group of diagonal matrices.
39. Let $J$, $\Lambda$ and $V = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda$ be as in Theorem 3.12.5, and $V' = \mathrm{c\text{-}Ind}_J^G \Lambda^{-1}$. For $f \in V$ and $f' \in V'$ put $\langle f, f' \rangle = \sum_{x \in J \backslash G} f(x) f'(x)$. Show that the sum is finite and well defined, that $\langle \rho(g) f, \rho'(g) f' \rangle = \langle f, f' \rangle$, and that $f = \sum_{x \in J \backslash G} f(x) \rho(x^{-1}) f_0$. Deduce that $\lambda(f) = \langle f, f_\lambda \rangle$ for $\lambda \in V^\vee$, that the pairing is nondegenerate on both sides, and that the inverse of the isomorphism $\Psi$ of Theorem 3.12.5 is $f' \mapsto \langle \cdot, f' \rangle$. Which hypothesis of Theorem 3.12.5 does this second proof of surjectivity avoid?
40. Let $L/K$ be a separable quadratic extension. ## Notes and history

Langlands formulated the local correspondence in his letter to Weil of 1967 and in [Lan70], as the local form of his functoriality conjectures. For $GL_2$, Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] constructed the local factors, proved the converse theorem, and built the representations attached to the dihedral parameters with the Weil representation. They also proved the local and global correspondence with quaternion algebras by the trace formula. Tunnell treated the tetrahedral parameters in 1978, using the base change of Langlands, and Kutzko [Kut80] completed the case of $GL_2$ in 1980 with a counting argument based on his classification of the supercuspidal representations. The book of Bushnell and Henniart [BH06] gives a proof without global methods. It constructs the supercuspidal representations from types, introduces the admissible pairs, and finds the character $\Delta_\xi$ of Theorem 3.3.20 from the $\varepsilon$-factors and the Langlands constants. Deligne's formula for the $\varepsilon$-factor of a Weil-Deligne representation and the inductivity of $\varepsilon$-factors in degree 0 are in [Tat79].

For $GL_n$, Henniart proved in 1993 that the $\varepsilon$-factors of pairs determine the correspondence. Laumon, Rapoport and Stuhler proved the existence in 1993 for $K$ of positive characteristic, by the cohomology of the moduli spaces of $\mathcal{D}$-elliptic sheaves. For $K$ of characteristic 0, Harris and Taylor [HT01] proved the existence with the cohomology of some simple Shimura varieties and of the Lubin-Tate tower, and Henniart [Hen00] gave a shorter proof of the existence. Scholze gave a new proof in 2013, with a characterization by the traces of Frobenius on nearby cycles. Fargues and Scholze construct the semisimple parameters for every reductive group by the geometrization of the correspondence, and Volume 12 explains it.

Labesse and Langlands [LL79] introduced the L-packets of $SL_2$ and the notion of L-indistinguishability, the first case of endoscopy. Gelbart and Knapp extended the analysis to $SL_n$ in 1982. The correspondence between the discrete series of $GL_n$ and the representations of division algebras is due to Rogawski (1983) and to Deligne, Kazhdan and Vignéras (1984), and Badulescu extended it to all inner forms and to positive characteristic. Gan and Takeda [GT11] proved the correspondence for $GSp_4$ in 2011 with the theta correspondence, after the classification of the non-supercuspidal representations by Sally and Tadić (1993) and the tables of Roberts and Schmidt (2007).

The Lean project proves local class field theory in the form of the correspondence for $GL_1$, `Langlands.localReciprocityGL1`, and the correspondence for split and induced tori, `Langlands.localLanglandsTorusSplit` and `Langlands.localLanglandsTorusInduced`. It states, without proof, the correspondence for $GL_n$ (`Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL`), for $GL_2$ with the explicit form on the principal series (`Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL2`), the restriction form of the L-packets of $SL_2$ (`Langlands.LocalLanglandsSL2`), the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence with the character identity (`Langlands.JacquetLanglandsGL2`), and the existence part of the theorem of Gan and Takeda (`Langlands.LocalLanglandsGSp4`). The file `Langlands/Local/GL1.lean` proves Schur's lemma for an admissible representation of a commutative group, builds $\mathrm{rec}_1$ from the local Artin map, and proves the conditions on central characters, twists and duals for it, the case $m = 0$, and the formula for the $L$-factor of $(\chi \circ \mathrm{art}_K^{-1}, 0)$. The file `Langlands/Local/GL1LFactor.lean` does Tate's local computation over every nonarchimedean local field and for every Schwartz-Bruhat function, and with it proves `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGL 1` with no hypothesis, as `Langlands.LocalGL1.localLanglandsGL_one`, see Theorems 3.2.14, 3.2.18 and 3.2.22. It also proves that the first form of this input, the proposition `Langlands.LocalGL1.GL1LFactor`, is false, because it allowed the zero representation, see Remark 3.2.23. None of these statements contains $\varepsilon$-factors, so none of them characterizes the correspondence. The statement `Langlands.LocalLanglandsGLFull` adds the $\varepsilon$-factors and the factors of pairs of generic representations, in the form of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika. The files `Langlands/Local/LanglandsFamilyPairs.lean`, `Langlands/Local/LanglandsFamilyPairsEps.lean` and `Langlands/Fidelity/LocalLanglandsFull.lean` prove it for $n = 1$, see Theorem 3.1.26. But its Galois $\varepsilon$-factor is a parameter, and Theorem 3.1.27 shows that its conditions do not fix it in dimension $2$, because the inductivity in degree $0$ is missing. Exercises 15 to 18 treat the tensor product, the two Galois $\varepsilon$-functions of Lean, and the generators. The tame side of $GL_2$ is in separate files, described in Remarks 3.3.22 to 3.3.24, and Exercises 19 to 22 treat the norm-one elements, the levels of twists and the minimal pairs. Section 3.9 follows [BH06], Chapters 4, 5 and 8, and the Lean files on chain orders, cuspidal types and compact induction, and Exercises 23 to 27 treat the Iwahori order, the even level, the two examples and the double cosets. Remarks 3.1.9, 3.1.14, 3.1.28, 3.1.30, 3.2.8, 3.2.15, 3.2.23, 3.3.26, 3.4.13, 3.5.7 and 3.6.9 list what each statement leaves out. Section 3.11 follows the Lean files `Langlands/Local/CIndAdmissible.lean`, `Langlands/Local/SupercuspidalLFactor.lean` and `Langlands/Local/GL2PrincipalSeriesInputs.lean`. Its proof of admissibility avoids the compactly supported coefficients of [BH06], 10.1, by a case split on the conjugates of the unipotent radical, and Remark 3.11.36 lists what is still open for $GL_2$, $p \ne 2$.
