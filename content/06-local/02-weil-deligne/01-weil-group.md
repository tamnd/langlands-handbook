## The Weil group and its representations

This section recalls the Weil group of $K$ from Volume 3, Chapter 1, and adds two things that the rest of the chapter needs. The first is the structure of the inertia group: modulo its pro-$p$ part it is abelian, and Frobenius acts on it by raising to the power $q^{-1}$. The second is the study of the continuous representations of $W_K$ on complex vector spaces. They are trivial on an open subgroup of inertia, and an irreducible one is an unramified twist of a representation with finite image. The example to keep in mind is a character of $K^\times$, which is a character of $W_K$ by local class field theory.

### The Weil group

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field, with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, uniformizer $\varpi$ and residue field $k$ with $q$ elements. Let $p$ be the characteristic of $k$. Fix a separable closure $\bar K$ of $K$ and let $G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K)$. The residue field $\bar k$ of $\bar K$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, and the residue map $G_K \to \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$ is surjective with kernel the inertia group $I_K$ (Volume 3, Proposition 1.2.3). Let $\mathrm{Frob}$ be the arithmetic Frobenius $x \mapsto x^q$ of $\bar k$.

**Definition 2.1.1 (Weil group).** The *Weil group* $W_K$ is the subgroup of the $\sigma \in G_K$ whose image in $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$ is an integral power $\mathrm{Frob}^{d(\sigma)}$. The integer $d(\sigma)$ is the *degree* of $\sigma$. The topology of $W_K$ is the one for which $I_K$, with its profinite topology, is an open subgroup.

This is Definition 1.3.1 and Definition 1.3.2 of Volume 3. The degree is a surjective homomorphism $d : W_K \to \mathbb{Z}$ with kernel $I_K$, so there is an exact sequence $1 \to I_K \to W_K \to \mathbb{Z} \to 1$ of topological groups, where $\mathbb{Z}$ is discrete.

**Definition 2.1.2 (geometric Frobenius and the norm).** An element $\Phi \in W_K$ is a *geometric Frobenius* if it acts on $\bar k$ by $x \mapsto x^{1/q}$, that is, if $d(\Phi) = -1$. The *norm* of $w \in W_K$ is $\|w\| = q^{d(w)}$. So $\|\Phi\| = q^{-1}$.

Each $w \in W_K$ can be written in exactly one way as $w = \Phi^m \sigma$ with $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $\sigma \in I_K$, namely with $m = -d(w)$. We use this decomposition many times. The Lean library normalizes the degree with the opposite sign: its map `Langlands.WeilGroup.degree` gives a geometric Frobenius the degree $1$. Its norm `Langlands.weilNorm` is $q$ to the power minus this degree. So the two norms agree, and `Langlands.geomFrob` is a fixed choice of $\Phi$.

**Proposition 2.1.3 (density).** The inclusion $W_K \to G_K$ is continuous and injective, and its image is dense.

*Proof.* The continuity is part of Volume 3, Proposition 1.3.3. Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $G_K$. We show that $W_K U = G_K$. The group $G_K / I_K U$ is a finite quotient of $G_K / I_K \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k) \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$, so it is generated by the image of $\mathrm{Frob}$. A lift of $\mathrm{Frob}$ to $W_K$ maps to this generator, and $I_K \subseteq W_K$. So the image of $W_K$ in the finite group $G_K / U$ contains $I_K U / U$ and a generator of the cyclic quotient $G_K / I_K U$. Hence it is all of $G_K / U$. Since the sets $gU$ form a basis of the topology of $G_K$, the image of $W_K$ is dense. $\square$

**Theorem 2.1.4 (local reciprocity).** There is a unique isomorphism of topological groups $\mathrm{rec}_K : K^\times \to W_K^{ab}$ that maps each uniformizer to the class of a geometric Frobenius and that induces, for each finite abelian extension $L / K$, a surjection $K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(L / K)$ with kernel $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. It satisfies $\|\mathrm{rec}_K(x)\| = |x|$ for $x \in K^\times$.

This is Theorem 1.4.4 and Theorem 2.3.3 of Volume 3. The formula for the norm is Volume 0, Remark 3.1.3. The Lean project proves the theorem; its proof is `Langlands.localReciprocityGL1`. By the theorem, $\chi \mapsto \chi \circ \mathrm{rec}_K^{-1}$ is a bijection from the characters of $W_K$ to the characters of $K^\times$, and this is the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_1$ (Volume 3, Corollary 2.3.6). We use it without comment: a character of $K^\times$ is also a character of $W_K$, and we use the same letter for both. Under this identification the absolute value $|\cdot|$ is the norm $\|\cdot\|$, a character $\chi$ is unramified if and only if it is trivial on $I_K$, and then $\chi(\Phi) = \chi(\varpi)$. For $s \in \mathbb{C}$ we write $\omega_s$ for the unramified character $w \mapsto \|w\|^s$. So $\omega_s(\Phi) = q^{-s}$, and $\omega_s$ corresponds to $|\cdot|^s$.

Let $L$ be a finite separable extension of $K$ in $\bar K$, with residue degree $f$ and ramification index $e$. Then $G_L$ is an open subgroup of $G_K$, and we want to see $W_L$ inside $W_K$.

**Proposition 2.1.5 (Weil groups of extensions).** The subgroup $W_K \cap G_L$ of $W_K$ is open of index $[L : K]$. As a topological group it is the Weil group $W_L$ of $L$. For $w \in W_L$ the degrees satisfy $d_K(w) = f \, d_L(w)$, so the norm of $W_K$ restricts to the norm of $W_L$.

Lean: `Langlands.WeilGroup.weilEquiv` (with `Langlands.WeilGroup.degree_weilHom` for the degrees)

*Proof.* An element $w \in G_L$ acts on the residue field $\bar k$, which is also the algebraic closure of the residue field $k_L$ of $L$, with $q^f$ elements. If $w$ acts as $\mathrm{Frob}^{n}$, then $n = f \, d_L(w)$ when $w \in W_L$, because the arithmetic Frobenius of $k_L$ is $\mathrm{Frob}^f$. Conversely, if $w \in W_K \cap G_L$, then $w$ fixes $k_L$, so $f$ divides $d_K(w)$ and $w \in W_L$. So $W_L = W_K \cap G_L$ and $d_K = f \, d_L$ on $W_L$. Then $\|w\|_K = q^{f d_L(w)} = q_L^{d_L(w)} = \|w\|_L$. The inertia group $I_L = I_K \cap G_L$ is open in $I_K$ and has its profinite topology in both groups, so the two topologies on $W_L$ agree. The map $W_K / W_L \to G_K / G_L$ is injective, since $W_L = W_K \cap G_L$, and it is surjective by Proposition 2.1.3, since $G_L$ is open. So the index is $[G_K : G_L] = [L : K]$. $\square$

In the Lean library the extension $L$ has its own Weil group, and `Langlands.WeilGroup.weilEquiv` identifies it with the open subgroup of $W_K$ of the elements that fix $L$. Exercise 1 shows that each open subgroup of finite index of $W_K$ is of this form.

### The inertia group

Let $K^{ur}$ be the maximal unramified extension of $K$ in $\bar K$, and let $K^t$ be the union of the fields $K^{ur}(\varpi^{1/m})$ for the integers $m \ge 1$ prime to $p$. Then $K^t$ is the maximal tamely ramified extension of $K$ in $\bar K$, and the *wild inertia group* is $P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K^t)$ [Ser79, Chapter IV]. It is a normal subgroup of $G_K$, and it is a pro-$p$ group: it is the unique Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup of $I_K$.

**Proposition 2.1.6 (tame inertia).** Let $m \ge 1$ be prime to $p$, let $\mu_m \subseteq \bar K$ be the group of $m$-th roots of unity, and let $\varpi^{1/m}$ be a root of $X^m - \varpi$.
1. The map $t_m(\sigma) = \sigma(\varpi^{1/m}) / \varpi^{1/m}$ is a surjective continuous homomorphism $I_K \to \mu_m$. It does not depend on the choice of the root or of the uniformizer.
2. For $w \in G_K$ and $\sigma \in I_K$ one has $t_m(w \sigma w^{-1}) = w(t_m(\sigma))$.
3. The maps $t_m$ induce an isomorphism $I_K / P_K \cong \varprojlim_m \mu_m \cong \prod_{\ell' \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1)$, where $\mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1) = \varprojlim_n \mu_{\ell'^n}$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* The polynomial $X^m - 1$ is separable over $k$, so $\mu_m \subseteq K^{ur}$, and $\sigma \in I_K$ fixes $\mu_m$. Each root of $X^m - \varpi$ is $\zeta \varpi^{1/m}$ with $\zeta \in \mu_m$, so $t_m(\sigma) \in \mu_m$. For $\sigma, \tau \in I_K$ we have $\sigma\tau(\varpi^{1/m}) = \sigma(t_m(\tau) \varpi^{1/m}) = t_m(\tau) t_m(\sigma) \varpi^{1/m}$, because $\sigma$ fixes $t_m(\tau)$. So $t_m$ is a homomorphism. It is continuous, since it is trivial on the open subgroup $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K^{ur}(\varpi^{1/m}))$. A different root is $\zeta \varpi^{1/m}$, and $\sigma(\zeta \varpi^{1/m}) / \zeta \varpi^{1/m} = t_m(\sigma)$. A different uniformizer is $u \varpi$ with $u \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$. The polynomial $X^m - u$ is separable modulo $\varpi$, so $u^{1/m} \in K^{ur}$ is fixed by $\sigma$, and $u^{1/m} \varpi^{1/m}$ gives the same $t_m$. The polynomial $X^m - \varpi$ is an Eisenstein polynomial over $K^{ur}$, so $K^{ur}(\varpi^{1/m})$ has degree $m$ over $K^{ur}$ and $t_m$ is surjective. This proves part 1. For part 2, write $w^{-1}(\varpi^{1/m}) = \zeta \varpi^{1/m}$ with $\zeta \in \mu_m$. Then $\sigma w^{-1}(\varpi^{1/m}) = \zeta t_m(\sigma) \varpi^{1/m}$ and
$$w \sigma w^{-1}(\varpi^{1/m}) = w(t_m(\sigma)) \, w(\zeta \varpi^{1/m}) = w(t_m(\sigma)) \, \varpi^{1/m}.$$
For part 3, the kernel of $t_m$ is $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K^{ur}(\varpi^{1/m}))$, and the intersection of these kernels is $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K^t) = P_K$. The image of $I_K$ in $\varprojlim \mu_m$ is dense, since each $t_m$ is surjective, and it is closed, since $I_K$ is compact. The last isomorphism is the Chinese remainder theorem. $\square$

Part 2 says that a geometric Frobenius acts on the tame quotient by $\sigma \mapsto \sigma^{1/q}$, since $\Phi$ acts on the roots of unity of order prime to $p$ by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{1/q}$. For an arithmetic Frobenius one gets $\sigma \mapsto \sigma^q$. So the tame quotient of $W_K$ is the group generated by $\Phi$ and a procyclic group with the relation $\Phi \sigma \Phi^{-1} = \sigma^{1/q}$. This relation is the origin of the relation between $r$ and $N$ in a Weil-Deligne representation.

**Definition 2.1.7 (tame character).** Let $\ell \ne p$ be a prime. The *tame $\ell$-adic character* is the homomorphism $t_\ell : I_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ with components $t_{\ell^n}$. After the choice of a generator $(\zeta_{\ell^n})_n$ of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ we view it as a homomorphism $t_\ell : I_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. The *cyclotomic character* $\chi_\ell : G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ is defined by $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^{\chi_\ell(\sigma)}$ for the roots of unity $\zeta$ of $\ell$-power order.

**Lemma 2.1.8 (properties of the tame character).** Let $\ell \ne p$.
1. The map $t_\ell$ is a continuous surjective homomorphism, and its kernel contains $P_K$.
2. For $w \in W_K$ and $\sigma \in I_K$ one has $t_\ell(w \sigma w^{-1}) = \chi_\ell(w) \, t_\ell(\sigma) = \|w\| \, t_\ell(\sigma)$.
3. Let $H$ be an open subgroup of $I_K$ and $u : H \to \Gamma$ a continuous homomorphism to a pro-$\ell$ group $\Gamma$. Then $u$ factors through $t_\ell$: there is a continuous homomorphism $\bar u : t_\ell(H) \to \Gamma$ with $u = \bar u \circ t_\ell$ on $H$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Part 1 follows from Proposition 2.1.6, since $t_\ell$ is the projection of $I_K / P_K$ onto the factor $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$. Part 2 follows from Proposition 2.1.6, part 2, and from $\chi_\ell(w) = \|w\|$ for $w \in W_K$ (Volume 0, Remark 3.1.3). For part 3, the group $u(H \cap P_K)$ is a continuous image of a pro-$p$ group, so it is a pro-$p$ subgroup of the pro-$\ell$ group $\Gamma$, and it is trivial. So $u$ factors through $H P_K / P_K$, an open subgroup of $I_K / P_K \cong \prod_{\ell' \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1)$. An open subgroup of this procyclic group is the closure of $n \prod_{\ell'} \mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1)$ for some $n \ge 1$, so it is the product of the groups $\ell'^{v_{\ell'}(n)} \mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1)$. The factors with $\ell' \ne \ell$ are pro-$\ell'$ groups, so $u$ kills them for the same reason as before. So $u$ factors through the projection to the factor $\ell^{v_\ell(n)} \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$, which is $t_\ell$ on $H$. The map $\bar u$ is continuous because $H$ is compact. $\square$

### Representations of the Weil group

**Definition 2.1.9 (representation of $W_K$).** A *representation* of $W_K$ is a continuous homomorphism $r : W_K \to GL(V)$, where $V$ is a complex vector space of finite dimension with its usual topology. It is *unramified* if $r(I_K) = 1$.

Lean: not stated yet

**Proposition 2.1.10 (open kernel).** A homomorphism $r : W_K \to GL(V)$ is continuous if and only if its kernel contains an open subgroup of $I_K$. In this case $r(I_K)$ is a finite group.

Lean: not stated yet (the structure `Langlands.WeilDeligneRep` asks for an open kernel)

*Proof.* The group $GL(V)$ has no small subgroups: let $U$ be the set of $g$ with $\|g - 1\| < 1$ for an operator norm. If a subgroup $H$ lies in $U$ and $g \in H$, then each eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $g$ satisfies $|\lambda^k - 1| < 1$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. This forces $|\lambda| = 1$, and then $\lambda = 1$, since otherwise some power $\lambda^k$ has argument in $[\pi/3, 5\pi/3]$ and $|\lambda^k - 1| \ge 1$. So $g$ is unipotent. If $g \ne 1$, the entries of $g^k = \exp(k \log g)$ grow with $k$, which contradicts $g^k \in U$. So $H = 1$. Now let $r$ be continuous. Then $r^{-1}(U)$ is a neighbourhood of $1$ in $W_K$, so it contains an open subgroup $J$ of the profinite group $I_K$. The subgroup $r(J)$ lies in $U$, so it is trivial. Conversely, if $\ker r$ contains an open subgroup of $W_K$, then $r$ is locally constant, hence continuous. Finally $\ker r \cap I_K$ is open in the compact group $I_K$, so it has finite index and $r(I_K)$ is finite. $\square$

So a representation of $W_K$ is a homomorphism with open kernel. This notion makes sense over any field, and the results of this subsection hold over any algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Let $r$ be a representation of $W_K$. Then $J = \ker r \cap I_K$ is an open normal subgroup of $W_K$, and $F = r(I_K)$ is a finite normal subgroup of $r(W_K)$. The group $r(W_K)$ is generated by $F$ and $r(\Phi)$.

**Proposition 2.1.11 (semisimplicity).** Let $r$ be a representation of $W_K$ on $V$.
1. There is an integer $m \ge 1$ such that $r(\Phi^m)$ is in the center of $r(W_K)$.
2. The following are equivalent: (a) $r$ is semisimple; (b) $r(\Phi)$ is a semisimple endomorphism; (c) $r(w)$ is semisimple for each $w \in W_K$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Conjugation by $r(\Phi)$ is an automorphism of the finite group $F$, so it has finite order $m$. Then $r(\Phi)^m$ commutes with $F$ and with $r(\Phi)$, so it commutes with $r(W_K)$. This is part 1. We use two facts on an endomorphism $g$ of $V$: if $g$ commutes with a semisimple $h$ and is semisimple, then $gh$ is semisimple; and if $g^k$ is semisimple for some $k \ge 1$, then $g$ is semisimple, because the unipotent part $u$ of $g$ satisfies $u^k = 1$ and a unipotent element of finite order is $1$ in characteristic $0$. Suppose (b) and let $w = \Phi^a \sigma$ with $\sigma \in I_K$. Then $w^m = \Phi^{am} \sigma'$ for some $\sigma' \in I_K$, and $r(w)^m = r(\Phi)^{am} r(\sigma')$ is the product of two commuting semisimple elements, since $r(\sigma')$ has finite order. So $r(w)^m$ is semisimple and so is $r(w)$. This proves (c), and (c) implies (b). Suppose (b) again. Then $A = r(\Phi)^m$ is semisimple and central, so $V$ is the direct sum of its eigenspaces $V_\alpha$, and they are stable under $W_K$. Choose $\beta$ with $\beta^m = \alpha$. On $V_\alpha$ the group generated by $F$ and $\beta^{-1} r(\Phi)$ is finite, since $\beta^{-1} r(\Phi)$ normalizes $F$ and its $m$-th power is $1$. A subspace of $V_\alpha$ is stable under $r(W_K)$ if and only if it is stable under this finite group. By Maschke's theorem $V_\alpha$ is semisimple, and (a) follows. Conversely, suppose (a). On each irreducible summand the central element $A$ acts by a scalar, by Schur's lemma. So $A = r(\Phi)^m$ is semisimple, and so is $r(\Phi)$. $\square$

So the field `semisimple` of the Lean structure `Langlands.WeilDeligneRep`, which asks that each $r(w)$ be semisimple, is the condition that $r(\Phi)$ be semisimple. Not every representation of $W_K$ is semisimple. The unramified representation with $r(\Phi) = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is continuous, and it is not semisimple. It does not extend to $G_K$, since $G_K / I_K$ is compact and the powers of $r(\Phi)$ are not bounded.

**Proposition 2.1.12 (irreducible representations).** Let $r$ be an irreducible representation of $W_K$. There are $s \in \mathbb{C}$ and a representation $r_0$ with finite image such that $r \cong r_0 \otimes \omega_s$. The representation $r_0$ extends to a continuous representation of $G_K$, and it factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(L / K)$ for a finite Galois extension $L / K$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Let $m$ be as in Proposition 2.1.11. By Schur's lemma $r(\Phi^m)$ is a scalar $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$. Choose $s$ with $q^{-ms} = c$ and put $r_0 = r \otimes \omega_{-s}$. Then $r_0(\Phi)^m = c \, q^{ms} = 1$. The image $r_0(W_K)$ is generated by the finite group $F$ and by $r_0(\Phi)$, which normalizes $F$ and has finite order, so it is finite. The kernel of $r_0$ is an open normal subgroup of finite index of $W_K$. By Exercise 1 it is $W_K \cap G_L$ for a finite Galois extension $L / K$. By Proposition 2.1.3 the map $W_K / W_L \to G_K / G_L = \mathrm{Gal}(L / K)$ is an isomorphism, so $r_0$ factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(L / K)$. $\square$

So the irreducible representations of $W_K$ are the Artin representations of Volume 3, Chapter 6, twisted by the characters $\omega_s$.

**Example 2.1.13 (induced representations of dimension 2).** Let $L / K$ be a separable quadratic extension, let $\tau \in W_K \setminus W_L$, and let $\theta$ be a character of $W_L$, that is, a character of $L^\times$. Let $\theta^\tau(w) = \theta(\tau^{-1} w \tau)$ for $w \in W_L$. By Mackey's formula the restriction of $\mathrm{Ind}_{W_L}^{W_K} \theta$ to $W_L$ is $\theta \oplus \theta^\tau$. By Frobenius reciprocity, $\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Ind}\, \theta) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{W_L}(\theta, \theta \oplus \theta^\tau)$. So $\mathrm{Ind}\, \theta$ is irreducible if and only if $\theta^\tau \ne \theta$. Under reciprocity for $L$, $\theta^\tau$ is the character $x \mapsto \theta(\bar x)$ of $L^\times$, where $x \mapsto \bar x$ is the nontrivial automorphism of $L / K$. So $\mathrm{Ind}\, \theta$ is irreducible if and only if $\theta$ does not factor through the norm $N_{L/K}$. In Chapter 3 these dihedral representations correspond to supercuspidal representations of $GL_2(K)$. Example 2.1.14 gives the case where $L / K$ is unramified and $\theta$ is tamely ramified.

**Example 2.1.14 (characters of the tame quotient).** Let $\theta$ be a character of $k_L^\times$, where $L$ is the unramified extension of degree $n$ of $K$ and $k_L$ has $q^n$ elements. By Proposition 2.1.6 the character $t_{q^n - 1}$ maps $I_K$ onto $\mu_{q^n - 1}$, which the reduction map identifies with $k_L^\times$. So $\theta \circ t_{q^n - 1}$ is a character of $I_K$ of order dividing $q^n - 1$. It extends to a character of $W_L$ by $\Phi^n \mapsto 1$. By part 2 of Proposition 2.1.6, its conjugate by $\Phi$ is $\theta^{q}$ composed with the same map, up to the identification. So $\mathrm{Ind}_{W_L}^{W_K}$ of this character is irreducible if and only if $\theta, \theta^q, \dots, \theta^{q^{n-1}}$ are distinct. These are the tamely ramified irreducible representations of dimension $n$ with trivial wild inertia. For $n = 2$ they correspond to the supercuspidal representations of depth zero of Example 1.4.9.
