# Weil-Deligne representations

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field with residue field of $q$ elements and characteristic $p$, and let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $K$ with split multiplicative reduction. Fix a prime $\ell \ne p$. The Tate uniformization $E(\bar K) = \bar K^\times / q_E^{\mathbb{Z}}$ gives a basis $e_1, e_2$ of the Tate module $V_\ell(E)$ in which an element $\sigma$ of the inertia group acts by the matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & v(q_E) t_\ell(\sigma) \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $t_\ell : I_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is the tame character. The image of inertia is infinite, and the representation is not semisimple. A continuous representation of the Weil group on a complex vector space cannot behave in this way: it is trivial on an open subgroup of the inertia group. So the $\ell$-adic representations that arise in arithmetic geometry are not complex representations of $W_K$, and they cannot be compared with representations of $GL_n(K)$ as they stand.

Grothendieck's monodromy theorem says that the example shows the only thing that can go wrong. On an open subgroup of the inertia group each $\ell$-adic representation has the form $\sigma \mapsto \exp(t_\ell(\sigma) N)$ for a nilpotent endomorphism $N$. Deligne observed that the pair $(r, N)$, where $r$ is the representation corrected by $\exp(-t_\ell N)$, has a meaning that does not depend on $\ell$. The representation $r$ is trivial on an open subgroup of inertia, $N$ is a nilpotent matrix, and the two satisfy one relation. Such a pair can be moved from $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$ to $\mathbb{C}$. For the Tate curve the pair is the representation $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$ of Section 2.2, and in Chapter 3 it corresponds to a twist of the Steinberg representation of Chapter 1. The local Langlands correspondence for $GL_n$ is a bijection between the irreducible representations of $GL_n(K)$ and the Frobenius-semisimple Weil-Deligne representations of dimension $n$, and both sides carry $L$-factors and $\varepsilon$-factors. This chapter builds the Galois side and the factors of both sides.

Section 2.1 recalls the Weil group from Volume 3, Chapter 1, describes the tame quotient of the inertia group and studies the representations of $W_K$. Section 2.2 defines Weil-Deligne representations, the representations $\mathrm{Sp}(n)$ and the Frobenius-semisimplification, and it classifies the Frobenius-semisimple representations. It also compares them with the $L$-parameters of Volume 2, Section 5.6. Section 2.3 proves Grothendieck's monodromy theorem and the equivalence between $\ell$-adic representations of $W_K$ and Weil-Deligne representations over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$. Section 2.4 defines the $L$-factor, the conductor and the $\varepsilon$-factor of a Weil-Deligne representation. Section 2.5 defines the standard $L$-factor of Godement and Jacquet of a representation of $GL_n(K)$ and computes it for the unramified, Steinberg and supercuspidal representations. Section 2.6 has the exercises and the notes.

We follow Tate's article "Number theoretic background" [Tat79], Sections 1 to 4, and Deligne's paper "Les constantes des équations fonctionnelles des fonctions $L$" (Antwerp II, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 349, 1973). For $GL_2$ we follow Chapters 6 and 7 of [BH06]. For the zeta integrals we follow the book of Godement and Jacquet (Lecture Notes in Mathematics 260, 1972). We use the notation of Volume 0, Section 3.1, and its table of conventions. In particular $\Phi$ is a geometric Frobenius, the norm on $W_K$ is $\|w\| = q^{d(w)}$, so that $\|\Phi\| = q^{-1}$, and a Weil-Deligne representation satisfies $r(w) N r(w)^{-1} = \|w\| N$. As in Chapter 1 we write $L(s, V)$ and $\varepsilon(s, V, \psi)$ with the variable $s$ first. Volume 3 writes $L(\chi, s)$ and $\varepsilon(\chi, s, \psi)$ for the same functions. Unless a statement says otherwise, the additive character $\psi$ of $K$ has conductor $\mathcal{O}_K$, as in Volume 3, Section 4.3. The Lean project has the Weil group in `Langlands/WeilGroup/`, the Weil-Deligne representations and their $L$-factors in `Langlands/Statements/WeilDeligne.lean`, and the zeta integrals of Godement and Jacquet in `Langlands/Statements/GodementJacquet.lean`. It has no $\varepsilon$-factors yet.
