## Exercises

1. Show that the commutator subgroup of $GL_n(K)$ is $SL_n(K)$. Deduce that each smooth character of $GL_n(K)$ is $\chi \circ \det$ for a unique smooth character $\chi$ of $K^\times$.
2. Let $G$ be a compact $\ell$-group. Show that each smooth representation of $G$ is a direct sum of irreducible representations of finite dimension, and that an irreducible smooth representation of $G$ factors through a finite quotient $G / J$.
3. Let $V = C_c^\infty(K)$ with the action of $K$ by translation. Show that $V$ is smooth, that $V^K = 0$ since the group $K$ is not compact, and that the functor $V \mapsto V^K$ is not exact on the smooth representations of $K$.
4. Let $\pi$ be an admissible representation of $G$ and let $J \subseteq J'$ be compact open subgroups. Show that $\dim V^{J'} \le \dim V^J$, and that $\pi \ne 0$ is irreducible if and only if, for each $J$ in a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$, the space $V^J$ is zero or a simple $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-module.
5. Let $T_\varpi$ and $R_\varpi$ be as in Section 1.2. Prove the relation $T_\varpi * T_\varpi = 1_{K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^2, 1) K_0} + (q + 1) R_\varpi$ in $\mathcal{H}(GL_2(K), K_0)$. Deduce the formal identity $\sum_{m \ge 0} T_{\varpi^m} X^m = (1 - T_\varpi X + q R_\varpi X^2)^{-1}$, where $T_{\varpi^m}$ is the sum of the characteristic functions of the double cosets of the matrices in $M_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ with determinant of valuation $m$. Compare with the Euler factor of a Hecke eigenform in Volume 4.
6. Compute the Satake transform of $1_{K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1, \dots, 1) K_0}$ in $GL_n(K)$. Show that it is $q^{(n-1)/2} (X_1 + \dots + X_n)$.
7. Let $P = MN$ be the parabolic subgroup of type $(1, n - 1)$ of $GL_n(K)$. Show that $\delta_P(\mathrm{diag}(a, m)) = |a|^{n-1} |\det m|^{-1}$.
8. Show that the trivial representation of $GL_2(K)$ is a quotient of $|\cdot|^{1/2} \times |\cdot|^{-1/2}$ by writing the invariant linear form $f \mapsto \int_{K_0} f(k) \, dk$.
9. Let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be unramified characters of $K^\times$ with $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|^{-1}$. Show that the spherical vector $f_0$ of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ spans the trivial subrepresentation, up to the twist $\eta \circ \det$. Show that the Steinberg quotient has no $K_0$-fixed vector, in two ways: with Proposition 1.1.4, and with the eigenvalue of $T_\varpi$.
10. Let $I$ be the Iwahori subgroup of $GL_2(K)$. Give $I$ the volume $1$. Show that the Iwahori-Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, I)$ is generated by $T_s = 1_{I s I}$, $T_\omega = 1_{I \omega I}$ and $T_\omega^{-1} = 1_{I \omega^{-1} I}$, with $s = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\omega = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \varpi & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and that $(T_s - q)(T_s + 1) = 0$. Find the action of $T_s$ on the line $\mathrm{St}^I$.
11. Let $\pi$ be an irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$ with central character $\omega_\pi$. Show that $\pi^\vee \cong \pi \otimes \omega_\pi^{-1}$ for the principal series and the Steinberg twists, by computing Jacquet modules.
12. Show that the subgroup $J = K^\times GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ is the normalizer of $GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ in $GL_2(K)$. Let $J' = \langle \omega \rangle I$, with $\omega$ as in Exercise 10. Show that $J'$ is the normalizer of $I$, that it is compact modulo $K^\times$, and that $J$ and $J'$ are the two maximal subgroups of $GL_2(K)$ that are compact modulo the center, up to conjugation.
13. Let $\rho$ be an irreducible supercuspidal representation of $GL_n(K)$. Show that the set of unramified characters $\chi$ of $K^\times$ with $\rho \otimes \chi \cong \rho$ is a finite cyclic group, of order dividing $n$. For $n = 2$ and $\rho$ of depth zero as in Example 1.4.9, show that this group has order $2$.
14. List the multisegments with supercuspidal support $\{1, \nu, \nu\}$ for $GL_3(K)$, where $\nu = |\cdot|$. Show that $1 \times \nu \times \nu$ has exactly two irreducible subquotients, and identify them.
15. Let $\psi$ be a nontrivial character of $K$ and $\pi$ an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$. Show that the Kirillov models for $\psi$ and $\psi_b$ are related by $\xi \mapsto \xi(b \, \cdot)$.
16. Let $\pi = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$ with $\chi_1, \chi_2$ unramified and $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \ne |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$, and let $\psi$ have conductor $\mathcal{O}_K$. Show that the spherical Whittaker function, normalized by $W(1) = 1$, satisfies $W(\mathrm{diag}(\varpi^m, 1)) = q^{-m/2} \frac{\alpha_1^{m+1} - \alpha_2^{m+1}}{\alpha_1 - \alpha_2}$ for $m \ge 0$, with $\alpha_i = \chi_i(\varpi)$ (read as a limit when $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2$), and that it vanishes for $m < 0$. This is the formula of Shintani and of Casselman and Shalika for $n = 2$.
17. Let $p$ be a prime, let $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ and let $K_0 = GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Write each of the matrices $g_1 = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} p & 1 \\ 1 & p \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $g_2 = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ p & p^{-1} \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ as $bk$ with $b$ upper triangular and $k \in K_0$, with the column operations of the proof of the Iwasawa decomposition in Section 1.3. Check that $|d| = \max(|r|, |s|)$ for the diagonal $(a, d)$ of $b$ and the lower row $(r, s)$ of $g_i$. Find all the decompositions of $g_1$. For unramified characters $\chi_1, \chi_2$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$, compute $f_0(g_2)$, where $f_0$ is the vector of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ fixed by $K_0$ with $f_0(1) = 1$.
18. Let $k = \mathbb{F}_q$ be a finite field, $G = GL_2(k)$, and $B$, $N$ and $w$ as in Proposition 1.3.9. Show that $|BwN| = |B| \, |N|$, and deduce $|G| = (q^2 - 1)(q^2 - q)$ from $G = B \sqcup BwN$. Show that $BwB = BwN$, and that the set $B \backslash G$ has $q + 1$ elements, one in $B$ and $q$ in the big cell.
19. Let $V = C_c^\infty(K)$, with $N \cong K$ acting by translation, $(n(y) \phi)(x) = \phi(x + y)$, and let $\phi = 1_{\mathfrak{p}} - q^{-1} 1_{\mathcal{O}_K}$. Write $\phi$ explicitly as a linear combination of vectors $n(y) \phi' - \phi'$. For $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, compute the average $A_{\mathfrak{p}^k}(\phi)$ of Section 1.3 over the compact open subgroup $n(\mathfrak{p}^k)$, and show that it vanishes if and only if $k \le 0$.
20. Let $\chi = |\cdot|^{-1/2} \otimes |\cdot|^{1/2}$, so that $V = |\cdot|^{-1/2} \times |\cdot|^{1/2}$ is the space of the locally constant functions on $B \backslash G = \mathbb{P}^1(K)$, and let $V_w$ be as in Proposition 1.3.10. Show that the composite $V_w \to V \to \mathrm{St}$ is an isomorphism of representations of $B$. Deduce that the restriction of $\mathrm{St}$ to $B$ is $C_c^\infty(K)$ with $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & y \\ 0 & d \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ acting by $\phi \mapsto \phi((dx + y)/a)$, and recover $r_B(\mathrm{St}) = |\cdot|^{1/2} \otimes |\cdot|^{-1/2}$ from Theorem 1.3.11.
21. Show that $SL_2(K)$ is generated by the groups $N = \{n(x)\}$ and $\bar N = \{\bar n(y)\}$. Hint: for $g = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in SL_2(K)$ with $\gamma \ne 0$, find $x$ and $y$ with $g = n(x) \bar n(\gamma) n(y)$. Deduce that the normal subgroup of $GL_2(K)$ generated by $N$ is $SL_2(K)$. This is the group theory of Step 4 of the second proof of Theorem 1.5.1.
22. Let $r \ge 1$. Show that the product map $(N \cap K_r) \times (T \cap K_r) \times (\bar N \cap K_r) \to K_r$ is a homeomorphism, and deduce $K_r = (\bar N \cap K_r)(T \cap K_r)(N \cap K_r)$. Show that $w = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in K_0$ is not in $(N \cap K_0)(T \cap K_0)(\bar N \cap K_0)$, so Lemma 1.5.7 fails for $K_0$. Let $I$ be the Iwahori subgroup, the set of the $g \in K_0$ whose lower left entry is in $\mathfrak{p}$. Show that $I = (N \cap K_0)(T \cap K_0)(\bar N \cap K_1)$, and that each element of $I$ has a unique such factorization.
23. Let $\sigma$ be the representation of $T$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ given by $\sigma(\mathrm{diag}(a, d)) = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \mathrm{ord}(a) - \mathrm{ord}(d) \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Show that $\sigma$ is a smooth representation, that it is not a direct sum of characters, and that it has exactly one quotient that is a character. This shows why the proof of Corollary 1.5.9 uses a common eigenvector of the transposes on the dual space, and not an eigenvector in $V_N$.

## Notes and history

The representation theory of $p$-adic groups started with the work of Mautner, who studied spherical functions in 1958 and constructed the first supercuspidal representations of $PGL_2$ by compact induction in 1964, and of Bruhat, who studied the induced representations of $p$-adic groups in 1961. Gelfand and Graev studied the representations of $GL_2(K)$ and $SL_2(K)$ in the early 1960s, in the framework of the representations of the adele group. The book of Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] gave the complete local theory of $GL_2$: the Kirillov model, the classification, the local factors and the local functional equation, and the local correspondence with the quaternion algebra. The book [BH06] presents the classification of Section 1.5 in the form we use.

The theory for general reductive groups was founded by Harish-Chandra, who introduced the cusp forms on $p$-adic groups and proved the admissibility of the supercuspidal representations and the local integrability of their characters. Jacquet introduced the Jacquet module and proved the subrepresentation theorem of Section 1.4. Howe and Casselman developed the theory of admissible representations further, and Casselman's notes "Introduction to the theory of admissible representations of $p$-adic reductive groups", which circulated from 1974, became the standard reference for Jacquet modules and their pairings. Bernstein and Zelevinsky wrote two long papers, "Representations of the group $GL(n, F)$ where $F$ is a non-archimedean local field" (Russian Mathematical Surveys, 1976) and "Induced representations of reductive $p$-adic groups I" (Annales scientifiques de l'École normale supérieure, 1977). They proved the geometric lemma, the reducibility criterion of Theorem 1.6.1 and the classification of the essentially square integrable representations. Zelevinsky finished the classification in "Induced representations of reductive $p$-adic groups II" (1980), with the multisegments and the representations $Z(\mathbf{a})$. Bernstein's decomposition is in his joint work with Deligne on the Bernstein center (1984). His second adjointness theorem came later, and it is proved in his lectures at Harvard. His lecture notes "Representations of $p$-adic groups", written up by Rumelhart, are the model for Sections 1.1 to 1.4. The book of Renard on the representations of $p$-adic reductive groups gives complete proofs of the theorems of Sections 1.3 and 1.4 for all reductive groups.

The uniqueness of Whittaker models for $GL_n$ is due to Gelfand and Kazhdan (1975) and to Shalika (1974). Rodier extended it to split reductive groups. The construction of all supercuspidal representations of $GL_2$ by compact induction is due to Kutzko (1978), with earlier work of Casselman, of Shalika and of Tanaka for special cases. Kutzko used it to prove the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_2$ in [Kut80], see Chapter 3. Bushnell and Kutzko extended the construction to $GL_n$ in their book "The admissible dual of $GL(N)$ via compact open subgroups" (1993). Harris and Taylor [HT01] and Henniart [Hen00] use this classification and the classification of Bernstein and Zelevinsky to reduce the local Langlands correspondence to the supercuspidal representations.

The Lean project has the vocabulary of this chapter in `Langlands/Statements/Smooth.lean`: smooth representations, admissibility, irreducibility, the contragredient, twists, matrix coefficients, central characters, supercuspidality in the form of compact support modulo the center, the principal series of $GL_2$, and the pointwise Harish-Chandra character. These are definitions, with two small proved lemmas on characters, and none is a node of the map. The file `Langlands/Smooth/Induction.lean` adds the smooth vectors, quotients and direct sums, the admissibility of subrepresentations, smooth induction with Frobenius reciprocity, the Jacquet module with its universal property, and the subgroups $B$, $N$ and $T$ of $GL_2$ with the unnormalized principal series. Its induction and its Jacquet module are not normalized, and they are defined for arbitrary subgroups. The file `Langlands/Smooth/PrincipalSeries.lean` adds the modulus character $\delta_B$ of the Borel subgroup of $GL_2$ and its square root (`Langlands.GL2.modulus`, `Langlands.GL2.sqrtModulus`), the normalized principal series `Langlands.normPrincipalSeries` with its comparison to the two older forms (`Langlands.normPrincipalSeries_eq`, `Langlands.normPrincipalSeries_iso`), the compactness of $GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ and the Iwasawa decomposition (`Langlands.GL2.isCompact_maxCompact`, `Langlands.GL2.iwasawa`, `Langlands.SmoothRep.ind_borel_ext`), the admissibility of a representation induced from a cocompact subgroup and of the principal series (`Langlands.SmoothRep.ind_isAdmissible`, `Langlands.principalSeries_isAdmissible`), the surjection from the Jacquet module of the principal series onto $\chi_1 \otimes \chi_2$ (`Langlands.principalSeriesJacquetEval_surjective`), and the right exactness of the Jacquet functor (`Langlands.SmoothRep.jacquetMap_exact`). The file `Langlands/Smooth/Bruhat.lean` adds the Bruhat decomposition of $GL_2$ with the uniqueness on the big cell (`Langlands.GL2.bruhat`, `Langlands.GL2.bigCell_unique`), the description of $V(N)$ by averages of Proposition 1.3.5 (`Langlands.SmoothRep.mem_jacquetKer_iff`), the full exactness of the Jacquet functor (`Langlands.SmoothRep.jacquetMap_shortExact`, `Langlands.SmoothRep.jacquetMap_unipotent_shortExact`), and the subspace $V_w$ of Proposition 1.3.10 (`Langlands.principalSeriesBigCell`). The file `Langlands/Smooth/JacquetPrincipal.lean` proves Theorem 1.3.11 for the unnormalized principal series: the exact sequence $0 \to V_w \to V \to \mathbb{C} \to 0$, the exact sequence of the Jacquet modules with $(V_w)_N$ identified with a character of $T$, and the dimension two of the Jacquet module (`Langlands.principalSeriesBigCell_shortExact`, `Langlands.principalSeriesJacquet_shortExact`, `Langlands.bigCellJacquet_iso`, `Langlands.finrank_principalSeriesJacquet`). Its proof uses coset sums in place of a Haar measure on $K$. The surjectivity of the map from $V_w$ to $C_c^\infty(K)$ and the geometric lemma for $GL_n$ are not in Lean yet. Seven files in `Langlands/Smooth` prove most of Section 1.5. The files `IrreducibilityCriterion.lean`, `ReducibleCase.lean` and `ReducibleQuotient.lean` prove the irreducibility criterion of Theorem 1.5.1. The file `Steinberg.lean` treats the case $\chi_2 = \chi_1 |\cdot|$, with the unique $G$-stable subspace and the irreducibility of the Steinberg quotient. The file `LengthTwo.lean` treats the case $\chi_1 = \chi_2 |\cdot|$, with the Steinberg subrepresentation. The file `Classification.lean` proves the classification for the irreducible representations with a nonzero Jacquet module of finite dimension, and the criterion for the isomorphism of two principal series. The file `JacquetFinite.lean` proves the Iwahori factorization of Lemma 1.5.7, Jacquet's lemma of Theorem 1.5.8 and Corollary 1.5.9. The Lean lines of Section 1.5 give the names and the extra hypotheses. In particular, the classification in Lean is for the representations with a nonzero Jacquet module, since the equivalence of the two definitions of supercuspidality is not in Lean yet. The Whittaker functionals, the generic representations and the Whittaker models of Section 1.7 are in `Langlands/Local/LanglandsFamilyPairs.lean` (`Langlands.IsWhittakerFunctional`, `Langlands.IsGeneric`, `Langlands.whittakerModel`), and their uniqueness is not in Lean yet.
