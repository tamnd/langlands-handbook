## Exercises

1. Show that the commutator subgroup of $GL_n(K)$ is $SL_n(K)$. Deduce that each smooth character of $GL_n(K)$ is $\chi \circ \det$ for a unique smooth character $\chi$ of $K^\times$.
2. Let $G$ be a compact $\ell$-group. Show that each smooth representation of $G$ is a direct sum of irreducible representations of finite dimension, and that an irreducible smooth representation of $G$ factors through a finite quotient $G / J$.
3. Let $V = C_c^\infty(K)$ with the action of $K$ by translation. Show that $V$ is smooth, that $V^K = 0$ since the group $K$ is not compact, and that the functor $V \mapsto V^K$ is not exact on the smooth representations of $K$.
4. Let $\pi$ be an admissible representation of $G$ and let $J \subseteq J'$ be compact open subgroups. Show that $\dim V^{J'} \le \dim V^J$, and that $\pi$ is irreducible if and only if $V^J$ is a simple $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-module for each $J$ in a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$.
5. Let $T_\varpi$ and $R_\varpi$ be as in Section 1.2. Prove the relation $T_\varpi * T_\varpi = 1_{K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^2, 1) K_0} + (q + 1) R_\varpi$ in $\mathcal{H}(GL_2(K), K_0)$. Deduce the formal identity $\sum_{m \ge 0} T_{\varpi^m} X^m = (1 - T_\varpi X + q R_\varpi X^2)^{-1}$, where $T_{\varpi^m}$ is the sum of the characteristic functions of the double cosets of the matrices in $M_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ with determinant of valuation $m$. Compare with the Euler factor of a Hecke eigenform in Volume 4.
6. Compute the Satake transform of $1_{K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1, \dots, 1) K_0}$ in $GL_n(K)$. Show that it is $q^{(n-1)/2} (X_1 + \dots + X_n)$.
7. Let $P = MN$ be the parabolic subgroup of type $(1, n - 1)$ of $GL_n(K)$. Show that $\delta_P(\mathrm{diag}(a, m)) = |a|^{n-1} |\det m|^{-1}$.
8. Show that the trivial representation of $GL_2(K)$ is a quotient of $|\cdot|^{1/2} \times |\cdot|^{-1/2}$ by writing the invariant linear form $f \mapsto \int_{K_0} f(k) \, dk$.
9. Let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be unramified characters of $K^\times$ with $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} = |\cdot|^{-1}$. Show that the spherical vector $f_0$ of $\chi_1 \times \chi_2$ spans the trivial subrepresentation, up to the twist $\eta \circ \det$. Show that the Steinberg quotient has no $K_0$-fixed vector, in two ways: with Proposition 1.1.4, and with the eigenvalue of $T_\varpi$.
10. Let $I$ be the Iwahori subgroup of $GL_2(K)$. Show that the Iwahori-Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, I)$ is generated by $T_s = 1_{I s I}$ and $T_\omega = 1_{I \omega I}$, with $s = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\omega = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \varpi & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and that $(T_s - q)(T_s + 1) = 0$. Find the action of $T_s$ on the line $\mathrm{St}^I$.
11. Let $\pi$ be an irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$ with central character $\omega_\pi$. Show that $\pi^\vee \cong \pi \otimes \omega_\pi^{-1}$ for the principal series and the Steinberg twists, by computing Jacquet modules.
12. Show that the subgroup $J = K^\times GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ is the normalizer of $GL_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ in $GL_2(K)$. Let $J' = \langle \omega \rangle I$, with $\omega$ as in Exercise 10. Show that $J'$ is the normalizer of $I$, that it is compact modulo $K^\times$, and that $J$ and $J'$ are the two maximal subgroups of $GL_2(K)$ that are compact modulo the center, up to conjugation.
13. Let $\rho$ be an irreducible supercuspidal representation of $GL_n(K)$. Show that the set of unramified characters $\chi$ of $K^\times$ with $\rho \otimes \chi \cong \rho$ is a finite cyclic group, of order dividing $n$. For $n = 2$ and $\rho$ of depth zero as in Example 1.4.9, show that this group has order $2$.
14. List the multisegments with supercuspidal support $\{1, \nu, \nu\}$ for $GL_3(K)$, where $\nu = |\cdot|$. Show that $1 \times \nu \times \nu$ has exactly two irreducible subquotients, and identify them.
15. Let $\psi$ be a nontrivial character of $K$ and $\pi$ an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation of $GL_2(K)$. Show that the Kirillov models for $\psi$ and $\psi_b$ are related by $\xi \mapsto \xi(b \, \cdot)$.
16. Let $\pi = \chi_1 \times \chi_2$ with $\chi_1, \chi_2$ unramified and $\chi_1 \chi_2^{-1} \ne |\cdot|^{\pm 1}$, and let $\psi$ have conductor $\mathcal{O}_K$. Show that the spherical Whittaker function satisfies $W(\mathrm{diag}(\varpi^m, 1)) = q^{-m/2} \frac{\alpha_1^{m+1} - \alpha_2^{m+1}}{\alpha_1 - \alpha_2}$ for $m \ge 0$, with $\alpha_i = \chi_i(\varpi)$, and that it vanishes for $m < 0$. This is the formula of Shintani and of Casselman and Shalika for $n = 2$.

## Notes and history

The representation theory of $p$-adic groups started with the work of Mautner in 1958, who constructed the first supercuspidal representations of $PGL_2$ by compact induction, and of Bruhat in his thesis of 1956, who studied the principal series. Gelfand and Graev studied the representations of $GL_2(K)$ and $SL_2(K)$ in the early 1960s, in the framework of the representations of the adele group. The book of Jacquet and Langlands [JL70] gave the complete local theory of $GL_2$: the Kirillov model, the classification, the local factors and the local functional equation, and the local correspondence with the quaternion algebra. The book [BH06] presents the classification of Section 1.5 in the form we use.

The theory for general reductive groups was founded by Harish-Chandra, who introduced the cusp forms on $p$-adic groups and proved the admissibility of the supercuspidal representations and the local integrability of their characters. Jacquet introduced the Jacquet module and proved the subrepresentation theorem of Section 1.4. Howe and Casselman developed the theory of admissible representations further, and Casselman's notes "Introduction to the theory of admissible representations of $p$-adic reductive groups", which circulated from 1974, became the standard reference for Jacquet modules and their pairings. Bernstein and Zelevinsky wrote two long papers, "Representations of the group $GL(n, F)$ where $F$ is a non-archimedean local field" (Russian Mathematical Surveys, 1976) and "Induced representations of reductive $p$-adic groups I" (Annales scientifiques de l'École normale supérieure, 1977). They proved the geometric lemma, the reducibility criterion of Theorem 1.6.1 and the classification of the essentially square integrable representations. Zelevinsky finished the classification in "Induced representations of reductive $p$-adic groups II" (1980), with the multisegments and the representations $Z(\mathbf{a})$. Bernstein's decomposition and his second adjointness theorem date from the early 1980s, in his joint work with Deligne on the Bernstein center (1984) and in his lectures at Harvard. His lecture notes "Representations of $p$-adic groups", written up by Rumelhart, are the model for Sections 1.1 to 1.4. The book of Renard on the representations of $p$-adic reductive groups gives complete proofs of the theorems of Sections 1.3 and 1.4 for all reductive groups.

The uniqueness of Whittaker models for $GL_n$ is due to Gelfand and Kazhdan (1975) and to Shalika (1974). Rodier extended it to all quasi-split groups. The construction of all supercuspidal representations of $GL_2$ by compact induction is due to Kutzko [Kut80], with earlier work of Casselman, of Shalika and of Tanaka for special cases. Kutzko used it to prove the local Langlands correspondence for $GL_2$, see Chapter 3. Bushnell and Kutzko extended the construction to $GL_n$ in their book "The admissible dual of $GL(N)$ via compact open subgroups" (1993). Harris and Taylor [HT01] and Henniart [Hen00] use this classification and the classification of Bernstein and Zelevinsky to reduce the local Langlands correspondence to the supercuspidal representations.

The Lean project has the vocabulary of this chapter in `Langlands/Statements/Smooth.lean`: smooth representations, admissibility, irreducibility, the contragredient, twists, matrix coefficients, central characters, supercuspidality in the form of compact support modulo the center, the principal series of $GL_2$, and the pointwise Harish-Chandra character. These are definitions, with two small proved lemmas on characters, and none is a node of the map. The file `Langlands/Smooth/Induction.lean` adds the smooth vectors, quotients and direct sums, the admissibility of subrepresentations, smooth induction with Frobenius reciprocity, the Jacquet module with its universal property, and the subgroups $B$, $N$ and $T$ of $GL_2$ with the unnormalized principal series. Its induction and its Jacquet module are not normalized, and they are defined for arbitrary subgroups. The other theorems of this chapter are not stated in the Lean project yet. The docstring of `Langlands.principalSeries` records the reducibility of Theorem 1.5.1 as a comment.
