# Smooth representations

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field and let $G = GL_2(K)$. The group $G$ acts on the space $V$ of locally constant functions $f : G \to \mathbb{C}$ with $f(bg) = f(g)$ for each upper triangular matrix $b$. This space is very large. It has no topology that we need, and the group acts on it with no continuity condition beyond one simple fact: each $f \in V$ is fixed by an open subgroup of $G$. This is the condition of smoothness. The space $V$ has a subspace of dimension one, the constant functions. The quotient is irreducible, and it is the Steinberg representation. The group $G$ has many representations of this kind, and the aim of this chapter is to show how to classify them.

The main tools are two functors. Parabolic induction makes a representation of $G$ from a representation of a Levi subgroup $M$, such as the diagonal torus of $GL_2$. The Jacquet module goes back from $G$ to $M$, and it is adjoint to induction. A representation whose Jacquet modules are all zero is supercuspidal. The supercuspidal representations are the building blocks: each irreducible representation is a subrepresentation of a representation induced from a supercuspidal one. For $GL_n$, Bernstein and Zelevinsky gave a complete classification of the irreducible representations in terms of the supercuspidal ones. The local Langlands correspondence of Chapter 3 is compatible with this classification. So the correspondence for $GL_n$ comes down to a bijection between the supercuspidal representations and the irreducible representations of the Weil group.

Section 1.1 defines smooth and admissible representations and proves Schur's lemma. Section 1.2 defines the Hecke algebra and shows how the representations with a fixed vector under a compact open subgroup $J$ correspond to modules over the Hecke algebra of $J$. Section 1.3 defines parabolic induction and Jacquet modules and proves their basic properties. Section 1.4 studies supercuspidal representations. Section 1.5 treats the principal series of $GL_2$ and the classification of the irreducible representations of $GL_2(K)$. Section 1.6 gives the classification of Bernstein and Zelevinsky in outline. Section 1.7 defines Whittaker models and proves their uniqueness for $GL_2$. The exercises and the notes close the chapter.

We follow the notes of Bernstein for the general theory and the first chapters of [BH06] for $GL_2$. The notation is that of Volume 0, Section 3.1: $\mathcal{O}_K$ is the ring of integers, $\mathfrak{p}_K$ its maximal ideal, $\varpi$ a uniformizer and $k$ the residue field, with $q$ elements. The absolute value has $|\varpi| = q^{-1}$. All representations are on complex vector spaces. The Lean project has the basic vocabulary of this chapter in the file `Langlands/Statements/Smooth.lean`. Its declarations are definitions and a few small lemmas. None of them is a node of the map.
