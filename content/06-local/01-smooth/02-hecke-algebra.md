## The Hecke algebra

A representation of a finite group is the same thing as a module over its group algebra. For an $\ell$-group the group algebra is replaced by the algebra of locally constant functions with compact support, under convolution. This algebra has no unit, but it has many idempotents, one for each compact open subgroup. The idempotents cut the category of smooth representations into pieces that are module categories over algebras with a unit. The most important piece is the one of the spherical representations of $GL_n(K)$, and the Satake isomorphism describes its algebra.

### The Hecke algebra and its modules

Let $G$ be a unimodular $\ell$-group with a Haar measure $dg$.

**Definition 1.2.1 (Hecke algebra).** The *Hecke algebra* $\mathcal{H}(G)$ is the space $C_c^\infty(G)$ of locally constant functions $f : G \to \mathbb{C}$ with compact support, with the convolution product
$$(f_1 * f_2)(g) = \int_G f_1(h) f_2(h^{-1} g) \, dh.$$
For a compact open subgroup $J$ let $e_J = \mathrm{vol}(J)^{-1} 1_J$. The *Hecke algebra of $J$* is $\mathcal{H}(G, J) = e_J * \mathcal{H}(G) * e_J$, the space of the functions in $\mathcal{H}(G)$ that are invariant under $J$ on the left and on the right.

Lean: not stated yet

The convolution of two functions in $\mathcal{H}(G)$ is in $\mathcal{H}(G)$. Each $e_J$ is an idempotent, and $e_J$ is the unit of $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$. The algebra $\mathcal{H}(G)$ is the union of the subalgebras $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$. It has a unit only when $G$ is discrete. The space $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$ has the basis of the characteristic functions $1_{JgJ}$ of the double cosets.

Let $(\pi, V)$ be a smooth representation. For $f \in \mathcal{H}(G)$ and $v \in V$ put
$$\pi(f) v = \int_G f(g) \pi(g) v \, dg.$$
The integrand is locally constant with compact support, so the integral is a finite sum. One checks that $\pi(f_1 * f_2) = \pi(f_1) \pi(f_2)$, and that $\pi(e_J)$ is the projection $e_J$ of Proposition 1.1.4. So $V$ is an $\mathcal{H}(G)$-module. It is *nondegenerate*: each $v \in V$ is $\pi(f) v'$ for some $f$ and $v'$, since $v = \pi(e_J) v$ for $J$ small.

**Proposition 1.2.2 (smooth representations as modules).** The functor $(\pi, V) \mapsto V$ is an equivalence from the category of smooth representations of $G$ to the category of nondegenerate $\mathcal{H}(G)$-modules. A subspace of $V$ is $G$-stable if and only if it is an $\mathcal{H}(G)$-submodule.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Let $M$ be a nondegenerate module. Each $m \in M$ is a finite sum $\sum f_i m_i$, and each $f_i$ is invariant on the left under some compact open subgroup. So $m = e_J m$ for some $J$. For $g \in G$ define $g \cdot m = (\lambda_g e_J) m$, where $\lambda_g e_J$ is the left translate $x \mapsto e_J(g^{-1} x)$. This does not depend on $J$ with $e_J m = m$. Indeed, if $J' \subseteq J$ and $e_J m = m$, then $(\lambda_g e_{J'}) m = (\lambda_g e_{J'} * e_J) m = (\lambda_g e_J) m$, since $e_{J'} * e_J = e_J$. Two such groups contain a third one, so the claim follows. One checks that this defines a smooth representation, that the two functors are inverse to each other, and that morphisms correspond. The last claim follows from the formula for $g \cdot m$ and from the definition of $\pi(f)$. $\square$

### Representations with fixed vectors

Let $J$ be a compact open subgroup. For a smooth representation $V$ the space $V^J = \pi(e_J) V$ is a module over $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$.

**Proposition 1.2.3 (the functor $V \mapsto V^J$).** Let $J$ be a compact open subgroup of $G$.
1. If $V$ is irreducible and $V^J \ne 0$, then $V^J$ is a simple $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-module.
2. The map $V \mapsto V^J$ is a bijection from the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth representations with $V^J \ne 0$ to the set of isomorphism classes of simple $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-modules.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* For part 1, let $0 \ne v \in V^J$ and $w \in V^J$. Since $V$ is irreducible, $w = \pi(f) v$ for some $f \in \mathcal{H}(G)$ by Proposition 1.2.2. Then $w = \pi(e_J) \pi(f) \pi(e_J) v = \pi(e_J * f * e_J) v$, with $e_J * f * e_J \in \mathcal{H}(G, J)$. So $v$ generates $V^J$.

For part 2 we construct an inverse. Let $E$ be a simple $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-module. The space $\mathcal{H}(G) e_J$ is a left $\mathcal{H}(G)$-module and a right $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$-module. Put $W = \mathcal{H}(G) e_J \otimes_{\mathcal{H}(G, J)} E$. It is a nondegenerate module, so a smooth representation, and $W^J = e_J \mathcal{H}(G) e_J \otimes E = E$. Each nonzero submodule $U$ of $W$ with $U^J = 0$ lies in the sum $W_0$ of all such submodules, and $W_0^J = 0$. Each submodule $U \not\subseteq W_0$ has $U^J \ne 0$, so $U^J = E$ since $E$ is simple, and then $U = W$, since $W$ is generated by $W^J$. So $V = W / W_0$ is irreducible with $V^J = E$. If $V'$ is irreducible with $V'^J \cong E$, the map $W \to V'$, $f \otimes x \mapsto \pi'(f) x$, is nonzero, so surjective. Its kernel contains $W_0$, since the image of $W_0$ has no $J$-fixed vector and $V'$ is irreducible. So $V' \cong V$. $\square$

The proposition reduces the study of the irreducible representations with a $J$-fixed vector to the study of the simple modules over the algebra with unit $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$. Admissibility says that these modules have finite dimension. For $J$ the Iwahori subgroup of $GL_n(K)$, the matrices in $GL_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$ that are upper triangular modulo $\mathfrak{p}$, the algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, J)$ is the affine Hecke algebra of Iwahori and Matsumoto. Its simple modules were classified by Kazhdan and Lusztig. The rest of this section treats the case $J = GL_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$.

### The spherical Hecke algebra

Let $G = GL_n(K)$ and $K_0 = GL_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$. Give $K_0$ the volume $1$. The algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$ is the *spherical Hecke algebra*. A smooth representation $V$ is *unramified*, or *spherical*, if $V^{K_0} \ne 0$.

By the theory of elementary divisors, $G$ is the disjoint union of the double cosets $K_0 \varpi^\lambda K_0$, where $\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n)$ runs over the non-increasing sequences of integers and $\varpi^\lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{\lambda_1}, \dots, \varpi^{\lambda_n})$. So the functions $1_{K_0 \varpi^\lambda K_0}$ form a basis of $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$.

**Proposition 1.2.4 (Gelfand's trick).** The spherical Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$ is commutative.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Let $\iota(g) = {}^t g$. It is an antiautomorphism of $G$ that preserves $K_0$ and the Haar measure, so $f \mapsto f \circ \iota$ is an antiautomorphism of $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$: $(f_1 * f_2) \circ \iota = (f_2 \circ \iota) * (f_1 \circ \iota)$. Each double coset $K_0 \varpi^\lambda K_0$ is stable under $\iota$, since $\varpi^\lambda$ is diagonal. So $f \circ \iota = f$ for each $f$ in the algebra, and the antiautomorphism is the identity. Then $f_1 * f_2 = f_2 * f_1$. $\square$

By Proposition 1.2.3 and Proposition 1.2.4, an irreducible unramified representation $V$ has $\dim V^{K_0} = 1$, since a simple module over a commutative algebra of countable dimension is of dimension one by the argument of Theorem 1.1.7. So $V^{K_0}$ is a character of $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$, and it determines $V$.

Let $T$ be the diagonal torus, $N$ the group of upper triangular unipotent matrices and $B = TN$. The modulus character of $B$ is $\delta_B(t n) = \prod_{i < j} |t_i / t_j|$. For $f \in \mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$ the *Satake transform* is the function on $T / T(\mathcal{O}_K) \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$ given by
$$\mathcal{S} f(t) = \delta_B(t)^{1/2} \int_N f(t n) \, dn,$$
with $N \cap K_0$ of volume $1$. We view $\mathcal{S} f$ as an element of $\mathbb{C}[X_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, X_n^{\pm 1}]$, where the monomial $X^\mu$ is the characteristic function of $\varpi^\mu T(\mathcal{O}_K)$.

**Theorem 1.2.5 (Satake isomorphism).** The Satake transform is an isomorphism of algebras
$$\mathcal{S} : \mathcal{H}(G, K_0) \to \mathbb{C}[X_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, X_n^{\pm 1}]^{S_n}.$$

Lean: not stated yet

*Sketch of proof.* The transform is a homomorphism, by the Iwasawa decomposition $G = B K_0$ and a change of variables. Its image is invariant under the symmetric group. This is the main point. Let $\pi_z$ be the unramified principal series of Section 1.3 with parameter $z \in (\mathbb{C}^\times)^n$. Then $\pi_z(f)$ acts on the spherical vector of $\pi_z$ by the scalar $\mathcal{S} f(z)$. For $w \in S_n$ the representations $\pi_z$ and $\pi_{w z}$ have the same irreducible subquotients, by Theorem 1.3.8, and the spherical vector lies in one of them. So $\mathcal{S} f(z) = \mathcal{S} f(w z)$ for $z$ in a dense set, and then for all $z$. For triangularity, let $\lambda$ be dominant. Then $\mathcal{S} 1_{K_0 \varpi^\lambda K_0} = q^{\langle \lambda, \rho \rangle} m_\lambda + \sum_{\mu < \lambda} c_{\lambda \mu} m_\mu$, where $m_\mu$ is the orbit sum of $X^\mu$ and $\mu < \lambda$ is the dominance order. The orbit sums of the dominant $\mu$ form a basis of the invariants, so $\mathcal{S}$ is a bijection. $\square$

For $n = 2$ put $T_\varpi = 1_{K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1) K_0}$ and $R_\varpi = 1_{\varpi K_0}$. Then $\mathcal{S} T_\varpi = q^{1/2} (X_1 + X_2)$ and $\mathcal{S} R_\varpi = X_1 X_2$, and $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0) = \mathbb{C}[T_\varpi, R_\varpi, R_\varpi^{-1}]$. This is the local form of the algebra of Hecke operators $T_p$ and $\langle p \rangle$ of Volume 4.

**Corollary 1.2.6 (unramified representations of $GL_n$).** The irreducible unramified representations of $GL_n(K)$ correspond to the unordered $n$-tuples $\{z_1, \dots, z_n\}$ of elements of $\mathbb{C}^\times$. The representation $\pi$ with parameter $\{z_i\}$ is the one in which $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$ acts on the line $V^{K_0}$ through the character $f \mapsto \mathcal{S} f(z_1, \dots, z_n)$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* The characters of $\mathbb{C}[X^{\pm 1}]^{S_n}$ are the evaluations at the points of $(\mathbb{C}^\times)^n / S_n$, since the invariants are the coordinate ring of this quotient variety. By Theorem 1.2.5 and Proposition 1.2.3, each character of $\mathcal{H}(G, K_0)$ comes from a unique irreducible unramified representation. $\square$

The parameter $\{z_1, \dots, z_n\}$ is the *Satake parameter* of $\pi$. We view it as the semisimple conjugacy class $\mathrm{diag}(z_1, \dots, z_n)$ in $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. This is the first appearance of the dual group: an unramified representation of $GL_n(K)$ is a semisimple conjugacy class in $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$. The local Langlands correspondence of Chapter 3 sends $\pi$ to the unramified representation $r$ of $W_K$ with $r(\Phi) = \mathrm{diag}(z_1, \dots, z_n)$ and $N = 0$, for the geometric Frobenius $\Phi$. With the normalization of the Satake transform by $\delta_B^{1/2}$, the parameter is unitary when $\pi$ is tempered. This is the normalization of Volume 0.

**Example 1.2.7 (the Hecke eigenvalue).** Let $\chi_1, \chi_2$ be unramified characters of $K^\times$ with $\chi_i(\varpi) = z_i$, and let $V$ be the space of locally constant functions on $GL_2(K)$ with $f(bg) = \chi_1(a) \chi_2(d) |a/d|^{1/2} f(g)$ for $b = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & x \\ 0 & d \end{smallmatrix}\right)$. This is the principal series of Section 1.5. Let $f_0$ be the function with $f_0(b k) = \chi_1(a) \chi_2(d) |a/d|^{1/2}$ for $k \in K_0$. It spans $V^{K_0}$. The double coset $K_0 \mathrm{diag}(\varpi, 1) K_0$ is the disjoint union of the $q + 1$ cosets $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} \varpi & b \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right) K_0$ with $b$ in a set of representatives of $k$, and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \varpi \end{smallmatrix}\right) K_0$. So
$$(\pi(T_\varpi) f_0)(1) = \sum_{x} f_0(x) = q \cdot z_1 q^{-1/2} + z_2 q^{1/2} = q^{1/2} (z_1 + z_2),$$
the sum over the $q + 1$ coset representatives. This agrees with $\mathcal{S} T_\varpi = q^{1/2}(X_1 + X_2)$. For the newform $f$ of weight $k$ of Volume 4 with $T_p f = a_p f$ and $p \nmid N$, the local component at $p$ is unramified with $q^{1/2}(z_1 + z_2) = p^{1 - k/2} a_p$ in the unitary normalization.
