## Exercises

1. Let $r_G$ be the regular representation of $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$, and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime of $F$, possibly ramified, with $e$, $f$ and $g$ as usual. Show directly from Definition 6.1.3 that $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$ is the space of functions on the set $I_\mathfrak{P} \backslash G$ of right cosets, that $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ permutes the $g f$ cosets in $g$ cycles of length $f$, and that $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, r_G) = (1 - N(\mathfrak{p})^{-fs})^{-g} = \prod_{\mathfrak{P} \mid \mathfrak{p}} (1 - N(\mathfrak{P})^{-s})^{-1}$. Compare with Corollary 6.3.4.
2. Show that $\overline{L(\bar s, \rho)} = L(s, \rho^\vee)$ for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$, and that $L(s, \rho)$ is real for real $s > 1$ if the character of $\rho$ is real. Show that $L(\sigma, \rho) > 0$ for real $\sigma > 1$ in that case.
3. Let $\rho$ be the representation of dimension 2 of $S_3$ for $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$, as in Example 6.3.5. Show that the prime $2$ is tamely ramified in $L$ with $I = A_3$, and that $f(\rho, 2) = 2$. Use $d_K = -108$ and Corollary 6.4.3 to show that $f(\rho, 3) = 3$. Deduce from Definition 6.4.1 that, in the lower numbering at $3$, $G_0 = S_3$, $G_1 = A_3$ and $G_2 = 1$.
4. In $S_3$, let $C_3$ and $C_2$ be subgroups of order 3 and 2. Show that $2 \cdot \mathbf{1} = \mathrm{Ind}_{C_3}^{S_3} \mathbf{1} + 2 \, \mathrm{Ind}_{C_2}^{S_3} \mathbf{1} - \mathrm{Ind}_{\{1\}}^{S_3} \mathbf{1}$, and that $\mathbf{1}$ itself is not an integral combination of the characters $\mathrm{Ind}_C^{S_3} \mathbf{1}$ with $C$ cyclic. For an $S_3$-extension $L/\mathbb{Q}$ with quadratic subfield $k$ and a cubic subfield $K$, deduce the relation $\zeta(s)^2 \zeta_L(s) = \zeta_k(s) \zeta_K(s)^2$.
5. Let $\rho$ be a representation with $\langle \rho, \mathbf{1} \rangle = 0$. Use Theorem 6.6.4 and Corollary 6.6.5 for $\rho^\vee$ to show that $\Lambda(-k, \rho) \ne 0$ for every integer $k \ge 0$. Deduce that $\mathrm{ord}_{s=0} L(s, \rho) = \sum_{v \mid \infty} \dim V^{D_w}$, where $D_w = \langle c_w \rangle$ at a real place and $D_w = 1$ at a complex place. Compute the order of $L(s, \rho)$ at $s = -k$ for $k \ge 1$, in terms of the numbers $n_v^+$, $n_v^-$ and $r_2$.
6. Let $L/\mathbb{Q}$ be an $S_3$-extension. Compute the characters $\lambda_C$ of the proof of Theorem 6.6.6 for the cyclic subgroups $C$ of $S_3$, and check the identity $\sum_C \mathrm{Ind}_C^{S_3} \lambda_C = 6(r_G - \mathbf{1})$. Write $(\zeta_L / \zeta)^6$ as the product of Hecke $L$-functions that the proof gives. Compare with $\zeta_L / \zeta = L(s, \mathrm{sgn}) L(s, \rho)^2$.
7. Let $V$ be a representation of dimension 2 of a group $G$. Show that $V^\vee \cong V \otimes \det(V)^{-1}$. Deduce that $L(s, \rho^\vee) = L(s, \rho \otimes \det \rho^{-1})$, and that $\rho$ is self dual if $\det \rho$ is trivial or if the character of $\rho$ is real.
8. Let $\rho : \mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}) \to GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ be odd and irreducible. Show that $\Lambda(s, \rho) = \Gamma_\mathbb{C}(s) L(s, \rho)$, and that $L(s, \rho)$ has a simple zero at every integer $s \le 0$. Check this against the weight 1 form of Example 6.7.10 and the functional equation of $\Lambda(s, f) = (2\pi)^{-s} \Gamma(s) L(s, f)$.
9. Let $A \in GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ and let $e$ be an idempotent matrix with $Ae = eA$. Show that $\det(1 - T A e) = \det(1 - T A \mid \mathrm{im}\, e)$. Show that the hypothesis $Ae = eA$ holds for $A = \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})$ and $e = e_I$. Give an example of a permutation matrix $A$ and an idempotent $e$ of rank 1 with $\det(1 - TAe) = 1$, to show that the hypothesis is needed for the Lean form of Remark 6.2.5.
10. Let $p$ be a prime and $k \ge 1$. Use Example 6.5.4 and Corollary 6.4.3 to show that $|d_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^k})}| = p^{p^{k-1}(pk - k - 1)}$. Show that the Artin conductor of a Dirichlet character, viewed as a character of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)/\mathbb{Q})$, is its conductor in the classical sense.
11. Let $Q_8$ be the quaternion group and let $\rho$ be its irreducible representation of dimension 2. Show that $\rho = \mathrm{Ind}_C^{Q_8} \psi$ for a cyclic subgroup $C$ of order 4 and a faithful character $\psi$ of $C$. For a $Q_8$-extension $L/\mathbb{Q}$, conclude that $L(s, \rho)$ is a Hecke $L$-function of a quadratic field, and that it is entire. Show that $\rho$ is not realizable over $\mathbb{R}$, although its character is real.

## Notes and history

Artin introduced his $L$-functions in "Über eine neue Art von $L$-Reihen" (Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg, 1923). He defined the factors at the unramified primes, proved the formal properties of Section 6.3 and the factorization of Dedekind zeta functions, and proved that some power of $L(s, \rho)$ is a product of abelian $L$-functions. To identify the abelian $L$-functions with Hecke $L$-functions he needed the reciprocity law. He stated it in the same paper and proved it in 1927, with a method of Chebotarev. In two papers of 1930 and 1931 Artin defined the factors at the ramified primes and the conductor, and found the functional equation with the factor $A(\rho)$. The integrality of the conductor and its description through ramification groups are in [Ser79], Chapter VI. The local constants $W(\rho)$ were later written as products of local root numbers by Langlands and Deligne, see [Tat79].

Aramata proved in 1933 that $\zeta_L / \zeta_F$ is entire for Galois $L/F$. Brauer proved his induction theorem in "On Artin's $L$-series with general group characters" (Ann. of Math., 1947), and with it the meromorphic continuation of all Artin $L$-functions and a second proof of the theorem of Aramata. Heilbronn's chapter in [CF67] treats Artin $L$-functions, Brauer's theorem and the theorem of Aramata and Brauer, and [Neu99], Chapter VII, gives the full theory with the conductor. Brauer's theorem has many other proofs. The one we sketched is in Serre's *Linear representations of finite groups*.

Fröhlich and Queyrut proved in 1973 that $W(\rho) = 1$ when $\rho$ is orthogonal, that is, realizable over $\mathbb{R}$. Example 6.6.7 is a case. For symplectic $\rho$, such as the representation of Exercise 11, $W(\rho) = \pm 1$, and Armitage found in 1972 a quaternion extension with $W(\rho) = -1$. Then $L(s, \rho)$ vanishes at $s = 1/2$, and so does $\zeta_L$. This answered a question on the zeros of Dedekind zeta functions at the center.

Weil proved in 1967 a converse theorem for $GL_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. It shows that a Dirichlet series whose twists by Dirichlet characters all have analytic continuation and the expected functional equation comes from a modular form. With it, Artin's conjecture for an odd $\rho$ of dimension 2 and all its twists is equivalent to Theorem 6.7.7 for $\rho$. Deligne and Serre proved the converse direction in [DS74]. Serre's conjecture, proved in [KW09] and its second part with [Kis09], gave the remaining odd cases over $\mathbb{Q}$.

In the Lean project, `Langlands.Fidelity.artinLFunction_one` is proved. It is Proposition 6.2.3. The statements `Langlands.ArtinConjecture` (node LGEHB4MGJK) and `Langlands.ArtinOdd2Q` (node LGX6BXWH0J) are stated and not proved. Remark 6.2.5 and Remark 6.7.9 describe how they differ from the forms in the text. The theorems of Brauer, of Langlands and Tunnell, and of Deligne and Serre are not stated in the project yet.
