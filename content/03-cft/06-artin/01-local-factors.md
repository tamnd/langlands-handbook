## Local factors

This section defines the Euler factor of an Artin $L$-function at a finite prime. At an unramified prime the factor is the inverse of a characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius element. At a ramified prime the Frobenius element is only defined modulo the inertia group, and Artin's idea is to let it act on the space of inertia invariants. We first recall the facts from the representation theory of finite groups that the chapter uses.

### Representations of finite groups

Let $G$ be a finite group. A *representation* of $G$ is a homomorphism $\rho : G \to GL(V)$, where $V$ is a complex vector space of finite dimension $n = \dim \rho$. We often write $V$ for $\rho$. Its *character* is the function $\chi_\rho(g) = \mathrm{tr}\, \rho(g)$. It is a class function, and $\chi_\rho(1) = n$. Each $\rho(g)$ has finite order, so it is diagonalizable and its eigenvalues are roots of unity. Hence $\chi_\rho(g^{-1}) = \overline{\chi_\rho(g)}$. The *dual* representation $\rho^\vee$ acts on $V^\vee = \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathbb{C})$ by $\rho^\vee(g) \lambda = \lambda \circ \rho(g^{-1})$, and its character is $\bar\chi_\rho$.

By Maschke's theorem every representation is a direct sum of irreducible representations. On class functions we use the inner product $\langle \alpha, \beta \rangle = |G|^{-1} \sum_{g \in G} \alpha(g) \overline{\beta(g)}$. The irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis of the space of class functions. So a representation is determined up to isomorphism by its character, and the multiplicity of an irreducible $\pi$ in $\rho$ is $\langle \chi_\rho, \chi_\pi \rangle$. The group $R(G)$ of *virtual characters* is the subgroup of class functions generated by the characters. It is a free abelian group on the irreducible characters, and it is a ring under pointwise multiplication. The *regular representation* $r_G$ is $G$ acting on $\mathbb{C}[G]$ by left multiplication. Its character is $|G|$ at $1$ and $0$ elsewhere, so $r_G = \sum_\pi \chi_\pi(1) \chi_\pi$, the sum over the irreducible characters.

Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ and let $W$ be a representation of $H$. The *induced representation* is $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G W = \mathbb{C}[G] \otimes_{\mathbb{C}[H]} W$. If $g_1, \dots, g_r$ are representatives of $G/H$, then $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G W = \bigoplus_i g_i \otimes W$, and $G$ permutes the summands as it permutes $G/H$. The *restriction* $\mathrm{Res}_H \rho$ is $\rho$ viewed as a representation of $H$. If $N$ is a normal subgroup and $\rho$ is a representation of $G/N$, the *inflation* of $\rho$ is the composite $G \to G/N \to GL(V)$. For $g \in G$ let ${}^g W$ be the representation of $gHg^{-1}$ on $W$ given by $x \mapsto \psi(g^{-1} x g)$, where $\psi$ is the action on $W$.

**Proposition 6.1.1 (induced representations).** Let $H \subset G$ and let $W$ be a representation of $H$ with character $\psi$.
1. The character of $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G W$ is $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi(g) = |H|^{-1} \sum_{x \in G,\ x^{-1} g x \in H} \psi(x^{-1} g x)$.
2. (Frobenius reciprocity) For each class function $\alpha$ on $G$, $\langle \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi, \alpha \rangle_G = \langle \psi, \mathrm{Res}_H \alpha \rangle_H$.
3. For each class function $\alpha$ on $G$, $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G(\psi \cdot \mathrm{Res}_H \alpha) = (\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi) \cdot \alpha$.
4. If $H \subset H' \subset G$, then $\mathrm{Ind}_{H'}^G \mathrm{Ind}_H^{H'} W \cong \mathrm{Ind}_H^G W$, and $(\mathrm{Ind}_H^G W)^\vee \cong \mathrm{Ind}_H^G (W^\vee)$.
5. (Mackey) Let $D$ be a subgroup of $G$. Then
$$\mathrm{Res}_D \mathrm{Ind}_H^G W \cong \bigoplus_{g \in D \backslash G / H} \mathrm{Ind}_{D \cap gHg^{-1}}^D \big(\mathrm{Res}_{D \cap gHg^{-1}} {}^g W\big),$$
the sum over a set of representatives of the double cosets $D g H$.

*Proof.* These are standard facts. Proofs are in Serre's *Linear representations of finite groups*, Chapter 7. For part 5, the summand $g \otimes W$ of $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G W$ spans, under $D$, the subspace $\bigoplus_{x \in DgH/H} x \otimes W$. The stabilizer in $D$ of the coset $gH$ is $D \cap gHg^{-1}$, and it acts on $g \otimes W$ through ${}^g W$. So this subspace is the induced representation in the formula. $\square$

### Decomposition groups and the Frobenius coset

Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a nonzero prime of $\mathcal{O}_F$, let $q = N(\mathfrak{p})$, and let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime of $L$ above $\mathfrak{p}$. We use the decomposition group $D_\mathfrak{P}$ and the inertia group $I_\mathfrak{P}$ of Volume 1, Definition 1.4.5. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.6, there is an exact sequence
$$1 \to I_\mathfrak{P} \to D_\mathfrak{P} \to \mathrm{Gal}(\kappa(\mathfrak{P}) / \kappa(\mathfrak{p})) \to 1.$$
The group on the right is cyclic, generated by $x \mapsto x^q$. The *Frobenius coset* at $\mathfrak{P}$ is the set of $\sigma \in D_\mathfrak{P}$ that map to $x \mapsto x^q$. It is a coset of $I_\mathfrak{P}$, and it is a single element $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ when $\mathfrak{p}$ is unramified, see Volume 1, Definition 1.4.7. We write $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ for any element of the coset. An element $\sigma \in G$ with $\sigma(x) \equiv x^q \pmod{\mathfrak{P}}$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_L$ lies in $D_\mathfrak{P}$, by the argument after Volume 1, Definition 1.4.7. So the Frobenius coset is exactly the set of such $\sigma$. For $\tau \in G$ we have $D_{\tau\mathfrak{P}} = \tau D_\mathfrak{P} \tau^{-1}$ and $I_{\tau\mathfrak{P}} = \tau I_\mathfrak{P} \tau^{-1}$, and $\tau$ maps the Frobenius coset at $\mathfrak{P}$ onto the Frobenius coset at $\tau\mathfrak{P}$ by conjugation.

By Volume 1, Theorem 2.5.3, $D_\mathfrak{P}$ is the Galois group of the extension of completions $L_\mathfrak{P}/F_\mathfrak{p}$, and $I_\mathfrak{P}$ is its inertia group. So the local factor below depends only on the restriction of $\rho$ to the local Galois group.

### The projector onto the inertia invariants

Let $D$ be a finite group, $I$ a normal subgroup, and $\rho : D \to GL(V)$ a representation with character $\chi$. Let $V^I$ be the subspace of vectors fixed by $I$, and let
$$e_I = \frac{1}{|I|} \sum_{\tau \in I} \rho(\tau) \in \mathrm{End}(V).$$

**Lemma 6.1.2 (the projector).** In this situation the following hold.
1. $e_I^2 = e_I$, the image of $e_I$ is $V^I$, and $V = V^I \oplus \ker e_I$.
2. For $\sigma \in D$, $\rho(\sigma)$ commutes with $e_I$. So $\rho(\sigma)$ preserves $V^I$ and $\ker e_I$.
3. For $\sigma \in D$, $\det(1 - T \rho(\sigma) e_I) = \det(1 - T \rho(\sigma) \mid V^I)$ in $\mathbb{C}[T]$.
4. For $\sigma \in D$ and $\tau \in I$, $\rho(\sigma\tau) e_I = \rho(\sigma) e_I$.
5. For $\sigma \in D$ and $m \ge 1$, $\mathrm{tr}\big((\rho(\sigma) e_I)^m\big) = |I|^{-1} \sum_{\tau \in I} \chi(\sigma^m \tau)$.

*Proof.* For $\tau' \in I$ we have $\rho(\tau') e_I = e_I$, because $\tau \mapsto \tau'\tau$ is a bijection of $I$. Hence $e_I^2 = e_I$ and the image of $e_I$ lies in $V^I$. If $v \in V^I$, then $e_I v = v$. This proves part 1. Part 4 follows from $\rho(\tau) e_I = e_I$. For part 2, conjugation by $\sigma$ is a bijection of $I$, because $I$ is normal. So $\rho(\sigma) e_I \rho(\sigma)^{-1} = e_I$. For part 3, write $A = \rho(\sigma)$. By part 2 the matrix of $A e_I$ in a basis adapted to $V = V^I \oplus \ker e_I$ is block diagonal, with the blocks $A|_{V^I}$ and $0$. So $\det(1 - TAe_I) = \det(1 - TA \mid V^I) \cdot \det(1 \mid \ker e_I)$. For part 5, parts 1 and 2 give $(A e_I)^m = A^m e_I = \rho(\sigma^m) e_I$, and the trace of $\rho(\sigma^m) e_I$ is $|I|^{-1} \sum_\tau \chi(\sigma^m \tau)$. $\square$

### The local factor

**Definition 6.1.3 (the local factor).** Let $\rho : G \to GL(V)$ be a representation, let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a nonzero prime of $F$ and let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime of $L$ above $\mathfrak{p}$. The *local factor* of $\rho$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ is
$$L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho) = \det\big(1 - \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}) N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s} \mid V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}\big)^{-1}, \qquad s \in \mathbb{C}.$$
Here $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ is any element of the Frobenius coset. The representation $\rho$ is *unramified* at $\mathfrak{p}$ if $\rho(I_\mathfrak{P}) = 1$.

The space $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$ is stable under $D_\mathfrak{P}$, by Lemma 6.1.2, and $I_\mathfrak{P}$ acts trivially on it. So $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})$ acts on $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$, and this action does not depend on the element of the coset. The factor is the inverse of a polynomial in $N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}$ of degree $\dim V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$ with constant term $1$. If $\rho$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$, then it is unramified at every prime above $\mathfrak{p}$, because the inertia groups are conjugate. A prime that is unramified in $L$ is unramified for every $\rho$. So $\rho$ is ramified at only finitely many primes.

**Lemma 6.1.4 (independence of the choices).** The factor $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho)$ does not depend on the choice of $\mathfrak{P}$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ or of $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ in its coset. It depends only on the character $\chi$ of $\rho$. More precisely, for $|N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}| < 1$,
$$\log L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho) = \sum_{m \ge 1} \frac{1}{m} \Big( \frac{1}{|I_\mathfrak{P}|} \sum_{\tau \in I_\mathfrak{P}} \chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}^m \tau) \Big) N(\mathfrak{p})^{-ms},$$
where $\log$ is the branch given by the series.

*Proof.* Independence of the element of the coset is Lemma 6.1.2, part 4. Let $\tau \in G$. Then $\rho(\tau)$ maps $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$ onto $V^{\tau I_\mathfrak{P} \tau^{-1}} = V^{I_{\tau\mathfrak{P}}}$, and it carries the action of $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ to the action of $\tau \mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P} \tau^{-1}$, which is in the Frobenius coset at $\tau\mathfrak{P}$. So the two characteristic polynomials agree. Since $G$ acts transitively on the primes above $\mathfrak{p}$ (Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.4), the factor does not depend on $\mathfrak{P}$. For the formula, let $A$ be any square matrix and $T$ a complex number with $|T| \cdot \max |\lambda| < 1$, the maximum over the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $A$. Then $-\log \det(1 - TA) = \sum_{m \ge 1} \mathrm{tr}(A^m) T^m / m$, because both sides equal $\sum_\lambda \sum_m \lambda^m T^m / m$. Apply this to $A = \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}) e_I$ and $T = N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}$. The eigenvalues of $A$ are $0$ and roots of unity, and Lemma 6.1.2, parts 3 and 5, give the formula. $\square$

In particular, isomorphic representations have the same local factors. The formula also shows that the factor has a meaning for a virtual character, and we use this in Section 6.3.

**Example 6.1.5 (simple cases).** Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime of $F$ and $q = N(\mathfrak{p})$.
1. For the trivial representation $\mathbf{1}$, $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}} = V$ and $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \mathbf{1}) = (1 - q^{-s})^{-1}$ at every prime, ramified or not. This is the Euler factor of the Dedekind zeta function $\zeta_F$.
2. Let $\chi : G \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ be a character. Then $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$ is $V$ if $\chi(I_\mathfrak{P}) = 1$ and $0$ otherwise. So $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \chi) = (1 - \chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}) q^{-s})^{-1}$ if $\chi$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$, and $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \chi) = 1$ otherwise. When $\chi$ is unramified, $\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})$ depends only on $\mathfrak{p}$, and we write $\chi(\mathfrak{p})$.
3. Let $\rho$ be unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$ and let $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ be the eigenvalues of $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})$. Then $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho) = \prod_i (1 - \alpha_i q^{-s})^{-1}$.
4. Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be unramified in $L$, with residue degree $f$ and $g$ primes above it, and let $\rho = r_G$. The element $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ has order $f$, and $\mathbb{C}[G]$ is a sum of $|G|/f = g$ copies of the regular representation of the cyclic group $\langle \mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P} \rangle$. The eigenvalues of a generator on the regular representation of a cyclic group of order $f$ are the $f$-th roots of unity, so $\det(1 - T\, r_G(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})) = (1 - T^f)^g$. Thus $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, r_G) = (1 - q^{-fs})^{-g} = \prod_{\mathfrak{Q} \mid \mathfrak{p}} (1 - N(\mathfrak{Q})^{-s})^{-1}$. This is the factor of $\zeta_L$ at the primes above $\mathfrak{p}$. Corollary 6.3.4 proves the same identity at the ramified primes.

**Remark 6.1.6 (the geometric normalization).** The table of conventions in Volume 0, Section 3.1, attaches to a representation $r$ of the Weil group of a local field $K$ on $V$ the factor $L(r, s) = \det(1 - q^{-s} r(\Phi) \mid V^{I_K})^{-1}$, where $\Phi$ is a geometric Frobenius. Let $\rho_\mathfrak{p}$ be the restriction of $\rho$ to $D_\mathfrak{P} = \mathrm{Gal}(L_\mathfrak{P}/F_\mathfrak{p})$, viewed as a representation of the Weil group of $F_\mathfrak{p}$. Then
$$L(\rho_\mathfrak{p}, s) = L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho^\vee).$$
Indeed, an $I$-invariant linear form $\lambda$ satisfies $\lambda = \lambda \circ e_I$, so it vanishes on $\ker e_I$. Hence restriction gives a $D_\mathfrak{P}$-equivariant isomorphism $(V^\vee)^{I} \cong (V^{I})^\vee$, with $I = I_\mathfrak{P}$. On $(V^I)^\vee$ the element $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ acts as the transpose of $\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P})^{-1} = \rho(\Phi)$, and a matrix and its transpose have the same characteristic polynomial. So the arithmetic factor of $\rho^\vee$ is the geometric factor of $\rho$, and the arithmetic factor of $\rho$ is the geometric factor of $\rho^\vee$. Since $\chi_{\rho^\vee} = \bar\chi_\rho$, the two conventions give the same theory with $\rho$ and $\rho^\vee$ exchanged.
