## The formal properties

Artin proved that his $L$-functions behave well under the three basic operations on representations: direct sum, inflation from a quotient, and induction from a subgroup. The proofs compare local factors prime by prime. The induction property is the important one. It shows that the Dedekind zeta function of every intermediate field is an Artin $L$-function, and Section 6.6 uses it to reduce the analytic theory to representations of dimension one.

### Two local lemmas

**Lemma 6.3.1 (decomposition groups in a quotient).** Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of $G$, let $L' = L^N$, and identify $G/N$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(L'/F)$. Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime of $L$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ and let $\mathfrak{P}' = \mathfrak{P} \cap L'$. Then the projection $G \to G/N$ maps $D_\mathfrak{P}$ onto $D_{\mathfrak{P}'}$, maps $I_\mathfrak{P}$ onto $I_{\mathfrak{P}'}$, and maps the Frobenius coset at $\mathfrak{P}$ into the Frobenius coset at $\mathfrak{P}'$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Let $\sigma \mapsto \sigma'$ be the restriction to $L'$. If $\sigma \in D_\mathfrak{P}$, then $\sigma'$ maps $\mathfrak{P}' = \mathfrak{P} \cap \mathcal{O}_{L'}$ to itself. If also $\sigma(x) \equiv x^{a} \pmod{\mathfrak{P}}$ for all $x \in \mathcal{O}_L$, then the same congruence holds modulo $\mathfrak{P}'$ for $x \in \mathcal{O}_{L'}$. With $a = 1$ this shows $I_\mathfrak{P} \to I_{\mathfrak{P}'}$, and with $a = N(\mathfrak{p})$ it gives the claim on the Frobenius cosets. Let $\tau' \in D_{\mathfrak{P}'}$ and let $\tau \in G$ be a lift. Then $\tau\mathfrak{P}$ and $\mathfrak{P}$ both lie above $\tau'\mathfrak{P}' = \mathfrak{P}'$. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.4, applied to $L/L'$, there is $h \in N$ with $h\tau\mathfrak{P} = \mathfrak{P}$. Then $h\tau \in D_\mathfrak{P}$ and it maps to $\tau'$. So $D_\mathfrak{P} \to D_{\mathfrak{P}'}$ is onto. Now let $\tau' \in I_{\mathfrak{P}'}$, and choose a lift $\tau \in D_\mathfrak{P}$. Its reduction $\bar\tau$ is an automorphism of $\kappa(\mathfrak{P})$ that is trivial on $\kappa(\mathfrak{P}')$. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.6, applied to $L/L'$, there is $h \in D_\mathfrak{P} \cap N$ with $\bar h = \bar\tau$. Then $h^{-1}\tau \in I_\mathfrak{P}$, and it maps to $\tau'$. $\square$

**Lemma 6.3.2 (induction and inertia invariants).** Let $D$ be a finite group and let $I$ be a normal subgroup with $D/I$ cyclic, generated by the class of $\varphi \in D$. Let $D'$ be a subgroup of $D$, let $I' = D' \cap I$ and $f = [D : D'I]$, and let $\varphi' \in D' \cap \varphi^f I$. Then the class of $\varphi'$ generates $D'/I'$. For each representation $W$ of $D'$, with $U = \mathrm{Ind}_{D'}^D W$,
$$\det(1 - T\varphi \mid U^I) = \det(1 - T^f \varphi' \mid W^{I'}).$$

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* The map $D'/I' \to D/I$ is injective with image $D'I/I$, a subgroup of index $f$ of a cyclic group. So the image is generated by the class of $\varphi^f$, which is the class of $\varphi'$. Let $\psi$ be the character of $W$ and $\chi$ the character of $U$. By Lemma 6.1.4 and its proof, it suffices to show that $\mathrm{tr}(\varphi^m e_I \mid U) = 0$ when $f \nmid m$, and that $\mathrm{tr}(\varphi^{fk} e_I \mid U) = f \, \mathrm{tr}(\varphi'^k e_{I'} \mid W)$ for $k \ge 1$. Indeed, then
$$\sum_{m \ge 1} \frac{\mathrm{tr}(\varphi^m e_I \mid U)}{m} T^m = \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{\mathrm{tr}(\varphi'^k e_{I'} \mid W)}{k} T^{fk},$$
and the exponentials of the two sides are the inverses of the two determinants. Since $D/I$ is abelian, conjugation by any $y \in D$ maps the coset $\varphi^m I$ onto itself. By Proposition 6.1.1, part 1,
$$\sum_{\tau \in I} \chi(\varphi^m \tau) = \frac{1}{|D'|} \sum_{y \in D} \sum_{\tau \in I} [y^{-1}\varphi^m\tau y \in D'] \, \psi(y^{-1}\varphi^m\tau y) = \frac{|D|}{|D'|} \sum_{x \in \varphi^m I \cap D'} \psi(x).$$
The set $\varphi^m I \cap D'$ is empty unless the class of $\varphi^m$ lies in $D'I/I$, that is, unless $f \mid m$. If $m = fk$, then $\varphi'^k \in \varphi^{fk} I$, so $\varphi^{fk} I \cap D' = \varphi'^k I'$. By Lemma 6.1.2, part 5,
$$\mathrm{tr}(\varphi^{fk} e_I \mid U) = \frac{|D|}{|I| \, |D'|} \sum_{\tau' \in I'} \psi(\varphi'^k \tau') = \frac{|D| \, |I'|}{|I| \, |D'|} \, \mathrm{tr}(\varphi'^k e_{I'} \mid W).$$
The factor is $|D/I| / |D'/I'| = f$. $\square$

### Artin's theorem

**Theorem 6.3.3 (Artin).** Let $L/F$ be a finite Galois extension with group $G$.
1. (Additivity) For representations $\rho$ and $\rho'$ of $G$, $L(s, \rho \oplus \rho') = L(s, \rho) L(s, \rho')$.
2. (Inflation) Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of $G$, let $L' = L^N$, and let $\rho$ be a representation of $G/N = \mathrm{Gal}(L'/F)$. Then $L(s, \mathrm{Inf}\, \rho, L/F) = L(s, \rho, L'/F)$.
3. (Induction) Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$, let $M = L^H$, and let $\psi$ be a representation of $H = \mathrm{Gal}(L/M)$. Then $L(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi, L/F) = L(s, \psi, L/M)$.

More precisely, in parts 1 and 2 the local factors at each prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $F$ agree, and in part 3, $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi) = \prod_{\mathfrak{q} \mid \mathfrak{p}} L_\mathfrak{q}(s, \psi)$, the product over the primes $\mathfrak{q}$ of $M$ above $\mathfrak{p}$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Part 1. The invariants of $I_\mathfrak{P}$ in $V \oplus V'$ are $V^{I_\mathfrak{P}} \oplus V'^{I_\mathfrak{P}}$, and the determinant is multiplicative on direct sums.

Part 2. Let $\mathfrak{P}' = \mathfrak{P} \cap L'$. By Lemma 6.3.1, $(\mathrm{Inf}\, \rho)(I_\mathfrak{P}) = \rho(I_{\mathfrak{P}'})$. So the two representations have the same space of inertia invariants. A Frobenius element at $\mathfrak{P}$ maps to a Frobenius element at $\mathfrak{P}'$, so the two endomorphisms of this space agree.

Part 3. Fix $\mathfrak{p}$ and a prime $\mathfrak{P} \mid \mathfrak{p}$ of $L$. Let $D = D_\mathfrak{P}$, $I = I_\mathfrak{P}$, $\varphi \in D$ a Frobenius element, $q = N(\mathfrak{p})$ and $T = q^{-s}$. Let $W$ be the space of $\psi$. For $g \in G$ put $D_g = D \cap gHg^{-1}$ and $I_g = I \cap gHg^{-1} = D_g \cap I$, and let $W_g$ be the representation ${}^g W$ of $D_g$, that is, $x \mapsto \psi(g^{-1}xg)$. By Mackey's formula (Proposition 6.1.1, part 5) and part 1 of this theorem,
$$L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \psi)^{-1} = \prod_{g \in D \backslash G / H} \det\big(1 - T\varphi \mid (\mathrm{Ind}_{D_g}^D W_g)^I\big).$$
We match the double cosets with the primes of $M$ above $\mathfrak{p}$. The primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ are the $g^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ for $g \in G$, and $g^{-1}\mathfrak{P} = g'^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ exactly when $Dg = Dg'$. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.4, applied to $L/M$, the primes of $M$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ correspond to the $H$-orbits of primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak{p}$. The $H$-orbit of $g^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ is the set of $x^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ with $x \in gH$. So $DgH \mapsto \mathfrak{q} = g^{-1}\mathfrak{P} \cap M$ is a bijection from $D \backslash G / H$ to the set of primes of $M$ above $\mathfrak{p}$.

Fix $g$ and let $\mathfrak{Q} = g^{-1}\mathfrak{P}$ and $\mathfrak{q} = \mathfrak{Q} \cap M$, with residue degree $f = f(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})$. In the extension $L/M$, with group $H$, the decomposition group of $\mathfrak{Q}$ is $D_\mathfrak{Q} \cap H = g^{-1} D_g g$ and the inertia group is $I_\mathfrak{Q} \cap H = g^{-1} I_g g$. Let $\varphi_\mathfrak{Q} \in H$ be a Frobenius element of $\mathfrak{Q}$ over $\mathfrak{q}$. It satisfies $\varphi_\mathfrak{Q}(x) \equiv x^{q^f} \pmod{\mathfrak{Q}}$, because $N(\mathfrak{q}) = q^f$. So $\varphi' = g\varphi_\mathfrak{Q}g^{-1}$ lies in $D_g$ and satisfies $\varphi'(x) \equiv x^{q^f} \pmod{\mathfrak{P}}$. Since $D/I$ acts faithfully on $\kappa(\mathfrak{P})$, this means $\varphi' \in \varphi^f I$. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.4.6, applied to $L/F$ and to $L/M$, we have $|D/I| = f(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p})$ and $|D_g/I_g| = f(\mathfrak{Q}/\mathfrak{q})$. As $f(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p}) = f(\mathfrak{Q}/\mathfrak{p}) = f(\mathfrak{Q}/\mathfrak{q}) f$, the index $[D : D_g I]$ is $f$. Conjugation by $g$ identifies $W$ with $W_g$, the space $W^{I_\mathfrak{Q} \cap H}$ with $W_g^{I_g}$, and the action of $\varphi_\mathfrak{Q}$ with the action of $\varphi'$. Hence
$$L_\mathfrak{q}(s, \psi)^{-1} = \det\big(1 - T^f \psi(\varphi_\mathfrak{Q}) \mid W^{I_\mathfrak{Q} \cap H}\big) = \det\big(1 - T^f \varphi' \mid W_g^{I_g}\big) = \det\big(1 - T\varphi \mid (\mathrm{Ind}_{D_g}^D W_g)^I\big),$$
where the last step is Lemma 6.3.2. Taking the product over the double cosets gives part 3. $\square$

### Virtual characters

By Lemma 6.1.4 the factor $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \rho)$ depends only on the character $\chi$ of $\rho$, and by Theorem 6.3.3, part 1, it is multiplicative in $\chi$. Every element of $R(G)$ is a unique integral combination $\chi = \sum_\pi n_\pi \chi_\pi$ of irreducible characters. We define $L(s, \chi) = \prod_\pi L(s, \chi_\pi)^{n_\pi}$. It is meromorphic on $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$, and in fact holomorphic and without zeros there by Theorem 6.2.2. Its local factors are $L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \chi) = \prod_\pi L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \chi_\pi)^{n_\pi}$. Since induction and inflation are additive on characters, the three statements of Theorem 6.3.3 hold for virtual characters.

**Corollary 6.3.4 (Dedekind zeta functions).** Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ and $M = L^H$.
1. $\zeta_M(s) = L(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H)$, with equality of the local factors at each prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $F$.
2. $\zeta_L(s) = L(s, r_G) = \prod_\pi L(s, \pi)^{\dim \pi}$, the product over the irreducible representations $\pi$ of $G$.
3. The character $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H - \mathbf{1}_G$ is the character of a representation, and $\zeta_M(s)/\zeta_F(s) = L(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H - \mathbf{1}_G)$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Part 1 is Theorem 6.3.3, part 3, with $\psi = \mathbf{1}_H$, together with Proposition 6.2.3 for $L/M$. Part 2 is part 1 for $H = 1$, since $\mathrm{Ind}_1^G \mathbf{1} = r_G$, and the decomposition $r_G = \sum_\pi \dim(\pi) \pi$ of Section 6.1. For part 3, Frobenius reciprocity gives $\langle \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H, \mathbf{1}_G \rangle = \langle \mathbf{1}_H, \mathbf{1}_H \rangle = 1$. So $\mathbf{1}_G$ occurs exactly once in $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H$, and the difference is the character of the complement. The formula follows from part 1 and Proposition 6.2.3. $\square$

Part 1 is an identity of Euler factors at every prime, ramified or not: $\prod_{\mathfrak{q} \mid \mathfrak{p}} (1 - N(\mathfrak{q})^{-s})^{-1} = L_\mathfrak{p}(s, \mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H)$. This is the reason for the inertia invariants in Definition 6.1.3. Part 3 reduces the question whether $\zeta_M/\zeta_F$ is entire to a question about Artin $L$-functions. Section 6.6 proves that it is entire when $M/F$ is Galois.

**Example 6.3.5 (a pure cubic field).** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^3 = 2$, and let $L = K(\zeta_3)$, the splitting field of $X^3 - 2$. The group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ acts faithfully on the three roots, so $G = S_3$. Its irreducible representations are $\mathbf{1}$, the sign $\varepsilon$ and the representation $\rho$ of dimension 2. Let $H = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, the stabilizer of $\alpha$. Then $\mathrm{Ind}_H^G \mathbf{1}_H$ is the permutation representation on the roots, which is $\mathbf{1} + \rho$. Corollary 6.3.4 gives
$$\zeta_K(s) = \zeta(s) L(s, \rho), \qquad \zeta_L(s) = \zeta(s) L(s, \varepsilon) L(s, \rho)^2.$$
The sign is inflated from $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})/\mathbb{Q})$, so $L(s, \varepsilon) = L(s, \chi_{-3})$ by Theorem 6.3.3, part 2, and the argument of Example 6.2.4. Let us compute the factors of $L(s, \rho)$. The discriminant of $X^3 - 2$ is $-108 = -2^2 \cdot 3^3$. For $p \ne 2, 3$ the prime $p$ is unramified, reduction modulo $\mathfrak{P}$ is injective on the roots, and $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ permutes the roots as $x \mapsto x^p$ permutes their reductions. Since the permutation character is $1 + \chi_\rho$,
$$\chi_\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p) = \#\{x \in \mathbb{F}_p : x^3 = 2\} - 1.$$
If $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, cubing is a bijection of $\mathbb{F}_p$, $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is a transposition, and $L_p(s, \rho) = (1 - p^{-2s})^{-1}$. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $2$ is a cube modulo $p$, then $\mathrm{Frob}_p = 1$ and $L_p(s, \rho) = (1 - p^{-s})^{-2}$. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $2$ is not a cube, $\mathrm{Frob}_p$ is a 3-cycle and $L_p(s, \rho) = (1 + p^{-s} + p^{-2s})^{-1}$. At $p = 2$, the polynomial $X^3 - 2$ is Eisenstein and $2$ is inert in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. So $e = 3$, $f = 2$, and $I = A_3$. At $p = 3$, the polynomial $(Y - 1)^3 - 2 = Y^3 - 3Y^2 + 3Y - 3$ is Eisenstein and $3$ ramifies in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. So $e = 6$ and $I = S_3$. In both cases $\rho$ has no nonzero inertia invariants, since $\rho|_{A_3}$ is the sum of the two nontrivial characters of $A_3$. So $L_2(s, \rho) = L_3(s, \rho) = 1$.

Finally $\rho = \mathrm{Ind}_{A_3}^{S_3} \psi$ for a nontrivial character $\psi$ of $A_3 = \mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}))$. So Theorem 6.3.3, part 3, gives $L(s, \rho) = L(s, \psi, L/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}))$, an $L$-function of a cyclic cubic extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. Section 6.5 identifies it with a Hecke $L$-function, and Example 6.6.7 computes its functional equation.
