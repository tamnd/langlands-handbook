# Artin L-functions

Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$. Dedekind proved that the quotient $\zeta_K(s)/\zeta(s)$ is an entire function, but the quotient is not a product of Dirichlet $L$-functions, because $K$ is not an abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$. Artin found the correct object in 1923. Let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$ be the Galois closure of $K$, with group $S_3$, and let $\rho$ be the irreducible representation of $S_3$ of dimension 2. Then $\zeta_K(s) = \zeta(s) L(s, \rho)$, where $L(s, \rho)$ is an Euler product whose factor at $p \ne 2, 3$ is $\det(1 - \rho(\mathrm{Frob}_p) p^{-s})^{-1}$. Artin attached such a function to every complex representation of the Galois group of a finite Galois extension of number fields. The construction contains the Dirichlet $L$-functions, the Dedekind zeta functions and the $L$-functions of abelian extensions as special cases.

The theory has a simple formal part and a deep analytic part. The formal part is the behaviour of $L(s, \rho)$ under sums, inflation and induction of representations. It is a computation with local factors, and it reduces many questions to representations of dimension one. Class field theory then identifies the $L$-functions of representations of dimension one with Hecke $L$-functions, and Chapter 5 proved their continuation and functional equation. Brauer's induction theorem writes every character as an integral combination of characters induced from characters of dimension one. These three steps give the meromorphic continuation and the functional equation of every Artin $L$-function. They do not show that $L(s, \rho)$ is entire for a nontrivial irreducible $\rho$, because the combination in Brauer's theorem has negative coefficients. This is Artin's conjecture. It is open, and it is one of the main motivations for the reciprocity conjectures of Langlands.

Section 6.1 recalls the representation theory of finite groups that we need, and it defines the local factor at a prime with the inertia invariants and the Frobenius. Section 6.2 defines $L(s, \rho)$ and proves that the Euler product converges for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$. Section 6.3 proves Artin's formal properties and writes the Dedekind zeta function as a product of Artin $L$-functions. Section 6.4 defines the Artin conductor and the gamma factors, and states the functional equation. Section 6.5 treats the abelian case through class field theory. Section 6.6 proves the induction theorems of Artin and Brauer and deduces the meromorphic continuation and the functional equation. Section 6.7 states Artin's conjecture, proves that reciprocity implies it, and describes the known cases.

In this chapter $F$ is a number field, $L/F$ is a finite Galois extension with group $G$, and all representations are on finite dimensional complex vector spaces. The Euler factors use the arithmetic Frobenius $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{P}$ of Volume 1, Definition 1.4.7, as in Artin's papers, in [Neu99] and in the Lean project. The rest of the handbook puts the geometric Frobenius $\Phi = \mathrm{Frob}^{-1}$ into the $L$-factors of representations of Weil groups, see Volume 0, Chapter 3. The two conventions differ by duality: the factor of $\rho$ with the geometric Frobenius is the factor of the dual representation $\rho^\vee$ with the arithmetic Frobenius. Remark 6.1.6 proves this, and we state each comparison with Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 in this form. Global $L$-functions put $s$ first, $L(s, \rho)$, as in Chapter 5.
