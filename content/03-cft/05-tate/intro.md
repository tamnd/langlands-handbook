# Tate's thesis

Chapter 4 stated the local functional equation of Tate and used it to define the $\varepsilon$-factor of a character of $K^\times$. This chapter proves that equation, and then it proves the global theorem that Tate's thesis was written for. Hecke proved in 1918 and 1920 that the $L$-function of a Hecke character has a meromorphic continuation and a functional equation. His proof used theta functions, one for each type of character. Tate replaced the theta functions by Fourier analysis on the adeles. In his proof the local and the global parts are separate, and the global part is a single argument for all characters.

Section 5.1 sets up the local harmonic analysis: additive characters, self-dual Haar measures, Schwartz-Bruhat functions and the local zeta integral $Z(f, \chi, s)$. Section 5.2 proves the local functional equation for every local field, archimedean or not, and computes the $L$-factors and the $\varepsilon$-factors of all characters. This proves Theorem 4.3.1, Proposition 4.3.4 and Remark 4.3.5. Section 5.3 proves the Poisson summation formula for $\mathbb{A}_F / F$ and its consequence, Tate's Riemann-Roch theorem. Section 5.4 defines the global zeta integral and proves its continuation and functional equation. The Dedekind zeta function and the analytic class number formula follow at once. Section 5.5 deduces Hecke's theorem, compares it with its statement in the Lean project, and treats Dirichlet $L$-functions as an example.

We use the normalization of Tate's thesis, Chapter XV of [CF67]. The additive character of $\mathbb{A}_F$ is the character $\psi$ of Volume 1, Section 3.3. Its component at a real place is $x \mapsto e^{-2\pi i x}$, with a minus sign. The additive Haar measures are self-dual for $\psi$, and the multiplicative Haar measures are those of Tate, given in Section 5.1. A different choice of $\psi$ or of the measures changes the local constants by explicit factors. Section 5.2 gives these factors. The global $\varepsilon$-factor does not depend on these choices.

The notation is that of Volume 1, Chapter 3, and of Chapter 4. In this chapter $K$ is a local field and $F$ is a number field. The Lean project writes $K$ for the number field. The idele norm is $|x|_\mathbb{A}$, as in Volume 1, and it is $\|x\|$ in the Lean project. We do not write $\|\cdot\|$ for it, because Chapter 4 uses $\|\cdot\|$ for the norm character of $W_K$. Local objects keep the argument order of Chapter 4: $L(\chi, s)$, $\varepsilon(\chi, s, \psi)$ and $Z(f, \chi, s)$. Global $L$-functions put $s$ first, as Hecke did: $L(s, \chi)$, $\Lambda(s, \chi)$ and $\varepsilon(s, \chi)$. The global zeta integral is $Z(f, \chi, s)$, like the local one.
