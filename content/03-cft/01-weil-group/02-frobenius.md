## The Frobenius and the inertia group

The Galois group of a finite field extension $k_n / k$ is cyclic of order $n$, with the generator $x \mapsto x^q$. This generator is natural: it does not depend on a choice. Its limit over all $n$ is the following automorphism.

**Definition 1.2.1 (arithmetic Frobenius).** The *arithmetic Frobenius* of $\bar k / k$ is the automorphism $\mathrm{Frob}_k(x) = x^q$. The *geometric Frobenius* is its inverse.

The map $n \mapsto \mathrm{Frob}_k^n$ extends to an isomorphism of topological groups $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$, where $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the profinite completion of $\mathbb{Z}$. In particular the subgroup generated by $\mathrm{Frob}_k$ is dense, and $\mathrm{Frob}_k$ has infinite order.

Let $\bar{\mathcal{O}}$ be the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in $\bar K$, and let $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ be a maximal ideal of $\bar{\mathcal{O}}$ over $\mathfrak{m}_K$. Such an ideal exists by the lying-over theorem, because $\bar{\mathcal{O}}$ is integral over $\mathcal{O}_K$. The field $\bar{\mathcal{O}} / \bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, and the text writes $\bar k$ for it.

Each $\sigma \in G_K$ maps $\bar{\mathcal{O}}$ to itself. If $\sigma(\bar{\mathfrak{m}}) = \bar{\mathfrak{m}}$, then $\sigma$ induces an automorphism $\bar\sigma$ of $\bar k$ over $k$. In fact every $\sigma$ fixes $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$: the valuation of $K$ extends uniquely to $\bar K$, and $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the set of elements of $\bar{\mathcal{O}}$ of positive valuation. The definitions of this chapter do not use this fact. They use the stabilizer $D$ of $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ in $G_K$, which is all of $G_K$.

**Definition 1.2.2 (residue map and inertia).** The *residue map* is the homomorphism $D \to \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$, $\sigma \mapsto \bar\sigma$. Its kernel $I_K$ is the *inertia group*.

**Proposition 1.2.3.** The residue map is surjective, and its kernel $I_K$ is the closed subgroup $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K^{ur})$.

*Proof.* Let $\sigma \in G_K$. The restriction of $\sigma$ to $K_n = K(\zeta)$ is determined by its action on $\zeta$, and the reduction of $\sigma(\zeta)$ is $\bar\sigma(\bar\zeta)$. By the theorem on unramified extensions, $\bar\sigma$ is trivial if and only if $\sigma$ fixes each $K_n$, that is, if $\sigma$ fixes $K^{ur}$. This proves the statement on the kernel. For surjectivity, let $\tau \in \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$. Its restrictions to the fields $k_n$ come from a compatible family of elements of $\mathrm{Gal}(K_n / K)$, thus from an element of $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ur} / K)$, and that element extends to $\bar K$. $\square$

A *Frobenius element* of $G_K$ is an element whose image under the residue map is $\mathrm{Frob}_k$. Two Frobenius elements differ by an element of $I_K$. Thus "the Frobenius" of $G_K$ is a coset of $I_K$ and not an element.
