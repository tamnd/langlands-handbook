## The Weil group

The quotient $G_K / I_K \cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ contains the dense subgroup $\mathbb{Z}$ generated by the Frobenius. The Weil group is the inverse image of this subgroup.

**Definition (Weil group).** The *Weil group* $W_K$ is the set of $\sigma \in D$ such that $\bar\sigma = \mathrm{Frob}_k^n$ for some integer $n$. The integer $n$ is unique, and it is the *degree* $d(\sigma)$ of $\sigma$.

Lean: `Langlands.WeilGroup`

The set $W_K$ is a subgroup of $G_K$. It contains $I_K$, and $d : W_K \to \mathbb{Z}$ is a surjective homomorphism with kernel $I_K$. Thus there is an exact sequence
$$1 \to I_K \to W_K \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0.$$
The group $W_K$ is not closed in $G_K$. Its closure is $G_K$, because $\mathbb{Z}$ is dense in $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$. If $W_K$ had the topology of a subspace of $G_K$, it would be a dense subgroup of a compact group and would have no useful structure. Weil gave it a finer topology.

**Definition (topology of the Weil group).** The topology of $W_K$ is the coarsest topology for which the inclusion $W_K \to G_K$ is continuous and the residue map $W_K \to \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$ is locally constant.

Lean: `Langlands.WeilGroup.instTopologicalSpace`

A subset $U$ of $W_K$ is open for this topology if and only if each set $U \cap d^{-1}(n)$ is open in the subspace topology. Thus $I_K$ is an open subgroup of $W_K$, its topology is the profinite topology of a closed subgroup of $G_K$, and each coset of $I_K$ is open.

**Proposition.** With this topology, $W_K$ is a topological group. The inclusion $W_K \to G_K$ is continuous, and the inertia group $I_K$ is open in $W_K$.

Lean: `Langlands.WeilGroup.isOpen_inertia`

*Proof.* The topology is the infimum of two topologies, the topology induced by the inclusion into $G_K$ and the topology induced by the residue map from the discrete topology on $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k)$. Each of the two maps is a homomorphism to a topological group, so each induced topology makes $W_K$ a topological group. The infimum of two group topologies is a group topology. The inclusion is continuous by the definition. The group $I_K$ is the inverse image of the point $\{1\}$, which is open in the discrete topology. $\square$

The group $W_K$ is locally compact, because $I_K$ is a compact open subgroup. It is not compact, because the quotient $W_K / I_K \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is infinite and discrete. Compare this with the decomposition $K^\times \cong \mathcal{O}_K^\times \times \mathbb{Z}$ of the first section: the compact part $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ corresponds to the inertia, and the discrete part generated by $\varpi$ corresponds to the powers of a Frobenius.

**Definition (geometric Frobenius).** An element $\Phi \in W_K$ is a *geometric Frobenius* if $\bar\Phi = \mathrm{Frob}_k^{-1}$, that is, if $\Phi$ acts on $\bar k$ as $x \mapsto x^{1/q}$.

Lean: `Langlands.WeilGroup.IsGeometricFrobenius`

There are two normalizations of local class field theory. In the arithmetic normalization, the reciprocity map sends a uniformizer to an arithmetic Frobenius. In the geometric normalization, it sends a uniformizer to a geometric Frobenius. Deligne introduced the geometric normalization, and the handbook uses it everywhere. With this choice the local Langlands correspondence sends the characters of $K^\times$ to the characters of $W_K$ without a sign, and it agrees with the action of Frobenius on the cohomology of varieties over finite fields. A statement with the wrong normalization is a different statement, and the project tests for this error in its statement checks.
