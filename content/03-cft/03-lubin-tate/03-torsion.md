## Torsion points and the Lubin-Tate extensions

Let $f \in \mathcal{F}_\varpi$. By Proposition 3.1.4, $F_f(\bar{\mathfrak{m}})$ is an $\mathcal{O}_K$-module with an action of $G_K$. For $n \ge 1$ let
$$\Lambda_{f,n} = \{x \in \bar{\mathfrak{m}} : [\varpi^n]_f(x) = 0\} = \{x \in \bar{\mathfrak{m}} : f^{(n)}(x) = 0\}$$
be its submodule of $\varpi^n$-torsion. The two descriptions agree because $[\varpi^n]_f = f^{(n)}$ by Theorem 3.2.3. The group $G_K$ maps $\Lambda_{f,n}$ to itself.

For $g \in \mathcal{F}_\varpi$ the isomorphism $[1]_{g,f}$ has coefficients in $\mathcal{O}_K$ and commutes with each $[a]$. Thus it maps $\Lambda_{f,n}$ onto $\Lambda_{g,n}$, and the map is $\mathcal{O}_K$-linear and $G_K$-equivariant. In questions about $\Lambda_{f,n}$ as a module with a Galois action, we can therefore choose $f$.

**Proposition 3.3.1 (the torsion module).** The $\mathcal{O}_K$-module $\Lambda_{f,n}$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_K / \varpi^n \mathcal{O}_K$. In particular it has $q^n$ elements, and its endomorphism ring is $\mathcal{O}_K / \varpi^n$.

*Proof.* We take $f = \varpi X + X^q$. Then $f^{(n)}$ is a monic polynomial of degree $q^n$, and it is congruent to $X^{q^n}$ modulo $\varpi$. Its constant term is 0, and its other coefficients except the leading one are divisible by $\varpi$. By the theory of the Newton polygon, all its roots in $\bar K$ have positive valuation, so they lie in $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$. The roots are simple. Indeed, the chain rule gives
$$(f^{(n)})'(x) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} f'(f^{(i)}(x)), \qquad f'(y) = \varpi + q y^{q-1}.$$
For $y \in \bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ the term $q y^{q-1}$ has valuation greater than $v(\varpi)$, since $q$ is 0 or divisible by $p$. Thus $f'(y) \ne 0$, and $(f^{(n)})'(x) \ne 0$ at each root $x$. Hence $\Lambda_{f,n}$ has exactly $q^n$ elements.

The module $\Lambda_{f,1}$ is killed by $\varpi$, so it is a vector space over $k$. It has $q$ elements, so it has dimension 1. The module $\Lambda_{f,n}$ is finite and killed by $\varpi^n$. By the structure theorem over the principal ideal domain $\mathcal{O}_K$, it is a sum $\bigoplus_j \mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^{e_j}$. The number of summands is the dimension of the $\varpi$-torsion $\Lambda_{f,1}$, which is 1. So $\Lambda_{f,n}$ is cyclic, and its order $q^n$ gives $\Lambda_{f,n} \cong \mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n$. $\square$

Each $\sigma \in G_K$ acts on $\Lambda_{f,n}$ by an $\mathcal{O}_K$-linear automorphism. By the proposition, this automorphism is multiplication by a unique unit of $\mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n$. This gives a homomorphism $G_K \to (\mathcal{O}_K / \varpi^n)^\times$.

**Definition 3.3.2 (Lubin-Tate extensions).** The field $K_{\varpi, n} = K(\Lambda_{f,n})$ is the extension of $K$ generated by $\Lambda_{f,n}$. The field $K_\varpi$ is the union of the fields $K_{\varpi,n}$ for $n \ge 1$.

The field $K_{\varpi,n}$ does not depend on the choice of $f \in \mathcal{F}_\varpi$. Indeed $\Lambda_{g,n} = [1]_{g,f}(\Lambda_{f,n})$, and $[1]_{g,f}(x) \in K(x)$ for each $x$, because $K(x)$ is complete and the coefficients of $[1]_{g,f}$ lie in $\mathcal{O}_K$.

**Theorem 3.3.3 (the Lubin-Tate extensions).** Let $n \ge 1$.
1. The extension $K_{\varpi,n} / K$ is totally ramified, abelian, and of degree $(q - 1) q^{n-1}$.
2. For each $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(K_{\varpi, n}/K)$ there is a unique $u \in (\mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n)^\times$ with $\sigma(\lambda) = [u]_f(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda \in \Lambda_{f,n}$. The map $\sigma \mapsto u$ is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\varpi,n}/K) \cong (\mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n)^\times$.
3. Let $f = \varpi X + X^q$ and let $\lambda \in \Lambda_{f,n} \smallsetminus \Lambda_{f,n-1}$. Then $\lambda$ is a uniformizer of $K_{\varpi,n}$, $K_{\varpi,n} = K(\lambda)$, and $N_{K_{\varpi,n}/K}(-\lambda) = \varpi$.

*Proof.* We use $f = \varpi X + X^q$, which is allowed by the remark after Definition 3.3.2. Consider the polynomial
$$g_n(X) = \frac{f^{(n)}(X)}{f^{(n-1)}(X)} = \frac{f(Y)}{Y} = \varpi + Y^{q-1}, \qquad Y = f^{(n-1)}(X),$$
with the convention $f^{(0)}(X) = X$. The polynomial $f^{(n-1)}$ is monic of degree $q^{n-1}$, congruent to $X^{q^{n-1}}$ modulo $\varpi$, with constant term 0. Thus $g_n$ is monic of degree $(q-1)q^{n-1}$, it is congruent to $X^{(q-1)q^{n-1}}$ modulo $\varpi$, and its constant term is $\varpi$. So $g_n$ is an Eisenstein polynomial. It is irreducible, and each of its roots generates a totally ramified extension of degree $(q-1)q^{n-1}$, of which it is a uniformizer. The roots of $g_n$ are the roots of $f^{(n)}$ that are not roots of $f^{(n-1)}$, since all roots of $f^{(n)}$ are simple. These are the elements of $\Lambda_{f,n} \smallsetminus \Lambda_{f,n-1}$.

Let $\lambda$ be such a root. By Proposition 3.3.1, $\lambda$ generates the cyclic module $\Lambda_{f,n}$, since it is not killed by $\varpi^{n-1}$. Thus each element of $\Lambda_{f,n}$ is $[a]_f(\lambda)$ for some $a$, and it lies in $K(\lambda)$. Hence $K_{\varpi,n} = K(\lambda)$, and this field is totally ramified of degree $(q-1)q^{n-1}$ with uniformizer $\lambda$.

The action of $G_K$ on $\Lambda_{f,n}$ gives a homomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\varpi,n}/K) \to (\mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n)^\times$. It is injective, because $\Lambda_{f,n}$ generates the field. Both groups have order $(q - 1)q^{n-1}$, so it is an isomorphism. In particular the Galois group is abelian. For the norm, the minimal polynomial of $\lambda$ is $g_n$, of degree $d = (q-1)q^{n-1}$. Thus $N(\lambda) = (-1)^d g_n(0)$ and $N(-\lambda) = (-1)^d N(\lambda) = g_n(0) = \varpi$. $\square$

**Example 3.3.4 ($\mathbb{Q}_p$).** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, $\varpi = p$ and $f = (1+X)^p - 1$. Then $f^{(n)}(X) = (1 + X)^{p^n} - 1$, so $\Lambda_{f,n} = \{\zeta - 1 : \zeta^{p^n} = 1\}$, and the field $K_{p,n}$ for $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^n})$. The polynomial $g_n$ is $\Phi_{p^n}(1 + X)$, the Eisenstein polynomial of Section 2.5. An automorphism $\sigma$ acts by $\sigma(\zeta - 1) = [a]_f(\zeta - 1) = \zeta^a - 1$. Thus the isomorphism of part 2 is the cyclotomic character $\sigma \mapsto a$ with $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^a$.

**Example 3.3.5 (the first layer).** Let $f = \varpi X + X^q$ and $n = 1$. The nonzero elements of $\Lambda_{f,1}$ are the roots of $X^{q-1} = -\varpi$. Thus $K_{\varpi,1} = K(\sqrt[q-1]{-\varpi})$, a tamely ramified Kummer extension of degree $q - 1$. By Example 3.2.5, $[\zeta]_f(\lambda) = \zeta \lambda$ for each root of unity $\zeta$ of order $q - 1$. For a unit $u$, let $\omega(u)$ be the root of unity of order dividing $q - 1$ with $\omega(u) \equiv u$ modulo $\varpi$, the Teichmuller representative. As $[u]_f$ depends on $u$ only modulo $\varpi$ on $\Lambda_{f,1}$, the automorphism attached to $u$ is $\lambda \mapsto \omega(u) \lambda$.

Taking the limit over $n$, the action of $G_K$ on all torsion points gives an isomorphism
$$\chi_\varpi : \mathrm{Gal}(K_\varpi / K) \to \varprojlim_n (\mathcal{O}_K/\varpi^n)^\times = \mathcal{O}_K^\times.$$
The composite $G_K \to \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ is the *Lubin-Tate character* of $\varpi$. For $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\varpi = p$ it is the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. The field $K_\varpi$ is totally ramified over $K$, so $K_\varpi \cap K^{ur} = K$.
