# Lubin-Tate theory

Chapter 2 deduced local reciprocity in the form of the Weil group from two inputs: the finite reciprocity law and the existence theorem. It also left the map $\mathrm{rec}_K$ implicit outside the unramified extensions. Lubin and Tate found in 1965 an explicit construction of the totally ramified abelian extensions of $K$. For $\mathbb{Q}_p$ it gives the fields $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^n})$, and the torsion points of a formal group take the place of the roots of unity.

Section 3.1 defines formal groups. Section 3.2 constructs the Lubin-Tate formal group of a uniformizer $\varpi$. Section 3.3 studies its torsion points and the fields $K_{\varpi, n}$ that they generate. Section 3.4 defines an explicit map $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$ and proves that it does not depend on $\varpi$. Section 3.5 deduces the norm groups of the fields $K_{\varpi,n}$, the local Kronecker-Weber theorem, the existence theorem and the formula $\mathrm{rec}_K = \mathrm{rec}_\varpi$.

The notation is that of Chapter 2. In addition $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the maximal ideal of the integral closure of $\mathcal{O}_K$ in $\bar K$, and for a power series $\varphi$ the text writes $\varphi^{(n)}$ for the $n$-fold composite $\varphi \circ \dots \circ \varphi$.
