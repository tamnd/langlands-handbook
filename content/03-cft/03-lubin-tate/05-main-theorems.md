## Local Kronecker-Weber and existence

This section uses the statements of Theorem 2.1.2 and Theorem 2.1.3, and the formal consequences in Section 2.1. It uses no other part of the cohomological theory.

**Theorem 3.5.1 (norm groups of the Lubin-Tate extensions).** Let $n \ge 1$. Then
$$N_{K_{\varpi,n}/K}(K_{\varpi,n}^\times) = \langle \varpi \rangle \times U^{(n)}.$$

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Let $N$ be the norm group, and let $u \in U^{(n)}$. By Theorem 3.3.3, part 3, applied to $\varpi$, the element $\varpi$ is the norm of $-\lambda$ for a suitable $\lambda \in K_{\varpi,n}$. Thus $\varpi \in N$. The same theorem applied to the uniformizer $u\varpi$ shows that $u\varpi$ is a norm from $K_{u\varpi,n}$. By Corollary 3.4.4 this field is $K_{\varpi,n}$. So $u \varpi \in N$, and $u = (u\varpi)/\varpi \in N$. This proves $\langle \varpi \rangle \times U^{(n)} \subset N$. The index of $\langle \varpi \rangle \times U^{(n)}$ in $K^\times = \langle \varpi \rangle \times \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ is the order of $(\mathcal{O}_K / \varpi^n)^\times$, which is $(q-1)q^{n-1}$. By Theorem 2.1.2 and Theorem 3.3.3, the index of $N$ is $[K_{\varpi,n} : K] = (q-1)q^{n-1}$. So the two groups are equal. $\square$

The proof needs Corollary 3.4.4 only to see that the units of $U^{(n)}$ are norms. For $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p$ odd, Section 2.5 found this by a direct computation with $p$-th powers. That computation fails for $p = 2$, and the theorem now gives the answer for all $p$. With $\varpi = p$ and $K_{p,n} = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^n})$,
$$N(\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^n})^\times) = p^{\mathbb{Z}} \times (1 + p^n \mathbb{Z}_p).$$
For $p = 2$ and $n = 2$ this says that the norm group of $\mathbb{Q}_2(i)$ is $2^{\mathbb{Z}} \times (1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2)$. So $-1$ is not a norm from $\mathbb{Q}_2(i)$, but $5 = 2^2 + 1^2$ and $2 = 1^2 + 1^2$ are norms.

**Corollary 3.5.2.** Let $n \ge 0$ and $m \ge 1$, and let $K_{\varpi,0} = K$. Then
$$N_{K_{\varpi,n} K_m / K}\big((K_{\varpi,n} K_m)^\times\big) = \langle \varpi^m \rangle \times U^{(n)}.$$

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.2.3 the norm group of $K_m$ is $\langle \varpi^m \rangle \times \mathcal{O}_K^\times$. By Theorem 3.5.1 the norm group of $K_{\varpi,n}$ is $\langle \varpi \rangle \times U^{(n)}$, and this also holds for $n = 0$. By Proposition 2.1.7, part 1, the norm group of the compositum is the intersection, which is $\langle \varpi^m \rangle \times U^{(n)}$. $\square$

The groups $\langle \varpi^m \rangle \times U^{(n)}$ are cofinal among the open subgroups of finite index. This gives the existence theorem.

**Theorem 3.5.3 (local existence).** Let $U$ be an open subgroup of finite index of $K^\times$. There is a unique finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$ with $N_{L/K}(L^\times) = U$. It satisfies $L \subset K_{\varpi,n} K_m$ for some $n \ge 0$ and $m \ge 1$.

*Proof.* The subgroups $U^{(n)}$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of 1 in $K^\times$, and $U$ is open, so $U^{(n)} \subset U$ for some $n$. Let $m$ be the index of $U$. The quotient $K^\times / U$ is a finite group of order $m$, so $\varpi^m \in U$. Thus $U$ contains $\langle \varpi^m \rangle \times U^{(n)}$, the norm group of $M = K_{\varpi,n} K_m$ by Corollary 3.5.2. By Proposition 2.1.7, part 3, $U$ is the norm group of a subfield $L$ of $M$. Uniqueness is Proposition 2.1.7, part 2. $\square$

This is Theorem 2.3.2. Together with Section 2.3 it completes the proof of Theorem 2.3.3, the local reciprocity law in the form of the Weil group, from the finite theorem and the classical theorem.

**Theorem 3.5.4 (local Kronecker-Weber).** Let $\varpi$ be a uniformizer of $K$. Then $K^{ab} = K_\varpi K^{ur}$. In particular $\mathbb{Q}_p^{ab}$ is the field generated over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by all roots of unity.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* The field $K_\varpi K^{ur}$ is abelian over $K$, by Theorem 3.3.3 and the product decomposition of Section 3.4. Let $L$ be a finite abelian extension of $K$. By Proposition 2.1.6 its norm group is open of finite index. As in the proof of Theorem 3.5.3, it contains $\langle \varpi^m \rangle \times U^{(n)}$ for some $m$ and $n$, which is the norm group of $K_{\varpi,n}K_m$. By Proposition 2.1.7, part 2, $L \subset K_{\varpi,n} K_m \subset K_\varpi K^{ur}$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\varpi = p$, the field $K_p$ is generated by the roots of unity of $p$-power order, by Example 3.3.4, and $K^{ur}$ is generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $p$. $\square$

The last step identifies the map. Theorem 2.1.3 asserts that $r_K$ exists, and Definition 3.4.1 gives a formula. They agree.

**Theorem 3.5.5 (the explicit reciprocity law).** For each uniformizer $\varpi$ of $K$, the map $r_K$ of Theorem 2.1.3 equals $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$. Thus for $u \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ and $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, the element $r_K(u\varpi^m)$ acts on $K^{ur}$ as $\Phi^m$ and on $\Lambda_{f,n}$ as $[u]_f$, for each $f \in \mathcal{F}_\varpi$ and each $n$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* Both maps are homomorphisms $K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(K^{ab}/K)$, by Theorem 3.5.4. The group $K^\times$ is generated by its uniformizers, so it is enough to compare them at a uniformizer $\varpi'$. By Theorem 3.4.3, $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi(\varpi') = \mathrm{rec}_{\varpi'}(\varpi')$. By Definition 3.4.1 this element is trivial on $K_{\varpi'}$ and equals $\Phi$ on $K^{ur}$. Now consider $r_K(\varpi')$. It equals $\Phi$ on $K^{ur}$ by property 1 of Theorem 2.1.3. For each $n$, $\varpi'$ is a norm from $K_{\varpi',n}$ by Theorem 3.3.3, part 3, so $r_K(\varpi')$ is trivial on $K_{\varpi',n}$ by property 2. Thus the two elements agree on $K_{\varpi'}$ and on $K^{ur}$. By Theorem 3.5.4 for $\varpi'$, they agree on $K^{ab} = K_{\varpi'} K^{ur}$. $\square$

For the map $\mathrm{rec}_K$ of Theorem 2.3.3 the same formula holds, with $W_K^{ab}$ viewed inside $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ab}/K)$ by Proposition 2.3.1. In the arithmetic normalization, $\mathrm{Art}_K(u \varpi^m)$ acts on $K^{ur}$ as $\mathrm{Frob}^m$ and on $\Lambda_{f,n}$ as $[u^{-1}]_f$. This is the form of the theorem in [Ser67], Section 3, and [Mil20], Chapter I.

**Example 3.5.6 ($\mathbb{Q}_p$).** Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $x = u p^m$ with $u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. By Example 3.3.4 and Theorem 3.5.5, $\mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(x)$ acts on the roots of unity as follows.
$$\mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(x)(\zeta) = \zeta^{u} \quad \text{if } \zeta^{p^n} = 1, \qquad \mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(x)(\xi) = \xi^{a^m} \quad \text{if } \xi^{e} = 1, \; p \nmid e,$$
where $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $ap \equiv 1$ modulo $e$. In particular $\mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(p)$ fixes all roots of unity of $p$-power order, and a unit $u$ fixes all roots of unity of order prime to $p$. The quadratic subfields give back Example 2.5.5. For instance, let $p$ be odd. The field $\mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt{p^*})$ with $p^* = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} p$ is the quadratic subfield of $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$. An element $\sigma$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ fixes it if and only if it is a square. Thus the character $\chi_{p^*}$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ is $\chi_{p^*}(u p^m) = \left(\frac{u}{p}\right)$, which is the Legendre symbol of the reduction of $u$.

**Remark 3.5.7 (what the construction proves).** The logic of the chapter is as follows. Theorem 2.1.2 gives the index of each norm group. Theorem 2.1.3 gives a map $r_K$ with the two properties. Lubin-Tate theory then gives the norm groups of the fields $K_{\varpi,n} K_m$, the existence theorem, the description of $K^{ab}$ and the explicit formula for $r_K$. One can also start from $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$. By Definition 3.4.1 and Corollary 3.5.2, the kernel of $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$ on $K_{\varpi,n}K_m$ is exactly the norm group of this field. So $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$ has property 2 for these fields. To get property 2 for their subfields, one needs a compatibility of $\mathrm{rec}_\varpi$ with norms, and [Mil20] proves it with the cohomological theory. Thus Lubin-Tate theory does not avoid cohomology in this account. It replaces the hard part of the existence theorem and makes the map explicit.
