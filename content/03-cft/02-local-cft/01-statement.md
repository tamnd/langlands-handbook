## The reciprocity law for finite abelian extensions

In this chapter $K$ is a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}_K$, residue field $k$ with $q$ elements, and normalized valuation $v = v_K$. A uniformizer is written $\varpi$. The absolute value is $|x| = q^{-v(x)}$. For $n \ge 1$ the group $U^{(n)} = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_K^n$ is the group of principal units of level $n$, and $U^{(0)} = \mathcal{O}_K^\times$. Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K$ and put $G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K / K)$. All extensions of $K$ in this chapter are subfields of $\bar K$. Let $K^{ab}$ be the union of the finite abelian extensions of $K$ in $\bar K$, and let $K_n$ be the unramified extension of degree $n$, as in Chapter 1.

On $K^{ur}$ there are two Frobenius elements. The arithmetic Frobenius acts on the residue field $\bar k$ as $x \mapsto x^q$, and the geometric Frobenius $\Phi$ is its inverse. For a finite unramified extension $L$ of $K$, the text writes $\Phi_{L/K}$ for the restriction of $\Phi$ to $L$. It generates $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$.

**Definition 2.1.1 (norm group).** Let $L$ be a finite extension of $K$. The *norm group* of $L$ is the subgroup $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ of $K^\times$.

The norm is multiplicative, so $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ is a subgroup. For $x \in K$ one has $N_{L/K}(x) = x^{[L:K]}$. Thus the norm group contains $(K^\times)^{[L:K]}$. The norm is transitive: if $K \subset L \subset M$, then $N_{M/K} = N_{L/K} \circ N_{M/L}$, so $N_{M/K}(M^\times) \subset N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. The valuation of a norm is controlled by the residue degree. If $L$ has residue degree $f$ and normalized valuation $v_L$, then
$$v_K(N_{L/K}(y)) = f \, v_L(y) \quad \text{for all } y \in L^\times.$$
This holds because the absolute value $|y|_L = |N_{L/K}(y)|_K$ is the normalized absolute value of $L$, and the residue field of $L$ has $q^f$ elements.

### The finite theorem

The first form of the main theorem treats one extension at a time.

**Theorem 2.1.2 (finite abelian local reciprocity).** Let $L$ be a finite abelian extension of $K$. There is a surjective homomorphism $K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ whose kernel is $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. In particular $K^\times / N_{L/K}(L^\times) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, and the index of the norm group is $[L : K]$.

The isomorphism of the theorem is not unique, because $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ may have many automorphisms. The classical theorem chooses the maps for all $L$ at once, so that they agree with each other and with the Frobenius.

**Theorem 2.1.3 (local reciprocity, classical form).** There is a unique homomorphism $r_K : K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(K^{ab} / K)$ with the following two properties.
1. For each uniformizer $\varpi$ of $K$, the restriction of $r_K(\varpi)$ to $K^{ur}$ is the geometric Frobenius $\Phi$.
2. For each finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$, the composite $r_{L/K} : K^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ of $r_K$ with the restriction is surjective, and its kernel is $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$.

Property 1 fixes $r_K$ on $K^{ur}$. Each $x \in K^\times$ is $x = \varpi^{n-1} (u \varpi)$ with $n = v(x)$ and $u$ a unit, and $u \varpi$ is a uniformizer. Thus $K^\times$ is generated by its uniformizers, and property 1 gives $r_K(x)|_{K^{ur}} = \Phi^{v(x)}$ for all $x$.

*Proof of uniqueness.* Let $r$ and $r'$ be two homomorphisms with properties 1 and 2. The group $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ab}/K)$ is the inverse limit of the groups $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. Thus it is enough to show that $r(x)$ and $r'(x)$ agree on each finite abelian $L$. Since $K^\times$ is generated by uniformizers, we can assume that $x = \varpi$ is a uniformizer. Let $m = [L : K]$ and let $M = L K_m$. Then $M$ is a finite abelian extension of $K$. Let $\sigma = r(\varpi)|_M$, and let $E \subset M$ be the fixed field of $\sigma$. The order of $\sigma|_L$ divides $m$, and $\sigma|_{K_m} = \Phi_{K_m / K}$ has order $m$. As $M$ is the compositum of $L$ and $K_m$, the order of $\sigma$ is exactly $m$. Thus $[M : E] = m$. The field $E$ is abelian over $K$, and $r_{E/K}(\varpi) = \sigma|_E = 1$. By property 2, $\varpi \in N_{E/K}(E^\times)$. By the valuation formula above, the residue degree of $E$ divides $v(\varpi) = 1$, so $E$ is totally ramified over $K$. Thus $E \cap K_m = K$, and the compositum $E K_m$ has degree $m$ over $E$. It follows that $E K_m = M$. Now apply the same facts to $r'$. Property 2 for $r'$ and $E$ gives $r'(\varpi)|_E = 1$, because $\varpi$ is a norm from $E$. Property 1 gives $r'(\varpi)|_{K_m} = \Phi_{K_m/K} = r(\varpi)|_{K_m}$. Thus $r(\varpi)$ and $r'(\varpi)$ agree on $E$ and on $K_m$, so they agree on $M$, and in particular on $L$. $\square$

The proof of existence of $r_K$ is long. The handbook does not give it, but the next remark describes its structure.

**Remark 2.1.4 (the cohomological proof).** Let $L / K$ be a finite Galois extension with group $G$ of order $n$. The proof has three steps. First, one computes the Brauer group: there is an isomorphism $\mathrm{inv}_K : H^2(\mathrm{Gal}(K^{ur}/K), (K^{ur})^\times) \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, built from the valuation and the Frobenius, and the inflation to $H^2(G_K, \bar K^\times)$ is an isomorphism. Second, one shows that $H^2(G, L^\times)$ is cyclic of order $n$ and that $H^1(G, L^\times) = 0$, which is Hilbert's Theorem 90. The *fundamental class* $u_{L/K}$ is the element of $H^2(G, L^\times)$ with invariant $1/n$. Third, the theorem of Tate and Nakayama says that the cup product with $u_{L/K}$ is an isomorphism $\hat H^{i}(G, \mathbb{Z}) \to \hat H^{i+2}(G, L^\times)$ for all $i$. For $i = -2$ this is an isomorphism $G^{ab} \to K^\times / N_{L/K}(L^\times)$, because $\hat H^{-2}(G, \mathbb{Z}) = H_1(G, \mathbb{Z}) = G^{ab}$ and $\hat H^0(G, L^\times) = K^\times / N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. Its inverse is the reciprocity map of $L/K$. With the sign conventions of Serre [Ser67], this inverse sends a uniformizer to the arithmetic Frobenius on an unramified $L$. Thus the map of Theorem 2.1.3 is $x \mapsto \theta(x^{-1})$, where $\theta$ is the map of [Ser67]. The details are in [Ser67] and in [Mil20], Chapters II and III.

**Remark 2.1.5 (the arithmetic normalization).** Many texts use the *Artin map* $\mathrm{Art}_K(x) = r_K(x^{-1})$. It sends a uniformizer to an arithmetic Frobenius on $K^{ur}$, and it has the same kernel on each finite extension. Serre [Ser67] and Milne [Mil20] use $\mathrm{Art}_K$. A formula in one normalization becomes a formula in the other after $x$ is replaced by $x^{-1}$. For a character $\chi$ of order 2, the two normalizations give the same character, because $\chi(x^{-1}) = \chi(x)$.

### Norm groups

The theorem has formal consequences for the norm groups. They do not need the cohomological proof, only the statement.

**Proposition 2.1.6 (norm groups are open).** Let $L$ be a finite abelian extension of $K$. The norm group $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ is an open subgroup of finite index of $K^\times$. Its intersection with $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ is $N_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_L^\times)$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* The index is finite by Theorem 2.1.2. By the valuation formula, $N_{L/K}(y)$ is a unit if and only if $y$ is a unit. This proves the second claim. The group $\mathcal{O}_L^\times$ is compact and the norm is continuous, so $N_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_L^\times)$ is compact, and thus closed in $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$. Its index in $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ is at most the index of $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ in $K^\times$, so it is finite. A closed subgroup of finite index is open, because its complement is a finite union of closed cosets. Thus the norm group contains an open subgroup of $K^\times$, and it is open. $\square$

**Proposition 2.1.7 (the lattice of norm groups).** Let $L$ and $L'$ be finite abelian extensions of $K$.
1. $N_{LL'/K}((LL')^\times) = N_{L/K}(L^\times) \cap N_{L'/K}(L'^\times)$.
2. $L \subset L'$ if and only if $N_{L'/K}(L'^\times) \subset N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. In particular $L = L'$ if and only if the two norm groups are equal.
3. Each subgroup of $K^\times$ that contains $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ is the norm group of a subfield of $L$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* For part 1, the inclusion of the left side in the right side follows from the transitivity of the norm. Conversely, let $x$ be in both norm groups. Then $r_{L/K}(x) = 1$ and $r_{L'/K}(x) = 1$ by Theorem 2.1.3. The element $r_{LL'/K}(x)$ restricts to these two elements, so it is trivial on $L$ and on $L'$. Thus it is trivial on $LL'$, and $x$ is a norm from $LL'$.

For part 2, the direct implication is the transitivity of the norm. Conversely, assume that $N_{L'/K}(L'^\times) \subset N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. By part 1, the norm group of $LL'$ equals the norm group of $L'$. By Theorem 2.1.2, the indices of these groups are $[LL' : K]$ and $[L' : K]$. Thus the two degrees are equal, $LL' = L'$, and $L \subset L'$.

For part 3, let $U$ be a subgroup that contains $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. The image $r_{L/K}(U)$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. Let $E$ be its fixed field. Then $r_{E/K}(x) = 1$ if and only if $r_{L/K}(x) \in r_{L/K}(U)$. Since $U$ contains the kernel of $r_{L/K}$, this holds if and only if $x \in U$. Thus $N_{E/K}(E^\times) = \ker r_{E/K} = U$. $\square$

The inertia subgroup of a finite abelian extension also has a description in terms of $K^\times$. For a finite abelian extension $L$ with residue degree $f$, let $I(L/K) \subset \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ be the inertia subgroup. It is the kernel of the restriction to $L \cap K^{ur} = K_f$, so it has index $f$.

**Proposition 2.1.8 (units and inertia).** Let $L$ be a finite abelian extension of $K$. Then $r_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times) = I(L/K)$.

*Proof.* Let $u$ be a unit. Then $r_K(u)$ acts on $K^{ur}$ as $\Phi^{v(u)} = 1$, so $r_{L/K}(u)$ is trivial on $K_f$, and $r_{L/K}(u) \in I(L/K)$. For the reverse inclusion, compare the indices. The map $r_{L/K}$ induces an isomorphism from $K^\times / N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. Under this isomorphism the subgroup $r_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ corresponds to $\mathcal{O}_K^\times N_{L/K}(L^\times)$. The valuation gives an isomorphism $K^\times / \mathcal{O}_K^\times N_{L/K}(L^\times) \cong \mathbb{Z} / v(N_{L/K}(L^\times))$. By the valuation formula, $v(N_{L/K}(L^\times)) = f \mathbb{Z}$. Thus $r_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ has index $f$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, the same index as $I(L/K)$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.1.9.** A finite abelian extension $L$ of $K$ is totally ramified if and only if its norm group contains a uniformizer. It is unramified if and only if its norm group contains $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$.

Lean: not stated yet

*Proof.* If $L$ has residue degree $f$, the valuations of the norms form $f \mathbb{Z}$. Thus a uniformizer is a norm only if $f = 1$. Conversely, if $f = 1$, then $\mathcal{O}_K^\times N_{L/K}(L^\times) = K^\times$, so some $u \varpi$ is a norm, and $u \varpi$ is a uniformizer. For the second claim, $\mathcal{O}_K^\times \subset N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ holds if and only if $r_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ is trivial. By Proposition 2.1.8 this means that $I(L/K)$ is trivial, that is, that $L$ is unramified. $\square$
