# Global class field theory

Which primes $p$ can be written as $p = x^2 + 5y^2$ with integers $x$ and $y$? Fermat and Euler found the answer by experiment, and Lagrange proved it: for $p \ne 2, 5$, this happens exactly when $p \equiv 1$ or $9$ modulo $20$. The condition $p \equiv 1, 3, 7, 9 \pmod{20}$ says that $p$ splits in $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, so $p = \mathfrak{p} \bar{\mathfrak{p}}$. The ring $\mathcal{O}_F = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ has class number 2, and $p = x^2 + 5y^2$ holds exactly when $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal. So the question asks for a description of the primes of $F$ in the trivial ideal class, and the answer is a congruence condition modulo 20. Class field theory explains this. There is an abelian extension $H = F(i)$ of $F$, unramified at every place, with $\mathrm{Gal}(H/F) \cong \mathrm{Cl}_F$. A prime $\mathfrak{p}$ is principal if and only if it splits completely in $H$. The field $H$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ and lies in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{20})$, so the splitting of $p$ in $H$ is a congruence condition modulo 20.

Global class field theory describes all the abelian extensions of a number field $F$ in terms of $F$ itself. The description has the same shape as the local one of Chapter 2, with the idele class group $C_F$ in the place of $K^\times$. There is a continuous surjective homomorphism $\mathrm{art}_F : C_F \to \mathrm{Gal}(F^{ab}/F)$, the global Artin map. It is the product of the local reciprocity maps of Chapter 2 over all the places of $F$, and it is trivial on the principal ideles. This last fact is the reciprocity law. It is a statement of the same kind as the product formula $\prod_v |x|_v = 1$, and it contains the law of quadratic reciprocity and all its generalizations. The kernel of $\mathrm{art}_F$ is the connected component of $C_F$. The open subgroups of finite index of $C_F$ correspond to the finite abelian extensions of $F$, and the norm group of an extension determines the extension. For $F = \mathbb{Q}$ every abelian extension is cyclotomic. In the language of Volume 0, the theorem says that the Hecke characters of finite order of $F$ are the characters of finite order of the Galois group, through $\mathrm{art}_F$. This is the global Langlands correspondence for $GL_1$, and it is the result that Chapter 6 used to prove the analytic properties of abelian Artin $L$-functions.

We follow [Mil20], Chapters V to VII, and Tate's chapter VII of [CF67]. For the abstract class field theory that the Lean port uses we follow [Neu99], Chapters IV and VI. In this chapter $F$ is a number field, $\bar F$ is a fixed algebraic closure, and all extensions of $F$ are in $\bar F$. The idele group is $J_F$, the idele class group is $C_F = J_F / F^\times$, and the norm of an idele is $|x|_\mathbb{A}$, as in Volume 1, Chapter 3. We use the geometric normalization of Volume 0, Chapter 3. Thus the local reciprocity map $r_K$ of Theorem 2.1.3 sends a uniformizer to a geometric Frobenius, and the global map sends a uniformizer at an unramified place $v$ to $\mathrm{Frob}_v^{-1}$, where $\mathrm{Frob}_v$ is the arithmetic Frobenius of Volume 1, Definition 1.4.7. The classical ideal-theoretic Artin map, which sends a prime to its arithmetic Frobenius, is the inverse of our map on ideles, and we write $\mathrm{Art}_F(x) = \mathrm{art}_F(x)^{-1}$ for it.
