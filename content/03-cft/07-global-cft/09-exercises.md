## Exercises

1. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}$ and $N \ge 3$. Use Proposition 7.1.4 to show directly that $\mathrm{Cl}_{N\infty} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_N \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times / \{\pm 1\}$. Compare with Example 7.1.5.
2. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $\mathfrak{m} = (3)$. Show that $\mathrm{Cl}_\mathfrak{m}$ is the quotient of $(\mathbb{Z}[i]/3)^\times \cong \mathbb{F}_9^\times$ by the image of the units $\{\pm 1, \pm i\}$, so that it has order 2. Show that $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12}) = F(\sqrt{3})$ is unramified over $F$ outside $(3)$ and tamely ramified at $(3)$. Deduce that $F_\mathfrak{m} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$.
3. Let $\mathfrak{m}$ and $\mathfrak{n}$ be moduli of $F$. Show that $F_\mathfrak{m} \cap F_\mathfrak{n} = F_{\gcd(\mathfrak{m}, \mathfrak{n})}$ and $F_\mathfrak{m} F_\mathfrak{n} \subset F_{\mathrm{lcm}(\mathfrak{m}, \mathfrak{n})}$. Give an example over $\mathbb{Q}$ where the inclusion is strict. (Hint: take $\mathfrak{m} = 4$ and $\mathfrak{n} = \infty$.)
4. Let $L/F$ be finite abelian and let $\chi$ be a character of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$, with fixed field $L_\chi$ of $\ker \chi$. Use Theorem 4.2.3 and Proposition 7.6.5 to show that the Artin conductor of $\chi$ is the finite part of $\mathfrak{f}(L_\chi/F)$. With Corollary 6.4.3, deduce that $\mathfrak{d}_{L/F} = \prod_\chi \mathfrak{f}(L_\chi/F)_0$. Check the formula for $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ with $p$ an odd prime.
5. Let $L = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{d})$ with $d \in \{-1, 3, 2, -2, 6, -6\}$. Find the norm group $N(L^\times)$ in $\mathbb{Q}_2^\times$, and show that the conductor exponent is 2 for $d = -1, 3$ and 3 for $d = \pm 2, \pm 6$. Deduce Example 7.6.6 at the prime 2.
6. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$. Its class number is 3. Show that the splitting field $E$ of $x^3 - x - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has degree 6 and contains $F$, and that $E/F$ is unramified. (Hint: the discriminant of the polynomial is $-23$.) Deduce that $E = H_F$. Show that a prime $p \ne 23$ has the form $x^2 + xy + 6y^2$ if and only if $x^3 - x - 1$ has three roots modulo $p$.
7. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$. Show that $h_F = 1$ and that the fundamental unit $2 + \sqrt{3}$ has norm $1$. Deduce from Example 7.1.6 that $h_F^+ = 2$. Show that $H_F^+ = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}, i)$, and check directly that this extension of $F$ is unramified at all finite places and ramified at both real places.
8. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathfrak{p} = (2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$. Show that $\mathfrak{p}$ is not principal and that $\mathfrak{p}^2 = (2)$. Show that $\mathfrak{p} \mathcal{O}_H = (1 + i) \mathcal{O}_H$ for $H = F(i)$. This is Theorem 7.6.10 for $F$. Show also that the prime $(3, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$ becomes principal in $H$.
9. Let $G$ be a finite group and $H$ a central subgroup of index $n$. Show that the transfer $G \to H$ is $g \mapsto g^n$. Deduce the group theoretic statement in the proof of Theorem 7.6.10 for the quaternion group $Q_8$, where $G' = \{\pm 1\}$.
10. Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension and let $\chi_{E/F}$ be the Hecke character of order 2 with kernel $F^\times N_{E/F}(J_E)$. Show that $\chi_{E/F, v}(\varpi_v) = 1$ if $v$ splits in $E$ and $-1$ if $v$ is inert. Deduce that $\zeta_E(s) = \zeta_F(s) L(s, \chi_{E/F})$. For $F = \mathbb{Q}$, show that $\chi_{E/\mathbb{Q}}$ is the Hecke character of the Kronecker symbol $\left(\frac{D}{\cdot}\right)$ of Example 5.5.6.
11. Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension with $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) = \{1, \sigma\}$, and let $j \in W_{E/F}$ map to $\sigma$. Show that $j^2 \in C_E$ is fixed by $\sigma$, so that $j^2 \in C_F$. Show that the class of $j^2$ in $C_F / N_{E/F}(C_E)$ does not depend on the choice of $j$. Use $H^2(\mathrm{Gal}(E/F), C_E) \cong \hat H^0(\mathrm{Gal}(E/F), C_E)$ and Theorem 7.8.2 to show that this class generates the group $C_F / N_{E/F}(C_E)$ of order 2. Compare with the local group $W_\mathbb{R}$, where $j^2 = -1$.
12. The Lean structure `Langlands.IsArtinMap` asks for three properties of a homomorphism $\mathrm{art} : C_K \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$: it is continuous, it is surjective, and $\mathrm{art}(\varpi_v)^{-1}$ is the arithmetic Frobenius at each finite place $v$ that is unramified in $L$, for every uniformizer $\varpi_v$ of $K_v$. Show that the third property makes $\mathrm{art}$ trivial on $\mathcal{O}_v^\times$ at these places. Use the weak approximation theorem to show that a homomorphism with the first and third properties is unique. So the statement `Langlands.GlobalReciprocity` determines the map of Definition 7.2.1.
13. Let $L/F$ be a finite extension, not necessarily Galois, and let $E$ be the maximal abelian subextension of $L/F$. Show that $N_{L/F}(C_L)$ is open of finite index in $C_F$. (Hint: use the Galois closure of $L/F$.) By Theorem 7.4.3, $N_{L/F}(C_L) = N_{M/F}(C_M)$ for a finite abelian extension $M/F$. Use Proposition 7.2.2, part 4, with the field $L$ in the place of $E$, to show that $\mathrm{art}_{M/F}(N_{L/F}(C_L)) = \mathrm{Gal}(M/M \cap L)$. Deduce that $M \subset E$ and that $N_{L/F}(C_L) = N_{E/F}(C_E)$.

## Notes and history

Weber defined the ray class groups in 1897, and he conjectured that each of them is the Galois group of an abelian extension. Hilbert conjectured in 1898 the main properties of the field $H_F$ of Theorem 7.6.8 and proved them for some quadratic fields. Furtwängler proved the existence of $H_F$ in 1907. Takagi proved the existence theorem and the classification of Corollary 7.4.4 in "Über eine Theorie des relativ Abel'schen Zahlkörpers" (J. Coll. Sci. Tokyo, 1920), in the language of ideal groups. He did not have a canonical isomorphism with the Galois group. Artin conjectured the reciprocity law in 1923 and proved it in "Beweis des allgemeinen Reziprozitätsgesetzes" (Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg, 1927), with an idea of Chebotarev. In 1929 Artin reduced the principal ideal theorem to group theory, and Furtwängler proved the group theoretic statement in 1930. Hasse's reports on class field theory, of 1926, 1927 and 1930, made the theory known.

Chevalley introduced the ideles in 1936 and gave in "La théorie du corps de classes" (Ann. of Math., 1940) a proof without analysis, in which the second inequality is proved by algebraic means. Weil defined the global Weil group in "Sur la théorie du corps de classes" (J. Math. Soc. Japan, 1951). The cohomological form of the theory, with class formations and the fundamental class, is due to Artin and Tate, in a seminar of 1951 and 1952 whose notes appeared as *Class field theory* in 1967. Tate's chapter of [CF67] is a short account of it. Neukirch gave the abstract class field theory of Theorem 7.3.6 in the 1980s. It avoids the cohomology of degree 2. It is in [Neu99], Chapter IV, and it is the route of the Lean port.

Golod and Shafarevich proved in 1964 that the class field tower can be infinite, and Roquette gave the example $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 \cdot 13})$ in his chapter of [CF67]. The problem of explicit generators for $F^{ab}$ is Hilbert's twelfth problem, see Remark 7.5.6. Langlands proposed the group $\mathcal{L}_F$ of Remark 7.8.6 in 1979, in his paper on automorphic representations, Shimura varieties and motives. Its existence is still open.

The Lean project proves the three statements of global class field theory on its map: global reciprocity `Langlands.GlobalReciprocity` (node LGVAKJJD8Y), the existence theorem `Langlands.GlobalExistence` (node LGX8K4ZEGW) and the theorem of Kronecker and Weber `Langlands.KroneckerWeber` (node LG06WS8A1Y). The proofs are in `Langlands/Bridge/NYLLGlobal.lean`, from the port of Remark 7.3.7. The port also has the ray class fields, the conductor and the two Hilbert class fields of Section 7.6. The principal ideal theorem, the bijection of Theorem 7.7.1 in the form of characters and the global Weil group of Section 7.8 are not stated in the Lean project yet.
